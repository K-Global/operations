# A03 — Safety Management System (SMS) and Quality · OM A

Company safety policy, risk management, and safety/quality assurance.

**Version** v1.0 · **Updated** 2026-07-25 · **Status** Verified

> Read-me: framework chapter. K Global is a VATSIM virtual airline — it implements the **principles** of an ICAO Annex 19 Safety Management System, scaled to a simulation environment. This is not, and does not claim to be, a regulator-approved safety programme. Flags: 🟥 restriction/hazard/hard requirement · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This chapter sets out how K Global manages safety and quality across its simulated operation. It adopts the four-component / twelve-element SMS framework of **ICAO Annex 19** and **ICAO Doc 9859 (Safety Management Manual)** as an organising model:

1. Safety policy and objectives
2. Safety risk management
3. Safety assurance
4. Safety promotion

At VA scale, "safety" means the integrity, realism, and disciplined conduct of our simulated flight operations — accurate procedures, honest reporting, and continuous improvement — rather than the prevention of real-world injury. The value is in exercising the *discipline* of a safety management system, not in regulatory compliance. 🟩

## 2. Safety policy and objectives

**Company safety-policy statement — 🟩 ratified 2026-07-25.** The following statement is K Global's adopted safety-policy commitment, **ratified 2026-07-25 (of record)**:

> *K Global is committed to conducting its simulated operations to the highest realistic standard of safety and procedural discipline. We foster an open, just reporting culture in which any member may raise a safety or realism concern without fear of blame. We commit to identifying hazards to the integrity of our operation, managing the associated risk, and continuously improving. Safety and realism take priority over the accumulation of hours, ranks, or awards.*

As the ratified accountable commitment of the operation, this statement is now of record. 🟩

**Safety objectives (framework):** operate published SOPs and OM procedures faithfully; report occurrences honestly and promptly (see A12); keep dispatch within declared capability and approvals (Fleet Capability Matrix, A09); measure and improve. 🟩

**Accountability.** Overall accountability for the safety and quality of the operation rests with K Global management. Every member holds a personal responsibility for the safety and realism of their own flights. 🟩

## 3. Hazard identification and safety risk management

K Global applies the standard **hazard → risk → mitigation** cycle:

- **Hazard identification.** Hazards to operational integrity are surfaced from occurrence reports (A12), member feedback, route/airport briefing reviews, and post-event debriefs. Anyone may raise a hazard. 🟩
- **Risk assessment.** Each identified hazard is assessed for likelihood and severity of its effect on the realism, integrity, or safe conduct of the simulated operation (a simple risk-matrix approach is sufficient at VA scale). 🟩 (risk-matrix approach adopted 2026-07-25)
- **Risk mitigation.** Where risk is judged unacceptable, a mitigation is defined — an SOP change, a briefing update, a training point (OM D), a MEL/capability clarification (A09), or a policy note. Mitigations are tracked to closure. 🟩
- **Risk register.** Accepted hazards and their mitigations are recorded in a **safety risk register**, fed principally by the occurrence-reporting loop (A12). 🟧 [register to be established]

## 4. Safety assurance

Safety assurance confirms that mitigations work and that the operation performs as intended:

- **Safety performance monitoring.** Track a small set of indicators appropriate to a VA — occurrence-report volume and closure rate, recurrence of the same issue, procedure-adherence trends from reviews. 🟧 [indicators to be defined]
- **Internal review / audit.** Periodic review that OM content is current and that operations are conducted per SOP. Content-currency is managed through the OM governance process (see the content governance). 🟩
- **Management of change.** Fleet, route-network, hub, or procedure changes are reviewed for their safety/realism impact before adoption. 🟩
- **Continuous improvement.** Findings feed back into the risk register (§3) and into SOP and training updates. 🟩

## 5. Safety promotion and just culture

- **Just culture.** K Global operates a **just-culture** principle: honest reports of errors and occurrences are welcomed and are not used punitively; only wilful misconduct, gross negligence, or repeated deliberate breach fall outside protection. The reporting mechanics live in **A12**. 🟩
- **Training.** Safety and procedural training is delivered through **OM D Training**; this chapter owns the *policy*, OM D owns the *delivery*. 🟩
- **Communication.** Safety messages, lessons learned, and briefing updates are promoted through the operation's normal member-communication channels and the Ops-Comms domain (NOTAMs / Alerts). 🟩

## 6. Quality and compliance monitoring

The quality/compliance-monitoring function checks that the operation actually complies with its own manuals and standards, and closes the loop with occurrence reporting:

- OM content is kept accurate, current, and internally consistent (governance in the content governance §1). 🟩
- Deviations found in review are logged as occurrences (A12) and, where systemic, raised as hazards (§3). 🟩
- The compliance-monitoring output feeds the safety-assurance picture (§4). 🟩
