# A13 — Rules of the Air and Airspace Compliance · OM A

Company policy for compliance with the rules of the air and airspace requirements.

**Version** v1.0 · **Updated** 2026-07-25 · **Status** Verified

> Read-me: this chapter states K Global's **policy** for complying with the rules of the air and airspace requirements — minimum flight altitudes, IFR/VFR discipline, the RVSM/PBN/datalink airspace-approval policy, FIR/airspace compliance, and altimetry / transition-altitude policy. It sets policy and points to the *method* — the RVSM/PBN/datalink/oceanic procedures in OM E and the airspace briefs in OM C. Flag legend: 🟥 restriction/hazard/hard requirement · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 1. General compliance policy

Every K Global flight is conducted in accordance with the **ICAO rules of the air** and the rules of every State whose airspace it enters or overflies. Where a State's rule is **more restrictive** than the company standard, the **State's rule applies**. The commander is responsible for compliance in flight; dispatch (OM A02) provides the airspace, NOTAM and restriction picture at planning and in flight.

- 🟥 **The stricter rule wins.** Where company policy, an overflown State's requirement, and an ATC clearance appear to conflict, the **most restrictive** safe course is taken and any doubt is resolved with ATC before acting.
- 🟩 **The commander remains responsible despite a clearance.** An ATC or radar clearance does not relieve the commander of responsibility for terrain and obstacle clearance, airspace compliance, or collision avoidance in visual conditions.

---

## 2. Minimum flight altitudes

- 🟥 **Never below the minimum operating altitude.** For any part of a flight the minimum operating altitude must never be below the relevant **Minimum Safe Altitude (MSA)** or **Sector Safe Altitude (SSA)** for the phase — or, when under an approved radar unit, the **radar-cleared altitude** — for night or IMC flight. These altitudes give a defined vertical clearance over terrain and known obstacles: **1,000 ft** for obstacles up to and including 5,000 ft elevation, **2,000 ft** above 5,000 ft.
- 🟧 **Radar-cleared altitudes may be narrower than company MSAs.** A radar-cleared altitude provides a smaller margin (typically 1,000 ft over a narrower band) than the wider-band MSAs on company/planning documents, so the two can differ. A radar clearance may be accepted, **but if there is any doubt about the aircraft's position, operate to the relevant MSA/SSA.**
- 🟥 **Adverse-condition and terrain corrections.** In strong wind, moderate/severe turbulence or mountain-wave conditions the minimum operating altitude is increased so the relevant MSA/SSA is not infringed (a substantial increase — on the order of 2,000 ft — where high winds are forecast at the MSA/SSA level, and greater clearance over a ridge in mountain-wave conditions). Where a State's established minimum altitude is higher than the company figure, the higher value applies.
- 🟩 **Off-route and re-route.** The planned altitude for each route segment must meet the relevant MSA. If a deviation from the planned track becomes necessary (weather, direct routing), terrain and obstacles near the new track are assessed and adequate clearance ensured before the deviation.

## 3. IFR / VFR discipline

- 🟥 **K Global operates IFR.** Company flights are conducted under **IFR**. An IFR flight plan must **not** be cancelled in flight, and **VFR-on-top / VFR** clearances must not be requested or accepted.
- 🟥 **Collision avoidance in visual conditions remains the crew's.** Even in controlled airspace, ATC may not provide information on VFR traffic; when maintaining VMC on an IFR flight plan the commander is responsible for collision avoidance. A sharp visual and TCAS lookout, with the right-of-way rules, is the foremost means of avoiding conflict — especially where no ATC service is available.
- 🟩 **Clearances are read back and cross-checked.** Clearances are read back in full (in particular runway entry/crossing/line-up/take-off/landing instructions, route/departure clearances, levels, altimeter settings and transition levels, headings, speeds, transponder and frequency instructions). The FMS is cross-checked against the clearance for correct runway and departure/arrival routing; any doubt is clarified with ATC.

## 4. Airspace-approval policy — RVSM, PBN and datalink

K Global holds and operates within the airspace approvals its network requires. This chapter states the **policy** that these approvals are held and complied with; the **method** — the equipment, height-keeping/navigation performance, flight-plan coding, entry/exit and contingency procedures — lives in the OM E documents cross-linked below.

- 🟥 **RVSM.** K Global's RVSM-approved fleet complies with the **Reduced Vertical Separation Minimum** regime (1,000 ft vertical separation FL290–FL410) — required dual independent altimetry, altitude-hold and alerting, height-keeping performance and the monitoring programme, correct flight-plan equipment coding, and the contingency set. *Method:* OM E `RVSM Operations.md`.
- 🟥 **PBN / RNP.** Flights are planned and flown to the **Performance-Based Navigation** specifications required by the route and terminal procedures (RNAV/RNP en-route, RNP APCH incl. LPV, and RNP AR where authorised), within K Global's navigation approvals. *Method:* OM E `PBN and RNP Operations.md`.
- 🟥 **Datalink / oceanic.** Where the airspace mandates it, flights comply with the **datalink** requirements (CPDLC / ADS-C, FANS, PBCS) and oceanic procedures. *Method:* OM E `Datalink and Oceanic Procedures.md`.
- 🟩 **Approvals are a company matter.** The holding of these approvals, the approved fleet, and the required equipment status are company/operational-control matters (OM A02 / OM A09); the crew's duty is to fly within them and to flight-plan the correct capability codes so crew and ATC hold the same expectation.

## 5. FIR and airspace compliance

- 🟥 **Comply with the airspace flown.** Every flight conforms to the ATC reporting and airspace procedures of the State/FIR it is operating in — position reporting, level/track reporting discipline (using only "leaving / passing / reaching"; reporting an inadvertent level exceedance immediately), and the reporting interval where none is laid down. Aircraft must not enter aerodrome traffic zones, danger or prohibited areas without the required clearance, and route deviations are planned clear of such areas.
- 🟩 **Brief the airspace before flying it.** The airspace and FIR to be flown or overflown are briefed from the **OM C Airspace** briefs, which apply the regimes above to each region/FIR; the per-aerodrome context is in the OM C aerodrome briefs.

---

## 6. Altimetry and transition altitude / level

- 🟥 **Use the published State transition altitude / level.** K Global sets altimetry by the **published transition altitude (TA) and transition level (TL) of the State/aerodrome** — the values are **not** a fixed company figure. [K Global OpsSpec 2026-07-25] Standard altimeter-setting discipline applies: aerodrome QNH below the TA, standard pressure (1013.2 hPa / 29.92 inHg) above the transition level, with the standby altimeter kept on QNH until above the MSA and TA on departure.
- 🟩 **Cross-check the setting change.** Altimeter settings and transition levels are read back and set as a crew action at the transition, and levels reported to ATC are passed to the nearest 100 ft to support Mode-C verification.

---

## 7. Oceanic and lateral-offset discipline (pointer)

- 🟩 **SLOP and oceanic discipline.** In oceanic and remote airspace, strategic lateral-offset (**SLOP**) and the oceanic contingency/weather-deviation procedures are applied as the airspace requires. This is a policy pointer only — the operating detail (offsets, contingency turns, weather-deviation procedure, datalink position reporting) is in **OM E `Datalink and Oceanic Procedures.md`**, applied per region in the OM C North Atlantic / Arctic / oceanic airspace briefs.
