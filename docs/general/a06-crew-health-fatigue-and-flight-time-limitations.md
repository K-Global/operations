# A06 — Crew Health, Fatigue and Flight-Time Limitations · OM A

Fitness to fly, fatigue management, and the K Global adaptation of "flight-hours / off-duty" for a VATSIM virtual airline — not a real FTL scheme, stated honestly.

**Version** v1.0 · **Updated** 2026-07-25 · **Status** Verified

> Flag legend: 🟥 restriction/hazard/hard requirement · 🟧 caution/unverified/to-ratify · 🟩 normal.

## Scope

- The fatigue-management principle for a hobby operation.
- Fitness to fly — self-declaration and in-flight incapacitation.
- The K Global flight-hours / off-duty scheme, and why it is **not** a regulatory FTL.
- A light reference to the real ICAO/EASA FTL frameworks the scheme gestures at.

---

## 1. Why this chapter is different

In a real airline, flight-time limitations (FTL) exist to keep fatigued crews out of cockpits, and they are hard law. **K Global is a VATSIM virtual airline flown for enjoyment.** Nobody is being paid, nobody is rostered against their will, and the "duty" is a person choosing to sit at a simulator. The manual states this plainly: **the scheme in this chapter is not a regulatory FTL and cannot substitute for one.** 🟩

What K Global keeps from the real discipline is the **principle** — a fatigued pilot flies worse, and long unbroken hours at a screen are bad for the person as well as the flight. The rules below exist to protect the member's wellbeing and to keep the operation realistic, not to satisfy an authority. 🟩

## 2. Fatigue-management principle

🟩 The single governing idea: **a pilot must not begin, or continue, a sector when tired to the point that safety of the (simulated) operation or their own wellbeing is compromised.** In practice, for a solo VATSIM pilot flying a two-crew aeroplane, this means:

- **Self-honesty is the whole mechanism.** There is no roster, no crewing desk and no medical officer to stop a tired pilot — only the pilot's own judgement. Treat "am I too tired to fly this well?" as a real go/no-go item.
- **Long-haul is where it bites.** A ten-hour sector flown solo overnight is a genuine fatigue exposure. The in-flight-rest / off-duty provision (§4) is the tool that makes long sectors sustainable, mirroring the real augmented-crew rest that keeps a live pilot on the flight deck.
- **Health duty of care.** K Global explicitly discourages excessive unbroken time at a screen for health reasons; the hours framework (§4) exists partly to nudge against marathon sessions flown only to accumulate hours or rank.

## 3. Fitness to fly

🟥 A pilot self-certifies fitness before every sector. Do not fly when unfit — for example when unwell, significantly fatigued, or under the influence of alcohol or any substance that would make flying a real aeroplane unlawful or unsafe. This self-declaration is the K Global stand-in for a medical certificate (there is no aeromedical requirement to join). 🟩

**In-flight incapacitation.** In the two-crew ideal, a co-pilot takes over if the Commander is incapacitated. For the solo VATSIM pilot the realistic action is simpler and non-punitive: if the pilot becomes unable to continue (real-life interruption, illness, or fatigue), **secure the aircraft and disconnect** rather than press on degraded. The online network's own procedures for leaving the network apply. There is no penalty for ending a sector early for a genuine fitness reason. 🟩

## 4. The K Global flight-hours / off-duty scheme

This scheme adapts the real "flight time, duty time and rest" structure into two simple, platform-enforceable controls. It is a **wellbeing-and-realism** device, not an FTL. 🟩

### 4.1 Rolling flight-hours guideline 🟩 ratified 2026-07-25

K Global applies a **rolling flight-hours guideline** over a moving window to discourage unrealistic hour-farming and marathon sessions — as a working figure, on the order of **~135 logged flight hours in a rolling 30-day period**. 🟩 The window and the number were ratified 2026-07-25 as the approved soft guideline (tunable later). The intent is a soft ceiling that flags when a member is flying far beyond a realistic amount, not a punitive cap; senior ranks may be permitted to waive it. This is deliberately generous — it exists to catch the extreme case, not to police normal enthusiasm.

### 4.2 In-flight off-duty (rest) on long sectors

🟩 On a **long-haul sector** (working threshold: planned length **over 5 hours**), the connected pilot may take an **off-duty rest period** in the cruise, modelling the real handover to a relief pilot on an augmented crew. The discipline (also stated in [[A04 — Crew Composition](a04-crew-composition.md)](a04-crew-composition.md) §5):

- remain at the controls for the early part of the sector (guideline: the first ~30 minutes after gear-up) before going off-duty;
- resume duty and be back on the flight deck in good time before descent (guideline: by ~60 minutes before destination);
- while off-duty, the **time is not logged as pilot-at-controls time** even though total block time for the sector is still recorded — so the log reflects hours actually flown;
- 🟥 the online network's presence and position-reporting requirements are **not** suspended by going off-duty — an off-duty period is a modelled rest, not permission to abandon the connection in a way VATSIM forbids.

### 4.3 What this scheme is not

🟧 The scheme has **no duty-day limit, no minimum rest-between-sectors, no cumulative-duty limit, and no commander's-discretion FTL extension** in the regulatory sense — because those concepts don't map onto a hobby flown whenever the member chooses. Where a real OM A06 would tabulate maximum flying duty periods and mandatory rest, K Global instead relies on the fatigue-management principle (§2) and the two controls above. If a future decision introduces a firmer duty/rest model, it belongs here and is flagged for that ratification. 🟧

## 5. Reference frameworks (light)

For realism and as the conceptual source of the principle above, the real frameworks are:

- **ICAO Annex 6** — fatigue management (prescriptive limits and/or Fatigue Risk Management Systems, FRMS).
- **EASA Part-ORO, Subpart FTL** (Regulation (EU) No 965/2012) — flight-time limitations and rest requirements for European commercial air transport, the closest regime to K Global's full-service network-group model.

These are cited as the discipline K Global gestures at; **none of their limits are adopted as binding here.** 🟩
