# A02 — Operational Control and Supervision · OM A

Dispatch and flight-following authority, and who holds operational control of a flight.

**Version** v1.0 · **Updated** 2026-07-25 · **Status** Verified

> Read-me: this chapter states K Global's operational-control policy — the concept, the dispatch/flight-following function, flight release and the shared responsibility between crew and dispatch. It sets *policy*; the FMS and phase-flow *method* lives in OM E `Flight Management and Operational Flows.md` and the fuel scheme in OM A A08 / OM E `Fuel Policy.md`. Flag legend: 🟥 restriction/hazard/hard requirement · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 1. Operational control — the concept

**Operational control** is the exercise of authority over the **initiation, continuation, diversion and termination** of a flight in the interest of safety and of the regularity and efficiency of the operation. Nothing in this chapter reduces the commander's ultimate authority for the safety of the aircraft, its occupants and cargo (OM A01) — operational control is exercised **in support of** the commander, not over the commander in flight.

- 🟩 **Initiation / authorisation.** A flight is authorised for release when the operational-flight-plan (OFP) package is complete, the aircraft is technically acceptable (OM A09 Minimum Equipment Policy), the required fuel and load are planned within limits (OM A08), and the destination and required alternates meet planning minima (§4).
- 🟥 **Continuation, diversion, termination — in flight the commander decides.** Once the aircraft is moving under its own power for flight, the decision to continue, hold, re-route, divert or terminate rests with the **commander**, informed by flight-following and by the current weather / NOTAM / fuel picture. Dispatch supports; it does not overrule the commander in flight.
- 🟩 **Two roles, one shared duty.** K Global exercises operational control through **two coordinated roles** — the **commander** (in-flight authority and final fuel/route/diversion decisions) and the **dispatch / flight-following** function (pre-flight release and in-flight watch). The two share responsibility for a safe, legal and efficient flight (§5).

## 2. Dispatch and flight-following

The dispatch/flight-following function is delivered on the **VAMSYS operations platform**, which is K Global's system of record for the schedule, the route network, aircraft assignment, and the ops/dispatch workflow. VAMSYS carries the network, fleet and airport data against which a flight is planned and released.

- 🟩 **Pre-flight dispatch.** Confirm the aircraft assignment and registration, the route and flight-number band for the sector, the technical status (MEL/CDL — OM A09), and that the planned fuel and load are within the airframe's limits (OM A08). Release is the confirmation that the flight may be initiated.
- 🟩 **Flight-following (flight watch).** For the duration of the flight the operation is followed for developing weather, NOTAM, airspace and diversion-support factors — the destination and alternate forecasts, en-route hazards, and anything that would change the fuel or diversion picture. The purpose is to catch a deteriorating picture **early**, while the crew still has options, and to make diversion support available if required.
- 🟥 **Currency of information.** The weather, NOTAM and airspace picture is **perishable**. It is pulled at planning and refreshed in flight; the OFP package and any pre-filed ATS flight plan are only valid against the conditions current at dispatch. A materially changed picture (destination going below minima, an alternate closing, a significant airspace or ATC restriction) is a re-plan trigger, not a footnote.

## 3. Flight release and the operational flight plan (OFP)

- 🟩 **The OFP is produced with SimBrief.** Every intended flight is planned on an **operational flight plan (OFP)** generated by **SimBrief**, K Global's flight-planning engine. The OFP is the master planning document for the sector: it builds the route, levels, cost index / cruise Mach, the fuel scheme (OM A08), the weights, the alternate selection and the navigation log, and it produces the AIS/Met briefing pack that supports flight preparation (§4).
- 🟥 **The commander is responsible for the released plan.** Before release the commander (or the crew on the commander's behalf) satisfies themselves that the OFP is sound — a **gross-error check** on route and initial departure/exit routing, on flight time and distance, on weights, and on the fuel figures — and that the aircraft type/registration, departure/destination/alternate aerodromes, planned levels and cost index are correct for the sector. An OFP that does not reflect the available meteorological and operational information is not a valid basis for release.
- 🟥 **Crew and ATC must hold the same plan.** The route flown, the OFP and any filed **ATS flight plan** must agree, with particular attention to the initial departure/exit routing. A discrepancy is resolved **before** it becomes an in-flight problem. The ATS flight plan is filed to match the OFP; the operator's own company/radio callsign is used as the flight identity.
- 🟩 **The navigation log is the running record.** In flight the OFP navigation log is the master record — actual vs planned fuel at each checkpoint, times, the clearance actually received where it differs from plan, and the fuel checks (OM A08 / OM E `Fuel Policy.md`).

## 4. Flight preparation — briefing, aerodromes and planning minima

Flight preparation is the evidence base for release. It is owned jointly by dispatch (who assembles it) and the commander (who must be satisfied by it).

- 🟥 **AIS and Met briefing.** Before every flight the commander must be in possession of the **AIS and meteorological information** relevant to the route and to any diversion that might be required, and must be satisfied that the ground facilities and services needed for the flight are available and adequate. The briefing pack generated with the OFP meets this requirement. It must cover the applicable **ATC procedures and restrictions, navigation and communication facilities, aerodromes and ground aids**, and — in winter — runway snow/slush state and its effect on performance and braking action. The Met review covers significant-weather charts and the forecasts for en-route conditions and for the anticipated conditions at destination, at every alternate, and at the departure aerodrome / take-off alternate.
- 🟩 **Aerodrome usability and briefing.** The commander must be adequately briefed for each aerodrome used. Area briefings and per-aerodrome briefings are held in **OM C Routes and Destinations** (the relevant OM section); the airspace to be flown or overflown is briefed from the **OM C Airspace** briefs. Aerodromes requiring extra consideration (non-standard approaches, unusual local weather, performance limitations, obstacle/layout factors) carry a heavier briefing obligation before use.
- 🟥 **Planning-minima and alternate logic (policy pointer).** For every flight the release must resolve: is a **take-off alternate** required (departure weather below landing minima or return otherwise not possible)? Is a **destination alternate** required, and are **one or two** needed? Is an **en-route alternate (ERA)** used (which enables the reduced contingency option — OM A08)? Do the destination and required alternates meet **planning minima** over the applicable time window, and are they operationally and politically acceptable? Is the fuel sufficient for the whole scheme? The detailed alternate-requirement and planning-minima method is operationalised in **OM E `Fuel Policy.md` §4**; the fuel figures come from the OFP.

## 5. Shared responsibility — crew and dispatch

- 🟩 **Complementary, not competing.** Dispatch/flight-following and the commander share responsibility for a safe, legal and efficient flight. Dispatch owns the completeness and currency of the release package and the flight-watch; the commander owns the acceptance of that package and every in-flight decision. Each depends on the other doing their part.
- 🟥 **The commander's authority is not delegated away.** Nothing in the dispatch process relieves the commander of responsibility for the conduct of the flight, for maintaining adequate obstacle clearance and airspace compliance (OM A13), or for carrying sufficient fuel (OM A08). Where the dispatch plan and the commander's judgement differ on a safety matter, the **more conservative** course is taken.
- 🟩 **Escalation and support.** If the in-flight picture develops beyond routine — a diversion, a fuel concern, an airspace or aircraft-status problem — the commander uses flight-following for support (updated weather, alternate suitability, coordination) while retaining the decision. A predicted landing below **final reserve** fuel is the **MAYDAY FUEL** emergency, not a management case (OM A08 / OM E `Fuel Policy.md`).
