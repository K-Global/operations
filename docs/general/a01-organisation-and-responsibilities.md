# A01 — Organisation and Responsibilities · OM A

The K Global management structure and post-holder responsibilities, and the command authority and chain of command that govern every flight.

## 1. Organisational model

K Global is organised on a **full-service network-group management model**: a single accountable head with a team of **nominated post-holders**, each owning one functional area of the operation. The structure mirrors the way a Part-ORO operator distributes responsibility — one person accountable for the whole, specialists accountable for the parts — scaled to a volunteer virtual airline.

Because K Global runs the full network-group market map under **one brand and one callsign (KAY "Kayak")**, segregated by business unit rather than by separate airlines, the post-holders are **group-wide**: each holds their function across all nine business units and all seven regions, rather than one per unit. This keeps policy coherent no matter which unit or hub a flight belongs to.

All roles are voluntary. Appointment, review, and removal of post-holders are decisions of the airline's senior management; the specific individuals are not named in this manual (governance rule of record — anonymity). Every appointment below is therefore listed by **role and remit only**; the structure is ratified 2026-07-25 (of record).

## 2. Accountable Manager and nominated post-holders

The **Accountable Manager** sits at the top of the structure. Each **post-holder** below reports to the Accountable Manager for their function and has a defined, non-overlapping remit. The Safety and the Quality/Compliance post-holders are deliberately **independent** of the operational-delivery posts so that oversight is not marking its own work.

| Post | One-line remit | Ratification |
|---|---|---|
| **Accountable Manager** | Overall authority and final responsibility for the airline — strategy, standards, and that the operation is resourced and run to this manual; appoints and holds the post-holders to account. | 🟩 ratified 2026-07-25 |
| **Post-holder, Flight Operations** | Owns how the airline flies — operational control policy, SOP standards, route and fleet operating policy; the reference authority for OM A operational chapters and OM E methods. | 🟩 ratified 2026-07-25 |
| **Post-holder, Crew Training** | Owns pilot competence — type, recurrent, and special-qualification training and the standards members are assessed against; the reference authority for OM D. | 🟩 ratified 2026-07-25 |
| **Post-holder, Ground Operations** | Owns the ground side — dispatch, turnaround, loading and ground-handling policy, and the airport-facing operating standards applied in OM C. | 🟩 ratified 2026-07-25 |
| **Post-holder, Continuing Airworthiness** | Owns the fleet as configured — aircraft/type register, permitted types and substitutions, minimum-equipment (MEL/CDL) dispatch policy, and the airframe data in OM B. | 🟩 ratified 2026-07-25 |
| **Post-holder, Safety / SMS** | Owns the Safety Management System — hazard identification, risk management, occurrence reporting, and safety assurance; independent of operational delivery, reporting to the Accountable Manager. | 🟩 ratified 2026-07-25 |

> The roles, their boundaries, and the number of posts are the **K Global structure of record**, ratified 2026-07-25. The framework is settled and the post-holder appointments are of record. 🟩

**How the posts interlock.** Flight Operations, Crew Training, Ground Operations, and Continuing Airworthiness are the four **delivery** posts — they run the operation and own the OM parts that describe it. Safety/SMS and Quality/Compliance are the two **oversight** posts — they assure that the delivery posts are operating to policy. Security spans both. The Accountable Manager is the single point at which all functions converge. Vacancies in a delivery post default upward to the Accountable Manager until filled.

## 3. Command authority and the chain of command

Structure sets who owns policy on the ground; **command** sets who is in charge in the air. The two meet at dispatch and part at the aircraft door: once a flight begins, the **Pilot-in-Command (PIC)** — the member designated as commander for that flight, whatever their rank or seniority — holds final authority.

### 3.1 Source and scope of authority

The PIC's authority rests on two things: the operating framework the airline adopts (modelled on ICAO Annex 6 / EASA Part-CAT command provisions), and the PIC's designation as commander for the flight. Signing for the flight as commander confers that authority and the responsibility that comes with it.

- 🟥 **The PIC has final authority over the operation and safety of the aircraft** for the duration of the command, and may give any command necessary to secure the safety of the aircraft and the persons and property carried. Within the simulation, all crew and occupants act on the PIC's lawful commands.
- 🟥 **The command period runs from the moment the PIC takes charge of the aircraft for the intended flight** (or first signs for it) **until the aircraft is shut down and handed over** at the end of the flight, or command is formally transferred. Responsibility for the aircraft, its occupants, and its cargo is continuous across that period.
- The PIC is responsible for the conduct of the flight in accordance with this manual, the applicable rules of the air, and — on online flights — the **VATSIM Code of Conduct** and live ATC instructions. Where a lawful network instruction and a company preference differ, the network instruction governs (see A00 §5).

### 3.2 General responsibilities

The PIC is accountable and responsible, throughout the command period, for:

- the **safety and security** of the aircraft, its occupants, and its cargo;
- the **safe, orderly, and efficient conduct** of the flight, operated to the standard method in OM E and the type procedures in OM B;
- the **welfare of the crew and passengers**, considering the downstream consequences of any decision, especially away from base where company support is limited; and
- **upholding the airline's standing** by personal example, on the network and in the community.

Commanders are expected to exercise leadership: to set the operating standard, brief and support other crew, give and accept constructive feedback, and help less-experienced members develop the skills and judgement they need. A concern about another member's performance or conduct should first be raised directly and constructively; if further intervention is warranted, it is referred to the relevant post-holder rather than handled on the network. Where a commander sees an opportunity to improve the operation, they are encouraged to raise it.

### 3.3 Specific responsibilities

Before and during each flight, the PIC shall take all reasonable steps to:

- **be current with the relevant procedures and rules** — the OM, the applicable rules of the air, and the online-network regulations in force. (The airline discharges part of this by incorporating the relevant framework into the OM; the PIC is responsible for knowing and applying it.)
- **satisfy themselves the flight can be conducted safely** — that briefing, weather, NOTAM, fuel (per A08 / OM E Fuel Policy), mass and balance, and aircraft configuration support the planned flight and any likely diversion, and that performance is adequate for the intended aerodromes.
- **ensure procedures and checklists are complied with**, and that the aircraft is operated within its approved limits.
- **ensure occupants are briefed** on safety-relevant matters appropriate to the flight, and secured for the critical phases.
- **remain responsible for terrain and obstacle clearance and for separation compliance**, irrespective of any ATC clearance, and apply greater safety margins whenever judged necessary.

🟥 **Emergency authority.** In a situation demanding immediate action in the interests of safety, the PIC may take any action they judge necessary and may **deviate from any rule, procedure, or method** in this manual to the extent required to preserve safety. Standard policy is otherwise to be followed unless there is an urgent and compelling reason to depart from it; the flexibility exists for the unforeseen, not for convenience.

### 3.4 Delegation and deputisation

- The PIC may **delegate tasks** — handling, radio, monitoring, briefing — to a qualified operating co-pilot or other crew, but **authority and final responsibility for the flight remain with the PIC** and are not delegable. 🟥
- On multi-crew or shared-cockpit flights, the operating co-pilot is the PIC's **nominated deputy**. Where duties are exchanged (for example a co-pilot flying a sector under supervision), the PIC's responsibility for the safe and efficient conduct of the aircraft is **unchanged**; the PIC supervises, sets the conditions under which the co-pilot operates, and restores the normal allocation of duties — or terminates the exchange — at their discretion, giving due regard to experience, conditions, and the demands of the aerodrome.
- **Off-flight-deck rest** on long-range sectors follows the crew-composition and augmentation policy in A04; handover of control during rest does not transfer command.
- On the ground, functional ownership follows the post-holder structure in §2; the chain from post-holder to member is the route for standards, training, and compliance matters between flights.
