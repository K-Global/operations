# General — Policy &amp; SOP

**OM A is the keystone.** It sets out K Global's general policy, management
structure, command authority, safety-management philosophy, and the SOP
framework that every other part of the Operations Manual hangs from. Where
Fleet (OM B), Network (OM C), Training (OM D) and Operations (OM E) apply a
rule to a type, a place, a syllabus or a manoeuvre, OM A is where that rule is
*owned* — the policy statement of record.

## How the manual fits together

| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| **General · OM A** | Company organisation, command authority, safety policy, and the SOP philosophy the rest of the manual implements. |
| **[Fleet · OM B](../fleet/index.md)** | Per-type airframe packs — dispatch data, checklists, QRH, systems — apply OM A/E policy to a specific type. |
| **[Network · OM C](../network/index.md)** | Airports, airspace and routes — apply OM A/E policy to a specific place. |
| **[Training · OM D](../training/index.md)** | Type, recurrent and special-qualification training — builds the competence OM A §A05/A06 require. |
| **[Operations · OM E](../flight-ops/index.md)** | Location-agnostic operating methods (ETOPS/EDTO, PBN, RVSM, fuel, LVO …) — the *method*; OM A owns the *policy* the method must satisfy. |

!!! note "Consistency"
    Where OM A owns a policy that OM E also applies operationally — notably
    **fuel &amp; payload** (A08) and **minimum-equipment dispatch** (A09) — the two
    must stay aligned: OM A states the policy, OM E states how the method applies
    it. A change to one requires a check against the other.
