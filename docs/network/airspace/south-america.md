# South America — airspace brief
**Scope:** Brazilian domestic airspace, South-Atlantic coast-in to the São Paulo TMA · **FIRs covered:** Atlântico (SBAO) · Recife (SBRE) · Brasília (SBBS) · Curitiba (SBCW) → São Paulo TMA (APP-SP)

## 3. Airspace structure

- **FIRs & boundaries (idents confirmed against the official AISWEB FIR filter — SBAO / SBRE / SBBS / SBCW / SBAZ):**
  - **Atlântico (SBAO)** — the vast **South-Atlantic oceanic FIR**, ATS by **ACC Atlântico operated by CINDACTA III (Recife)**, extending from near the Brazilian coast **east to the 10°W meridian** (~13 million km²; abuts **Dakar Oceanic (GOOO)** and the EUR-SAM corridor to the NE — *handoff comes FROM* the EUR-SAM Corridor briefing). **Procedural/oceanic** — radar and ADS-B coverage begin only near the coast.
  - **Recife (SBRE)** — NE continental FIR, **ACC-RE / CINDACTA III**. First continental radar sector on coast-in.
  - **Brasília (SBBS)** — the large **central FIR, ACC-BS / CINDACTA I** — most of the interior descent transits here.
  - **Curitiba (SBCW)** — **southern FIR, ACC-CW / CINDACTA II** — contains the São Paulo terminal complex approach.
  - **São Paulo TMA** — terminal airspace served by **APP-SP ("Controle São Paulo")**, one of the densest in South America (§6). Regional-centre jurisdiction **CRCEA-SE** (confirm exact controlling unit).
- **Classes & level bands:** continental controlled airspace (upper CTA/UTA Class A in the RVSM band). RVSM **FL290–FL410**. Altitudes in **feet**, QNH in **hPa**.
- **Oceanic / procedural area:** the Atlântico FIR is the only procedural portion on this segment — CPDLC/ADS-C or HF until radar pickup near the coast.
- **Entry/exit & transition points:** coast-in fix (…**VUNOX** on the planned string) marks the oceanic→continental handoff; thereafter airway/RNAV structure through Recife → Brasília → Curitiba into the São Paulo TMA via the assigned STAR (planned **SANPA1A** to SBGR — planning context only, fly the charted version).

## 4. Equipment, approvals & separation

- **RVSM:** **FL290–FL410** — RVSM approval mandatory (AIP-Brasil **ENR 2.2 "RVSM airspace"**; ICAO-standard band, Brazil full-RVSM). exact ENR wording not rendered — confirm on eAIP/current AIRAC. 777-300ER is RVSM-approved — confirm operator approval on file.
- **PBN / RNP:** Brazil operates an extensive **RNAV upper-route network** and **RNAV STAR/SID** structure (the SANPA arrivals into SBGR are RNAV). Expect **RNAV 5** enroute and **RNAV 1 / RNP** terminal; **exact spec per route/procedure — verify on current AIRAC** (AIP-Brasil ENR 1.8 / ENR 3, not rendered).
- **Datalink:** **CPDLC + ADS-C (FANS 1/A)** in the **Atlântico oceanic** FIR (primary in the procedural area alongside HF). **Not required** in continental radar airspace (confirm any FL/area trigger).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** Mode S standard; **ADS-B** used for surveillance continentally. **ADS-B Out mandate:** DECEA phased requirement — exact applicability/effective date **not reached** — verify at planning. 777-300ER is ADS-B Out equipped .
- **Separation:** continental **radar** separation (standard ICAO); oceanic Atlântico uses **procedural / PBCS** minima where CPDLC/ADS-C-equipped, HF procedural where not .

## 5. Communications

- **VHF coverage:** dense and reliable once **continental** (Recife/Brasília/Curitiba FIRs and the São Paulo TMA) — a normal radar-vectored VHF environment.
- **HF + CPDLC (Atlântico oceanic):** in the **Atlântico FIR** the primary means are **CPDLC/ADS-C** (FANS) and, where not datalink-equipped, **HF** with SELCAL. HF over the South Atlantic is "poor and sensitive to atmospheric conditions" (DECEA) — CPDLC is preferred. **Note (sim):** HF is not simulated in X-Plane / on VATSIM — an assigned **VHF** frequency is used in lieu on the online network.
- **SATVOICE:** may back up the oceanic leg (route-dependent) .
- **ATC language:** **Portuguese and English** are both used. **ICAO English is available on request**, but **expect Portuguese on frequency** between the controller and Brazilian domestic traffic — a real-world **TEM**: reduced party-line awareness and occasional readback ambiguity. Brief it, request English, and confirm anything unclear.
- **Inter-pilot / emergency VHF:** air-to-air **123.45 MHz**; emergency **121.5 MHz**.
- **Position reporting (procedural):** required at compulsory reporting points in the Atlântico FIR where ADS-C does not cover; standard radar (no procedural reporting) once continental.

## 6. Routing & levels

- **Principal route structure:** oceanic random/airway routing across Atlântico to the coast-in fix (…**VUNOX**), then the **continental RNAV/airway spine** SW through Recife → Brasília → Curitiba FIRs, joining the assigned **STAR** into the São Paulo TMA. Planned STAR **SANPA1A** for **SBGR RWY 10R** — **planning context only**; the cleared arrival/runway is ATC's.
- **São Paulo TMA — the headline item:** one of the **busiest, most complex terminal areas in South America**. **GRU (SBGR)**, **Congonhas (SBSP/CGH)** and **Viracopos (SBKP/VCP)** all feed the **same terminal airspace**. Expect **extended radar vectoring, speed control, late runway assignment, and holding** at the published TMA holds. Keep the second landing runway (10L/10R parallel pair at SBGR) armed in the box. Congonhas sits immediately SW under the **CGO VOR (116.9)** — high crossing/adjacent traffic.
- **Free Route Airspace:** not a feature of this sector's fixed-airway/RNAV structure .
- **Metric vs feet:** **Brazil uses feet for altitudes/flight levels and hPa (QNH)** — **not** metric levels. Speeds in knots, Mach in cruise. **Transition altitude is per-airport**: **SBGR TA = 8,000 ft**, transition level by QNH. Set QNH in hPa on descent.
- **Typical level/flow:** westbound arrival descends out of the RVSM band through the interior; step-down and TMA speed/level restrictions applied by ATC. 777-300ER heavy arrival (LAW ~239.5 t) — plan the descent early against the plateau (field 2,461 ft) and possible holding fuel.

## 7. Terrain, MORA & driftdown

- **Coast-in / interior:** the NE-Brazil coast-in (Fortaleza/Natal/Recife belt) and the central interior are **low to moderate** terrain — MORA is not the binding constraint enroute.
- **Destination end — the terrain that matters:** the **São Paulo Paulista plateau** (~2,300–2,900 ft) with the **Serra do Mar escarpment falling to the coast S–SE of SBGR**. SBGR field elevation **2,461 ft**. Read the **MSA/MORA off the current-AIRAC enroute chart and the SANPA STAR plate**. - **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** no high-terrain driftdown issue on the oceanic/coast-in legs; near the destination the plateau is modest — standard terrain awareness on the arrival.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not a factor (tropical/subtropical, warm).

## 9. Special-use airspace & overflight

- **Conflict / prohibited / risk zones:** **none** — Brazil is benign for conflict-zone and overflight-permit purposes on a domestic arrival.
- **Danger / restricted / military areas:** Brazil publishes numerous **P/R/D areas** (military training, ranges, the Alcântara/space areas in the far N, terminal-area restrictions) — by chart/NOTAM. Pull live at §11; check the day's NOTAMs for TMA restrictions.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic:** none — a domestic arrival into Brazilian airspace; customs/PoE handled at SBGR (intl PoE).

> **Conflict-zone currency:** Re-check ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository / state NOTAMs and operator routing policy at planning — this list is durable context, not live clearance.

## 10. Contingency procedures

- **Oceanic in-flight contingency (Atlântico FIR):** apply the **globally-standardised ICAO Doc 4444 oceanic contingency** (obtain a revised clearance first; if unable, turn **≥30°** to establish a same-direction **5 NM lateral offset**, squawk 7700, lights on, broadcast 121.5/123.45, apply the level offset) — identical framework to the NAT text in North Atlantic §10 and reproduced by DECEA for Atlântico oceanic ops (confirm Atlântico-specific offset values on AIP ENR 1.x / the EUR-SAM Corridor briefing).
- **SLOP:** Strategic Lateral Offset (right-only, up to 2 NM) is applied in the South-Atlantic oceanic airspace as in other oceanic regions — confirm current AIP application.
- **Continental / TMA contingency:** standard radar environment — comms-failure and weather-deviation handled by **ATC vectors**; follow ICAO/AIP-Brasil comms-failure procedure (squawk 7600, continue per last clearance / expected routing) exact ENR wording not rendered.
- **Weather deviation (TMA):** request a **vector/heading** around cells from São Paulo Control — a normal radar-vectored request, not the oceanic offset procedure, once continental.

## 11. Seasonal, environmental & live data

- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **ITCZ on coast-in:** the **Intertropical Convergence Zone** sits near/over the NE Brazilian coast and can throw **embedded convection and heavy build-ups** across the coast-in transition (SBFZ/SBNT/SBRF belt). Position varies seasonally (further N in austral winter) — expect line-of-sight convective cells and use radar tilt on descent.
  - **Tropical build-ups over the interior:** afternoon/evening **convective towers** over the central plateau (Brasília FIR), **worst in the austral wet season (Nov–Mar)**; quieter in the July dry season inland but still possible.
  - **SE-Brazil winter fog/stratus (destination):** **July = SE-Brazil winter** — **morning radiation fog / low stratus on the Paulista plateau** is a genuine minima threat at SBGR. CAT II/III capability and the RNP backup matter on a fog day; SBKP/SBBR as alternates.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs (incl. TMA/P-R-D restrictions), **SIGMET / convective advisories (ITCZ line, interior CBs)**, TAF/TREND for SBGR + alternates (fog watch), the **São Paulo TMA flow / holding** picture, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned STAR/runway.
