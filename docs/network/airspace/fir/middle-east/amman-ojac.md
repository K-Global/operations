# Amman (OJAC) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the entire Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan, containing **Queen Alia Intl (OJAI, Amman)** and **King Hussein Intl (OJAQ, Aqaba)** · **Parent area brief:** [Middle East](../../middle-east.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Tel Aviv (LLLL) W · Damascus (OSTT) N · Baghdad (ORBB) NE/E · Jeddah (OEJD) SE/S

**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — FIR containment and adjacency verified vs CARC AIP chart data (AIRAC AMDT 19/2022); **the regional security/airspace picture is live/perishable and flagged 🟥 (re-check at planning)**; ATSU/ANSP unit nomenclature, exact lateral-limit coordinates and vertical/sector split flagged 🟧

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Amman is a **radar-controlled continental FIR** covering the whole of Jordan, bridging the Eastern Mediterranean/Levant to the Gulf. Routings and levels here are planning context — file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances and any active NOTAM restriction. Terminal procedures for aerodromes inside the FIR (OJAI, OJAQ) live in the airport briefings (§9). **Jordanian airspace is subject to a live regional security picture (§10) — an active EASA Conflict Zone Information Bulletin currently advises against operating in this FIR at all altitudes; this MUST be re-checked at planning (§11) as it changes by NOTAM.** Open 🟧 items are in §12. ## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** the Amman FIR covers the **entire Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan** — from the northern border with Syria, across the eastern desert bordering Iraq and Saudi Arabia, to the narrow Red Sea coastal strip at Aqaba in the extreme south-west. A compact, single-state continental FIR bridging the Eastern Mediterranean/Levant FIR set to the Gulf FIR set.
- **Confirmed containment:** 🟩 **Queen Alia Intl (OJAI, Amman/Zizya)** and **King Hussein Intl (OJAQ, Aqaba)** both sit in the Amman FIR (OJAC) — the country's only two international aerodromes and both served by K Global.
- **Vertical structure:** 🟧 controlled airspace to the upper limit; no published upper/lower ACC split was found in reachable sources — treat as a single FIR/UIR (GND–UNL) pending confirmation at CARC AIP ENR 2.1. Terminal Class C control (Queen Alia CTR, SFC–5,500 ft) sits beneath the enroute structure at OJAI.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):** confirmed against CARC AIP chart data (AIRAC AMDT 19/2022, FIR-boundary index maps OJP9/OJP10):
  - **W — Tel Aviv (LLLL, Israel).** The Levant boundary along the Israel/Jordan border (per the Israel AIP ENR 2.1 boundary description, the Tel Aviv FIR's eastern edge runs along "the Israel/Jordan border").
  - **N — Damascus (OSTT, Syria).** 🟥 A conflict-affected neighbour; Damascus FIR is itself under an active EASA CZIB (avoid all levels) per the Middle East area brief.
  - **NE/E — Baghdad (ORBB, Iraq).** 🟥 Also under an active EASA CZIB (avoid all levels).
  - **SE/S — Jeddah (OEJD, Saudi Arabia).** The largest MID-region FIR; the Amman/Jeddah boundary runs across the southern Jordanian desert toward the Gulf of Aqaba.
  - **Four-country border complex (Aqaba/OJAQ area):** 🟧 Jordan's only coastline, at the head of the **Gulf of Aqaba**, sits within a few kilometres of Israel (Eilat), Egypt (Sinai/Taba) and, across the Gulf, Saudi Arabia — a uniquely tight international-boundary knot. OJAQ's own preferred-alternate set (HESH/Egypt, LLBG/Israel, OJAI/Jordan) reflects this proximity; confirm which FIR (Amman, Tel Aviv, or Cairo/HECC) has jurisdiction over any given transition fix near Aqaba before planning an approach/departure that skirts the border.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 not confirmed from reachable sources; Amman TACC (Terminal Area Control Centre) is referenced as the coordinating unit for FIR-wide contingency management in ICAO MID material, suggesting a terminal/enroute split exists, but the exact sector count and boundaries were not obtained this pass.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Regulator:** 🟩 **Civil Aviation Regulatory Commission (CARC)** — Jordan's civil aviation authority, also the ANSP overseer.
- **Lower/enroute ATSU:** 🟧 **Jordan Air Navigation Service (JANS)**, working under CARC oversight; **Amman TACC** is named in ICAO MID contingency-workshop material as the operational coordination point for the FIR during the June 2025 regional-conflict airspace closure. Exact ACC nomenclature and area-of-responsibility split not independently confirmed against AIP ENR 2.1.
- **Terminal ATSU:** **Amman Approach** (OJAI, 128.9) and **Aqaba Approach** (OJAQ) provide radar terminal service; **Queen Alia Tower** (119.8) and **King Hussein Tower** control their respective aerodromes.
- **FIS / FSS:** CARC AIS/ARO; no AFIS/uncontrolled pockets identified for the two served aerodromes (both H24 ATS).
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 radar at both terminal areas; enroute FIR-wide surveillance basis (radar vs procedural coverage over the eastern desert) not confirmed — treat conservatively pending AIP confirmation.

The OJAI airport brief cross-checks an Amman Control (Centre) frequency of **128.500 MHz** against a network-sim source only 🟧 — not independently AIP-confirmed.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** CARC AIS/FIS for information service.
- **Language:** 🟩 **English** (ICAO) throughout.
- **Channel spacing:** 🟩 **25 kHz** VHF (no 8.33 kHz mandate).
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 Jordan is dominated by an **elevated interior plateau** (Amman/OJAI sits at 2,397 ft) descending to desert in the east and to the Jordan Rift Valley/Dead Sea (below sea level) and the Red Sea coast (Aqaba/OJAQ, 175 ft) in the west/south-west. No significant close-in high terrain of CFIT concern was identified at either served aerodrome's AIP obstacle data.
- **Boundary terrain:** the Dead Sea depression lies on the western FIR edge toward the Tel Aviv FIR boundary; not itself a hazard, but a notable elevation contrast (below sea level) within a short distance of Amman's plateau. No high mountain belt comparable to the Hajar or Asir ranges exists within or immediately adjacent to Jordan.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟩 not a significant terrain-driven constraint given the generally benign topography; the binding constraint on any escape/diversion plan in this FIR is **airspace availability** (§10), not terrain.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟩 not a significant driver — the operative seasonal factor is **summer heat** (OJAI reference temperature 31.5°C; OJAQ 40.1°C), a mild density-altitude consideration rather than a true hot-and-high performance limit at either field's elevation.

## 10. Special-use airspace

> 🟥 **REGIONAL-SECURITY / LIVE-AIRSPACE ADVISORY — this section is perishable. Re-check the current CZIB/NOTAM picture at planning (§11); Jordan's airspace status has changed rapidly during past regional escalations, including a full-FIR closure.**

- **Regional security exposure (the headline hazard):** 🟥 Jordan sits directly between the Tel Aviv, Damascus and Baghdad FIRs at the centre of the wider Levant/Gulf conflict picture (see the [Middle East area brief](../../middle-east.md) §9). As at build date, **EASA CZIB 2026-08** (issued 22 Jul 2026, valid until 31 Aug 2026 unless reviewed earlier) advises operators **not to operate within the Amman FIR (OJAC) at any altitude or flight level**, citing a sustained increase in Iranian missile and, to a lesser extent, drone attacks against assets in Jordan since mid-July 2026, and the associated risk of civil-aircraft misidentification, missile overflight, interception activity and falling debris. This CZIB **supersedes** an earlier, lower-tier EASA Information Note (issued 14 Jul 2026, "take potential risk into account") that had covered Israel/Jordan/Oman/Saudi Arabia jointly. France issued a parallel avoid-notam (LFFF F1578/26). *(Third-party risk-database classification places Jordan at a lower tier than Lebanon/Iraq/Iran — Jordan's own airspace has, as at recent reporting, remained open and operating normally despite the CZIB — but the specific advisory reference, validity and on-the-ground status are live data: confirm the current CZIB/NOTAM state at planning before relying on any routing. No party, cause or political position is asserted here.)*
- **Contingency precedent:** 🟧 during the June 2025 regional escalation, Jordan implemented a **full closure of the Amman FIR** (all arrivals, departures and overflights suspended) under CARC/JANS management, later refined to hybrid/dynamic contingency procedures as the risk picture evolved (ICAO MID Region contingency workshop material, Feb 2026). This establishes that a **full-FIR closure at short notice is a real, precedented outcome** for this FIR, not merely a theoretical risk.
- **Military restricted / danger / prohibited:** 🟧 Jordan publishes restricted/danger/prohibited areas per the CARC AIP (e.g. the OJAI chart index references a P.D. area near Amman/the Dead Sea); activation and status are AIP/NOTAM-published — confirm active at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 standard Jordanian civil overflight/entry for scheduled operations under normal conditions; confirm no operator/state-specific routing directive applies given §10's live picture.

> **SUA / security currency:** re-check the **EASA CZIB, state NOTAMs (Jordan + surrounding), ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository and operator routing policy** at planning — the §10 picture is durable awareness that this is a live-risk FIR, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO standard — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance/filed route and levels, attempt the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Jordan-specific refinement at CARC AIP ENR 1.8/GEN; in the current security environment, deviating from a cleared routing without coordination carries elevated risk.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical via ATC in this controlled FIR; request the deviation from ATC and comply with the vector/level issued. No oceanic offset table applies.
- **Regional-security contingency:** 🟥 plan every operation against the current CZIB/closure map — given the June 2025 precedent, be prepared for short-notice FIR closure and have a pre-briefed reroute/diversion option that does not rely on an assumption that Amman FIR remains open.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Summer heat (Jun–Sep)** — extreme temperatures at both served fields (OJAQ especially, ref. temperature 40.1°C); a mild density-altitude consideration.
  - **Dust/sandstorm events** — consistent with the wider regional shamal pattern; can reduce visibility with little warning, especially at the elevated OJAI plateau.
  - **Occasional winter fog/low stratus** at OJAI; rare light winter precipitation in the wider Amman area.
  - **Volcanic ash / space weather:** not a planning driver at these latitudes.
