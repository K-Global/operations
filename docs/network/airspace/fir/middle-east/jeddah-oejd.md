# Jeddah (OEJD) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the **entire Kingdom of Saudi Arabia**, from the eastern Red Sea coast across the Arabian Peninsula interior to the western Gulf coast; the **largest FIR in the ICAO MID region**, containing **King Khaled Intl/Riyadh (OERK)**, **King Abdulaziz Intl/Jeddah (OEJN)**, **King Fahd Intl/Dammam (OEDF)**, **Prince Mohammad Bin Abdulaziz/Madinah (OEMA)**, **Abha (OEAB)** and the Kingdom's remaining domestic/regional fields · **Parent area brief:** [Middle East](../../middle-east.md) · **Adjacent FIRs:** Amman (OJAC) N · Baghdad (ORBB) NE · Kuwait (OKAC) NE · Bahrain (OBBB) E — **eastern-sector interface, see §3** · Doha/Qatar (OTDF) E · Emirates (OMAE) E · Muscat (OOMM) SE · Sanaa (OYSC) S · Cairo (HECC) W (across the Red Sea)

**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Jeddah is a single, **radar/ADS-B-surveillance-controlled FIR spanning the whole of Saudi Arabia** — the backbone of the surviving "southern bypass" corridor linking Egypt/Red Sea traffic to the Gulf hubs. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances and any active managed-corridor / ESCAT restrictions. Terminal procedures for aerodromes inside the FIR live in the airport briefings (§9). **The FIR's southwest sector, along the Yemen border, is subject to short-notice ESCAT (Emergency Security Control of Air Traffic) activation — a live/perishable hazard covered in §10 and re-checked at planning (§11).** Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** the Jeddah FIR covers the **entire Kingdom of Saudi Arabia** — the eastern Red Sea coast and its offshore waters, the whole Arabian Peninsula interior (Riyadh/Najd, the Asir highlands), and the western Gulf/Arabian Gulf coast around Dammam/Dhahran/Jubail. At **~2 million km²** of underlying territory it is the **largest FIR in the ICAO MID region** — a single national FIR, unlike the compact city-state FIRs surrounding it (Bahrain, Qatar, Kuwait, UAE).
- **Confirmed containment:** 🟩 **OERK (Riyadh), OEJN (Jeddah), OEDF (Dammam), OEMA (Madinah), OEAB (Abha)** and the Kingdom's remaining regional/domestic aerodromes — **OEGN (Jizan), OEGS, OEHL (Hail), OENG (Najran), OEPA (Al Qaisumah), OESK (Al Jouf), OETB, OETF (Taif), OEYN (Yanbu)** — all sit inside the single Jeddah FIR.
- **Vertical structure:** 🟧 controlled airspace to the upper limit — **Class A** in the upper band; Class C/D TMAs around the four hub-class aerodromes (Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, Madinah). Confirm the exact class-per-band split at Saudi AIP ENR 2.1.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Amman (OJAC, Jordan).** Toward the Levant.
  - **NE — Baghdad (ORBB, Iraq)** and **Kuwait (OKAC).** The short northern desert boundary; both neighbouring FIRs carry active conflict-zone advisories — see the companion **[Baghdad (ORBB) FIR brief](baghdad-orbb.md)**.
  - **E — Bahrain (OBBB).** 🟥 **The eastern-sector interface of operational note** — Jeddah FIR's eastern (Gulf-coast) sector, which feeds King Fahd Intl/Dammam (OEDF) and the Dammam/Dhahran approach, abuts the small, enclave-like **Bahrain FIR (OBBB)**, itself sandwiched between Jeddah, Kuwait and Doha/Qatar (OTDF) airspace over the western Gulf. Traffic transiting the Gulf-coast corridor crosses this boundary; confirm the exact boundary geometry and handoff point at Saudi AIP ENR 2.1 / Bahrain AIP. 🟧
  - **E (further) — Doha/Qatar (OTDF)** and **Emirates (OMAE).** Toward the central and southern Gulf hubs.
  - **SE — Muscat (OOMM, Oman).** Toward the Arabian Sea flank.
  - **S — Sanaa (OYSC, Yemen).** 🟥 **The Kingdom's most operationally sensitive boundary** — the southwest Jeddah-FIR sector directly abuts Yemeni airspace and is the source of the ESCAT/Houthi hazard (§10).
  - **W — Cairo (HECC, Egypt), across the Red Sea.** The Egypt–Saudi leg of the southern-bypass corridor.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 a single national FIR of this size is necessarily worked as multiple area sectors (interior, Gulf-coast, Red Sea-coast, and the SW/Yemen-border sector); the exact sector map is not published in the sources reviewed — confirm at Saudi AIP ENR 2.1/6. The SW sector is functionally distinct because it is the one subject to ESCAT.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Enroute ATSU:** 🟧 **Jeddah ACC** (working name inferred from the FIR ident) under **GACA**; radar/ADS-B surveillance control of the FIR. Exact ATSU designation, site and any Riyadh-based secondary centre to confirm at Saudi AIP ENR 1.1/2.1.
- **Terminal ATSU:** 🟧 approach control units serve Riyadh (OERK), Jeddah (OEJN), Dammam (OEDF) and Madinah (OEMA); precise unit names/AoR to confirm at AIP AD sections.
- **FIS / FSS:** Saudi **AIS/FIS** (GACA); not normally a factor for an IFR hub transit remaining in controlled airspace.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 radar + ADS-B on the developed network; no procedural pockets on a normal IFR routing between the K Global-relevant fields.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in this FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — ICAO MID region RVSM. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 **RNAV 5** enroute (MID RNAV 5 implementation complete); **RNAV 1 / RNP 1** terminal SID/STAR at the hub TMAs; **RNP APCH** arrivals. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟩 **not applicable** — Saudi Arabia uses **25 kHz** VHF spacing (8.33 kHz is an ICAO EUR mandate).
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟩 **CPDLC available from FL290 and above throughout the Jeddah FIR** for FANS 1/A and FANS 1/A+-equipped aircraft, plus **DCL** (departure clearance delivery) at the major Saudi towers — the most complete datalink coverage of any FIR referenced in this pack. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md). Confirm active participating sectors at planning.
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** transponder required; **ADS-B Out** underpins the surveillance service. Confirm equipage on the OFP.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a Kingdom-length transit is handed sequentially across Jeddah ACC's area sectors and the relevant approach unit; exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC/AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** Saudi **AIS/FIS** (GACA) for information service.
- **Language:** 🟩 **English** (ICAO) throughout.
- **Channel spacing:** 🟩 **25 kHz** VHF (no 8.33 kHz mandate).
- **Congestion — a live operational caveat:** 🟧 with southern-bypass traffic concentrated through this FIR, member reports describe **severe frequency congestion and blocked transmissions in the OEJD/Jeddah and adjacent Cairo (HECC) FIRs**, with crews at times unable to raise ATC for extended segments (per Middle East area brief §5). Keep TCAS/anti-collision lights active; consider a **PAN** broadcast if an un-cleared deviation becomes necessary.
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **not implemented** in the European sense — a fixed ATS-route network with tactical vectoring around the four hub TMAs.
- **Airway spine (southern-bypass backbone):** the **P/L/M/N-series** UL/UM routes forming the Gulf trunk pass through Jeddah FIR linking Egypt (Cairo) to Muscat/Emirates — 🟥 the usable set is currently **reduced to managed corridors/temporary routes** on the wider MID network; confirm the current-AIRAC picture at planning.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** boundary transition fixes toward Amman/Baghdad/Kuwait (N/NE), Bahrain/Qatar/Emirates (E), Muscat (SE), Sanaa (S, avoidance-relevant) and Cairo (W, Red Sea crossing); the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string.
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **King Khaled Intl/Riyadh (OERK)**, **King Abdulaziz Intl/Jeddah (OEJN)**, **King Fahd Intl/Dammam (OEDF)**, **Prince Mohammad Bin Abdulaziz/Madinah (OEMA)**, **Abha (OEAB)** — the served majors — plus **Jizan (OEGN)**, **OEGS**, **Hail (OEHL)**, **Najran (OENG)**, **Al Qaisumah (OEPA)**, **Al Jouf (OESK)**, **OETB**, **Taif (OETF)** and **Yanbu (OEYN)**. Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 mostly **low desert** across the Najd interior and the Gulf coast — generally benign. The exception is the **Asir/Sarawat Mountains** in the **southwest**, rising to **~3,000 m (Jabal Sawda ~3,133 m / ~10,280 ft)** near the Yemen border — the highest terrain in the Kingdom and a factor for the SW fields (OEAB/Abha and neighbours).
- **Boundary terrain:** the Asir highlands continue toward the Yemen (Sanaa FIR, OYSC) boundary to the south — noted for completeness; not a cruise factor on the Kingdom's main N–S/E–W trunks.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟧 not primarily a terrain constraint across most of the FIR given field density on the interior/Gulf-coast trunk; in the **SW sector**, the binding constraint is **where you are permitted to divert** given the active ESCAT/attack risk (§9/§10), not terrain clearance.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟩 not relevant — hot climate; the operational temperature driver is **high-temperature/density-altitude** performance in summer (see OM E and the airport briefs), not cold-temperature correction.

## 10. Special-use airspace

> 🟧 **SECURITY-AFFECTED SECTOR — this section is perishable. Re-check the current EASA Information Note / Saudi NOTAM picture at planning (§11); the SW-sector/ESCAT status changes rapidly.**

- **ESCAT (Emergency Security Control of Air Traffic) — the FIR's defining hazard:** 🟥 Saudi authorities may activate ESCAT **by NOTAM, with no advance warning**, in the **southwest of the Jeddah FIR** — the sector containing **Abha (OEAB), Jizan/OEGN, Najran/OENG, Sharurah (OESH), Wadi Al Dawasir (OEWD), Al Bahah (OEBA) and Bisha (OEBH)** — when Saudi air defences respond to Houthi drone/ballistic-missile activity from Yemen. Under ESCAT, operators can expect **abrupt rerouting directly out of the affected airspace or direction to land at the nearest airport**. As at build date, **OEAB, OEGN, OENG and OESH were NOTAM-closed (Jul 2026)** following a direct Houthi missile/drone attack. *(Per Saudi NOTAM OEJD W0120/21 and subsequent state advisories — durable awareness that the SW sector is ESCAT-live; confirm the current NOTAM/closure state at planning.)*
- **Regional risk context:** 🟧 Saudi Arabia carries an EASA **Information Note** (caution / take-risk-into-account — not an "avoid all levels" CZIB) covering Israel, Jordan, Oman and Saudi Arabia; several states (France, Italy, Canada, USA) separately advise enhanced caution and risk assessment specifically for the OEJD/Jeddah FIR, citing missile/drone activity and the possibility of spillover from the wider regional conflict. This is a materially lower risk tier than the "avoid all levels" advisories on Iran/Iraq/Yemen — see the companion **[Baghdad (ORBB) FIR brief](baghdad-orbb.md)** for contrast.
- **Military restricted / danger / prohibited:** 🟧 Saudi Arabia publishes extensive **restricted/danger/prohibited areas** (military ranges, sensitive sites) across the FIR; activation and status are AIP/NOTAM-published — confirm active at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 standard Saudi civil operation for a normal domestic/regional transit, **but** current SW-sector restrictions and any operator/state-specific routing directives apply — confirm at planning.

> **SUA / ESCAT currency:** re-check the **EASA Information Note, Saudi NOTAMs, ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository and operator routing policy** at planning — the §10 picture is durable awareness, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO standard — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance / filed route and levels, attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Saudi refinement at **AIP ENR 1.8/GEN**.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — request the deviation from ATC and comply with the vector/level issued. No oceanic offset table applies.
- **ESCAT contingency:** 🟥 if ESCAT is activated while operating in or near the SW sector, **comply immediately with ATC instructions** — expect rerouting or an instruction to land at the nearest available airport; monitor the appropriate frequency continuously in that area per Saudi/state guidance.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Shamal winds & blowing dust (summer + winter)** — reduce visibility across the Gulf coast and interior; can trigger LVO/rapid RVR collapse at the hubs.
  - **Summer heat & density altitude (Jun–Sep)** — a hot-and-high performance driver, particularly at the Asir-highland fields (OEAB and neighbours).
  - **Convection** is limited but occurs seasonally.
  - **Volcanic ash / space weather:** not a planning driver at these latitudes.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** the **current EASA Information Note / Saudi NOTAM picture for the SW sector (ESCAT status, field closures)** — the mission-critical item for this FIR, enroute NOTAMs and active restricted/danger areas, SIGMET/AIRMET (dust, convection), CPDLC active-sector confirmation, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
