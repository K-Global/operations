# Tel Aviv (LLLL) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the State of Israel and adjoining airspace out to the FIR boundary (a compact Levant-coast FIR), containing **Ben Gurion (LLBG)** · **Parent area brief:** [Middle East](../../middle-east.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Beirut (OLBB) N · Damascus (OSTT) NE (per FIR-boundary description — "Israel/Lebanon and Syria cease-fire lines") · Amman (OJAC) E (Israel/Jordan border) · Cairo (HECC) SW (Israel/Egypt border) · Nicosia (LCCC) W — offshore Mediterranean, 🟧 not independently confirmed as directly bordering

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Tel Aviv is a **radar-controlled coastal FIR** covering Israel and the adjacent Eastern Mediterranean approaches, split into Northern and Southern sectors each with parallel civil and military (IDFAF) control. Routings and levels here are planning context — file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for Ben Gurion (LLBG) live in the airport briefing (§9). **🟥 STANDING CAUTION — REGIONAL SECURITY/AIRSPACE TENSION: this FIR and the surrounding Eastern Mediterranean/Levant airspace are subject to a heightened, NOTAM-driven closure/restriction risk given the wider regional security environment, and GNSS jamming/interference has been reported in the area. This is factual operational caution, not political commentary — verify the current status (NOTAMs, state airspace-status bulletins, any active EASA CZIB/Information Note) before every single operation into this FIR; do not rely on this file's snapshot.** Open 🟧 items are in §12. ## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent (confirmed, primary source):** per Israel AIP ENR 2.1, the Tel-Aviv FIR boundary runs: **3306N 03506E** — along the **Israel/Lebanon and Syria cease-fire lines**, and the **Israel/Jordan border**, to **2928N 03457E** — **2928N 03454E** — along the **Israel/Egypt border** to **3118N 03413E** — **3150N 03400E**, continuing **clockwise on a half-circle of 46 NM centred on BGN VOR** (Ben Gurion) to **3243N 03432E** — thence following a line drawn **27 NM parallel to the coastline** to **3306N 03435E** — **3306N 03506E**, closing the boundary. Vertical limits **GND to UNL**. `[Israel AIP ENR 2.1, cycle effective 2022-11-03]`
- **Confirmed containment:** 🟩 **Ben Gurion Intl (LLBG)** sits within the FIR, inside the Ben-Gurion TMA (§3, sub-sectors below).
- **Internal division — two sectors, each civil/military split:**
  - **Northern Sector** — bounded 314200N/0353100E, 314600N/0350800E, 315500N/0345300E, 315600N/0344200E, 315600N/0342900E northward along the FIR boundary (Lebanon/Syria cease-fire lines, Israel/Jordan border). Comprises three sub-sectors: (1) **over land, domestic IFR/CVFR only** — worked by **IDFAF ACC** as **"PLUTO" control** (Hebrew, H24); (2) **over water, IFR/CVFR** — worked by the civil **Northern Sector ACC** as **"Tel-Aviv Control"** (English, H24); (3) **land & water, IFR overflights to/from the Amman FIR plus upper-airspace traffic** — worked as **"Tel-Aviv East"** (English, H24, civil unit). `[Israel AIP ENR 2.1]`
  - **Southern Sector** — the remainder of the FIR south of the Northern Sector boundary, along the Israel/Jordan and Israel/Egypt borders. Civil traffic worked by **"South Control"** (English, H24); the domestic over-land sub-sector is worked by **IDFAF ACC** as **"HAGAV" control** (Hebrew, H24). `[Israel AIP ENR 2.1]`
- **Vertical structure:** 🟧 the FIR itself is GND–UNL with no published intermediate ACC split found (unlike, e.g., a lower/upper FIR-UIR pair); the Ben-Gurion TMA beneath is itself finely sectorised (below).
- **Ben-Gurion TMA sectorisation (terminal, confirmed primary source):** the TMA is divided into **Western**, **Northern Sea Route**, **Southern Sea Route**, **Northern Beach**, **Southern Beach**, **Northern Central**, **Southern Central**, **Central** and **Eastern** sectors, each with its own lateral box and a distinct vertical band (from as low as 1,200 ft in the Southern Sea Route sector up to 10,000 ft in the Eastern sector) `[Israel AIP ENR 2.1]`. This is a dense, multi-layered terminal structure feeding a single coastal hub — expect tactical vectoring through several of these sectors on any LLBG arrival/departure.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Beirut (OLBB, Lebanon).** 🟥 Currently under an active EASA CZIB (avoid all levels) — see the Beirut FIR brief.
  - **NE — the Israel/Syria cease-fire line airspace (Damascus FIR, OSTT).** 🟥 Also CZIB-affected per the Middle East area brief.
  - **E — Amman (OJAC, Jordan).** The boundary runs along the Israel/Jordan border; the "Tel-Aviv East" sub-sector specifically manages overflights to/from this FIR.
  - **SW — Cairo (HECC, Egypt).** The boundary runs along the Israel/Egypt border.
  - **W — Nicosia (LCCC, Cyprus), offshore.** 🟧 the FIR's own boundary description extends only "27 NM parallel to the coastline" plus a 46 NM arc around BGN VOR — a fairly tight offshore limit; whether Nicosia FIR is the immediate seaward neighbour (vs. an intervening area) was not independently confirmed. A 2021-vintage FAA advisory groups LLLL/OJAC/OLBB/LCCC as a regional Eastern-Mediterranean adjacency set (tier-4 corroboration only).

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Civil ANSP:** 🟩 **Israel Airports Authority (IAA)** — provides ATS for the civil sectors of the Tel-Aviv FIR and the Ben-Gurion TMA/Approach/Tower.
- **Military ATSU:** 🟧 **Israel Defense Force Air Force (IDFAF)** operates the domestic over-land sub-sectors of both the Northern ("PLUTO") and Southern ("HAGAV") Sectors, working IFR/CVFR domestic traffic in Hebrew — a civil/military split unique among the K Global network's Middle East FIRs. Civil international transit traffic is expected on the civil-unit sectors (Tel-Aviv Control, Tel-Aviv East, South Control).
- **FIS / FSS:** IAA AIS/FIS; no uncontrolled pockets identified — the FIR is controlled GND–UNL throughout.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 radar throughout; 🟧 **GNSS integrity is a live regional caveat** — jamming/interference has been reported in the area (§10) as a surveillance/navigation caveat rather than a primary-surveillance gap.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **not implemented** in the European sense — a fixed ATS-route network with tactical vectoring, consistent with the wider MID region, funnelling into the dense multi-sector Ben-Gurion TMA (§3).
- **Airway spine:** 🟥 per the Middle East area brief, the region's usable route set toward/away from this FIR is currently constrained by the wider conflict picture; specifically, routings that would transit the Lebanon/Syria boundary sectors of this FIR are affected by the CZIB status of the neighbouring Beirut/Damascus FIRs. Confirm the flyable route at planning — it may be smaller than the published network.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the "Tel-Aviv East" sub-sector specifically manages the Amman-FIR overflight/entry-exit traffic; southern entry/exit toward Cairo (Egypt) is worked by South Control. The SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Ben Gurion Intl (LLBG)** — Israel's sole K Global-served international gateway, within the finely sectorised Ben-Gurion TMA (§3). Terminal detail is in the airport briefing (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 the Tel-Aviv FIR covers the **flat Israeli coastal plain and the adjacent Eastern Mediterranean** — LLBG itself sits at 134 ft AMSL with no close-in high terrain of CFIT concern on any arrival/departure/missed-approach path (per the LLBG airport brief). The Judean Hills lie inland but are not a factor for standard instrument procedures at the served field.
- **Boundary terrain:** no significant high-terrain belt straddles this FIR's boundary in a way that is operationally significant for a standard LLBG transit; unlike the Amman or Emirates FIRs, Tel-Aviv has no comparable mountain-range consideration.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟩 not a terrain-driven constraint given the benign coastal-plain topography. The FIR's real escape/diversion constraint is **airspace availability across the neighbouring conflict-affected FIRs** (§10), not terrain.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟩 not relevant — Mediterranean coastal climate; the operative seasonal driver is occasional khamsin/sharav heat events (§11), not cold-temperature correction.

## 10. Special-use airspace

> 🟥 **STANDING CAUTION — REGIONAL SECURITY/AIRSPACE TENSION. This section is perishable. Re-check the current NOTAM/state-bulletin/CZIB picture at planning (§11) before every single operation into this FIR — the situation changes at short notice. This is a factual operational caution; no party, cause or political position is asserted.**

- **Regional security exposure (the headline hazard):** 🟥 Tel Aviv (LLLL) FIR and the surrounding Eastern Mediterranean/Levant airspace are subject to a variable, NOTAM-driven closure/restriction risk given the wider regional security environment. As at build date, EASA's **Information Note on the threat and risk situation in the airspace of the Middle East and Persian Gulf** (issued 14 Jul 2026, valid to 31 Aug 2026) covers Israel (Tel Aviv FIR — LLLL) alongside Jordan, Oman and Saudi Arabia at a **"take potential risk into account in risk assessment and routing decisions"** tier — a lighter-weight advisory than the "avoid at all altitudes" CZIBs currently in force for the neighbouring Iran (Tehran/OIIX), Iraq (Baghdad/ORBB), Lebanon (Beirut/OLBB) and Jordan (Amman/OJAC) FIRs (§10 of the Amman and Beirut FIR briefs; Middle East area brief §9). *(This tiering reflects the situation as researched at build date and is itself live/perishable — confirm the current EASA classification for Israel specifically before relying on it.)*
- **GNSS jamming & spoofing — 🟥 reported in the area.** Consistent with the pervasive Eastern Mediterranean/Gulf pattern documented in the Middle East area brief, GNSS jamming/interference has been reported affecting the Tel Aviv FIR and Ben Gurion arrivals/departures. Brief the DME/DME/IRU cross-check and conventional-navaid fallback (§5), and notify ATC early if GNSS degradation is suspected.
- **Military / restricted / danger areas:** 🟧 Israel publishes restricted/danger/prohibited areas (military ranges, sensitive sites) across the FIR, consistent with the civil/military sector split described in §3; activation and status are AIP/NOTAM-published — confirm active at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 standard Israeli civil international arrival for scheduled operations; confirm no operator/state-specific routing directive applies given the §10 live picture, and note that some states have historically restricted their own operators' overflight in this FIR and neighbouring airspace during periods of heightened tension.

> **SUA / security currency:** re-check the **EASA CZIB/Information Note, state NOTAMs (Israel + surrounding), ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository and operator routing policy** at planning — the §10 picture is durable awareness that this is a live-risk FIR, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO standard — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance/filed route and levels, attempt the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Israel-specific refinement at AIP ENR 1.8/GEN; in the current security environment, deviating from a cleared routing without coordination carries elevated misidentification risk given the civil/military sector split (§3/§4).
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical via ATC in this controlled FIR; request the deviation from ATC and comply with the vector/level issued. No oceanic offset table applies.
- **GNSS-degradation contingency:** 🟥 with jamming/spoofing reported (§10), expect FMS position drift and spurious terrain/GPWS alerts — cross-check DME/DME/IRU, advise ATC, request radar vectors, and follow OM E GNSS-interference guidance.
- **Regional-security contingency:** 🟥 plan every operation against the current CZIB/Information Note/closure map; pre-brief a usable alternate that does not itself sit in a currently CZIB-affected FIR (see §9's OLBA caveat).
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Khamsin/sharav events** (hot, dry, dust-laden easterly wind, mainly spring/autumn) — can reduce visibility and add crosswind/turbulence considerations at LLBG.
  - **Low-visibility events (RVR <800 m / vis <1200 m)** occur but are infrequent for this coastal Mediterranean field; **no CAT III backup exists at LLBG** — an LVP event is a real diversion driver (see LLBG airport brief).
  - **Convection** is generally limited over this coastal climate zone; **volcanic ash/space weather** are not planning drivers at these latitudes.
