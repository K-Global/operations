# Muscat (OOMM) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the Sultanate of Oman plus a large Arabian Sea oceanic sector off its southern/eastern coast, containing **Muscat Intl (OOMS)** and **Salalah (OOSA)** · **Parent area brief:** [Middle East](../../middle-east.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Emirates (OMAE) NW · Tehran (OIIX) N (Strait of Hormuz / Musandam approach) · Sanaa (OYSC) SW/W (Yemen — partial ATS delegation to Muscat ACC, oceanic) · Karachi (OPKR) E (Pakistan, across the Gulf of Oman/Arabian Sea) 🟧

**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — FIR structure/adjacency corroborated vs the K Global Middle East area brief and public tier-4 sources; **oceanic/Yemen boundary and precise adjacency decimals 🟧 unverified**; **conflict-zone spillover is live/perishable and flagged 🟥 (re-check at planning)**

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Muscat is a **mixed FIR** — continental radar/ADS-B airspace over Oman itself, plus a large **procedural Arabian Sea oceanic sector** to the south/east where the FIR functions as a remote/oceanic gateway on the region's southern-bypass flank. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC. Terminal procedures for OOMS and OOSA live in the airport briefings (§9). **The FIR sits adjacent to the Strait of Hormuz conflict-zone core (§10) — re-check the live picture at planning.** Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** the Muscat FIR covers the **entire Sultanate of Oman** — including the **Musandam Peninsula exclave** at the tip of the Strait of Hormuz — plus a **large Arabian Sea oceanic sector** extending south/east of the Omani coast toward the Karachi/Mumbai flank. It is the K Global network's **southern-bypass exit** toward the Indian Ocean/South Asia.
- **Confirmed containment:** 🟩 **Muscat Intl (OOMS)** and **Salalah (OOSA)** both sit inside the Muscat FIR (OOMM).
- **Vertical structure:** 🟧 controlled airspace to the upper limit over continental Oman (**Class A** upper band, Class C/D Muscat/Salalah TMAs beneath); the oceanic sector is **procedural** with RNAV 10 (RNP 10) separation. Confirm the exact Class-A floor and oceanic lower limit at Oman AIP ENR 2.1.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **NW — Emirates (OMAE, UAE).** The K Global bridge-hub FIR; shared **Hajar Mountains** terrain along this boundary (see the [Emirates (OMAE) FIR brief](emirates-omae.md) §8).
  - **N — Tehran (OIIX, Iran).** 🟥 **The sensitive boundary** at the Musandam/Strait-of-Hormuz tip — the current conflict-zone core sits immediately across this line (§10).
  - **SW/W — Sanaa (OYSC, Yemen).** Oceanic boundary over the Arabian Sea; per the Middle East area brief, ATS on **portions of certain Sanaa-FIR airways is temporarily delegated to Muscat ACC** given Yemen's degraded/closed status — a durable operational quirk of this FIR. 🟧
  - **E — Karachi (OPKR, Pakistan).** 🟧 Oceanic boundary across the Gulf of Oman/Arabian Sea — unconfirmed against AIP; treat as tier-4 geography pending confirmation.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 not confirmed publicly at the sector level — expect a continental Oman ACC sector set (Muscat/Salalah flows) plus a separate oceanic/procedural working position for the Arabian Sea sector. Confirm at Oman AIP or in-sim charts.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Continental ATSU:** 🟧 **Muscat Area Control Centre (Oman ACC)** — radar/ADS-B surveillance control over Omani airspace; terminal approach control at Muscat (OOMS) and Salalah (OOSA).
- **Oceanic/procedural ATSU:** 🟧 the Arabian Sea sector is worked procedurally; the exact unit name and the **Sanaa-FIR delegation extent** (§3) are not confirmed publicly — pull from Oman AIP/NOTAM.
- **FIS / FSS:** Oman **AIS/FIS** (PACA); not normally a factor for an IFR routing that stays within controlled/procedural service.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 radar + ADS-B over continental Oman; 🟧 **no radar/ADS-B** over the oceanic sector — procedural position reporting applies there.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Muscat FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — MID region RVSM. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 **RNAV 5** enroute over continental Oman; **RNAV 1/RNP 1** terminal; **RNP APCH** arrivals; 🟧 **RNAV 10 (RNP 10)** over the Arabian Sea oceanic sector (per the Middle East area brief). See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md). Confirm oceanic navigation approval is held before planning the oceanic sector.
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟩 **not applicable** — the MID region uses **25 kHz** VHF spacing.
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 **no confirmed CPDLC coverage** in this FIR (unlike Jeddah/Emirates upper airspace) — voice primary throughout; FANS-1/A datalink would be the relevant standard for the oceanic sector if introduced. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** transponder required continental; **ADS-B Out** underpins continental surveillance. Confirm equipage on the OFP; ADS-B has no coverage over the oceanic sector.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a continental transit is worked across **Muscat ACC** sectors feeding OOMS/OOSA approach; the oceanic sector uses HF position-report frequencies. Fly the current-AIRAC/AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** Oman **AIS/FIS** (PACA).
- **Language:** 🟩 **English** (ICAO) throughout.
- **Channel spacing:** 🟩 **25 kHz** VHF (no 8.33 kHz mandate).
- **HF / SATVOICE:** 🟧 required over the Arabian Sea oceanic sector where VHF/radar coverage ends; SATVOICE as long-range backup. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard; **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **not implemented** in the European sense — a fixed ATS-route network over continental Oman with tactical vectoring around Muscat/Salalah; procedural oceanic tracks over the Arabian Sea sector.
- **Airway spine:** Muscat is the **southern-bypass exit** toward the Indian Ocean/South Asia, feeding traffic from the Jeddah/Emirates corridor onward across the Arabian Sea oceanic sector. The usable set is **NOTAM-dependent** given the adjacent conflict picture (§10).
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the FIR-boundary transition fixes toward Emirates (NW) and the oceanic entry points on the Arabian Sea flank; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Muscat Intl (OOMS)** and **Salalah (OOSA)** — both K Global-served fields. Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 the **northern Hajar Mountains** belt (shared with the Emirates FIR boundary) is the significant high terrain, relevant to OOMS-area arrivals/departures. Elsewhere the continental FIR is largely low desert/coastal, and the oceanic sector is entirely over water.
- **Boundary terrain:** the Hajar terrain continues NW across the **Emirates FIR (OMAE)** boundary — see the [Emirates (OMAE) FIR brief](emirates-omae.md) §8 for the Jebel Jais detail.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟧 not a constraint over continental Oman (dense-enough field coverage near the coast); 🟥 **a genuine constraint over the oceanic sector**, where the nearest usable field may be a significant distance away — plan the ETP/critical-point against the current closure map, not just distance.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟩 not relevant — hot climate; the operational temperature driver is **high-temperature/density-altitude** performance (see OM E and the airport briefs), not cold-temperature correction.

## 10. Special-use airspace

> 🟥 **CONFLICT-ZONE-ADJACENT AIRSPACE — this section is perishable. Re-check the current CZIB/NOTAM picture at planning (§11).**

- **Conflict-zone spillover (the headline hazard):** 🟥 the Muscat FIR itself is assessed **Risk Level 3 (Caution)** and, unlike Bahrain/Kuwait/Qatar/Emirates, is **not** under a blanket avoid-all-levels CZIB — Oman airspace **remains open** and is described as a **key part of the southern-bypass route** around the wider Gulf conflict zone, carrying significant Europe–Asia overflight traffic. However, the current **EASA CZIB 2026-07R1** specifically advises operators **not to operate over the waters of the Gulf of Oman within the OOMM/Muscat FIR west of longitude 58°E**, and a separate EASA Information Note flags **general caution** for Oman given the wider Israel/Jordan/Saudi risk picture. France has issued a caution (not avoid) NOTAM for Oman. *(This is durable awareness that OOMM sits adjacent to the conflict core; the specific advisory reference and the 58°E line are live data — confirm the current CZIB/NOTAM state at planning before relying on any oceanic routing.)*
- **Sanaa-FIR ATS delegation:** 🟧 per the Middle East area brief, ATS on portions of certain Sanaa (Yemen, OYSC) airways is **temporarily delegated to Muscat ACC** given Yemen's degraded/closed status — a durable structural note, not itself a hazard, but it means Muscat ACC workload/coverage extends beyond the nominal FIR boundary on the SW flank.
- **Military restricted / danger / prohibited:** 🟧 Oman publishes restricted/danger/prohibited areas per the AIP; activation and status are AIP/NOTAM-published — confirm active at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 standard Omani civil operation for a normal southern-bypass transit; confirm any operator/state-specific routing directive at planning given the adjacent conflict picture.

> **SUA / conflict currency:** re-check the **EASA CZIB, French/state NOTAMs, ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository and operator routing policy** at planning — the §10 picture is durable awareness, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO standard — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance/filed route and levels, attempt the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Omani AIP refinement at ENR 1.8/GEN.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical over continental Oman — request the deviation from ATC. 🟧 Over the oceanic sector, apply the ICAO Doc 4444 in-flight contingency method (strategic offset, weather deviation) as for any procedural/oceanic airspace — see [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Salalah khareef monsoon (Jun–Sep)** — low ceilings, drizzle, reduced visibility at OOSA and the southern coast.
  - **Summer heat & density altitude** — a hot-and-high performance driver at both fields (see OM E and the airport briefs).
  - **Shamal dust** — occasional reduced-visibility events in the north, less pronounced than in the central Gulf.
  - **Volcanic ash / space weather:** not a planning driver at these latitudes.
