## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** the Bahrain FIR covers the **Kingdom of Bahrain** (the main island plus its causeway-connected islands) and a **compact sector of the western/central Persian Gulf**. It was historically one of the larger and busiest MID-region FIRs, controlling Gulf airspace well beyond Bahrain's own territory — including, until 2022, the airspace over Qatar. 🟧 Its current extent is **smaller** following the **Doha FIR (OTDF)** carve-out (phased 2022–2023; QCAA now controls Qatari airspace surface-to-unlimited, including over international waters).
- **Confirmed containment:** 🟩 **Bahrain Intl (OBBI)** — the Kingdom's sole international gateway — sits inside the Bahrain FIR (OBBB).
- **Vertical structure:** 🟧 controlled airspace to the upper limit — **Class A** upper band, **Class C/D** Bahrain TMA beneath. 🟧 Transition Level FL150 / Transition Altitude 13,000 ft is reported tier-4; confirm at Bahrain AIP ENR 2.1/1.7.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **E — Emirates (OMAE, UAE).** The K Global bridge-hub FIR; see the [Emirates (OMAE) FIR brief](emirates-omae.md).
  - **SE — Doha/Qatar (OTDF).** The **newest MID-region boundary** (formalised 2022–2023) — Qatar now fully manages its own airspace, where Bahrain previously did.
  - **S/SW — Jeddah (OEJD, Saudi Arabia).** The largest MID-region FIR, forming the backbone of the southern-bypass corridor.
  - **NW — Kuwait (OKAC).** The compact mid-Gulf FIR currently **overflight-prohibited** (see the companion [Kuwait (OKAC) FIR brief](kuwait-okac.md)).
  - **N — Tehran (OIIX, Iran).** 🟥 **The sensitive boundary** down the middle of the Gulf — the focus of the current conflict-zone picture (§10), as for the Emirates FIR.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 not confirmed publicly at the sector level for a compact FIR of this size; expect a small number of area sectors feeding the Bahrain TMA. Confirm at Bahrain AIP or in-sim charts.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Enroute ATSU:** 🟧 **Bahrain area control** — radar/ADS-B surveillance control of the FIR, based at/near Bahrain Intl (Muharraq).
- **Terminal ATSU:** 🟧 Bahrain approach control serves OBBI; precise unit name/AoR split to confirm at Bahrain AIP.
- **FIS / FSS:** Bahrain **AIS/FIS** (BCAA); not a factor for an IFR routing that stays in controlled airspace throughout.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 continuous **radar + ADS-B**; no procedural pockets on a normal IFR routing. 🟧 **GNSS integrity is degraded by reported GPS spoofing/jamming** in the region — a surveillance/navigation caveat (§10), consistent with the wider Gulf picture documented in the Middle East area brief.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a transit is handed across the Bahrain area-control sectors and the Bahrain approach unit; the exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC/AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** Bahrain **AIS/FIS** (BCAA).
- **Language:** 🟩 **English** (ICAO) throughout.
- **Channel spacing:** 🟩 **25 kHz** VHF (no 8.33 kHz mandate).
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **not implemented** in the European sense — a fixed ATS-route network with tactical vectoring around the Bahrain TMA.
- **Airway spine:** 🟥 Bahrain sits on the **central-Gulf trunk** between Kuwait/Saudi Arabia and Qatar/Emirates; note that **routing across this compact FIR is currently constrained** by the conflict-zone picture (§10) — the flyable route set is smaller than the published network and changes by NOTAM. Confirm at planning.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the FIR-boundary transition fixes toward Emirates/Doha/Jeddah/Kuwait and the Bahrain TMA arrival transitions; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Bahrain Intl (OBBI)** — the Kingdom's sole international gateway. Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 no significant terrain — Bahrain is a low, largely flat island group with extensive land reclamation; the surrounding Gulf sector is entirely low/water. Not a cruise or terminal terrain-clearance driver.
- **Boundary terrain:** none of note across any adjacent FIR boundary.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟩 not a terrain-limited problem, but 🟧 **diversion-field density is thin within this compact FIR** (only OBBI) — plan the nearest suitable field across the Emirates/Doha/Jeddah boundary if needed.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟩 not relevant — hot climate; the operational temperature driver is **high-temperature/density-altitude** performance in summer (see OM E and the OBBI airport brief), not cold-temperature correction.

## 10. Special-use airspace

> 🟥 **CONFLICT-ZONE / LIVE-SECURITY AIRSPACE — this section is perishable. Re-check the current CZIB/NOTAM picture at planning (§11); the state of the Gulf situation changes rapidly.**

- **Conflict-zone exposure (the headline hazard):** 🟥 Bahrain is assessed **Risk Level 3 (Caution)** and sits at the centre of the ongoing Iran-related regional tension, hosting significant US military facilities. The current **EASA CZIB 2026-07R1 advises operators not to operate in the airspace of Bahrain (Bahrain FIR – OBBB) at any altitude or flight level**, alongside Kuwait, Qatar, UAE and the Gulf-of-Oman waters west of 58°E. France has issued a NOTAM (LFFF F1579/26, current as at build date) requesting French operators not to enter Bahrain's airspace at all. The FIR **closed and reopened several times** during Feb–Jul 2026 before returning to broadly open operation; March 2026 saw Iranian missile/drone attacks affect airspace around Bahrain. Reported hazards include **missile/drone activity and falling intercept debris**, and **GPS spoofing/jamming across the FIR** consistent with the wider Gulf picture. *(This is durable awareness that OBBB is a live-risk FIR; the specific advisory reference and validity are live data — confirm the current CZIB/NOTAM state at planning before relying on any routing.)*
- **Military restricted / danger / prohibited:** 🟧 Bahrain publishes restricted/danger/prohibited areas per the AIP (military facilities are extensive relative to the country's small size); activation and status are AIP/NOTAM-published — confirm active at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 standard Bahraini civil operation for a normal transit, **but** current regional restrictions and any operator/state-specific routing directives apply — confirm at planning.

> **SUA / conflict currency:** re-check the **EASA CZIB, French/state NOTAMs, ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository and operator routing policy** at planning — the §10 picture is durable awareness, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO standard — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance/filed route and levels, attempt the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Bahraini AIP refinement at ENR 1.8/GEN; note that in the current security environment, deviating from a cleared/managed corridor without coordination carries elevated risk — comply with ATC and published contingency routings.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — **request the deviation from ATC** and comply with the vector/level issued. No oceanic offset table applies.
- **GNSS degradation contingency:** 🟧 with **GPS spoofing/jamming reported**, be prepared for FMS position drift/spurious terrain or nav alerts — cross-check with DME/DME/IRU, advise ATC, and follow OM E guidance on GNSS-interference response.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Summer heat & density altitude (Jun–Sep)** — extreme temperatures; a hot-and-high performance driver at OBBI.
  - **Shamal dust and reduced visibility** — blowing dust events can trigger LVO; a terminal driver.
  - **Volcanic ash / space weather:** not a planning driver at these latitudes.
