# Yerevan (UDDD) — FIR Briefing

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Yerevan is a **radar-controlled continental FIR** over a small, high-terrain country in the South Caucasus, at the northern edge of the Middle East/Gulf conflict-avoidance picture — it has seen **increased transit traffic** as operators route north around closed Gulf/Iranian corridors. Routings and levels here are planning context — file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical radar-ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for the aerodrome inside the FIR (UDYZ) live in the airport briefing (§9). This FIR sits amid **closed land borders and limited-routing context** with two of its four neighbours (§10, factual, non-political) — live restrictions are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** the Yerevan FIR covers the **whole Republic of Armenia** — a small, landlocked, mountainous country in the South Caucasus, bordered by Georgia (N), Azerbaijan (E, and its Nakhchivan exclave to the SW), Iran (S) and Turkey (W). The FIR is compact relative to its Gulf/Middle East neighbours, but sits at a genuinely significant crossroads: the **northern edge of the Middle East conflict-avoidance routing picture** described in the Middle East area brief's "northern bypass" corridor.
- **Confirmed containment:** 🟩 **Zvartnots Intl (UDYZ)**, Armenia's principal international gateway near Yerevan, sits within the Yerevan FIR (UDDD).
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Tbilisi (UGGG, Georgia).** Shares **Free Route Airspace South Caucasus (FRASC)** with Yerevan FIR (§7) — the closest operational partnership among Armenia's neighbours.
  - **E — Baku (UBBA, Azerbaijan).** 🟧 The Armenia–Azerbaijan relationship has been marked by conflict and closed borders (Nagorno-Karabakh and related tension); civil-aviation overflight relations and any current routing constraint should be confirmed at planning — this is a **factual context note**, not a political assessment, and the situation has been reported as improving at times.
  - **S — Tehran (OIIX, Iran).** The Iranian border to the south; Iran is itself a conflict-affected FIR under the wider Middle East picture (see the Middle East area brief §9) — relevant if a routing ever approaches this boundary.
  - **W — Ankara (LTAA, Turkey).** 🟧 The Turkish border; the **civil land border between Armenia and Turkey has been closed since 1993**, and civil-aviation relations have varied with the political relationship over time (including periods of airspace restriction on Armenian-flagged aircraft, and periods of normalisation — e.g. new direct scheduled air service between Istanbul and Yerevan reported in 2026). See the companion [Istanbul (LTBB)](istanbul-ltbb.md) brief §10 for the Turkish-side note.
- **Vertical structure:** 🟧 not independently confirmed; treat as a single ACC structure (the "Yerevan" ATC Centre) covering the compact FIR unless the current ARMATS AIP indicates a lower/upper split.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 the Yerevan ATC Centre houses ACC, APP, Flight Information and Air Traffic Flow Management working positions under a single ATC Supervisor structure per ARMATS's own description — consistent with a FIR of this compact size; exact enroute sector count not published here.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Enroute ATSU:** **"Yerevan" Air Traffic Control Centre (UDDD, ARMATS)** — radar-controlled area control for the whole FIR; ARMATS is a 100%-state-owned CJSC established to provide air navigation services within Armenian airspace.
- **Terminal ATSU:** Zvartnots (UDYZ) approach/TMA control, integrated within the same Yerevan ATC Centre structure (ACC/APP/FIS/ATFM positions co-located per ARMATS).
- **FIS / FSS:** ARMATS **Flight Information Service**, co-located with ACC/APP at the Yerevan ATC Centre.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 radar-controlled; 🟧 exact radar/ADS-B coverage extent and any procedural pockets (e.g. over the highest terrain) not independently confirmed.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **FRASC — Free Route Airspace South Caucasus** is implemented across the **Yerevan and Tbilisi FIRs** (Armenia and Georgia), allowing direct point-to-point routeing between defined entry/exit points rather than a fixed airway network across that combined area. Confirm current FL floor and hours of applicability (H24 vs time-limited) at planning.
- **Airway spine (where FRA not available):** below the FRA floor, or where FRASC does not apply (e.g. toward the Azerbaijan or Iran boundary), the **fixed ATS-route network** governs; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact structure.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** FRASC entry/exit points at the Georgia (Tbilisi FIR) boundary, and conventional boundary fixes toward Azerbaijan/Iran/Turkey; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Traffic pattern note (durable, live-adjacent):** 🟧 the Yerevan FIR has seen **materially increased transit traffic** during periods when the Middle East "northern bypass" corridor around the Caucasus is used to avoid closed Gulf/Iranian airspace (see the Middle East area brief §6) — a durable pattern to be aware of, though the specific traffic level is a live/perishable data point, not stored here.
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Zvartnots Intl (UDYZ)** — the sole K Global-served field and Armenia's principal international gateway, near Yerevan. Terminal detail is in the airport briefing (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 Armenia is a **genuinely mountainous country** — the Lesser Caucasus range crosses the north, and **Mount Aragats (~4,090 m / ~13,420 ft)**, Armenia's highest peak, sits north-west of Yerevan. The Zvartnots (UDYZ) terminal area itself sits on a plateau at significant elevation (Yerevan sits around ~1,000 m / ~3,300 ft, with the airport itself lower in the Ararat valley) — confirm exact field elevation in the UDYZ airport brief.
- **Boundary terrain:** 🟧 **Mount Ararat (~5,137 m / ~16,850 ft)**, though geographically adjacent to Armenia, lies just across the **Turkish border** — a prominent, very high landmark visible from the Ararat valley and relevant to situational terrain awareness near the western boundary, even though it sits in Ankara-FIR (Turkish) territory, not inside Yerevan FIR.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟧 the combination of **surrounding high terrain and a compact FIR with a single served aerodrome** means driftdown/escape planning should be taken seriously here — not the benign, dense-diversion picture of a flat continental FIR. Confirm MEA/MORA and escape-route planning against current charts; this is flagged for attention rather than fully modelled in this brief.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟧 relevant at the terminal level given the elevation and continental climate (cold winters) — see the UDYZ airport brief and OM E Cold Weather Operations, not an enroute cruise factor here.

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **Closed land borders — factual context, not political commentary:** 🟧 Armenia's **land borders with Turkey (west) and Azerbaijan (east) have been closed** for regular civilian crossing for an extended period (the Turkish border since 1993; Azerbaijan border crossings largely closed amid the ongoing regional dispute), leaving **Georgia and Iran** as Armenia's open land-border connections. This is noted here as a **planning-relevant fact about the surrounding environment** — it shapes which neighbouring states are realistic diversion/alternate options — and **not** as a statement on the underlying political situation, which this document does not comment on.
- **Civil-aviation overflight relations (factual, variable):** 🟧 civil-aviation relations between Armenia and its western/eastern neighbours have **varied with the political relationship** — periods of airspace restriction on Armenian-registered aircraft by Turkey have been reported, alongside periods of normalisation (including new direct scheduled air service reported between Istanbul and Yerevan in 2026). **Confirm the current state of any bilateral routing constraint at planning** — this is a live, changeable fact, not a durable rule.
- **Military / restricted / danger areas:** 🟧 not published/verify — a compact FIR bordering an area of periodic regional tension (Armenia–Azerbaijan) may carry NOTAM-driven restrictions from time to time; confirm current AIP/NOTAM status at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 standard civil operation for a normal Zvartnots-bound transit is not expected to require special permits under settled conditions, but **given the variable bilateral picture with two of Armenia's four neighbours, confirm current requirements at planning** rather than assuming none apply.

> **SUA / border-context currency:** re-check current state NOTAMs, any bilateral routing restriction, and the ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository / OPSGROUP-safeairspace regional picture at planning — this list is durable, factual context, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** 🟧 assume ICAO-standard comms-failure procedure (squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance/filed route and levels, attempt the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5); confirm any ARMATS-specific refinement at the current AIP.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — **request the deviation from ATC** and comply with the vector/level issued. No oceanic offset table applies.
- **GNSS degradation contingency:** 🟧 the wider Caucasus/Gulf-avoidance traffic surge has coincided with reported GNSS interference in nearby conflict-adjacent airspace (see the Middle East area brief §9); while not specifically documented for Yerevan FIR itself, brief the DME/DME/IRU cross-check and conventional-navaid fallback (OM E) as a precaution given the surrounding environment.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Winter cold / icing** — a continental-mountain climate; significant cold-weather terminal considerations at Zvartnots (elevation, winter conditions) — see OM E Cold Weather Operations and the UDYZ airport brief.
  - **Mountain-wave / turbulence** — plausible given the surrounding Lesser Caucasus terrain, particularly on approach; a terminal/descent consideration rather than an enroute cruise one.
  - **Convection** in the warmer months over the Ararat valley and surrounding highlands; handled tactically.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, the current bilateral civil-aviation/border-restriction picture with Turkey and Azerbaijan, active restricted/danger areas, SIGMET/AIRMET, GNSS-interference advisories (regional), and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
