# Baghdad (ORBB) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the **Republic of Iraq**, containing **Baghdad Intl (ORBI)**, **Erbil Intl (ORER)**, **Basrah Intl (ORMM)** and **Jalal Talabani Intl/Sulaymaniyah (ORSJ)** · **Parent area brief:** [Middle East](../../middle-east.md) · **Adjacent FIRs:** Ankara (LTAA, Turkey) N · Tehran (OIIX, Iran) E · Kuwait (OKAC) SE · Jeddah (OEJD, Saudi Arabia) S/SW · Amman (OJAC, Jordan) W · Damascus (OSTT, Syria) NW

**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Baghdad is a single, notionally radar-controlled FIR whose surveillance and control infrastructure remain degraded by decades of conflict; it is **technically open to overflight** but is treated by most operators as unflyable. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. **🟥 STANDING CAUTION — CONFLICT ZONE: Iraqi airspace carries active conflict-zone advisories (EASA CZIB, FAA SFAR), reported GPS/GNSS jamming and spoofing, and a history of sudden closures. Crews MUST verify current airspace status and NOTAMs before every operation that could touch this FIR — nothing in this file substitutes for that check (§9/§11).** This is a factual operational caution, not a political statement. Terminal procedures for aerodromes inside the FIR live in the airport briefings (§9). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** the Baghdad FIR covers the **whole of the Republic of Iraq** — the Mesopotamian plain between the Tigris and Euphrates, the northern Kurdistan region (Erbil/Sulaymaniyah), and the southern approach to the Persian/Arabian Gulf around Basrah.
- **Confirmed containment:** 🟩 **Baghdad Intl (ORBI)**, **Erbil Intl (ORER)**, **Basrah Intl (ORMM)** and **Jalal Talabani Intl/Sulaymaniyah (ORSJ)** all sit inside the single Baghdad FIR (ORBB).
- **Vertical structure:** 🟧 not independently verified in the sources reviewed; treat class-per-band as **unconfirmed** pending direct AIP access — see §12.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Ankara (LTAA, Turkey).** The northern boundary, near the Kurdistan region; the area has seen periodic Turkish military air activity along the border.
  - **E — Tehran (OIIX, Iran).** 🟥 **A sensitive boundary** — per public secondary sources a **temporary Transfer of Service (TOS) arrangement above FL195** has applied between Baghdad and Tehran ACCs at times; both FIRs carry active conflict-zone advisories, and reported **GPS jamming/spoofing in northern Iraq has reportedly driven aircraft toward inadvertent entry into Iranian airspace** — confirm current TOS/boundary-handling arrangements at AIP/NOTAM before relying on this. 🟧
  - **SE — Kuwait (OKAC).** Kuwait FIR overflight is separately prohibited outright (see the Middle East area brief §6) — not a routing option regardless of the Baghdad-FIR picture.
  - **S/SW — Jeddah (OEJD, Saudi Arabia).** Toward the Arabian Peninsula; see the companion **[Jeddah (OEJD) FIR brief](jeddah-oejd.md)**.
  - **W — Amman (OJAC, Jordan).** Toward the Levant.
  - **NW — Damascus (OSTT, Syria).** Also a high-risk, conflict-affected FIR (see the Middle East area brief §9); not an easier alternative to Baghdad.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 not published in the sources reviewed. The only routing detail with public corroboration is the set of specific airways used by operators who do still overfly: **M860 / L602 / L718 northbound** and **UM688 southbound**, all constrained to **FL320 and above** (French AIC 011/26) — see §5/§9.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Enroute ATSU:** 🟧 **Baghdad ACC** (working name) under **ICAA**; the FIR is nominally controlled but ATC capacity, radar coverage and staffing have been rebuilt in stages since 2003 and should not be assumed to match a developed-Gulf-FIR standard. Confirm current operational status at AIP/NOTAM before any planning reliance.
- **Terminal ATSU:** 🟧 approach units serve ORBI (Baghdad), ORER (Erbil), ORMM (Basrah) and ORSJ (Sulaymaniyah); precise unit names/AoR not confirmed in the sources reviewed.
- **FIS / FSS:** 🟧 Iraq AIS/FIS (ICAA) — coverage and reliability not independently verified.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟧 degraded relative to the developed Gulf FIRs; treat any surveillance-dependent procedure (e.g., reduced separation) as unconfirmed without a current AIP check.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which nominally apply in this FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them, and — given the standing conflict-zone caution — equipment applicability here is secondary to the go/no-go decision in §9.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 nominally **FL290–FL410** (ICAO MID region RVSM) — see [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md). 🟥 **Independent of RVSM status, a hard vertical floor applies for many operators**: **FAA SFAR 77** prohibits US operators below **FL320** (valid through 31 Oct 2027); **UK AIP ENR 1.1** and **Canada AIC 17/26** recommend not below **FL320**; **Germany AIC 08/25** recommends not below **FL280**; **France AIC 011/26** restricts French operators to specific airways (M860/L602/L718 northbound, UM688 southbound) **at or above FL320** — see §9 for full detail.
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟧 **RNAV 5** enroute assumed per the regional MID implementation but **not independently confirmed for Iraq** — see [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md). 🟥 With **GPS jamming/spoofing reported in northern Iraq** (current Iraqi NOTAM, Jul 2026), treat GNSS-dependent navigation as unreliable in this FIR and brief conventional-navaid/DME-DME/IRU fallback before any consideration of transit.
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟧 assumed **not applicable** (25 kHz MID-region standard) — not independently confirmed.
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 **no CPDLC availability confirmed** for this FIR in the sources reviewed; plan for **voice-only** operation. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** and **ADS-B Out** expected per MID-region/ICAO norms; confirm equipage requirement and reliability of ground reception at planning — surveillance quality in this FIR is not guaranteed.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟧 not applicable/confirmed — Iraq is not part of any FRA implementation; plan the published fixed ATS-route network if this FIR is ever used.
- **Airway spine (for reference only — see §9 before considering use):** the historically-used entry corridor runs **eastern Iraq, parallel to (but clear of) Iranian airspace** — specifically **M860 (between GADSI and NINVA)**, **UM688**, **L602 (between TASMI and ALPET)** and **L718 (between ALPET and KABAN)**, per French AIC 011/26, all requiring **FL320 or above** at all times.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 the **Mesopotamian plain** (centre/south, including Baghdad and Basrah) is generally **low and flat**. Terrain rises toward the **northeast (Kurdistan region / Erbil, Sulaymaniyah)** as the **Zagros foothills** begin — a terminal consideration at ORER/ORSJ rather than a cruise factor.
- **Boundary terrain:** the higher **Zagros Mountains** proper lie across the Iran (Tehran FIR, OIIX) boundary to the east — noted for completeness; not relevant given that corridor is avoided.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟥 the binding constraint in this FIR is **not terrain** but **where it is safe to divert or descend** given the active conflict/air-defence picture — an engine-out or decompression event over Iraq could require descent into contested airspace. This is a first-order planning consideration, not a secondary one.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟩 not a primary driver — hot arid climate; summer heat/density-altitude is the more relevant temperature factor (terminal, see airport briefs if ever used).

## 10. Special-use airspace

*(Numbered §10 per template; the conflict-zone material below is the substance of §9's caution and is consolidated here per the standard template structure — cross-referenced from §9 above.)*

> 🟥 **CONFLICT-ZONE / LIVE-SECURITY AIRSPACE — the defining feature of this FIR. This section is perishable. Re-check the current CZIB/NOTAM picture at planning (§11); do not treat this file's snapshot as current. This is a FACTUAL operational caution — no political commentary is intended or implied.**

- **EASA Conflict Zone Information Bulletin (CZIB 2026-05, issued 8 Jul 2026, valid to 31 Aug 2026):** 🟥 **advises operators not to operate within the airspace of Iraq (ORBB/Baghdad FIR) at any altitude or flight level.** Following military strikes involving Iran (28 Feb 2026) and subsequent regional retaliation, Iraqi airspace continues to be affected by **recurrent Iranian drone and ballistic-missile attacks, activation of air-defence systems, and the continued presence and operations of Iranian-backed militant groups (IBMGs)**. EASA assesses a **high risk of misidentification or unintended escalation at all altitudes and flight levels**, and notes Iraq's ability to reliably enforce airspace closures/restrictions is constrained by limited coordination and a fragmented security environment.
- **FAA SFAR 77 (issued 16 Oct 2024, extended to 31 Oct 2027):** 🟥 prohibits US air carriers, US commercial operators, FAA-certificated airmen and US-registered aircraft operators from operating in the Baghdad FIR (ORBB) **below FL320**, citing risk from Iranian-aligned militia group activity and third-party military operations not reliably deconflicted with civil aviation.
- **Other states:** 🟧 **France** (AIC 011/26, active since 28 Aug 2025) prohibits French operators from entering ORBB except on specific airways (M860/L602/L718 northbound, UM688 southbound) at or above **FL320**, per the exact routings in §7; **Italy** recommends avoiding ORBB entirely per EASA guidance; **UK** (AIP ENR 1.1) and **Canada** (AIC 17/26) recommend not below **FL320**; **Germany** (AIC 08/25) recommends not below **FL280**.
- **GNSS jamming & spoofing:** 🟥 current Iraqi NOTAM (ORBB A0279/26, issued 4 Jul 2026, valid to 4 Oct 2026) warns of **GPS jamming/spoofing interference in the northern part of Iraq** and instructs crews to inform ATC immediately and expect radar vectoring.
- **History / pattern (durable awareness, not a live status):** the FIR has been **repeatedly closed and reopened** in recent years in step with regional escalation — closed following the Mar 2026 US/Israel strikes on Iran and Iranian retaliation; reopened Jun 2026 after a ceasefire; risk assessed as continuing (EASA CZIB current as at build date) despite the reopening, due to non-state-actor activity and air-defence unpredictability. Earlier events include drone/missile strikes near Erbil (ORER) and ORSU in Apr 2023, and attacks affecting Baghdad Intl (ORBI) and Erbil air base in 2021.
- **Military / restricted / danger / prohibited areas:** 🟧 not independently catalogued in the sources reviewed beyond the conflict-zone advisories above; assume extensive military-controlled airspace given the security context and confirm at AIP/NOTAM if this FIR is ever assessed for use.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 Iraq nominally permits civil overflight (the FIR is not formally closed as at build date), but the practical effect of the advisories above is that **most operators, including this operation, should not plan to use this airspace** absent a fresh, specific risk assessment and command-level authorisation.

> **Conflict-zone currency:** re-check the **ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository, the current EASA CZIB, all applicable state NOTAMs (Iraq, France, Italy, UK, Germany, Canada, USA/FAA), and OPSGROUP/safeairspace** at planning — this section is durable awareness of a standing risk, not live clearance, and it changes at short notice.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO standard — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance / filed route and levels, attempt the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟥 In the current security environment, **any un-coordinated deviation carries elevated misidentification risk** — this is a materially higher-consequence scenario in ORBB than in a developed Gulf FIR.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** 🟧 radar-tactical where ATC capacity allows; given degraded surveillance, do not assume timely ATC response — see §4.
- **GNSS-degradation contingency:** 🟥 with **jamming/spoofing reported in northern Iraq**, expect FMS position drift and spurious terrain/GPWS alerts; cross-check DME/DME/IRU, advise ATC immediately, request radar vectors, and follow OM E GNSS-interference guidance.
- **Conflict-driven contingency:** 🟥 there is **no published safe-divert guidance for an in-flight event over ORBB while the CZIB stands** — this is precisely why the FIR is avoided. Any planning that could put the aircraft over Iraq requires a fresh, specific risk assessment before dispatch, not reliance on this durable brief.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):** extreme summer heat/dust across the Mesopotamian plain; shamal-pattern winds; not a primary planning driver relative to the security picture above.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** the **current EASA CZIB, FAA SFAR/NOTAM status, all applicable state NOTAMs, ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository, and OPSGROUP/safeairspace** (the single mission-critical item for this FIR) — plus GNSS-interference advisories and enroute NOTAMs if any operational scenario ever requires reassessing this FIR. **Not stored here.**
