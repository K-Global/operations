# Doha (OTDF) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the State of Qatar plus its surrounding Gulf waters, containing **Hamad Intl (OTHH)** · **Parent area brief:** [Middle East](../../middle-east.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Bahrain (OBBB) W/NW · Emirates (OMAE) E · Jeddah (OEJD) S (Saudi Arabia) · Tehran (OIIX) N 🟧

**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — FIR structure/adjacency corroborated vs the K Global Middle East area brief and public tier-4 sources; **the Doha FIR is the newest in the MID region (established 2022–2023) — some structural detail is still settling and flagged 🟧**; **conflict-zone status is live/perishable and flagged 🟥 (re-check at planning)**

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Doha is a **compact, dense, radar/ADS-B-surveillance-controlled FIR** at the centre of the Gulf, the **newest FIR in the MID region** — Qatar only began fully managing its own airspace (surface to unlimited, including international waters) in **March 2023**, having previously been controlled by Bahrain. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical radar-ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for OTHH live in the airport briefings (§9). **This FIR is under an active EASA conflict-zone advisory (Gulf tensions) — the SUA/conflict picture in §10 is live and MUST be re-checked at planning (§11).** Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** the Doha FIR covers the **State of Qatar** and its surrounding Gulf territorial and international waters. It is bounded by the **limit of Qatar's territorial waters and the sovereign boundary between Qatar and Saudi Arabia**, then the limit of Qatar/Bahrain territorial waters, per the QCAA implementation circular.
- **Confirmed containment:** 🟩 **Hamad Intl (OTHH)** — Qatar's sole active international gateway (Doha Intl/OTBD is closed to scheduled commercial traffic) — sits inside the Doha FIR (OTDF).
- **Vertical structure:** 🟧 **Phase 1** (from 8 Sep 2022) gave Doha control **surface to FL245** over most of Qatar's landmass, with **Bahrain FIR retaining control above FL245 and over offshore routes**. **Phase 2** (from 23 Mar 2023) completed the handover — Doha now controls **surface to unlimited altitude, including over international waters**. Confirm the current-AIRAC class-per-band at Qatar AIP ENR 2.1.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **W/NW — Bahrain (OBBB).** The FIR that **previously controlled Qatari airspace** before the 2022–2023 handover — see the [Bahrain (OBBB) FIR brief](bahrain-obbb.md) for the reciprocal boundary note.
  - **E — Emirates (OMAE, UAE).** The K Global bridge-hub FIR; see the [Emirates (OMAE) FIR brief](emirates-omae.md).
  - **S — Jeddah (OEJD, Saudi Arabia).** The largest MID-region FIR, along the Qatar/Saudi land boundary.
  - **N — Tehran (OIIX, Iran).** 🟧 QCAA is reported to have signed a similar operational agreement with **Iran** alongside Saudi Arabia, Bahrain and the UAE when activating the Doha FIR — the northern boundary sits across Gulf waters toward Iranian airspace, the same conflict-sensitive line noted for the Emirates and Bahrain FIRs (§10).
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 not confirmed publicly at the sector level for this newly-established FIR; expect a compact area-sector set feeding the Doha TMA. Confirm at Qatar AIP or in-sim charts.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Enroute ATSU:** 🟧 **Doha area control** — radar/ADS-B surveillance control of the FIR, based at/near Hamad Intl (OTHH). QCAA signed operational agreements with Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, the UAE and Iran to activate and interconnect the FIR with its neighbours.
- **Terminal ATSU:** 🟧 Doha approach control serves OTHH; precise unit name/AoR split to confirm at Qatar AIP.
- **FIS / FSS:** Qatar **AIS/FIS** (QCAA); not a factor for an IFR routing that stays in controlled airspace throughout.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 continuous **radar + ADS-B**; no procedural pockets on a normal IFR routing. 🟧 **GNSS integrity is degraded by reported GPS spoofing/jamming** in the region — a surveillance/navigation caveat (§10), consistent with the wider Gulf picture in the Middle East area brief.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Doha FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — MID region RVSM. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 **RNAV 5** enroute; **RNAV 1/RNP 1** terminal; **RNP APCH** arrivals. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md). 🟧 With GPS spoofing/jamming reported in the region, confirm GNSS/RNP integrity monitoring and any conventional-navaid fallback at planning.
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟩 **not applicable** — the MID region uses **25 kHz** VHF spacing.
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 **not confirmed** for this FIR (unlike the Jeddah FIR/Emirates upper airspace, per the Middle East area brief) — plan on VHF voice primary. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** transponder required; **ADS-B Out** underpins the surveillance service. Confirm equipage on the OFP.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **not implemented** in the European sense — a fixed ATS-route network with tactical vectoring around the dense Doha terminal complex.
- **Airway spine:** Doha sits on the **central-Gulf trunk** between Bahrain/Saudi Arabia and Emirates; 🟥 note that **routing is currently under a CZIB advisory** (§10) even though the FIR remains operationally open — the flyable route set should be confirmed by NOTAM, not assumed from the published network.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the FIR-boundary transition fixes toward Bahrain/Emirates/Jeddah and the Doha TMA arrival transitions; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Hamad Intl (OTHH)** — Qatar's primary international gateway and the FIR's only significant K Global-served aerodrome (Doha Intl/OTBD is closed to scheduled commercial traffic). Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 no significant terrain — Qatar is a low, flat peninsula; the surrounding Gulf sector is entirely low/water. Not a cruise or terminal terrain-clearance driver.
- **Boundary terrain:** none of note across any adjacent FIR boundary.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟩 not a terrain-limited problem, but 🟧 **diversion-field density is thin within this compact FIR** (effectively only OTHH) — plan the nearest suitable field across the Bahrain/Emirates/Jeddah boundary if needed.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟩 not relevant — hot climate; the operational temperature driver is **high-temperature/density-altitude** performance in summer (see OM E and the OTHH airport brief), not cold-temperature correction.

## 10. Special-use airspace

> 🟥 **CONFLICT-ZONE / LIVE-SECURITY AIRSPACE — this section is perishable. Re-check the current CZIB/NOTAM picture at planning (§11); the state of the Gulf situation changes rapidly.**

- **Conflict-zone exposure (the headline hazard):** 🟥 Qatar is assessed **Risk Level 3 (Caution)**; despite the wider Gulf tensions, Qatar airspace (the OTDF/Doha FIR) **remains open and is reported to be operating largely normally**. However, Qatar was **directly affected during the conflict** — a **missile strike was reported near Al Udeid Air Base close to Doha in March 2026** — and the current **EASA CZIB 2026-07R1 advises operators not to operate in the OTDF/Doha FIR at any altitude or flight level**, alongside Bahrain, Kuwait and UAE. France has issued a NOTAM (LFFF F1581/26, current as at build date) requesting French operators not to enter Qatar's airspace at all. The FIR **closed and reopened several times** during Feb–Jul 2026 before returning to normal overflight operation. *(This is durable awareness that OTDF is a live-risk FIR despite its "operating normally" status; the specific advisory reference and validity are live data — confirm the current CZIB/NOTAM state at planning before relying on any routing.)*
- **Military restricted / danger / prohibited:** 🟧 Qatar publishes restricted/danger/prohibited areas per the AIP, including around **Al Udeid Air Base**; activation and status are AIP/NOTAM-published — confirm active at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 standard Qatari civil operation for a normal transit, **but** current regional restrictions and any operator/state-specific routing directives apply — confirm at planning.

> **SUA / conflict currency:** re-check the **EASA CZIB, French/state NOTAMs, ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository and operator routing policy** at planning — the §10 picture is durable awareness, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO standard — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance/filed route and levels, attempt the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Qatari AIP refinement at ENR 1.8/GEN; note that in the current security environment, deviating from a cleared/managed corridor without coordination carries elevated risk — comply with ATC and published contingency routings.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — **request the deviation from ATC** and comply with the vector/level issued. No oceanic offset table applies.
- **GNSS degradation contingency:** 🟧 with **GPS spoofing/jamming reported**, be prepared for FMS position drift/spurious terrain or nav alerts — cross-check with DME/DME/IRU, advise ATC, and follow OM E guidance on GNSS-interference response.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Summer heat & density altitude (Jun–Sep)** — extreme temperatures; a hot-and-high performance driver at OTHH.
  - **Shamal dust and reduced visibility** — blowing dust events can trigger LVO; a terminal driver.
  - **Volcanic ash / space weather:** not a planning driver at these latitudes.
