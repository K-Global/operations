# Beirut (OLBB) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the Lebanese Republic and its adjoining Mediterranean coastal airspace, containing **Rafic Hariri Intl (OLBA)** · **Parent area brief:** [Middle East](../../middle-east.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Nicosia (LCCC, Cyprus) W — offshore Mediterranean · Damascus (OSTT, Syria) E/N (Lebanon–Syria land border) · Tel Aviv (LLLL, Israel) S (Lebanon–Israel "Blue Line")

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Beirut is a **radar-controlled coastal FIR** covering the whole of Lebanon and its narrow Mediterranean coastal strip, sandwiched between Israel and Syria. Routings and levels here are planning context — file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for Rafic Hariri Intl (OLBA) live in the airport briefing (§9). **🟥 STANDING CAUTION — REGIONAL SECURITY/AIRSPACE TENSION: as at build date, an active EASA Conflict Zone Information Bulletin advises operators NOT to operate in this FIR at any altitude, and multiple states (France, Italy, Germany, UK, Canada) have issued parallel avoid-notices. GNSS jamming/spoofing has been reported and Lebanon's own ANSP has issued a NOTAM recommending conventional (non-GNSS) navigation. This is factual operational caution, not political commentary — verify the current status before every single operation into this FIR; do not rely on this file's snapshot.** Open 🟧 items are in §12. ## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** the Beirut FIR covers the **entire Lebanese Republic** — a narrow Mediterranean coastal state roughly 217 km long and 10–80 km wide — plus its adjoining coastal/territorial airspace over the Eastern Mediterranean. Lebanon's compact geography means this is one of the smallest FIRs in the MID region, wedged directly between Israel and Syria. 🟧 Exact lateral-limit coordinates were not obtained from a reachable primary AIP this pass; the FIR is inferred to be bounded by the national land border (Syria) and the international sea boundary/adjacent-FIR delimitation (Cyprus, offshore) — verify against Lebanon DGCA AIP ENR 2.1.
- **Confirmed containment:** 🟩 **Rafic Hariri Intl (OLBA, Beirut)** — Lebanon's sole international gateway and only K Global-served field — sits within the Beirut FIR.
- **Vertical structure:** 🟧 not confirmed from reachable sources; treat as a single FIR/UIR (GND–UNL, or equivalent) pending AIP confirmation. Beirut ACC provides enroute radar control per the OLBA airport brief.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary) — inferred from national geography and regional advisory groupings, 🟧 not individually AIP-confirmed:**
  - **W — Nicosia (LCCC, Cyprus).** Offshore Mediterranean boundary; Cyprus lies roughly 225 km north-west across open water. A 2021 FAA advisory (via Safe Airspace) groups OLBB together with LLLL/OJAC/LCCC as an Eastern-Mediterranean regional adjacency/advisory set.
  - **E / N — Damascus (OSTT, Syria).** 🟥 The Lebanon–Syria land border forms the FIR's eastern and northern edge; Damascus FIR is itself under an active EASA CZIB (avoid all levels) per the Middle East area brief — meaning **both of Beirut's non-maritime neighbours to landward carry the same "avoid all levels" status**, a materially significant fact for any contingency/diversion planning in this corner of the network.
  - **S — Tel Aviv (LLLL, Israel).** The Lebanon–Israel boundary ("Blue Line") forms the FIR's southern edge; Tel Aviv FIR carries its own standing regional-security caution (see that FIR's brief) though at a lower EASA advisory tier than Beirut's current CZIB.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 not confirmed from reachable sources; given the FIR's small size and single international gateway, a simple enroute/terminal (Beirut ACC / Beirut Approach) split is assumed pending AIP confirmation.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟥 the **Mount Lebanon range** rises close to OLBA's eastern side — **Mount Sannine (~2,628 m / 8,622 ft)** sits roughly 19 NM ESE of the field, with the higher **Qurnat as Sawda massif (~3,088 m / 10,131 ft, Lebanon's highest point)** further north near Bcharre/the Cedars. The field's own published ILS RWY 16 approach chart states that **circling is not authorised east and south of the airport** — a direct, chart-confirmed terrain/obstacle restriction (per the OLBA airport brief §3.1).
- **Boundary terrain:** the Mount Lebanon / Anti-Lebanon range complex continues east toward the Lebanon–Syria border (Damascus FIR side); not independently profiled this pass, but consistent with a generally mountainous eastern/interior Lebanon.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟧 the Mount Lebanon terrain is a genuine consideration for any escape-route planning east of OLBA; combined with the §10 security picture (Damascus FIR to the east is also CZIB-affected), the practical escape/diversion options from this FIR are constrained on **both** the terrain axis (east) and the security axis (east and south into Syria/toward Israel) — plan conservatively.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟩 not relevant — OLBA is coastal/sea-level (85 ft); Mediterranean climate, no cold-soak/freeze driver.

## 10. Special-use airspace

> 🟥 **STANDING CAUTION — REGIONAL SECURITY/AIRSPACE TENSION. This FIR is currently the network's single highest advisory tier in the Middle East region. This section is perishable — re-check the current NOTAM/CZIB picture at planning (§11) before every single operation. This is factual operational caution; no party, cause or political position is asserted.**

- **Regional security exposure (the headline hazard):** 🟥 as at build date, **EASA CZIB 2026-06** (issued 08 Jul 2026, valid until 31 Aug 2026 unless reviewed earlier) advises operators **not to operate within the airspace of Lebanon, FIR Beirut (OLBB), at all altitudes and flight levels**. The bulletin cites the wider Middle East conflict environment beginning 28 Feb 2026, Israeli strikes within Lebanese territory, Hezbollah operations against Israel, and recurrent violations of the ceasefire agreed 16 Apr 2026 (extended 23 Apr and 19 Jun 2026) and the Trilateral Framework Agreement signed 26 Jun 2026 — assessed as producing **high risk to civil aircraft at all altitudes and flight levels** given limited Lebanese-state airspace-deconfliction capability. Safe Airspace (OPSGROUP) independently classifies Lebanon at **"Risk Level One — Do Not Fly"**, its highest tier — a materially higher classification than Jordan (Risk Level Three — Caution) or Israel (Information Note tier only). Parallel avoid-notices/recommendations are in force from **France** (Notam LFFF F1449/26), **Italy** (LIRR E1452/26), **Germany** (EDWW B0436/26), the **UK** (EGTT V0015/26) and **Canada** (AIC 17/26); some states permit limited OLBA operations only where arrivals/departures remain over the Mediterranean Sea. *(No party, cause or political position is asserted here — the operative instruction is to check the live status before every flight.)*
- **GNSS jamming & spoofing — 🟥 confirmed by Lebanon's own ANS.** Lebanon's own aeronautical authority has issued **NOTAM OLBB A0103/26** (15 May 2026 – 15 Aug 2026) warning of possible loss of GPS/GNSS signal due to jamming/spoofing in the Beirut FIR, and recommending **conventional ATS routes, STARs and approach procedures (ground-navaid-referenced)** as the primary mitigation, with RNAV(GNSS) STARs and RNP approaches flyable only at pilot discretion. This has been reported since Nov 2023 per Safe Airspace. Brief the DME/DME/IRU cross-check and conventional-navaid fallback (§5), and prefer the conventional procedure set at OLBA while this NOTAM stands.
- **Military / restricted / danger areas:** 🟧 not detailed in reachable sources for Lebanon specifically; assume standard MID-region AIP/NOTAM-published restricted/danger areas exist and confirm at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 a public trip-support aggregator reports no PPR/slot requirement for OLBA under normal conditions (per the OLBA airport brief); this is entirely secondary to the §10 security advisory, which is the controlling consideration while it stands.

> **SUA / security currency:** re-check the **EASA CZIB, Lebanese + surrounding-state NOTAMs, ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository, Safe Airspace and operator routing policy** at planning — the §10 picture is durable awareness that this is currently the network's highest-risk FIR, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO standard — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance/filed route and levels, attempt the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Lebanon-specific refinement not confirmed; in the current security environment, deviating from a cleared routing without coordination carries elevated misidentification risk.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical via ATC in this controlled FIR; request the deviation from ATC and comply with the vector/level issued. No oceanic offset table applies.
- **GNSS-degradation contingency:** 🟥 per Lebanon's own NOTAM (§10), **prefer conventional (ground-navaid) routes/approaches over RNAV(GNSS)/RNP while the jamming NOTAM stands**; cross-check DME/DME/IRU, advise ATC promptly, and follow OM E GNSS-interference guidance.
- **Regional-security contingency:** 🟥 plan every operation against the current CZIB/closure map; given that **both landward neighbouring FIRs (Damascus, and to a lesser tier Tel Aviv) carry their own security advisories**, a contingency divert from OLBB is a genuinely constrained problem — pre-brief a specific, currently-open alternate before departure, not a generic "nearest suitable" assumption.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Winter frontal systems** — mild, wet winters bring occasional reduced visibility/low cloud off the sea.
  - **Summer haze** and periodic regional dust transport, consistent with the wider Mediterranean/Levant pattern.
  - **Localised mechanical turbulence** possible on strong easterly-flow days given the close-in Mount Lebanon range (§8) — a geography-based inference, not an AIP-sourced statistic.
  - **Volcanic ash / space weather:** not planning drivers at these latitudes.
