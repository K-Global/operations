# Norway (ENOR) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the consolidated Norway FIR (mainland Norway and its territorial airspace), containing **Oslo-Gardermoen (ENGM)** · **Parent area brief:** [Europe (Continental)](../../europe.md) · **Adjacent FIRs:** Sweden (ESAA) E · Copenhagen (EKDK), Denmark S, across the Skagerrak · Finland (EFIN) short land border NE · Russia (Murmansk, ULMM) far NE 🟧 confirm · **Bodø Oceanic (ENOB)** N/W — a separate, adjacent ICAO FIR also served by Avinor, interfacing further with Iceland (Reykjavik, BIRD) and Scottish (EGPX) at the NAT/polar boundary
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Norway is a **fully radar/surveillance-controlled continental FIR** with a distinctive high-terrain and high-latitude character — no procedural segment within ENOR itself, though its **Bodø Oceanic (ENOB)** neighbour is a genuine oceanic/polar sector. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical radar-ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for Oslo-Gardermoen (ENGM) live in the airport briefing (§9). Live restrictions (NOTAMs, AUP/UUP conditional-route status, SIGMETs, ash bulletins) are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Norway FIR (ENOR) covers **mainland Norway** in its entirety — from the Oslo/Skagerrak region in the south-east, along the western fjord coast, across the central highlands, to Finnmark and the Barents Sea coast in the far north.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 single consolidated FIR; the operational lower/upper split (if AIP-published as a separate CTA/UTA structure) is not independently re-derived here — confirm at **AIP Norway ENR 2.1**.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **E — Sweden (ESAA, LFV).** Along the long Norway–Sweden land border.
  - **S — Copenhagen (EKDK), Denmark.** Across the Skagerrak — a maritime FIR boundary.
  - **NE — Finland (EFIN, Fintraffic ANS).** A short land border in northern Lapland; both Norway and Finland are **NEFAB** partners (§7).
  - **Far NE — Russia (Murmansk, ULMM).** 🟧 Exact FIR ident and boundary geometry not independently confirmed here — verify at AIP/current sector charts if ever relevant to routing near Kirkenes.
  - **N/W — Bodø Oceanic (ENOB), also Avinor.** A distinct ICAO FIR covering the Norwegian Sea/North Atlantic/Arctic, interfacing further out with **Iceland (Reykjavik, BIRD)** and the **Scottish FIR (EGPX)** at the North Atlantic/polar operational boundary. Not transited on a mainland Oslo routing but organisationally and operationally linked (same ANSP, Bodø ATCC).
- **Sectorisation:** Norway ACC is organised across the **Oslo (Røyken), Stavanger and Bodø** centres as a single unified command structure (since the 2013 reorganisation), each responsible for defined geographic sectors of the mainland FIR; Bodø additionally runs the oceanic sectors of ENOB.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Lower/upper-airspace ATSU:** 🟩 **Avinor Flysikring — Norway ACC**, radar/surveillance control across the mainland FIR from the **Oslo (Røyken)** and **Stavanger** centres (southern/central sectors) and the **Bodø** centre (northern sectors), including the ENGM approach/TMA feed.
- **Oceanic-adjacent ATSU:** 🟧 **Bodø ATCC** additionally provides service in the adjacent **Bodø Oceanic (ENOB)** FIR — a genuinely oceanic/polar sector distinct from ENOR, relevant only if a routing extends into the North Atlantic/Arctic beyond the mainland.
- **FIS / FSS:** Norwegian **Flight Information Service** provided by Avinor for lower/uncontrolled airspace; not a factor for an IFR hub transit that stays in controlled airspace throughout.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 continuous radar + **ADS-B** over the mainland FIR; Bodø Oceanic additionally uses **space-based ADS-B (Aireon)** across its full oceanic area of responsibility.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Norway FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — ICAO EUR RVSM (in force across the EUR region). See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 **RNAV 5 (B-RNAV)** enroute across ECAC upper airspace; **RNP 1** for terminal SID/STAR; **RNP APCH** for arrivals — per the EU **PBN Implementing Rule (EU) 2018/1048**. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟥 **mandatory** for IFR/GAT radios in the ICAO EUR region (**Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 1079/2012**; full implementation completed 31 Dec 2018).
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 **CPDLC ATN B1 over VDL Mode 2**, expected/mandated above **FL285** in the EU datalink service area (**Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/310**, amending 29/2009). VHF voice remains primary. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** transponder required; **ADS-B Out (1090 MHz ES) mandatory** for aircraft >5,700 kg MTOW or >250 kt — per **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/386 (SPI IR)**.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a mainland transit is handled sequentially by **Norway ACC** sectors (Oslo/Røyken, Stavanger and/or Bodø depending on routing) with handoff to the adjacent-FIR ACC (Sweden, Denmark or Finland) at the boundary; the exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC/AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** Norwegian **FIS** (Avinor) for information service in lower/uncontrolled airspace.
- **Language:** 🟩 **Norwegian and English**; English is always available on the controlled sectors.
- **8.33 kHz:** 🟥 all enroute frequencies are **8.33 kHz-spaced** — mandatory equipage (§5).
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **Norway operates FRA (AFRA — Avinor Free Route Airspace)** as part of the **North European Functional Airspace Block (NEFAB)**, together with **Estonia, Finland and Latvia** (NEFAB agreement in force 23 Dec 2012; ANSP cooperation agreement Jun 2012 — Avinor, EANS, Finavia/Fintraffic ANS, LGS). 🟧 Norway's mainland FRA floor is commonly cited as **FL135**, with **FL195** in the adjacent Bodø Oceanic FIR (ENOB) up to FL660 — confirm the current published floor at **AIP Norway ENR 1.3**. NEFAB FRA connected cross-border to the **Denmark–Estonia–Finland–Latvia–Sweden** free-route area on **25 May 2017**. This sits within the wider **Borealis Alliance / North European Free Route Airspace (NEFRA)** programme now linking nine North European ANSPs (Avinor, Naviair, Fintraffic ANS, LFV, EANS, LGS, Isavia, IAA, NATS) into a single free-route area, with cross-border implementation achieved in 2023 and full harmonised implementation targeted by end of 2026.
- **Airway spine (where FRA not available / RAD-constrained):** below the FRA floor and where the RAD restricts direct routeing, the **fixed ATS-route network** applies — including the SID/STAR feeder structure into ENGM.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** FRA horizontal entry/exit points at the FIR boundary and the terminal transition fixes feeding Oslo-Gardermoen; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Oslo-Gardermoen (ENGM)**. Terminal detail is in the airport briefing (§9). 🟧 Norway's wider aerodrome network (Bergen, Stavanger, Trondheim, Tromsø and numerous short-field regional/coastal fields) has no OM C brief yet — plan-time only.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟥 the **Scandinavian mountains (Scandes)** run through central, western and northern Norway, culminating at **Galdhøpiggen (~2,469 m / 8,100 ft)** — the highest point in Northern Europe. This is a genuine driftdown/MORA consideration for any routing beyond the immediate Oslo lowlands.
- **Local terrain at Oslo-Gardermoen:** 🟩 ENGM itself sits in a relatively low-lying valley/lowland area in the south-east, away from the highest relief; terminal-area terrain is addressed in the airport briefing (§9).
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟧 relevant for any transit crossing the central/western highlands or the northern sectors — the wide dispersion of Norwegian aerodromes along the coast provides some mitigation, but this is a **materially different terrain profile from the flat Danish/Baltic FIRs** and should be planned per-route rather than assumed benign.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** applied at the terminal aerodromes in winter, notably at ENGM — see the airport briefing (§9) and OM E Cold Weather Operations.

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **Military TRA / TSA:** 🟧 Norway carries military TRA/D areas activated via **AUP/UUP**, including NATO-relevant training/exercise areas concentrated in the north (e.g. around Bodø and Finnmark, reflecting Norway's role in Nordic/NATO air defence). Confirm active status at planning.
- **Danger / restricted / prohibited areas:** 🟧 named danger/restricted areas are AIP/NOTAM-published — pull live at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟩 none for standard EU/EEA/Schengen-area operation; Norway is not an EU member but participates in EASA/SES arrangements relevant to this brief.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** per **SERA (Standardised European Rules of the Air, Regulation (EU) No 923/2012)**, which Norway applies as an EASA/SES-participating state — squawk **7600**, continue per the SERA IFR comms-failure rules (last acknowledged clearance / filed route and levels), and attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Norwegian refinement at **AIP ENR 1.8/GEN**.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — **request the deviation from ATC** and comply with the vector/level issued. No oceanic weather-deviation offset table applies within ENOR itself. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Winter operations** — extensive snow/ice, short daylight and cold-temperature altimetry corrections, particularly pronounced given Norway's latitude and terrain; a significant driver at ENGM and throughout the network (see the airport briefing and OM E Cold Weather Operations).
  - **Mountain-wave / terrain-induced turbulence** — possible in the lee of the Scandinavian mountains under strong wind conditions; tactical, ATC/crew-managed.
  - **High-latitude / polar interface** — the adjacent **Bodø Oceanic (ENOB)** sector extends into genuinely high-latitude airspace where **space weather (solar events, aurora)** can affect HF communications and, at extreme geomagnetic latitudes, GNSS performance; not a material factor for an ENGM-focused mainland transit but noted given the FIR's adjacency to the Arctic/polar operating environment.
  - **Volcanic ash (strategic wildcard)** — **Icelandic eruptions** can propagate ash across North European/North Atlantic upper airspace and have historically closed large volumes of it (2010). **VAAC London** is the responsible advisory centre for this sector.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, **AUP/UUP** (active TRA/CDR status) and current **RAD**, SIGMET/AIRMET, **VAAC London** volcanic-ash advisories, space-weather bulletins if routing touches the Bodø Oceanic sector, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
