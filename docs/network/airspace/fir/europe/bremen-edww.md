# Bremen (EDWW) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — northern Germany (North Sea & Baltic coast, Lower Saxony, Bremen, Hamburg, Schleswig-Holstein, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Berlin-Brandenburg, northern Saxony-Anhalt) · **Parent area brief:** [Europe (Continental)](../../europe.md) · **Adjacent FIRs:** Langen (EDGG) S · Amsterdam (EHAA) SW/W · Copenhagen (EKDK) N · Warszawa (EPWW) E
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Bremen is a **fully radar/surveillance-controlled continental FIR** — no procedural or oceanic segment. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical radar-ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for aerodromes inside the FIR (EDDB, EDDH, EDDV …) live in the airport briefings (§9). Live restrictions (NOTAMs, AUP/UUP conditional-route status, SIGMETs, ash bulletins) are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Bremen FIR covers **northern Germany** — the North Sea and Baltic coastlines, Lower Saxony, the city-states of Bremen and Hamburg, Schleswig-Holstein, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Berlin-Brandenburg and the northern part of Saxony-Anhalt. It is the northernmost of the three German FIRs, bordering **Langen (EDGG)** to the south.
- **Vertical split:** 🟩 **Lower airspace GND–FL245** is worked by **Bremen ACC (EDWW)**. 🟧 **Upper airspace above FL245** is worked in part by **Maastricht UAC (EDYY)** — EUROCONTROL confirms Maastricht UAC's area of responsibility as the upper airspace (FL245–FL660) over **Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands and north-west Germany** — which covers the western/north-western portion of the Bremen FIR. The corresponding eastern portion (Berlin/Mecklenburg/toward the Polish boundary) is **not confirmed here** as either remaining with a DFS unit or also falling under MUAC — flagged for verification against the current AIP and Letters of Agreement.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **S — Langen (EDGG, DFS).** Central/south-western German FIR.
  - **N — Copenhagen (EKDK), Denmark.** Across the Jutland/German border and the Baltic.
  - **E — Warszawa (EPWW), Poland.**
  - **SW/W — Amsterdam (EHAA, LVNL), Netherlands.**
- **Upper-airspace neighbour:** 🟧 the Bremen-FIR upper airspace interfaces directly with **Maastricht UAC (EDYY)** to the west (delegated portion) and with **Karlsruhe UAC (EDUU)** further south near the Langen-FIR boundary. The exact MUAC/Bremen-ACC lateral split is documented in a Letter of Agreement between the two units; the precise boundary geometry is not re-derived here.
- **Sectorisation:** Bremen ACC is divided into multiple ACC sectors (grouped broadly by geography — e.g. a western/coastal group and an eastern group toward Berlin) that combine/split with traffic and staffing; the transit-relevant fact is the **GND–FL245 (Bremen ACC) / FL245+ (delegated to Maastricht UAC over the western part)** band split.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Lower-airspace ATSU:** 🟩 **Bremen ACC (EDWW, DFS)** — radar/surveillance control, GND–FL245, including the approach/TMA feeds for EDDB, EDDH, EDDV and the other in-FIR terminals.
- **Upper-airspace ATSU:** 🟧 **Maastricht UAC (EDYY, EUROCONTROL)** for the delegated western/north-western upper airspace; the residual eastern-upper arrangement is unconfirmed (§3).
- **FIS / FSS:** German **Flight Information Service (FIS)** provided by DFS for lower/uncontrolled airspace; not a factor for an IFR hub transit that stays in controlled airspace throughout.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 continuous radar + **ADS-B**; no procedural pockets on a normal IFR routing.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Bremen FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — ICAO EUR RVSM (in force across the EUR region). See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 **RNAV 5 (B-RNAV)** enroute across ECAC upper airspace; **RNP 1** for terminal SID/STAR at the busy terminals; **RNP APCH** for arrivals — per the EU **PBN Implementing Rule (EU) 2018/1048**. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟥 **mandatory** for IFR/GAT radios in the ICAO EUR region (**Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 1079/2012**; full implementation completed 31 Dec 2018).
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 **CPDLC ATN B1 over VDL Mode 2**, expected/mandated above **FL285** in the EU datalink service area (**Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/310**, amending 29/2009). Maastricht UAC is a lead European datalink provider; VHF voice remains primary. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** transponder required; **ADS-B Out (1090 MHz ES) mandatory** for aircraft >5,700 kg MTOW or >250 kt — per **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/386 (SPI IR)**.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a hub transit is handed sequentially **Bremen ACC (lower) → Maastricht UAC (upper, over the delegated western portion)** or onward to the adjacent DFS upper unit further south/east; the exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC/AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** German **FIS** (DFS) for information service in lower/uncontrolled airspace.
- **Language:** 🟩 **German and English**; English is always available on the controlled sectors.
- **8.33 kHz:** 🟥 all enroute frequencies are **8.33 kHz-spaced** — mandatory equipage (§5).
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **DFS Free Route Airspace applies above FL245** across the DFS area of responsibility, which explicitly includes **FIR Bremen** alongside FIR München and UIR Rhein/EDUU — H24 availability was reached across the DFS AoR from **10 Oct 2019**. 🟧 Over the portion of upper airspace delegated to Maastricht UAC, **FRA@MUAC** applies instead (H24, **FL245–FL660**) — the two systems are harmonised but operated by different providers either side of the delegation boundary.
- **Airway spine (where FRA not available / RAD-constrained):** below the FRA floor and where the RAD restricts direct routeing, the **fixed ATS-route network** applies — including the SID/STAR feeder structure into the in-FIR terminals.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** FRA horizontal entry/exit points at the FIR boundary and the terminal transition fixes feeding EDDB/EDDH/EDDV; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB)**, **Hamburg (EDDH)**, **Hannover (EDDV)**. Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9). 🟧 **Bremen (EDDW)** itself sits inside the FIR that bears its name but has no OM C airport brief yet — build if it becomes a planned alternate.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 flat throughout — the **North German Plain**, the lowest-relief of the three German FIRs. No high terrain belt of note.
- **Boundary terrain:** 🟩 none of significance; the nearest meaningful relief (central German uplands, then the Alps) lies well south, across the **Langen (EDGG)** and **München (EDMM)** boundaries.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** not a constraint anywhere in this FIR — flat terrain and a dense diversion-aerodrome network remove any terrain-limited escape problem.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** applied at the terminal aerodromes in winter, not in enroute cruise — see the airport briefings (§9).

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **Military TRA / TSA:** 🟧 **extensive military TRA/TSA** activated **daily via the AUP/UUP** (Airspace Use Plan / Updated Use Plan); associated **Conditional Routes (CDR1/2/3)** open and close accordingly, and the **RAD (Route Availability Document)** governs which levels/segments are flyable. The SimBrief route is validated against the current RAD — confirm active TRAs/CDRs at planning.
- **Danger / restricted / prohibited areas:** 🟧 numerous **ED-R (restricted)** and **ED-D (danger)** areas, plus **offshore wind-farm** restricted/danger zones along the North Sea and Baltic coasts (ranges, military training, offshore infrastructure). Named areas and status are AIP/NOTAM-published — pull live at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟩 none — standard EU/ECAC operation, no permits.

> **SUA currency:** re-check active TRA/TSA/CDR status (AUP/UUP) and the current RAD at planning — this list is durable context, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** per **SERA (Standardised European Rules of the Air, Regulation (EU) No 923/2012)** — squawk **7600**, continue per the SERA IFR comms-failure rules (last acknowledged clearance / filed route and levels), and attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any German refinement at **AIP ENR 1.8 / GEN**.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — **request the deviation from ATC** and comply with the vector/level issued. No oceanic weather-deviation offset table applies. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **North Sea / Baltic coastal weather** — fog, low stratus and strong surface winds affecting the coastal terminals (EDDH, EDDB) more than enroute cruise.
  - **Summer convection** — embedded CB / squall lines across the North German Plain; handled tactically.
  - **Winter terminal icing / low visibility / snow** at the in-FIR terminals — a terminal, not enroute, driver; see the airport briefings and OM E LVO/Cold-Weather docs.
  - **Volcanic ash (strategic wildcard)** — **Icelandic eruptions** can propagate ash across European upper airspace and have historically closed large volumes of it (2010). **VAAC London** is the responsible advisory centre.
  - **Space weather:** negligible at these mid-latitudes — no HF/GNSS-degradation planning driver here.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, **AUP/UUP** (active TRA/CDR status) and current **RAD**, SIGMET/AIRMET (convection, marine weather), **VAAC London** volcanic-ash advisories, conflict-zone bulletins, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
