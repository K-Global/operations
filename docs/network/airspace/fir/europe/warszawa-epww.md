# Warszawa (EPWW) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — Poland, including the **Warsaw (EPWA) hub**, and the Polish sector of the southern Baltic Sea · **Parent area brief:** [Europe (Continental)](../../europe.md) — 🟧 that brief covers the DE/FR/ES/PT continental corridor only and does **not** cover Poland/Central-Eastern Europe; this FIR brief closes that scope gap · **Adjacent FIRs:** Bremen (EDWW) W · Praha (LKAA) SW · Bratislava (LZBB) S 🟧 · Lviv (UKLV) SE — see conflict-zone note §10/§11 · Minsk (UMMV) E 🟧 · Vilnius (EYVL) NE 🟧 · Kaliningrad (UUWV) N — Russian exclave 🟧 · Malmö (ESMM) N — over the Baltic Sea 🟧
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Warszawa is a **fully radar/surveillance-controlled continental FIR** — no procedural or oceanic segment. It is a low-terrain, benign FIR structurally, but sits on the **eastern edge of ECAC/NATO airspace**: sustained GNSS interference in the northern part of the FIR, and a temporary restricted zone along the eastern border, are current factual features of this FIR that a purely structural European brief would miss. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical radar-ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for the aerodrome inside the FIR (EPWA) live in the airport briefing (§9). Live restrictions are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Warszawa FIR essentially coincides with Poland's geographical borders, apart from its northern part, which extends over a sector of the **southern Baltic Sea**.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 treated as a single designator (EPWW) for FIR/UIR purposes; not independently confirmed in fine detail against the full AIP structure this pass.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):** 🟧 the fullest available public corroboration (a Polish VACC/IVAO community source, tier-4) states the FIR borders **Vilnius, Minsk, Lviv, Bratislava, Praha and Berlin FIRs to the east, south and west**, plus common Baltic Sea boundaries with **Malmö and Kaliningrad FIRs to the north**. Cross-referencing against current German FIR nomenclature (see the Langen (EDGG) FIR brief) suggests the western neighbour named "Berlin FIR" in that legacy source is now **Bremen FIR (EDWW)** — German airspace was consolidated post-1990 and the historic East German "Berlin FIR" no longer exists as a separate designator. Re-stated with current idents:
  - **W — Bremen (EDWW, DFS), Germany.** 🟧 legacy source names "Berlin FIR"; current designator asserted as Bremen, not independently re-verified against the current AIP.
  - **SW — Praha (LKAA, ANS CR), Czech Republic.**
  - **S — Bratislava (LZBB), Slovakia.** 🟧 not independently re-verified this pass.
  - **SE — Lviv (UKLV), Ukraine.** Ukrainian airspace remains closed to civil overflight (§10/§11) — a factual adjacency, not a routing consideration on this network.
  - **E — Minsk (UMMV), Belarus.** 🟧 not independently re-verified this pass; Belarusian airspace carries its own overflight-avoidance considerations for many operators — not asserted as policy here, flagged for awareness.
  - **NE — Vilnius (EYVL), Lithuania.** 🟧 not independently re-verified this pass.
  - **N — Kaliningrad (UUWV), Russia (exclave).** 🟧 the Kaliningrad Oblast is a Russian exclave on the Baltic coast bordering north-eastern Poland; relevant primarily as the reported source region for the GNSS jamming/spoofing noted in §1/§10.
  - **N — Malmö (ESMM), Sweden.** 🟧 over the Baltic Sea; not independently re-verified this pass.
- **Sectorisation:** Warszawa ACC is divided into multiple named/lettered sectors (public references include **D, T, C, G, R, B** and an OAT/G sector) that combine/split with traffic and staffing — not itemised in detail here; the transit-relevant fact is that the whole FIR is a single radar-controlled block under one ACC.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Controlling ATSU:** 🟩 **Warszawa ACC (EPWW, PANSA)** — radar/surveillance control across the FIR, including the Warsaw (EPWA) TMA feed.
- **FIS / FSS:** Polish FIS for lower/uncontrolled airspace; not a factor for an IFR hub transit remaining in controlled airspace.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 continuous radar + **ADS-B** for ATC purposes; 🟧 **GNSS-dependent onboard navigation/surveillance may itself be degraded** in the north of the FIR by the jamming/spoofing activity noted in §1 — this is a navigation-performance consideration, not an ATC surveillance gap.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in this FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — ICAO EUR RVSM. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 **RNAV 5 (B-RNAV)** enroute · **RNP 1** terminal SID/STAR · **RNP APCH** arrivals — EU **PBN Implementing Rule (EU) 2018/1048**. 🟧 GNSS-dependent PBN procedures in the north of the FIR should account for the reported jamming/spoofing environment — cross-check against current guidance at planning. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟥 **mandatory** — **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 1079/2012**.
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 **CPDLC ATN B1 over VDL Mode 2**, expected/mandated above **FL285** in the EU datalink service area (**Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/310**) — PANSA participates. VHF voice remains primary. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** transponder required; **ADS-B Out (1090 MHz ES) mandatory** for aircraft >5,700 kg MTOW or >250 kt — **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/386 (SPI IR)**.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a hub transit is worked by **Warszawa ACC** and handed to the adjacent-FIR ACC at the boundary (Bremen, Praha, Bratislava, or — outside this network's routing — Lviv/Minsk/Vilnius/Kaliningrad/Malmö); exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC/AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** Polish FIS for information service in lower/uncontrolled airspace.
- **Language:** 🟩 **Polish and English**; English always available on the controlled sectors.
- **8.33 kHz:** 🟥 all enroute frequencies are **8.33 kHz-spaced** — mandatory equipage (§5).
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **POLFRA (Polish Free Route Airspace)** — PANSA manages Free Route Airspace within the Warszawa FIR, allowing direct point-to-point routeing between defined entry/exit points in place of the fixed ATS route network, subject to airspace availability. 🟧 Exact vertical/lateral limits and cross-border FRA connectivity (e.g. with Baltic FRA to the north-east, or German/Czech free-route cells to the west/south) are not itemised in this pass — confirm at planning.
- **Airway spine (where FRA not available / RAD-constrained):** below the FRA floor and in terminal airspace, the fixed ATS-route network applies, including the SID/STAR feeder structure into Warsaw (EPWA).
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** FRA horizontal entry/exit points at the FIR boundary and the terminal transition fixes feeding Warsaw; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Warsaw (Chopin, EPWA)**. Secondary Polish international fields — **Kraków-Balice (EPKK)**, **Gdańsk (EPGD)** — sit inside the FIR but have no OM C brief yet (§9). Terminal detail for EPWA is in the airport briefing (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 Poland lies mostly on the flat **North European Plain**. The only significant terrain is in the far south, at the **Sudetes** (SW, near the Czech border) and the **Carpathian foothills / Tatra Mountains** (S, on the Slovak border — the Tatras rise above 2,500 m, but sit right at the border, at the extreme southern edge of the FIR). Central and northern Poland, including the Warsaw corridor, are low and benign.
- **Boundary terrain:** the Tatra/Carpathian high terrain is shared with Slovakia and is not a factor for a routing that stays in the central/northern part of the FIR.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** not a cruise-level constraint for a Warsaw-area transit; would only become relevant for a track hugging the far southern border.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** applied at the terminal aerodrome in winter — see the airport briefing (§9).

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **Military TRA / TSA:** 🟧 Polish airspace carries military training areas activated via AUP/UUP; associated Conditional Routes and the RAD govern flyable levels/segments. Confirm active TRA/TSA at planning.
- **Eastern-border restricted zone (factual, neutral):** 🟧 PANSA has maintained a **restricted airspace zone along Poland's eastern border (designated EP R131, in force from 10 June 2026)**, continuing flight curbs first introduced after airspace-security incidents in September 2025 (reported drone incursions), at the request of Poland's Armed Forces Operational Command on national-security grounds. This is stated here as a durable structural fact of the current FIR environment; exact lateral/vertical extent and current activation status must be pulled live at planning — not asserted as fixed geometry in this brief.
- **Conflict-zone / Ukraine (UKxx) FIR-closure adjacency:** 🟧 the Warszawa FIR's SE border adjoins **Ukrainian FIRs (Lviv UKLV and others)**, which have been closed to civil overflight since the conflict onset in February 2022. This is a factual, neutral geographic adjacency, presented without commentary — it is not transited by this network, but the boundary is a live feature of this FIR's eastern edge.
- **Danger / restricted / prohibited areas:** 🟧 additional named areas are AIP/NOTAM-published — pull live at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟩 none for standard EU/ECAC operation within central/western Poland; no permits required for this network's routing.

> **SUA currency:** re-check active TRA/TSA status (AUP/UUP), the current RAD, the EP R131 eastern-border zone status, and conflict-zone bulletins at planning — this list is durable context, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** per **SERA (Standardised European Rules of the Air, Regulation (EU) No 923/2012)** — squawk **7600**, continue per the SERA IFR comms-failure rules (last acknowledged clearance / filed route and levels), and attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Polish refinement at **AIP Poland ENR 1.8/GEN**.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — **request the deviation from ATC** and comply with the vector/level issued. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **GNSS interference (live-planning item):** 🟧 sustained GPS/GNSS jamming and spoofing has been reported across **northern Poland** (and Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Finland, Sweden) through 2025–2026, attributed to transmitters in the Kaliningrad area. Pull current EASA/NOTAM guidance on GNSS reliability before planning any RNAV/GNSS-dependent procedure in the northern part of the FIR; maintain conventional-navaid backup awareness per OM E.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Summer convection** — embedded CB/hail over the North European Plain; thunderstorm avoidance handled tactically.
  - **Winter terminal icing / low visibility / snow** at Warsaw — a terminal, not enroute, driver; see the airport briefing and OM E LVO/Cold-Weather docs.
  - **Volcanic ash (strategic wildcard)** — Icelandic eruptions can propagate ash across European upper airspace; **VAAC London** is the responsible advisory centre for this sector.
  - **Conflict-zone / GNSS-interference bulletins** — given the FIR's exposure on both fronts, re-check ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository, EASA GNSS-interference bulletins, and NOTAMs at every planning cycle.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, AUP/UUP (active TRA/TSA status, EP R131 eastern-border zone) and current RAD, SIGMET/AIRMET, VAAC London volcanic-ash advisories, EASA GNSS-interference bulletins, conflict-zone bulletins, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
