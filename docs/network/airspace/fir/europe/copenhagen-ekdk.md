## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Copenhagen FIR covers **all of Denmark** — the Jutland peninsula, Zealand (incl. the Copenhagen metropolitan area), Funen and the other Danish islands, and Bornholm in the Baltic. It is a single national FIR (no internal FIR subdivision as in Germany or France).
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 Naviair provides **single-tier ACC control** for the Copenhagen FIR from surface/sea level up to the upper control limit (typically **FL660**), without a separate lower-ACC/upper-UAC organisational split of the kind seen in the German FIRs. This is stated as the general Nordic pattern; the precise AIP-published vertical breakdown (CTA/UTA nomenclature) is not independently re-derived here — confirm at **AIP ENR 2.1**.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **S — Bremen (EDWW, DFS), Germany.** Across the Danish–German land border and the western Baltic.
  - **E — Sweden (ESAA, LFV).** Across the Øresund strait — **the Copenhagen (EKCH) and Malmö (ESMS) TMAs interface directly**; historically operated as an integrated cross-border unit under NUAC HB (2012–2019) and still coordinated closely under the DK-SE FAB (§4).
  - **N — Norway (ENOR, Avinor).** Across the Skagerrak/Kattegat — a maritime FIR boundary rather than a land one.
- **Sectorisation:** København ACC is divided into sectors covering the Danish mainland/islands and the Øresund cross-border interface with Malmö; the transit-relevant fact is continuous single-tier radar control from a Danish departure/arrival through to the adjacent-FIR handoff.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Lower/upper-airspace ATSU:** 🟩 **København ACC (EKDK, Naviair)** — radar/surveillance control across the Copenhagen FIR, including the EKCH and EKBI approach/TMA feeds.
- **Cross-border integration:** 🟧 Copenhagen ACC operates within the **DK-SE Functional Airspace Block** together with **Malmö and Stockholm ACCs (LFV, Sweden)**. The three centres were run by a single joint company, **NUAC HB** (Naviair + LFV), from 1 Jul 2012 until its **closure on 31 Aug 2019**; each ANSP now runs its own centre again, continuing close operational cooperation (shared COOPANS ATM platform, Joint Programme Office) rather than a single merged ATS provider. Confirm the current-year cooperation arrangement if it materially affects cross-border coordination assumptions.
- **FIS / FSS:** Danish **Flight Information Service (FIS)** provided by Naviair for lower/uncontrolled airspace; not a factor for an IFR hub transit that stays in controlled airspace throughout.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 continuous radar + **ADS-B**; no procedural pockets on a normal IFR routing.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Copenhagen FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — ICAO EUR RVSM (in force across the EUR region). See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 **RNAV 5 (B-RNAV)** enroute across ECAC upper airspace; **RNP 1** for terminal SID/STAR; **RNP APCH** for arrivals — per the EU **PBN Implementing Rule (EU) 2018/1048**. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟥 **mandatory** for IFR/GAT radios in the ICAO EUR region (**Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 1079/2012**; full implementation completed 31 Dec 2018).
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 **CPDLC ATN B1 over VDL Mode 2**, expected/mandated above **FL285** in the EU datalink service area (**Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/310**, amending 29/2009). VHF voice remains primary. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** transponder required; **ADS-B Out (1090 MHz ES) mandatory** for aircraft >5,700 kg MTOW or >250 kt — per **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/386 (SPI IR)**.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a hub transit is handled by **København ACC** throughout the Danish portion, handing off to **Malmö ACC** (E, over the Øresund), **Bremen ACC** (S) or the appropriate Norwegian sector (N) at the FIR boundary; the exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC/AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** Danish **FIS** (Naviair) for information service in lower/uncontrolled airspace.
- **Language:** 🟩 **Danish and English**; English is always available on the controlled sectors.
- **8.33 kHz:** 🟥 all enroute frequencies are **8.33 kHz-spaced** — mandatory equipage (§5).
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **DK-SE FAB Free Route Airspace** applies in the Danish and Swedish upper airspace, first implemented in **2011**. 🟧 The FRA floor is commonly cited as **FL285** (i.e. immediately below the FL290 RVSM floor) — confirm the current published floor at **AIP Denmark ENR 1.3**, as the DK-SE FAB and NEFAB partners have discussed lowering it further. DK-SE FRA was cross-border linked to the **NEFAB FRA** (Norway/Estonia/Finland/Latvia) on **25 May 2017**, and to the **MUAC/DFS cross-border FRA area** (Belgium, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Germany) from **25 Apr 2019** — part of the wider **Borealis Alliance / North European Free Route Airspace (NEFRA)** programme now linking nine North European ANSPs (Avinor, Naviair, Fintraffic ANS, LFV, EANS, LGS, Isavia, IAA, NATS) into a single free-route area, with cross-border implementation achieved in 2023 and full harmonised implementation targeted by end of 2026.
- **Airway spine (where FRA not available / RAD-constrained):** below the FRA floor and where the RAD restricts direct routeing, the **fixed ATS-route network** applies — including the SID/STAR feeder structure into EKCH and EKBI.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** FRA horizontal entry/exit points at the FIR boundary and the terminal transition fixes feeding Copenhagen/Billund; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Copenhagen/Kastrup (EKCH)** and **Billund (EKBI)**. Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 flat throughout — Denmark's highest natural point is **Møllehøj (~171 m / 561 ft, Jutland)**. No terrain factor at any operational level.
- **Boundary terrain:** 🟩 none of significance; Sweden's Scandes/Kebnekaise high terrain lies far to the north in the **Sweden FIR (ESAA)**, not adjacent to a Danish transit.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** not a constraint anywhere in this FIR — flat terrain and a dense diversion-aerodrome network (EKCH, EKBI, plus ESMS Malmö just across the Øresund) remove any terrain-limited escape problem.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** applied at the terminal aerodromes in winter, not in enroute cruise — see the airport briefings (§9).

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** per **SERA (Standardised European Rules of the Air, Regulation (EU) No 923/2012)** — squawk **7600**, continue per the SERA IFR comms-failure rules (last acknowledged clearance / filed route and levels), and attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Danish refinement at **AIP ENR 1.8/GEN**.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — **request the deviation from ATC** and comply with the vector/level issued. No oceanic weather-deviation offset table applies. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **North Sea / Baltic coastal weather** — fog, low stratus and strong surface winds, particularly affecting EKCH; a terminal, more than enroute, driver.
  - **Winter terminal icing / low visibility / snow** at EKCH and EKBI — see the airport briefings and OM E LVO/Cold-Weather docs.
  - **Volcanic ash (strategic wildcard)** — **Icelandic eruptions** can propagate ash across North European upper airspace and have historically closed large volumes of it (2010). **VAAC London** is the responsible advisory centre.
  - **Space weather:** negligible at this latitude — no HF/GNSS-degradation planning driver here (unlike the Norway/Arctic sector further north).

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, **AUP/UUP** (active TRA/CDR status) and current **RAD**, SIGMET/AIRMET, **VAAC London** volcanic-ash advisories, conflict-zone bulletins, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
