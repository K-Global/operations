# Guayaquil (SEFG) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the whole of Ecuadorian airspace (there is one national FIR/UTA), containing **Mariscal Sucre Intl (SEQM), Quito** — a **high-elevation Andean hub** · **Parent area brief:** [South America](../../south-america.md) · **Adjacent FIRs:** Bogotá (SKED, Colombia) N · Lima (Peru) S · CENAMER (Central America, oceanic/NW) · others — 🟧 idents not fully verified this pass
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — built from public DGAC Ecuador (AIS) sources; several fields flagged 🟧

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Guayaquil FIR (with its upper counterpart, the **Guayaquil UTA**) is Ecuador's single national FIR/UTA, administered from **ACC Guayaquil**. Its headline feature is the **high-elevation, mountain-ringed destination (SEQM, ~7,910 ft)** deep in the Andes — routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for SEQM live in the airport briefing (§9). Live restrictions are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Guayaquil FIR/UTA (SEFG) covers **the whole of Ecuador**, both the coastal lowlands (where the ANSP's ACC is physically based, at Guayaquil) and the Andean interior (where the capital, Quito, and SEQM sit). Ecuador operates **a single national FIR** — there is no separate Quito-area FIR; the ACC's location at Guayaquil is an administrative fact, not a lateral-coverage boundary.
- **Confirmed containment:** 🟩 **Mariscal Sucre Intl (SEQM)**, Ecuador's principal gateway serving the capital, Quito, sits inside Guayaquil FIR/UTA — the airport itself is at Tababela, roughly 18 miles east of central Quito, replacing the former in-city airport (closed 2013) that had even tighter terrain constraints.
- **Vertical structure:** 🟧 Guayaquil FIR (lower) and the **Guayaquil UTA** (upper) together form Ecuador's controlled-airspace structure; exact lower/upper split level not verified this pass — confirm AIP Ecuador ENR 2.1.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Bogotá (SKED, Colombia).** Confirmed via published cross-border coordination between ACC Guayaquil and neighbouring ACCs.
  - **S — Lima (Peru).** Confirmed via the same coordination material — traffic crossing the common Guayaquil/Lima boundary is governed by Letters of Agreement between the two ACCs.
  - **NW (oceanic/Central America) — CENAMER (Central American FIR system).** Referenced in the same coordination material as a fourth party to the shared-boundary procedures (alongside Lima and Bogotá) — geometry not detailed this pass.
  - **E (Amazon/Brazil interface):** 🟧 not verified — Ecuador's eastern Amazonian territory likely interfaces with Brazil's FIR system at extreme range; not confirmed this pass.
- **Sectorisation:** not itemised this pass; the operationally relevant fact is that **ACC Guayaquil** is the single controlling authority for the national FIR/UTA, coordinating with Bogotá, Lima and CENAMER ACCs at the respective boundaries.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in Guayaquil FIR/UTA and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — confirmed applicable in Guayaquil airspace (published rule restricting ACC-to-ACC transfer of non-RVSM-certified aircraft in this band, exceptions only for state/maintenance/humanitarian flights), consistent with the ICAO SAM-region standard band (Doc 7030). See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟧 an **RNAV route structure** exists in the Guayaquil Upper FIR (confirmed by the published SLOP application on RNAV/upper conventional routes); exact enroute spec not verified. **Terminal RNP AR at SEQM is expected** given the surrounding Andean terrain and the vectored, terrain-constrained arrival environment reported for Quito, but not confirmed this pass — check current AIRAC. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟧 not verified — confirm whether Ecuador has adopted 8.33 kHz or retains 25 kHz VHF spacing.
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 not verified this pass. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟧 Mode S expected; ADS-B mandate/date not verified — confirm equipage requirement on the OFP.
- **High-elevation performance regime:** 🟥 **SEQM ~7,910 ft field elevation** places it in K Global's high-elevation working trigger (≳8,000 ft is the nominal threshold; SEQM sits just under it but is treated as a high-elevation field given its terrain-constrained environment) — density-altitude-driven TODR/climb-gradient/LDR effects and payload/fuel penalties apply. See [`OM E — High-Elevation Aerodrome Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/high-elevation-aerodrome-operations.md).

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟧 not applicable in the European sense; Ecuador is presumed to operate a fixed ATS-route/RNAV network — the confirmed **SLOP application on RNAV and upper conventional routes** implies a structured route network rather than point-to-point FRA.
- **Airway spine:** upper RNAV/conventional routes feed Quito from the Bogotá (N) and Lima (S) boundary crossings, per the published inter-ACC coordination; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string for a given routing.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** FIR-boundary transition fixes from Bogotá FIR (N) and Lima FIR (S); the Quito-area arrival transition onto the SEQM approach — not itemised this pass.
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Mariscal Sucre Intl (SEQM)** — Quito, the capital, and Ecuador's high-elevation Andean gateway. (José Joaquín de Olmedo Intl, SEGU/GYE, the coastal ACC-host airport at Guayaquil, is also in-FIR but does not carry an OM C brief this pass.) Terminal detail for SEQM is in the airport briefing (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟥 **Quito sits in the Andes at roughly 7,910 ft field elevation (SEQM)**, in a valley/plateau setting flanked by high peaks on both sides — the Ecuadorian Andes run as two parallel cordilleras, with active volcanoes including **Cotopaxi (~19,347 ft) and Pichincha (two summits, ~15,400–15,700 ft)** in the wider vicinity. MORA around Quito is high and terrain is a foreground factor, not a distant enroute consideration.
- **Approach environment:** 🟧 published operational reference material describes Quito arrivals routed via an ILS approach after a VOR fix, or via **vectors around and between the surrounding mountains** for the parallel-runway approach — consistent with a terrain-constrained, procedurally demanding arrival; confirm exact procedure/minima on the current AIRAC approach charts before use.
- **Boundary terrain:** the Andes continue across the Bogotá FIR boundary to the north and the Lima FIR boundary to the south — consistently high, mountainous terrain along this stretch of the Andean corridor.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟥 a first-order consideration given the sustained high terrain around Quito — escape routing and oxygen/driftdown planning must account for high minimum altitudes; do not assume a standard low-terrain escape profile.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟧 relevant given the high-altitude, cool Andean climate — see OM E Cold Weather Operations and the airport brief.

- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical via ATC where surveillance is available — request the deviation and comply with the vector/level issued; confirm procedural fallback for any non-radar pockets, especially around Quito's terrain-constrained approach environment.
