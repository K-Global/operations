# Houston ARTCC (KZHU) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single ARTCC (US domestic "FIR") — southern/southeastern Texas, Louisiana, southern Mississippi, southwestern Alabama and the northern **Gulf of Mexico**, containing **George Bush Intercontinental (KIAH)** · **Parent area brief:** [North America (Canadian Domestic + Western US)](../../north-america.md) · **Adjacent ARTCCs:** Fort Worth (KZFW) NW/W · Memphis (KZME) N · Atlanta (KZTL) NE · Jacksonville (KZJX) E · Miami (KZMA) SE · Mexican CTA/FIRs (Monterrey, Mérida) S · Havana CTA (Cuba) SE, offshore
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference, not a chart or clearance. Houston Center is a **fully radar/ADS-B-controlled continental ARTCC** over land, with a large **Gulf of Mexico overwater sector** where offshore surveillance historically relied more heavily on non-radar/procedural backup and dedicated Gulf routing structure than the land-based sectors. Routings and levels here are planning context — file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for the aerodromes inside the ARTCC live in the airport briefings (§9). Live restrictions (NOTAMs, hurricane/tropical advisories, SIGMETs) are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. ARTCC structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Houston Center covers **southern Texas, Louisiana, southern Mississippi, southwestern Alabama**, and a large area of the **Gulf of Mexico north of approximately 24°30'N** — public sources give the combined area as roughly **277,000–280,000 sq mi**. 🟧 (precise figure and current boundary — verify FAA ENR.)
- **Vertical structure:** as with any US ARTCC, **one facility** works the whole column, combining/splitting sectors with traffic and staffing; the Gulf overwater sector is worked distinctly from the land sectors given its different surveillance/routing environment (§2). 🟧 (current low/high sector count — verify FAA/VATUSA ZHU facility documentation.)
- **Adjacent ARTCCs (by boundary):**
  - **NW / W — Fort Worth (KZFW, FAA).** North/central Texas.
  - **N — Memphis (KZME, FAA).** Louisiana/Mississippi northern boundary.
  - **NE — Atlanta (KZTL, FAA).** Eastern boundary toward the Southeast.
  - **E — Jacksonville (KZJX, FAA).** Handles the eastern Gulf/Florida panhandle approaches.
  - **SE — Miami (KZMA, FAA).** Shares a boundary further offshore/southeast.
  - **S — Mexican CTA/FIRs (Monterrey, Mérida).** The Houston CTA/FIR's southern border adjoins Mexican-controlled airspace; **at least 100 NM lateral separation** is coordinated across the Houston/Monterrey/Mérida boundary region. 🟧 (exact boundary geometry — verify AIP Mexico / FAA ENR.)
  - **SE (offshore) — Havana CTA (Cuba).** The south-eastern Gulf boundary adjoins Havana-controlled airspace. 🟧
- **Sectorisation:** transit-relevant fact is that the whole column is one FAA facility (no separate UAC); land sectors feed **Houston TRACON (I90)** for KIAH, while the Gulf sector is worked as a distinct overwater block. 🟧 (current sector map — pull from FAA/VATUSA ZHU documentation.)

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in Houston ARTCC and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — required per **14 CFR 91.180**. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟩 RNAV enroute on the **Q-route (high-altitude RNAV)** and **T-route (low-altitude RNAV)** structure; **RNAV SID/STAR** and **RNP APCH** at KIAH. Standard RNAV-1/RNAV-2 airframe capability suffices. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟩 **not required** for a normal domestic/Gulf-coastal routing — VHF voice continuous near-shore; PDC available at major terminals as a convenience. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** with **ADS-B Out** required — **14 CFR 91.225**; **1090ES required at and above FL180** (UAT/978 MHz permitted only below 18,000 ft), effective 01 JAN 2020, including over the Gulf.
- **Overwater / ETOPS-EDTO considerations:** 🟧 Gulf-crossing routings raise standard overwater equipment (life rafts/survival equipment per 14 CFR 91.509) and diversion-time considerations for twin-engine aircraft — see [`OM E — ETOPS-EDTO Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/etops-edto.md) for applicability; the Gulf crossing distance from KIAH is short relative to oceanic ETOPS sectors but the equipment requirement still applies over water.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ARTCC sectors / frequencies:** a transit is handed sequentially through **Houston Center** land/Gulf sectors and onward to the adjacent-ARTCC center (**Fort Worth / Memphis / Atlanta / Jacksonville / Miami**) or to **Houston TRACON (I90)** at the terminal end; the exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC/chart frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / advisory:** US **Flight Service** (Leidos) for flight-information/advisory service in uncontrolled airspace.
- **Language:** 🟩 **English**.
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 low throughout — Gulf-coastal plain and open water; no significant terrain-driven MORA/driftdown constraint for a KIAH-area transit. 🟧 (per-quadrant grid-MORA — take from FAA ENR charts if a specific route requires it.)

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **Gulf of Mexico Warning Areas — 🟧:** numerous offshore **Warning Areas (W-prefix)** over the Gulf tied to military training and to the dense offshore energy/helicopter-support activity; a high-level airline transit is normally clear of them, but confirm active status at planning, especially near the coast.
- **Inland MOA / restricted areas:** 🟧 scattered military training areas across Texas/Louisiana; not expected to be a factor for a KIAH-terminating transit but confirm at planning if radar-vectored off the direct route.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟩 none for the US domestic transit; the Mexican and Cuban FIR boundaries to the south are handled at the ATC/FIR level and not an overflight-permit matter for a KIAH-terminating transit.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** apply the standard **US domestic IFR lost-comms** procedure (route/altitude per last clearance / expected / filed — 14 CFR 91.185; squawk **7600**); continuous VHF coverage over land makes prolonged loss unlikely — confirm coverage assumptions for far-offshore Gulf routings.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — **request the deviation from ATC** and comply with the vector/level issued; convective weather deviation around Gulf-coast thunderstorm cells is a routine seasonal factor. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Atlantic/Gulf hurricane season (Jun–Nov):** 🟥 tropical cyclones and their outer bands can close KIAH and force widespread rerouting; the defining seasonal hazard for this ARTCC.
  - **Summer convective activity:** 🟧 Gulf-coast sea-breeze thunderstorms are frequent in the afternoon/evening, driving tactical deviations and arrival-flow constraints.
  - **Fog:** 🟧 Gulf-coast advection fog can affect KIAH visibility, particularly in winter/spring with onshore flow over cooler ground.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, tropical-cyclone/hurricane advisories (in season), SIGMET/AIRMET (convection, turbulence), active Gulf Warning Area status, the assigned RNAV SID/STAR/runway config, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
