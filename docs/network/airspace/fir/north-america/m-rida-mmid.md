# Mérida (MMID) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — south-eastern Mexico / Yucatán Peninsula, extending over a Gulf of Mexico and NW-Caribbean oceanic sector, including the **Cancún resort gateway (MMUN)** · **Parent area brief:** [North America](../../north-america.md) · **Adjacent FIRs:** Mexico (MMFR, SENEAM) W · Havana (MUFH, IACC Cuba) E/NE · Houston Oceanic (KZHU, FAA) N (Gulf of Mexico) · Central American FIRs (Guatemala/Cenamer) S 🟧
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference, not a chart or clearance. Mérida FIR is administered by **SENEAM** as one of Mexico's four national ACCs, covering the **Yucatán Peninsula and an adjoining Gulf of Mexico / Caribbean oceanic sector**. The defining features are the **Gulf/Caribbean maritime exposure driving a hurricane-season hazard window** and the FIR's role as the eastern gateway of SENEAM's multi-FIR national structure, bordering Cuban (Havana) airspace. Routings and levels here are planning context — file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for MMUN live in the airport briefing (§9). Live restrictions are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

- **Lateral extent:** Mérida FIR covers the **Yucatán Peninsula (states of Yucatán, Quintana Roo, Campeche)** and extends over an adjoining **Gulf of Mexico and north-western Caribbean oceanic sector**, per SENEAM's description of Mérida ACC bordering Mexico ACC to the west. 🟧 (exact oceanic boundary decimals with Houston Oceanic and Havana FIR — VERIFY AIP México ENR 2.)
- **Vertical split:** no separate lower/upper ATSU split identified; treat as a single-column ACC pending AIP confirmation. 🟧
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):** **Mexico (MMFR)** W, **Havana (MUFH, Cuba)** E/NE across the Gulf/Yucatán Channel approach, **Houston Oceanic (KZHU, FAA)** N over the Gulf of Mexico, and **Central American FIRs (Guatemala/Cenamer)** S. 🟧 (boundary detail unverified — confirm AIP México ENR 2 and adjacent-state AIPs.)

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 the Yucatán Peninsula is uniformly **low-lying and flat** (karst limestone plain) — no significant MORA constraint in this FIR, in contrast to the Mexico City (MMFR) or western-cordillera sectors.
- **Boundary terrain:** none of operational significance at the Havana/Houston Oceanic/Central American boundaries — the constraint in this FIR is maritime weather (§11), not terrain.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟩 not a terrain-driven concern in this FIR; standard oceanic/overwater engine-out and diversion planning applies over the Gulf/Caribbean extension — dense coastal alternates on the peninsula itself.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not applicable — tropical/subtropical low-elevation FIR.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** apply the ICAO-standard lost-communications procedure (route/level per last clearance / expected / filed) pending confirmation of any Mexico-specific refinement; squawk **7600**. 🟧
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical via ATC where surveillance is available; over the oceanic Gulf/Caribbean extension a procedural fallback may apply — not independently confirmed. 🟧
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):** 🟥 **Atlantic hurricane season (roughly 1 June – 30 November, climatological peak August–October)** directly affects this FIR's Gulf of Mexico / Caribbean sector and the Cancún terminal area — tropical cyclones and their outer convective bands can force widespread rerouting, terminal closures and diversion planning at MMUN. This is the FIR's principal durable seasonal hazard, distinct from the terrain-driven hazards of neighbouring FIRs.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, current AIP México restricted-area status, tropical cyclone advisories / SIGMET-TC and general SIGMET/AIRMET, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
