# Nassau (MYNA) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the Bahamas archipelago and the Turks & Caicos Islands area, with lower-level control delegated locally and upper/oceanic airspace integrated with Miami Oceanic · **Parent area brief:** [North America](../../north-america.md) · **Adjacent FIRs:** Miami (KZMA, FAA — domestic + oceanic) N/W · Havana (MUFH, Cuba) SW · Port-au-Prince / Camagüey approaches SE 🟧
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference, not a chart or clearance. Nassau FIR is administered by the **Bahamas Civil Aviation Authority (BCAA)** and covers the Bahamian archipelago together with the **Turks and Caicos Islands** area at its south-eastern extremity. The defining feature is a **layered control structure**: Nassau provides the lower CTA/approach-level service while upper/oceanic levels and some inter-island legs are worked or backed up by **Miami Oceanic/Center** under service-delegation arrangements — a materially different picture from a single-ANSP continental FIR. Routings and levels here are planning context — file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for MBPV live in the airport briefing (§9). Live restrictions are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Nassau FIR covers the **Bahamian archipelago** (Grand Bahama, Abaco, Andros, New Providence/Nassau, Eleuthera, the southern "Family Islands") and extends south-east to the **Turks and Caicos Islands**, a separate British Overseas Territory whose airspace is served within this FIR structure. 🟧 (precise lateral boundary coordinates — VERIFY Bahamas AIP ENR 2 / Turks & Caicos AIP if separately published.)
- **Vertical split — 🟧 the FIR's defining structural feature:** third-party operator guidance describes the **Nassau CTA/FIR as extending from the surface to approximately 6,000 ft MSL**, with the **Miami Oceanic CTA/FIR** controlling the airspace above that level over Bahamian territory. This is a materially different vertical structure from a conventional single-ANSP FIR (contrast Havana or the Mexican FIRs, which are single-column). **This figure has not been independently verified against the Bahamas AIP** and should be confirmed before being relied upon operationally.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):** **Miami (KZMA, FAA)** N/W — both domestic and oceanic, given the layered structure above — **Havana (MUFH, Cuba)** SW, and **Port-au-Prince/Camagüey** approaches to the SE toward Haiti/eastern Cuba. 🟧 (confirm against Bahamas AIP and adjacent-state AIPs.)
- **Sectorisation:** not independently identified in detail; Nassau Approach/Tower serves the New Providence terminal area, with the Family Islands and Turks & Caicos served by a mix of local approach control (where established, e.g. Providenciales) and higher-level service from Miami. 🟧

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Enroute/lower ATSU:** **Bahamas Civil Aviation Authority (BCAA)** — Nassau ACC/Approach for the New Providence terminal area and the lower CTA.
- **Upper-airspace / oceanic ATSU:** 🟧 **Miami Oceanic CTA/FIR (FAA)** is described in public operator guidance as controlling the airspace above the Nassau CTA's ~6,000 ft ceiling; some inter-island flights (e.g. Freeport–Providenciales) are reported as being worked by **Miami Center** with handoff to local approach only in the terminal phase. This service-delegation pattern should be confirmed against the Bahamas AIP before being treated as definitive.
- **FIS / FSS:** not independently identified — verify Bahamas AIP ENR 1.
- **Surveillance basis:** radar assumed in the New Providence (Nassau) terminal area; surveillance basis over the Family Islands and the Turks & Caicos sector not independently confirmed — likely a mix of radar (where equipped) and procedural. 🟧

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 the Bahamas and Turks & Caicos are **low-lying limestone/coral islands** with no significant elevation (typically well under 500 ft) — no MORA constraint anywhere in this FIR.
- **Boundary terrain:** none of operational significance — the FIR's hazard profile is entirely maritime/weather-driven (§11), not terrain-driven.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟩 not a terrain-driven concern; standard overwater engine-out and diversion planning applies given the extensive open-water exposure between islands, particularly on the Nassau–Turks & Caicos leg.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not applicable — tropical/subtropical FIR.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** apply the ICAO-standard lost-communications procedure (route/level per last clearance / expected / filed), noting the layered BCAA/Miami control structure may affect which unit to attempt re-contact with; squawk **7600**. 🟧
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical via ATC where surveillance is available (New Providence terminal area, upper-level Miami-integrated airspace); procedural fallback likely over the outer Family Islands/Turks & Caicos sector — not independently confirmed which applies where. 🟧
