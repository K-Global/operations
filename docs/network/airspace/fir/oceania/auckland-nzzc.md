# Auckland (NZZC) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the **New Zealand domestic FIR** (North and South Islands and their immediate coastal approaches), contains the **Auckland (NZAA)** and **Wellington (NZWN)** hubs · **Parent area brief:** [Oceania](../../oceania.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Auckland Oceanic (NZZO) — surrounding oceanic FIR, same ANSP, procedural — N/E/S/W all sides beyond the domestic boundary · Brisbane (YBBB) far W (Tasman handoff, via NZZO) · Nadi (NFFF) far N (Fiji, via NZZO) · Nouméa/New Caledonia sector (delegated within Nadi) far NW (via NZZO)
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. **NZZC is the radar/ADS-B-surveilled domestic FIR** over New Zealand's North and South Islands; it is bordered on every side by **Auckland Oceanic (NZZO)** — one of the world's largest oceanic FIRs (~5.87 M km²), worked by **"Auckland Radio"** as procedural oceanic control over the South Pacific where NZZC's ground surveillance ends. NZZO is not a vertical UIR split of NZZC — it is the laterally-adjacent oceanic FIR surrounding the New Zealand domestic airspace, run by the same ANSP (**Airways New Zealand**). Both are covered in this single brief since NZZO has no significant terminal/continental content of its own — it is the oceanic gateway around NZZC. Routings and levels here are planning context — file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances and the oceanic clearance. Terminal procedures for NZAA/NZWN live in the airport briefings (§9). Live restrictions are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** **NZZC** covers **New Zealand's North and South Islands and their immediate coastal/offshore approaches** — a comparatively compact domestic FIR by area next to its oceanic neighbour. **Auckland Oceanic (NZZO)** wraps around it on every side, extending across the **South Pacific** to boundaries with Brisbane (Tasman), Nadi (Fiji, north), and the Pacific-island oceanic FIRs to the east; NZZO is one of the world's largest oceanic FIRs at **~5.87 M km²**.
- **NZZC / NZZO relationship:** 🟩 **not a vertical split** — NZZC is the surveilled domestic FIR over the landmass and near coast; NZZO is the laterally-adjacent oceanic FIR beyond it, both operated by **Airways New Zealand**. The practical boundary is where ground radar/ADS-B coverage ends and procedural oceanic control (Auckland Radio) begins.
- **Vertical split within NZZC:** 🟧 Class A upper airspace over the main jet routes; confirm the exact vertical steps at the NZ AIP Part 71.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary, via NZZO):**
  - **W — Brisbane (YBBB, Airservices Australia).** Tasman Sea oceanic handoff; note the **eastern Tasman off the NSW/Queensland coast is worked from Brisbane**, so a westbound Auckland–Australia routing may transit Brisbane oceanic before/after the NZZC/NZZO leg.
  - **N — Nadi (NFFF), Fiji.** South Pacific oceanic boundary.
  - **NW — Nouméa/New Caledonia sector** — delegated within the **Nadi FIR (NFFF)**, not a separate FIR (see the [Nouméa brief](noum-a-nwww.md) for the full nuance); reached via NZZO/NFFF.
  - **Other Pacific-island oceanic FIRs** (Rarotonga/Cook Islands area, Tonga, Samoa) — Airways New Zealand provides **contracted ATC services** for Tonga, Samoa and the Cook Islands airspace, distinct from NZZC/NZZO themselves.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 Auckland Control works domestic sectors (e.g. Oceanic Radar, Raglan) feeding NZAA/NZWN; the transit-relevant fact is **NZZC (radar) vs NZZO (procedural)** and which of the surrounding FIRs (Brisbane/Nadi) a given oceanic routing ultimately joins.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Domestic ATSU:** 🟩 **Airways New Zealand** — towers/approach at **17 airports**, a **surveillance centre at Christchurch**, and **Auckland Control** (incl. Oceanic Radar and Raglan sectors) feeding NZAA/NZWN en-route.
- **Oceanic control:** 🟩 **Auckland Oceanic Control Centre ("Auckland Radio"), Auckland** — procedural, **ADS-C/CPDLC (FANS-1/A)** with **space-based ADS-B (Aireon)** supplementing tracking over NZZO.
- **FIS:** Airways NZ flight information service; the NZ domestic IFR trunk stays in controlled airspace throughout.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 radar/ADS-B over NZZC; procedural + ADS-C + space-based ADS-B over NZZO.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in NZZC/NZZO and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 applicable across NZZC and NZZO with **directional level bands** — south/west tracks FL300/320/340/360/380/400; north/east tracks FL310/330/350/370/390/410. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟩 mature PBN — RNP APCH standard at NZ international aerodromes; RNP considered by traffic density/route structure per the NZ AIP. **Oceanic (NZZO) requires RNAV 10/RNP 10 baseline, RNP 4 for reduced PBCS separation** where applicable — see the region-wide parameters in the [Oceania area brief](../../oceania.md) §4. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟩 not applicable — this is an ICAO EUR mandate; standard VHF channel spacing applies in NZ.
- **Datalink (CPDLC/ADS-C):** 🟥 **CPDLC + ADS-C (FANS-1/A)** is the backbone of NZZO oceanic separation and tracking; domestic CPDLC is also used within NZZC. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 NZ controlled airspace is **ADS-B-based**; Mode S transponder required for IFR. Confirm current CAA NZ mandate scope.

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a NZAA/NZWN transit is worked by **Auckland Control** domestic sectors (Oceanic Radar, Raglan, etc.) at the terminal; exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current AIP frequencies, not a stored table.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Domestic network:** 🟩 the NZ jet network (RNAV/RNP airways + SID/STAR) feeds **NZAA, NZWN, NZCH** under radar/ADS-B control.
- **Oceanic routes:** 🟧 Tasman crossings toward Brisbane/Melbourne FIRs, and South Pacific routes toward Nadi (Fiji)/New Caledonia (via Nadi) and the Cook Islands, are flown as **User Preferred Routes** with an oceanic clearance, ADS-C/CPDLC login and (where applicable) SLOP — see the [Oceania area brief](../../oceania.md) §6 for the region-wide AUSOTS-retirement/UPR context.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the NZZC/NZZO domestic-to-oceanic transition points, and the oceanic entry/exit fixes toward Brisbane (W) and Nadi (N); the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string.
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Auckland Intl (NZAA, hub)**, **Wellington Intl (NZWN, hub)**, Christchurch (NZCH) and the wider NZ domestic network. Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟥 the **Southern Alps** running the length of the South Island — **Aoraki/Mount Cook 3,724 m (12,218 ft)** — is the FIR's dominant terrain feature; drives the driftdown/escape floor for any routing over/near the South Island and generates strong **mountain-wave and CAT** in a westerly flow. The North Island is comparatively low, with isolated volcanic peaks (Ruapehu ~2,797 m/9,177 ft, Taranaki ~2,518 m/8,261 ft).
- **Boundary terrain:** none of operational significance across the NZZC/NZZO or adjacent-FIR boundaries — the oceanic boundaries are over water.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** a genuine constraint near/over the **South Island Alps** — plan escape routing away from the range; not a constraint over the rest of the domestic FIR (dense diversion fields). Over **NZZO**, ETP/critical-point planning applies given sparse oceanic diversions.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** relevant in winter at higher/southern NZ aerodromes (South Island) — a terminal item; see the airport briefings (§9) if/when a South Island field is added.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** squawk **7600**; continue per the **NZ AIP ENR 1** IFR comms-failure rules (last clearance/filed route and levels), attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. Over NZZO follow the oceanic contingency procedure (CPDLC/HF, standard offset).
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical within NZZC — request the deviation from ATC. **Over NZZO apply the ICAO oceanic weather-deviation procedure** (offset, level change, broadcast on 121.5). Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Southern Alps mountain wave / CAT** — strongest in westerly flow, South Island.
  - **Cook Strait / Wellington wind and turbulence** — a persistent, well-known NZ hazard (see the NZWN airport brief for terminal-specific detail).
  - **Volcanic ash — VAAC Wellington** covers New Zealand and the SW Pacific (Ruapehu, Tongariro, Whakaari/White Island, and Vanuatu volcanoes) — can propagate ash across NZZC/NZZO upper airspace.
  - **South Pacific tropical-cyclone season (Nov 1–Apr 30, peak Jan–Mar)** — can affect NZZO's northern reaches and drive re-routes; NZ itself is south of the main basin but ex-tropical systems can reach the North Island.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, restricted-area status, SIGMET/AIRMET (CAT, wind, convection), **VAAC Wellington** volcanic-ash advisories, oceanic-clearance/CPDLC-ADS-C login requirements, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
