# Melbourne (YMMM) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the **southern half of Australia plus the Southern & Indian Oceans**, and the FIR that **contains Sydney (YSSY)** as well as Melbourne, Canberra, Adelaide and Perth · **Parent area brief:** [Oceania](../../oceania.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Brisbane (YBBB) N (northern-Australia + Coral/Tasman) · Auckland Oceanic (NZZO) E · Ujung Pandang/Jakarta (WAAF/WIIF) N-NW · Johannesburg Oceanic (FAJO) far W · Mauritius (FIMM), Male (VRMF), Colombo (VCCF) NW oceanic
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-25 · **Status** Draft — provisional; FIR extents/ownership verified against Airservices Australia, and the **Sydney-in-YMMM** boundary fact confirmed (see note below); exact class-per-band and oceanic sector split flagged 🟧

> **Read-me — FIR identity, verified:** Sydney (YSSY) is in the **Melbourne FIR (YMMM)**, **not** the Brisbane FIR. Airservices Australia states that **Brisbane Centre's airspace begins 45 nm (83 km) *north* of Sydney** — so Sydney lies just inside the **northern edge of the Melbourne FIR**, and Sydney en-route is worked by **Melbourne Centre**. This brief therefore covers **YMMM** as the correct single FIR for the Sydney hub.
>
> Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. YMMM is a **mixed continental-radar / oceanic-procedural FIR** — radar/ADS-B surveillance over the populated continent, **procedural oceanic** control (ADS-C / space-based ADS-B / CPDLC-FANS) over the vast Southern and Indian Ocean sectors. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for aerodromes inside the FIR live in the airport briefings (§9). Live restrictions are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. ## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Melbourne FIR (**YMMM**) covers the **southern half of continental Australia** — NSW (incl. Sydney), the ACT, Victoria, Tasmania, South Australia and the southern portion of Western Australia (incl. Perth) — **plus** an enormous **oceanic area**: the **Southern Ocean to the South Pole** and the **Indian Ocean** westward toward Africa. It is one of the world's largest FIRs by area.
- **Northern boundary with Brisbane (YBBB):** 🟩 the YBBB/YMMM line crosses NSW roughly **45 nm north of Sydney** (near the Hunter/Newcastle region) and continues west across the continent — Brisbane owns the **northern** half, Melbourne the **southern**. **Sydney sits just inside the Melbourne side of this line.**
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 **Class A** upper airspace over the jet network; lower Class C/D around the terminals and E/G below/outside — confirm the exact vertical steps at the Australia AIP ENR. RVSM applies **FL290–FL410**.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Brisbane (YBBB, Airservices).** The other Australian FIR; the boundary is ~45 nm N of Sydney.
  - **E — Auckland Oceanic (NZZO, Airways NZ).** Tasman Sea oceanic handoff (note: the eastern Tasman/Coral oceanic off the NSW coast is worked from **Brisbane**, so eastbound oceanic clearances may involve YBBB before NZZO).
  - **N / NW — Ujung Pandang (WAAF) / Jakarta (WIIF), Indonesia.**
  - **Far W / NW oceanic — Johannesburg Oceanic (FAJO), Mauritius (FIMM), Male (VRMF), Colombo (VCCF)** across the Indian Ocean.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 Melbourne Centre works many continental and oceanic sectors that combine/split with traffic; the transit-relevant fact is **continent (radar) vs oceanic (procedural)** and the **YBBB boundary 45 nm N of Sydney**.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **En-route ATSU:** 🟩 **Melbourne Centre (YMMM), Airservices Australia** — one of Australia's two en-route centres (the other is Brisbane Centre, YBBB). Sydney en-route is worked by **Melbourne Centre**; the terminal environment is worked by **Sydney TCU / Approach** and **Sydney Tower**.
- **Oceanic control:** 🟩 the Southern/Indian-Ocean sectors are **procedural**, supported by **ADS-C / CPDLC (FANS-1/A)** and **space-based ADS-B (Aireon)** for tracking beyond ground radar.
- **FIS:** Airservices flight information service; the mainland IFR trunk stays in controlled airspace throughout.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 **radar + ground ADS-B (70+ stations)** over the populated continent — Australia is a mature ADS-B environment; **space-based ADS-B + ADS-C** over the ocean.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Melbourne FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — Australian RVSM. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 mature PBN — **RNP 2 / RNAV 5** enroute, **RNP 1** terminal (SID/STAR), **RNP APCH** arrivals per the Australian AIP. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟩 **not applicable** — 8.33 kHz is an ICAO EUR mandate; standard 25 kHz VHF applies in Australia.
- **Datalink (CPDLC / ADS-C):** 🟥 **ADS-C and CPDLC (FANS-1/A)** are used across the **oceanic** sectors for separation and tracking; **space-based ADS-B (Aireon)** extends surveillance over the Southern/Indian Ocean. Domestic CPDLC is also used. FANS-1/A oceanic — see [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **ADS-B Out (1090 MHz ES) is mandatory** in Australian controlled airspace (CASA); **Mode S** transponder required.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a Sydney transit is worked by **Melbourne Centre** en-route sectors and the **Sydney TCU/Tower** at the terminal; the exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **Oceanic:** 🟧 **HF / SATVOICE / CPDLC** for the Southern/Indian-Ocean sectors where VHF ends.
- **Language:** 🟩 **English** throughout.
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Continental network:** 🟩 the mainland jet network (RNP/RNAV airways + SID/STAR) feeds the Sydney, Melbourne, Canberra, Adelaide and Perth terminals under radar/ADS-B control; the Sydney–Melbourne–Brisbane "golden triangle" trunk is dense, structured traffic.
- **Oceanic routes:** 🟧 the **Tasman** (to New Zealand — note the eastern oceanic off NSW is worked from **Brisbane**), the trans-**Indian-Ocean** tracks toward Africa/Middle East, and Southern-Ocean routings are **procedural**, flown on organised/random tracks with oceanic clearance, ADS-C/CPDLC and (where applicable) SLOP.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the continental boundary fixes toward Brisbane (YBBB) and the oceanic entry/exit fixes toward Auckland (NZZO) and the Indonesian/Indian-Ocean boundaries; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Sydney (YSSY, hub)**, **Melbourne (YMML)**, **Canberra (YSCB)**, **Adelaide (YPAD)**, **Avalon (YMAV)**, **Perth (YPPH)**, Hobart (YMHB) and the wider southern network. Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9). *Brisbane (YBBN) and the northern/tropical fields sit in **Brisbane FIR (YBBB)** — not this FIR.*

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 mostly low. The one significant belt is the **Great Dividing Range / Australian Alps** in the SE — the **Snowy Mountains (Mt Kosciuszko ~7,310 ft / 2,228 m)** SW of Sydney and near Canberra — plus the **Blue Mountains (~3,500 ft)** immediately west of Sydney. The interior and coastal plains are low; not a cruise-level factor at FL290+.
- **Boundary terrain:** none of operational significance across the adjacent-FIR boundaries; the oceanic boundaries are over water.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** not a constraint over the continent (dense diversion fields, low terrain away from the Alps). **Over the oceanic Southern/Indian-Ocean sectors, ETP/critical-point and depressurisation-escape planning IS relevant** given the long over-water legs and sparse diversions — an ETOPS/EDTO consideration for trans-oceanic routings.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** relevant in winter at the higher/southern terminals (Canberra, alpine strips) — a terminal, not enroute, item; see the airport briefings (§9).

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** squawk **7600**; continue per the **Australian AIP ENR 1.** IFR comms-failure rules (last clearance / filed route and levels), attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. Over oceanic sectors follow the oceanic contingency procedure (CPDLC/HF, standard offset).
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical over the continent — **request the deviation from ATC**. **Over oceanic sectors apply the ICAO oceanic weather-deviation procedure** (offset, level change, broadcast on 121.5). Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Subtropical jet / CAT** — strong upper winds and clear-air turbulence across SE Australia, especially winter.
  - **Winter cold fronts / low visibility** at the southern terminals (Melbourne, Adelaide, Canberra); **mountain wave** over the Australian Alps.
  - **Summer convection** — inland thunderstorms and, in the northern reaches of the FIR, tropical influence; **bushfire smoke** can reduce visibility in summer.
  - **Volcanic ash** — **VAAC Darwin** covers the region; **Indonesian and New Zealand volcanoes** can propagate ash across the Australian/oceanic upper airspace — the strategic wildcard for the oceanic and northern sectors.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, restricted/danger-area status, SIGMET/AIRMET (CAT, convection, fronts), **VAAC Darwin** volcanic-ash advisories, oceanic-clearance requirements, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
