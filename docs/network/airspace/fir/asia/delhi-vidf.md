# Delhi (VIDF) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — northern India, containing the national-capital hub **Indira Gandhi Intl (VIDP)** · **Parent area brief:** [Asia](../../asia.md) · **Adjacent FIRs:** Lahore (OPLR) NW · Karachi (OPKR) W (Pakistan, per AAI LOA) · Kathmandu (VNSM) NE · Mumbai (VABF) S/SW · Kolkata (VECF) E
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Delhi is a **radar/ADS-B-controlled continental FIR** — no oceanic segment — but it sits directly against the sensitive India–Pakistan boundary (Lahore/Karachi) and interfaces Nepal (Kathmandu) and the two other Indian mainland FIRs (Mumbai, Kolkata). Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical radar-ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for VIDP live in the airport briefing (§9). Live restrictions (NOTAMs, fog/CAT III status, cross-border airspace notices) are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. ## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Delhi FIR (VIDF) covers **northern India** — the National Capital Region, the Indo-Gangetic plain and the northern states — bounded by Pakistan (NW/W), Nepal (NE) and the Indian Mumbai and Kolkata FIRs (S/SW and E respectively). It is one of **five Indian FIRs/sub-FIRs** (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, and the Guwahati sub-FIR), all under AAI.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 **Delhi ACC works the FIR surface-to-upper-limit**; no separately-branded upper-area control centre. Confirm the exact vertical/class split at **AIP ENR 2.1**. Cruise levels for hub transits sit in the RVSM band (FL290–410).
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **NW — Lahore (OPLR, Pakistan CAA).** The primary north-western boundary; a politically sensitive interface subject to periodic restriction (India–Pakistan airspace tension has closed this boundary to overflight in past episodes — durable risk, not a constant state).
  - **W — Karachi (OPKR, Pakistan CAA).** AAI's own Delhi contingency planning lists both Karachi and Lahore ACCs as directly-affected adjacent authorities; Karachi's primary boundary is further south against Mumbai FIR, but Delhi/Karachi coordination applies on the western contingency routes.
  - **NE — Kathmandu (VNSM, Nepal CAA).** The Himalayan-front boundary; high terrain rises sharply just across it.
  - **S/SW — Mumbai FIR (VABF, India/AAI).** Historic AAI contingency sectorisation splits Delhi airspace along **ATS route P628**: south of P628 falls to Mumbai's designated contingency authority.
  - **E — Kolkata FIR (VECF, India/AAI).** North of P628 falls to Kolkata's designated contingency authority (illustrative of the internal AAI sector logic, not a live clearance rule).
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 Delhi ACC divides into internal sectors combining/splitting with traffic; **Varanasi** and **Nagpur** are AAI sub-ACCs that interface the Delhi/Mumbai/Kolkata boundary region. Exact sector geometry is AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC frequencies.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Lower- & upper-airspace ATSU:** 🟩 **Delhi ACC** ("Delhi Control", AAI) works the whole FIR surface-to-upper-limit; no separate UAC. Approach/terminal control for VIDP feeds off the ACC.
- **FIS / FSS:** flight information service is provided by Delhi ACC; not a distinguishing factor for an IFR hub transit remaining in controlled airspace.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 **radar + ADS-B mosaic** (AAI cites a nationwide network of ~12 radars, 14 ADS-B ground stations and one space-based ADS-B feed integrated into the Auto Track-III automation platform) — continuous coverage over the FIR's populated core; 🟧 confirm coverage continuity over any sparsely-instrumented pockets at the AIP.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Delhi FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410 inclusive** — India-wide RVSM (implemented 2003, harmonised with ICAO Asia/Pacific). See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 **RNAV 5 (B-RNAV)** on the enroute ATS-route network; **RNP 1** for terminal SID/STAR; **RNP APCH** for arrivals (GAGAN SBAS-supported LPV at equipped aerodromes, incl. VIDP). See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 **ADS-C/CPDLC** capability exists on the Delhi/Mumbai Auto Track-III automation; domestic mandate scope, login sector and ATN vs FANS basis are **unconfirmed for continental Delhi FIR operations** (oceanic ADS-C/CPDLC use is well-established on the Mumbai/Chennai oceanic legs — §5 of those briefs). See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** transponder required; **ADS-B Out mandated at/above FL290** on designated continental PBN routes from 1 Jan 2019, **extended to FL285** on named routes from Jun 2023 (DGCA/AAI AIP Supplement) — confirm the current route list and FL threshold at the AIP Supplement in force.
- **8.33 kHz:** 🟧 not confirmed as an India-wide mandate (this is primarily a EUR-region requirement); standard 25 kHz VHF spacing believed to apply domestically — confirm at AIP GEN 3.4.
- **Low-visibility / CAT III:** 🟥 VIDP is CAT IIIB-equipped (relevant to LVPs, not enroute FIR structure) — see [`OM E — Low Visibility Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/low-visibility-operations.md) and the VIDP airport briefing (§9).

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a hub transit is worked by **Delhi ACC ("Delhi Control")** across its sectors and handed to the adjacent-FIR ACC (Lahore, Karachi, Kathmandu, Mumbai, Kolkata) at the boundary; exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC/AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** Delhi ACC provides flight information service in the FIR.
- **Language:** 🟩 **English** (ICAO standard); Hindi also used domestically.
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air (per AAI TIBA/contingency material).

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟧 the Delhi FIR is **fixed ATS-route / airway-structured** (India operates ~210 ATS routes nationally: 118 domestic, 92 international, including RNAV "L/M/N/P/Q" designators); not a continental FRA in the European sense. Confirm any flow-management/preferred-route programme at ENR/NOTAM.
- **Airway spine:** Delhi sits on the **NW India trunk** between the Pakistan boundary (Lahore/Karachi) and the Gangetic-plain corridor toward Kolkata/Bangladesh, and on the **north–south spine** toward Mumbai and onward to the Arabian Sea. **Route P628** is a structurally significant east–west reference (used as the AAI internal Delhi/Mumbai/Kolkata contingency sector boundary).
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** boundary entry/exit fixes feeding the VIDP terminal area; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Indira Gandhi Intl (VIDP, New Delhi)** — the sole K Global-served aerodrome in this FIR.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 **generally low** — the FIR is dominated by the flat Indo-Gangetic plain. Terrain rises sharply only at the northern fringe.
- **Boundary terrain:** 🟥 the **Himalayan front** rises abruptly just across the **Kathmandu-FIR boundary (NE)** — a significant driftdown/escape consideration for any routing that approaches that edge, though not a factor for a normal VIDP hub transit routed through the plains.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** not a constraint over the plains core of the FIR; becomes relevant only near the Himalayan/Nepal boundary — plan escape routing away from that edge if affected.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟧 winter cold-soak corrections may apply at VIDP in the coldest months — see the VIDP airport briefing and [`OM E — Cold Weather Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/cold-weather-operations.md).

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **Military / restricted / ADIZ:** 🟥 significant military-restricted airspace and **Air Defence Identification Zone (ADIZ)** considerations exist near the **India–Pakistan boundary** (Lahore/Karachi interface); activation and overflight approval are **AIP/NOTAM-published** and carry genuine political sensitivity — this boundary has been closed to civil overflight during past India–Pakistan tension episodes. Confirm active status and any overflight-approval requirement at planning.

- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟥 **prior DGCA/AAI overflight approval is required** for foreign operators transiting Indian FIRs generally (per AAI contingency-plan material) — this is a durable procedural requirement, not merely a contingency-period rule; confirm current permit process.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO comms-failure procedure — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance/filed route and levels, attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Delhi-specific refinement at **AIP ENR 1.8 / GEN**.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — **request the deviation from ATC** and comply with the vector/level issued. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Winter dense fog / CAT III season** — 🟥 the defining seasonal hazard at VIDP, roughly **December–February** (radiation fog compounded by winter smog/haze over the NCR). VIDP is CAT IIIB-capable; low-visibility procedures (LVPs) are routinely activated and can still ground non-CAT-III-qualified aircraft/crews or force widespread delay/diversion. See [`OM E — Low Visibility Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/low-visibility-operations.md) and the VIDP airport briefing.
  - **Pre-monsoon convection (Apr–Jun)** — dust storms ("andhi") and thunderstorm squalls over northern India; reduced visibility and gusty/shifting winds.
  - **SW monsoon (~Jun–Sep)** — heavy rain and convective activity, generally less disruptive at Delhi than at the coastal Mumbai/Chennai FIRs.
  - **Volcanic ash:** 🟩 low exposure for a purely northern-India routing; **VAAC Darwin** is the responsible regional centre for the wider Bay-of-Bengal/Barren-Island source region (relevant chiefly to the Chennai/Kolkata FIRs, not Delhi) — pull VA advisories at planning regardless.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, VIDP fog/CAT III and LVP status, active ADIZ/restricted-area and India–Pakistan boundary notices, SIGMET/AIRMET, VAAC Darwin volcanic-ash advisories, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
