# Karachi (OPKR) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — southern Pakistan and a substantial **Arabian Sea oceanic/procedural sector**, containing **Jinnah Intl Karachi (OPKC)** · **Parent area brief:** [Asia](../../asia.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Lahore (OPLR, Pakistan/PCAA) N · Delhi (VIDF, India/AAI) E · Muscat (OOMM, Oman) S · Tehran (OIIX, Iran) W · Kabul (OAKX, Afghanistan) NW
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — verified vs public PCAA/EASA/regional-security material; several structural facts (exact class-per-band, CPDLC status, precise RVSM effective date) 🟧 unconfirmed from a direct PCAA AIP source

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Karachi is a **radar-controlled continental FIR over southern Pakistan with an Arabian Sea oceanic component**, and it sits directly on one of the world's more operationally sensitive borders. Two features are **factual, neutral operational-planning items, not a political statement**: (1) the **India–Pakistan border area carries a documented history of GNSS interference (jamming/spoofing)** correlated with periods of regional tension, and (2) **western sectors of the FIR (Baluchistan) have an EASA-advised minimum overflight altitude** due to a security risk assessment unrelated to routine ATC. Both are live/perishable and must be re-checked at planning (§9/§11), not treated as fixed facts from this brief. Hot-season performance planning at Karachi is also a factor — see §11 and the OPKC airport brief. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for OPKC live in the airport briefing (§9). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Karachi FIR (OPKR) covers **southern Pakistan** — Sindh and Baluchistan provinces — roughly the band from **23°N to 30°N**, plus a substantial **Arabian Sea oceanic sector** to the south. Pakistani airspace is divided into exactly two FIRs: **Karachi (OPKR)** in the south and **Lahore (OPLR)** in the north/Punjab.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 not individually confirmed; Karachi ACC is understood to work the FIR surface-to-upper-limit as a single unit. Confirm at **AIP ENR 2.1**.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Lahore (OPLR, Pakistan, PCAA).** The internal Pakistani FIR boundary, roughly along the Punjab/Sindh-Baluchistan divide.
  - **E — Delhi (VIDF, India, AAI).** The eastern, India–Pakistan land boundary — a border with documented GNSS-interference history (§9) and periodic mutual airspace-closure precedent during tension episodes (§11).
  - **S — Muscat (OOMM, Oman).** The Arabian Sea maritime boundary, the FIR's oceanic gateway southbound.
  - **W — Tehran (OIIX, Iran).** The western land boundary.
  - **NW — Kabul (OAKX, Afghanistan).** The north-western land boundary.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 not published in the sources reached; fly the current-AIRAC frequencies for the day's sector plan.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 not implemented; the Karachi FIR is **fixed ATS-route structured**, consistent with the rest of the region.
- **Airway spine:** the FIR is a **west-gateway crossroads** — the trunk between the Indian subcontinent (Delhi, E) and the Middle East/Gulf (via Muscat, S, and Tehran, W), with a further route toward Central Asia via Kabul (NW).
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** boundary fixes toward Lahore (N), Delhi (E), Muscat (S), Tehran (W) and Kabul (NW); the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Jinnah Intl, Karachi (OPKC)** — the network destination and Pakistan's principal international gateway.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 low/coastal near Karachi itself; the FIR's **Baluchistan sector** includes the **Kirthar Range** and the **Baluchistan plateau** — moderate terrain (broadly a few thousand feet to ~2,000–3,000 m in places), well short of Himalaya-class MORA. Take per-quadrant grid-MORA from the current ENR chart for any routing that transits inland Baluchistan.
- **Boundary terrain:** higher terrain (Hindu Kush foothills) rises across the Kabul-FIR (Afghanistan) boundary to the NW; not a Karachi-FIR cruise constraint on a normal OPKC transit.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟧 not expected to bind at RVSM cruise levels on the coastal/southern routing; confirm terrain-clearance margins for any inland Baluchistan routing.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not a significant driver — the FIR is predominantly hot/arid; see §11 for the opposite (heat-performance) concern.

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **Air Defence Clearance (ADC) / ADIZ:** 🟥 Pakistan requires an **ADC (Air Defence Clearance) number** for flights operating through its airspace, including the Karachi FIR, consistent with the broader South-Asian ADC pattern (Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Nepal). Detail is published at **Pakistan AIP ENR 2.2**; confirm current process, lead time and any route exemption at planning — treat as a **mandatory entry procedure**, not optional.
- **Baluchistan / Khyber Pakhtunkhwa overflight-altitude advisory:** 🟥 **EASA maintains a Conflict Zone Information Bulletin advising operators not to conduct flights below FL260** over Pakistan's Baluchistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa provinces, reflecting an ongoing security risk assessment (militant activity) unrelated to routine ATC. This is a **live, advisory-driven altitude floor**, not a permanent ATC restriction — re-verify the current EASA CZIB/state NOTAM before planning any routing that would take the aircraft below FL260 over these provinces.
- **Military / danger / restricted areas:** 🟧 named areas are AIP/NOTAM-published; not individually catalogued here.
- **Overflight-permit considerations:** 🟧 Pakistan requires overflight permits for foreign operators per standard practice; confirm current requirement at planning.

> **SUA currency:** re-check the ADC process, the Baluchistan/KP FL260 advisory, and any active danger/restricted-area NOTAM at planning — this list is durable context, not live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO standard — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance/filed route and levels, attempt the previous/adjacent frequency and **121.5**. 🟧 Confirm any Pakistan-specific refinement at AIP ENR 1.8/GEN.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — request the deviation from ATC and comply with the vector/level issued.
- **Border security & GNSS interference — 🟧 factual, neutral, live/perishable:**
  - **GNSS jamming/spoofing** has been documented in the airspace around Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad, and is reported by Indian authorities as concentrated near the shared border, correlating with periods of regional tension. Expect possible **FMS position drift and spurious alerts** in border-proximate sectors during such periods; cross-check DME/DME/IRU, advise ATC and follow OM E GNSS-interference guidance.
  - **Airspace-closure precedent:** Pakistan and India have, on prior occasions of heightened tension (including a 2025 episode), temporarily and reciprocally restricted or closed parts of their airspace to each other's/international traffic; a ceasefire-type situation can remain fragile with activity resuming on short notice. This is a **live operational-risk item, not a fixed structural fact** — re-check NOTAMs and operator routing policy immediately before any Karachi-FIR routing near the eastern boundary.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Extreme summer heat** — 🟥 Karachi regularly sees peak-season temperatures into the mid-to-high 40s °C; this is a **density-altitude / takeoff-performance planning driver** at OPKC (see the OPKC airport brief for field-specific detail) rather than an enroute hazard.
  - **SW monsoon (~Jun–Sep)** — 🟧 the monsoon reaches southern Pakistan with reduced intensity compared with India/Bangladesh, but can still produce convective activity and locally heavy rain in season.
  - **Dust/haze** — 🟧 seasonal dust storms and reduced visibility are a known feature of the Sindh/Baluchistan lowlands, particularly in the pre-monsoon hot season.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, current **ADC process**, the live **India–Pakistan border/airspace-closure and GNSS-interference picture** (EASA CZIB, safeairspace/OPSGROUP, state NOTAMs), the **Baluchistan/KP FL260 advisory** status, SIGMET/AIRMET, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
