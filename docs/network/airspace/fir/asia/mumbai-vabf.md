# Mumbai (VABF) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — western India plus a very large **Arabian Sea oceanic sector**, containing **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Intl (VABB/Mumbai)** · **Parent area brief:** [Asia](../../asia.md) · **Adjacent FIRs:** Karachi (OPKR) N/NW · Delhi (VIDF) N (land) · Chennai (VOMF) E/SE (land) · Colombo/Male-adjoining sector S · Muscat (OOMM) W (oceanic) · Sana'a (OYSC) W (oceanic) · Mogadishu (HCSM) SW (oceanic) · Seychelles (FSSS) S (oceanic) · Mauritius (FIMM) S (oceanic, distant) · Male (VRMF) SW (oceanic)
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Mumbai FIR has a **radar/ADS-B-controlled continental core** around the VABB terminal area, but it also owns an **enormous oceanic upper-airspace block over the Arabian Sea** reaching toward the Gulf, the Horn of Africa and the Indian Ocean island states — the largest oceanic responsibility of any Indian FIR. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances (radar continental / procedural-oceanic as applicable). Terminal procedures for VABB live in the airport briefing (§9). Live restrictions (NOTAMs, monsoon/cyclone bulletins) are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Mumbai FIR (VABF) covers **western India** (Maharashtra/Gujarat and the western coastal belt) plus a **vast Arabian Sea oceanic block** extending west/south-west toward the Gulf states, the Horn of Africa and the Indian Ocean island states — by far the largest lateral extent of the Indian FIRs, because Mumbai ACC carries oceanic responsibility on behalf of the region analogous to Chennai's Bay-of-Bengal block.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 **Mumbai ACC works the FIR surface-to-upper-limit** continental side; the oceanic sector's vertical/procedural structure is a distinct operating environment. Confirm the exact band split at **AIP ENR 2.1**. Cruise levels for hub transits sit in the RVSM band (FL290–410).
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N/NW — Karachi (OPKR, Pakistan CAA).** The principal north-western land/coastal boundary; a politically sensitive interface (see §10).
  - **N — Delhi FIR (VIDF, India/AAI).** Land boundary north; AAI's Delhi contingency sectorisation designates Mumbai as the responsible authority south of ATS route P628.
  - **E/SE — Chennai FIR (VOMF, India/AAI).** Land boundary east, toward the Deccan plateau and on to the Bay of Bengal side.
  - **W — Muscat ACC (Oman)** and **Sana'a FIR (Yemen)** — oceanic Arabian Sea boundaries confirmed in AAI's Mumbai contingency-plan adjacent-authority list.
  - **SW — Mogadishu FIR (Somalia)** — oceanic, per the same AAI contingency material.
  - **S — Seychelles FIR and Mauritius FIR** — far oceanic Indian Ocean boundaries, per the same source.
  - **SW — Male FIR (VRMF, Maldives)** — oceanic, toward the southern Indian Ocean.
  - **Colombo (VCCF, Sri Lanka)** interfaces the Mumbai oceanic sector's southern reaches alongside Chennai's; exact tri-point geometry not verified here — confirm at ENR.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 Mumbai ACC divides into continental sectors around VABB plus a distinct oceanic sector structure; exact geometry is AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC frequencies.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Lower- & upper-airspace ATSU:** 🟩 **Mumbai ACC** ("Mumbai Control", AAI) works the whole FIR surface-to-upper-limit continental side; no separate UAC. Approach/terminal control for VABB feeds off the ACC. The oceanic sector is worked as a distinct operating environment within the same ACC organisation.
- **FIS / FSS:** flight information service is provided by Mumbai ACC/OCC (Oceanic Control Centre) for the respective continental/oceanic portions.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 radar + ADS-B over the continental core (part of AAI's nationwide multi-radar/ADS-B mosaic feeding Auto Track-III automation); 🟧 the oceanic sector's radar/ADS-B extension offshore is not confirmed — plan on a procedural, HF/ADS-C basis over open Arabian Sea water unless current AIP data confirms otherwise.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Mumbai FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410 inclusive** — India-wide RVSM (implemented 2003). See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 **RNAV 5 (B-RNAV)** continental enroute; **RNP 1** terminal; **RNP APCH** arrivals (GAGAN SBAS-supported at VABB). 🟧 Oceanic-sector navigation basis (RNP 10/RNAV 10-equivalent, consistent with adjoining Gulf/East-Africa oceanic airspace) is not confirmed in AAI public material — verify before planning an oceanic Mumbai-FIR routing. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 **ADS-C/CPDLC confirmed in oceanic use** — AAI operational/contingency material cites the **VABF** AFTN/ADS-C logon address for the Mumbai oceanic sector, with **HF as the secondary/backup** means. Continental-sector CPDLC mandate scope is unconfirmed. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** required; **ADS-B Out mandated at/above FL290** (FL285 on named routes from Jun 2023) on designated continental PBN routes (DGCA/AAI AIP Supplement) — confirm applicability to any specific continental routing; oceanic-sector ADS-B coverage not confirmed.
- **8.33 kHz:** 🟧 not confirmed as an India-wide mandate; standard 25 kHz VHF spacing believed to apply — confirm at AIP GEN 3.4.
- **Oceanic procedures:** 🟥 HF, SLOP and oceanic contingency procedures apply on the Arabian Sea sector — see [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md) and [`OM E — ETOPS-EDTO Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/etops-edto.md) if the routing carries an extended-diversion-time segment.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a hub transit is worked by **Mumbai ACC ("Mumbai Control")** across its continental sectors and handed to the adjacent-FIR ACC (Karachi, Delhi, Chennai) at the land boundary, or to the **Mumbai Oceanic Control Centre** for the Arabian Sea sector; exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC/AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** Mumbai ACC/OCC provides flight information service in the FIR.
- **Language:** 🟩 **English** (ICAO standard).
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air; HF as required over the oceanic sector.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟧 continental Mumbai FIR is **fixed ATS-route / airway-structured** (part of India's ~210-route ATS network); the oceanic sector runs on established oceanic tracks/ATS routes rather than a European-style FRA. Confirm any flow-management programme at ENR/NOTAM.
- **Airway spine:** Mumbai sits on the **west-coast north–south spine** (Delhi–Mumbai–Chennai/Bengaluru corridor) and is the **gateway to the Arabian Sea oceanic network** toward the Gulf, East Africa and the Indian Ocean island states — a defining long-haul routing feature for K Global's western/African/Middle-Eastern reach.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** boundary entry/exit fixes feeding the VABB terminal area and the oceanic entry points toward Karachi/Muscat/Sana'a/Mogadishu/Seychelles/Mauritius/Male; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Intl (VABB, Mumbai)** — the sole K Global-served aerodrome in this FIR.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 **generally low near VABB** (coastal plain); the **Western Ghats** rise inland to the south-east but sit away from the immediate hub-transit corridor. Confirm any MORA relevance for routings that cut closer to the Ghats.
- **Boundary terrain:** not a cruise-level factor toward the oceanic sector (open water) or toward Delhi/Chennai FIRs (plains/plateau).
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟥 **the defining escape consideration in this FIR is the oceanic sector**, not terrain — long stretches of the Arabian Sea block have sparse diversion coverage; plan ETP/critical-point and driftdown analysis per OM E ETOPS-EDTO procedures for any qualifying oceanic segment.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not applicable — tropical/coastal, no cold-temperature terminal-correction driver.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO comms-failure procedure — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance/filed route and levels. 🟧 Confirm any Mumbai-specific refinement at **AIP ENR 1.8 / GEN**; oceanic comms-failure follows the applicable oceanic/TIBA procedure.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical continental (**request the deviation from ATC**); the **ICAO general weather-deviation procedure** (offset and level change with position broadcast) applies over the oceanic sector where radar/CPDLC contact is degraded. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **South-West monsoon (~Jun–Sep)** — 🟥 the defining seasonal hazard; heavy, sustained convective rain and reduced visibility at VABB, with embedded thunderstorms over the Arabian Sea sector. Significant terminal disruption is common.
  - **Arabian Sea tropical cyclones** — 🟧 pre-monsoon (Apr–Jun) and post-monsoon (Oct–Dec) cyclone windows affect the Arabian Sea oceanic sector and the western Indian coast; less frequent than Bay-of-Bengal activity but increasing in recent years — monitor IMD/JTWC bulletins in season.
  - **Coastal haze/humidity:** reduced visibility in the humid pre-monsoon build-up.
  - **Volcanic ash:** 🟩 low exposure on a normal VABB-hub routing; the wider region's VAAC coverage (Darwin for the Barren Island/Bay-of-Bengal source, and adjacent VAACs for any Gulf/East-African sources) should be checked per the specific oceanic routing flown.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, active danger/restricted-area status (Arabian Sea, India–Pakistan boundary), monsoon/cyclone SIGMET/AIRMET, applicable VAAC advisories, oceanic-sector NOTAMs, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
