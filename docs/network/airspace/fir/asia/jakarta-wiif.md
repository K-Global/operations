# Jakarta (WIIF) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — western Indonesia (Sumatra, Java, western Kalimantan, and — since the 21 Mar 2024 realignment — the Riau Islands and Natuna), containing **Soekarno-Hatta (WIII)** · **Parent area brief:** [Asia](../../asia.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Singapore (WSJC) N — realigned boundary, 21 Mar 2024 · Kuala Lumpur (WMFC, Malaysia) NW · Ujung Pandang (WAAF, Indonesia — Makassar ATS Centre) E · oceanic boundary toward Melbourne/Brisbane (Australia) S, at range
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — verified vs AirNav Indonesia AIP structure + ICAO APAC framework and the OPSGROUP realignment corroboration; exact ACC sector split flagged 🟧; 🟧 **realignment/boundary detail is comparatively recent (2024) — re-verify currency at planning**

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Jakarta is a **radar/ADS-B-controlled continental-plus-littoral FIR** covering western Indonesia — no oceanic procedural segment on a normal hub transit to WIII, though the FIR's outer reaches (toward the southern Indian Ocean and the Ujung Pandang boundary) are more sparsely served. The **21 Mar 2024 (AIRAC 2405) Singapore/Jakarta boundary realignment** — bringing the Riau Islands and Natuna into the Jakarta FIR — is a recent, structurally significant change; brief it explicitly. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical radar-ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for WIII live in the airport briefing (§9). Live restrictions (NOTAMs, volcanic-ash advisories, SIGMETs) are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Jakarta FIR (WIIF) covers **western Indonesia** — Sumatra, Java, western/central Kalimantan (Indonesian Borneo) — and, since the **21 March 2024 boundary realignment (AIRAC 2405)**, the **Riau Islands and Natuna** in the South China Sea, which previously sat within a delegated part of the Singapore FIR. The realignment expanded the Jakarta FIR by roughly **9.5%** to a published area of **2,842,725 km²**. Indonesia's second domestic FIR, **Ujung Pandang (WAAF)**, covers the remainder of the archipelago (Sulawesi, eastern islands) to the east and is worked separately from Makassar.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 **Jakarta ACC works the FIR surface-to-upper-limit**; no separately-branded upper-area control centre confirmed. The cruise band for hub transits sits in the RVSM upper airspace (FL290–410). Confirm the exact band split at **AIP ENR 2.1**.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Singapore (WSJC).** 🟧 **Realigned effective 21 Mar 2024**: the Riau/Natuna transfer moved this boundary north, and aircraft in the realigned sectors now receive ATS directly from AirNav Indonesia rather than via a Singapore-delegated arrangement. See the **[Singapore (WSJC) FIR brief](singapore-wsjc.md)** for the Singapore-side account of the same change.
  - **NW — Kuala Lumpur (WMFC, Malaysia).** Across the southern Malacca Strait — see the **[Kuala Lumpur (WMFC) FIR brief](kuala-lumpur-wmfc.md)**.
  - **E — Ujung Pandang (WAAF, Indonesia).** The internal Indonesian FIR boundary toward Sulawesi/eastern Indonesia, worked from Makassar.
  - **S — oceanic boundary toward Melbourne (YMMM) / Brisbane (YBBB), Australia.** 🟧 At long range over the Indian Ocean/southern approaches; not a factor on a normal WIII hub transit — noted for completeness of the published adjacency list.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 Jakarta ACC is divided into internal sectors (e.g. published "Sector A/B" designations referenced in route-charge material); the transit-relevant fact is the **single Jakarta ATS-Centre block** feeding the WIII terminal area. Exact sector geometry is AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC frequencies.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **Lower- & upper-airspace ATSU:** 🟩 **Jakarta ACC** (Jakarta Air Traffic Service Centre, AirNav Indonesia) works the whole FIR surface-to-upper-limit; no separate UAC confirmed. Approach/terminal control for WIII feeds off the ACC.
- **FIS / FSS:** flight information service is provided by Jakarta ACC for the FIR; not a factor for an IFR hub transit that stays in controlled airspace throughout.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟩 radar + ADS-B on the core Sumatra/Java/WIII network; 🟧 confirm the surveillance basis over the newly-incorporated Natuna/SCS sector and the southern oceanic fringe at ENR.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Jakarta FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410 inclusive** — ICAO APAC RVSM, applicable across Jakarta, Ujung Pandang, Kuala Lumpur and Singapore FIRs on a common regional basis. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟥 **RNAV / RNP** enroute; terminal RNP SID/STAR; **RNP APCH** at WIII. Indonesia has also been progressing **User-Preferred Routes (UPR)** and cross-FIR-boundary operations toward regional FRA readiness — 🟧 confirm current implementation status at ENR. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **Datalink (CPDLC):** 🟧 availability/mandate band not individually verified for the Jakarta FIR — confirm at ENR/NOTAM. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode S** transponder required; **ADS-B** surveillance across the main route network.
- **8.33 kHz:** not an APAC requirement; standard 25 kHz VHF spacing applies.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a hub transit is worked by **Jakarta ACC** across its sectors and handed to the adjacent-FIR ACC (Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Ujung Pandang) at the boundary; the exact sector split and frequencies are AIRAC-dependent — fly the current-AIRAC/AIP frequencies, not a stored table.
- **FIS / lower airspace:** Jakarta ACC provides flight information service in the FIR.
- **Language:** 🟩 **English** (ICAO standard); Indonesian also used domestically.
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟧 the Jakarta FIR is **fixed ATS-route / airway-structured**; Indonesia is on record progressing **UPR and cross-FIR-boundary trials** toward eventual regional FRA readiness — confirm current status at ENR/NOTAM before treating any direct-routing capability as standing policy.
- **Airway spine:** the FIR sits on the **southern SE-Asian trunk** — the corridor linking Singapore/Kuala Lumpur (N) with the Java/Sumatra network and onward toward the Indian Ocean and eastern-Indonesia (Ujung Pandang) flows. WIII is the principal terminal for this traffic.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the boundary entry/exit fixes feeding the WIII terminal area, including the newly-published Natuna-sector fixes following the 2024 realignment; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Soekarno-Hatta (WIII)** — the served K Global aerodrome. Singapore–Changi (WSSS) and Kuala Lumpur (WMKK) sit across the boundary in their respective FIRs — not this FIR.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 **the Barisan mountain range** runs the length of Sumatra along the FIR's western side, and **Java carries active volcanic terrain** (several peaks exceeding 3,000 m) — relevant grid-MORA on Sumatra/Java-transit routings; the WIII terminal area itself is low-lying coastal plain.
- **Boundary terrain:** no unusual high terrain immediately across the Singapore/Kuala Lumpur boundaries (N/NW) relevant to a WIII cruise transit; the Sumatra/Java terrain sits within this FIR itself, not across a boundary.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** 🟧 on Sumatra/Java-transit routings, confirm the route-specific grid-MORA against the driftdown ceiling; on a direct WIII hub transit the diversion-field density around Java mitigates this. Not built route-by-route here.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not applicable — equatorial, no cold-temperature terminal correction driver.

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **Military / danger areas:** 🟧 danger, restricted and military areas exist across the archipelago (Sumatra/Java approaches, the Natuna/SCS sector); activation is **AIP/NOTAM-published** and can affect the trunk routes. Confirm active areas at planning.
- **Volcanic-exclusion zones:** 🟥 the **Indonesian volcanic arc (Sumatra/Java)** is among the most active on Earth; eruption-driven airspace closures and ash-avoidance zones are a recurring, durable feature, not an edge case. **VAAC Darwin** is the responsible advisory centre for the region.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 Indonesia has been noted (in the context of the 2024 realignment) as actively enforcing overflight-permit requirements in the Riau/Natuna sector — confirm current permit requirements for the operator at planning.
- **Restricted/prohibited pockets:** named areas and status are AIP/NOTAM-published — pull live at planning.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** ICAO comms-failure procedure — squawk **7600**, continue per the last acknowledged clearance / filed route and levels, and attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any Indonesian refinement at **AIP ENR 1.8 / GEN**.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical — request the deviation from ATC and comply with the vector/level issued. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Equatorial convection year-round** — deep CB and embedded thunderstorms over Sumatra/Java and the surrounding seas; the dominant enroute/terminal hazard, handled tactically.
  - **Monsoon seasons** — **wet/NW monsoon (~Nov–Mar)** and **dry/SE monsoon (~May–Sep)**, with heavy rain and reduced visibility at WIII particularly in the wet season.
  - **Volcanic ash** — 🟥 recurring eruptions across the Sumatra/Java arc can close WIII and the surrounding airways with little notice; **VAAC Darwin** issues the advisories — pull VA advisories at planning. See [`OM E — Volcanic Ash Avoidance`](../../../../flight-ops/volcanic-ash-avoidance.md).
  - **Haze** — trans-boundary/local biomass-burning haze (chiefly the drier mid-year period) reduces visibility across Sumatra and the Strait-facing sectors.
  - **Typhoons:** 🟩 Indonesia sits near the equator, south of the main tropical-cyclone track — direct typhoon strikes are not a feature of this FIR.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, active danger/restricted-area and volcanic-exclusion-zone status, SIGMET/AIRMET (convection, haze), **VAAC Darwin** volcanic-ash advisories, current overflight-permit requirements, and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels.
