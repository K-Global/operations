## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Accra FIR (**DGAC**) covers the airspace over **Ghana, Togo and Benin**, plus a large sector over the **Atlantic Ocean in the Gulf of Guinea**. It is one of the few African FIRs whose lateral extent spans three sovereign states under a single controlling authority (GCAA, Ghana).
- 🟧 **Togo-corridor anomaly:** public sourcing (IVAO documentation, tier-4 corroboration only) indicates **Togo retains ATS responsibility for the corridor between Lomé and Niamtougou, up to FL260**, inside the otherwise GCAA-controlled DGAC FIR — a legacy of Togo's ASECNA membership overlapping with Ghana's FIR-level delegation. Treat as a **genuine complexity, not an error**: confirm current-AIRAC handling of this corridor before overflight planning through Togo.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 lower controlled airspace (terminal CTAs/CTRs and the airway network) beneath the RVSM upper band; exact lower/upper handoff level is AIP-defined — confirm at **Ghana AIP ENR 2.1**. RVSM applies **FL290–FL410**.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Niamey (DRRR, ASECNA/Niger–Burkina Faso–Mali sector).** Boundary toward the Sahel; see the Niamey FIR brief for the standing 🟥 security caution beyond this boundary.
  - **W — Abidjan (DIII, ASECNA/Côte d'Ivoire).** DGAC does **not** border Roberts FIR directly — Abidjan (Côte d'Ivoire) sits between Ghana and the Roberts FIR states (Liberia/Sierra Leone/Guinea).
  - **E — Kano (DNKK, Nigeria).**
  - **S — Gulf of Guinea oceanic**, contained **within DGAC itself** rather than handed to a separate oceanic FIR.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 internal ACC sector boundaries (Accra vs. any Togo/Benin sub-sectorisation) are AIP/AIRAC-dependent — fly the current sectorisation, not a stored one.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **ATSU:** 🟩 **Ghana Civil Aviation Authority (GCAA)** — Accra ACC — is the ANS provider for the DGAC FIR and also Ghana's safety regulator (a combined regulator/ANSP model common among non-ASECNA West African states). 🟧 The Togo corridor exception (§3) means a small internal segment may be worked under Togolese/ASECNA arrangements — confirm live.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟧 radar/surveillance is expected around **Accra (DGAA)**; coverage over Kumasi, the Togo/Benin sectors and the Gulf-of-Guinea oceanic reach is not confirmed and should be treated as procedural until verified.
- **FIS:** flight information / procedural service provided by GCAA outside radar cover.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **Ghana has FRA available FL290–FL460 in the DGAC/Accra FIR, between latitudes 2°N and 11°N** — direct planning between defined points within that band and latitude range. Confirm the current FRA point set / conditions at planning.
- **Airway spine (where FRA not available / below FL290):** the fixed ATS-route network feeds Accra, Kumasi, Lomé and Cotonou and connects to the Niamey, Abidjan and Kano boundaries.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the FRA/airway boundary fixes toward the three adjacent continental FIRs and the Gulf-of-Guinea oceanic sector; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Accra (DGAA)**, **Kumasi (DGSI)** in Ghana; **Lomé (DXXX)** in Togo; **Cotonou (DBBB)** in Benin. Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 the FIR is predominantly low coastal plain and savanna. The only notable relief is the **Akwapim–Togo Ranges** astride the Ghana–Togo border (Mount Agou, Togo's highest point, ≈986 m / ≈3,235 ft) — modest and not a cruise-level factor.
- **Boundary terrain:** none of operational significance toward the adjacent FIRs.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** not a constraint over most of the FIR; the coastal diversion spread (Accra, Lomé, Cotonou) covers most routings.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not a factor — tropical climate.
