# Tripoli (HLLL) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the **whole of Libya** (HLLL is the country's **only** FIR), spanning the Mediterranean coastal strip and the Libyan Sahara · **Parent area brief:** [Africa](../../africa.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Tunis (DTTC) W · Algiers (DAAA) far SW (via the long desert border) 🟧 · Cairo (HECC) E · Niamey (DRRR) / ASECNA structure S (Niger border) 🟧 · N'Djamena (FTTT) / ASECNA structure SE (Chad border) 🟧 · Malta/Valletta and Rome-area Mediterranean FIRs N 🟧 confirm exact split

**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — 🟥 **standing conflict-zone caution — factual, no politics.** FIR ident/ANSP/route geometry confirmed against public EASA/Safe Airspace/ICAO sources; the security and NOTAM picture is dynamic and **must be re-verified before every operation** — nothing in this brief substitutes for the current NOTAM/advisory check at planning (§11).

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. **Libyan airspace is under standing international conflict-zone advisories** and is treated by most operators as **avoid/no-fly below FL320 except a narrow set of published corridors** (§7/§10). **Tripoli International Airport (HLLT) has been closed since 2014**; **Mitiga (HLLM)** has been the operating airport serving Tripoli since 2018. This brief states the durable, factual geometry — airspace-management degradation, published transit corridors, GNSS-interference reporting and the airport-closure fact — without characterising the underlying conflict. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route, comply with tactical ATC clearances where any exist, and **treat every live NOTAM/advisory check as mandatory, not optional**, for this FIR. Live restrictions are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Tripoli FIR (**HLLL**) is coterminous with **Libya** — the country's **only** FIR, from the Mediterranean coast in the north across the Libyan Sahara to the southern desert borders.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 nominal lower/upper structure exists on paper; in practice the operative vertical constraint is the **conflict-zone floor of FL320** on the permitted transit corridors (§7) — this supersedes any routine lower/upper AIP split for planning purposes.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **W — Tunis (DTTC, Tunisia/OACA)** — see the [Tunis (DTTC) FIR brief](tunis-dttc.md).
  - **SW — Algiers (DAAA, Algeria/ENNA)**, via the long desert border 🟧 — see the [Algiers (DAAA) FIR brief](algiers-daaa.md).
  - **E — Cairo (HECC, Egypt/NANSC)** — see the [Cairo (HECC) FIR brief](cairo-hecc.md).
  - **S — the ASECNA structure (Niamey DRRR, Niger border)** 🟧 — one of the permitted transit-corridor NOTAMs (§7) has historically originated from the Niamey/Dakar side.
  - **SE — the ASECNA structure (N'Djamena FTTT, Chad border)** 🟧.
  - **N — Malta/Valletta and Rome-area Mediterranean FIRs** 🟧 confirm exact maritime split.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 not meaningfully verifiable given current conditions — do not assume a routine ACC sector structure is being actively worked.

- **ATSU (nominal):** **Tripoli ACC**, under **LYCAA (Libyan Civil Aviation Authority)**.

- **FIS:** 🟧 not meaningfully verifiable — do not plan on a functioning flight-information service being available throughout this FIR.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Standing transit regime (durable, re-check live):** 🟥 operators are advised **not to operate within Libyan airspace (HLLL) below FL320**, except for operations to coastal airports under strict conditions. **Above FL320**, transit is permitted only via published **bi-directional route segments**:
  - **M999 (ZARZAITINE–SEBHA)**
  - **G655/M214 (SEBHA–GARIN)**
  - 🟧 a **second permitted corridor** has been referenced in more recent (2026) national airspace-warning updates in addition to the original two — **confirm the current, complete corridor set** at planning; do not rely on this list as exhaustive.
  - Some states' route-segment guidance additionally references **R778 (DITAR–KUFRA–TONBA)** at FL320 and above — 🟧 confirm current applicability.
- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** not applicable — this FIR operates under the conflict-zone transit regime above, not a routine FRA/airway structure.
- **Airway spine (below the advisory floor):** 🟥 **do not plan operations below FL320** except to a coastal airport under the specific conditions referenced above — this is not a routine "airway spine" briefing item for this FIR.
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟥 **Tripoli Intl (HLLT) has been closed since 2014**; **Mitiga (HLLM)** has served as Tripoli's operating international gateway since 2018 (§9). Terminal detail for HLLT is nonetheless in the airport briefing per network requirement (§9), but the operational fact of the closure and the HLLM substitution is the material planning point.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 Libya is predominantly **low coastal plain and desert plateau/hamada**, with modest upland areas (e.g. the Jebel Nafusa south of Tripoli, and the Jebel Akhdar in the east) — not a significant driftdown factor relative to the conflict-zone constraints above.
- **Boundary terrain:** no major terrain issue at the adjacent-FIR boundaries.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** secondary to the §10 go/no-go decision; if operating in this FIR at all, escape planning must additionally account for the **restricted, narrow-corridor routing** — there is no "off corridor" escape option below FL320.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not generally a factor — arid/Mediterranean-desert climate.

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **🟥 Standing conflict-zone advisory (durable geometry — re-check live every time):**
  - **EASA CZIB-2017-02R20** — do not operate in Libyan airspace below FL320 except to coastal airports under strict conditions; this advisory has been **extended to 31 JANUARY 2027**.
  - **Total operating bans** are in force for some states' operators (reported for the US and UK, among others); other states maintain varying levels of restriction and warning.
  - **GNSS interference** — Libya has shown a **documented mix of jamming and spoofing** linked to regional conflict/military activity; treat GPS-position integrity as suspect throughout this FIR.
  - **Degraded air navigation service / NOTAM reliability** — airspace management and ANS may be unavailable or degraded; the **absence of timely or reliable aeronautical information (NOTAMs)** is itself flagged as an operational risk factor by independent risk assessors.
  - **Assessment:** independent conflict-zone risk databases assess Libyan airspace overall as **high risk**, with guidance to **avoid the FIR entirely** outside the narrow permitted-corridor exception.
- **Airport-closure fact:** 🟥 **HLLT (Tripoli Intl) has been closed since 14 JUL 2014** (damage sustained during 2014 conflict); **HLLM (Mitiga)** has been the sole international gateway serving Tripoli since 2018 — factual, operational status, not a political characterisation.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟥 given the standing advisory, **overflight of Libyan airspace should be treated as a go/no-go decision at the dispatch/operational-control level**, not a routine permit-lead-time item — confirm current company policy and the live advisory status before any routing that touches HLLL.

> **SUA / security currency:** this is the **single most dynamic item in the FIR brief set** — re-check the current EASA CZIB status, Safe Airspace/OPSGROUP bulletins, state NOTAMs and company routing policy **before every operation** that could touch Libyan airspace. This document is durable factual context only, **never** a substitute for the live check.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** squawk **7600**; given the communications-reliability caveats above, do **not** assume a routine comms-failure procedure will be met with ATC response — maintain the broadcast/lights/ACAS discipline per ICAO Doc 4444 general contingency procedure and prioritise clearing the area/corridor.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** treat as **non-radar/procedural-at-best**; apply the ICAO Doc 4444 general contingency and weather-deviation procedure (offset, level change, broadcast intentions) — there is no assured tactical ATC backstop in this FIR.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Ghibli** — hot, dust-laden southerly wind off the Sahara, reducing visibility, most disruptive in spring.
  - **Security wildcard** — the standing conflict-zone advisory (§10) is the dominant and overriding planning factor for this FIR, above any routine seasonal-weather consideration.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h) — mandatory, not optional for this FIR:** the current **EASA CZIB** status and validity, **Safe Airspace / OPSGROUP** conflict-zone bulletins, state/company NOTAMs (including any GNSS-interference NOTAMs), the **complete current permitted-corridor set** (§7), and company operational-control's go/no-go decision. This brief does not authorise operation into HLLL — it is background only.
