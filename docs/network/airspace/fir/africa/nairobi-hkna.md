# Nairobi (HKNA) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the **whole of Kenya**, including the **Nairobi (HKJK) hub** and **Mombasa (HKMO)** on the coast · **Parent area brief:** [Africa](../../africa.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Khartoum (HSSS) N · Addis Ababa (HAAA) N · Mogadishu (HCSM) E · Seychelles (FSSS) E/SE (oceanic) · Dar es Salaam (HTDC) S · Entebbe (HUEC) W
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — provisional; FIR ident/RVSM/authority verified against public sources, but exact ACC sector split, class letters per band and live radar serviceability are flagged 🟧

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Nairobi FIR is a **mixed radar/procedural continental FIR** in line with ICAO AFI/ESAF practice — radar/surveillance around the Nairobi terminal area, more limited elsewhere. Nairobi sits at the **East-Africa crossroads**: traffic between Southern Africa, the Gulf/Middle East, South Asia and the Horn of Africa converges over this FIR, and **HKJK is a high-elevation hub** requiring performance planning. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for aerodromes inside the FIR live in the airport briefings (§9). Live restrictions — NOTAMs and the Horn-of-Africa/Somalia security picture on the eastern boundary — are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Nairobi FIR (**HKNA**) is coterminous with **Kenya** — it is the country's single FIR, extending from the Kenya/Somalia and Kenya/Ethiopia territorial boundaries in the north/east, along the Indian Ocean coast in the southeast, and along the Kenya/Tanzania and Kenya/Uganda land boundaries in the south/west. 🟧 Precise coordinate-defined boundary points are AIP-published — confirm at **Kenya AIP ENR 2.1**.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 lower controlled airspace (terminal CTAs/CTRs and the airway network) beneath the RVSM upper band; the lower/upper handoff level is AIP-defined — confirm at **ENR 2.1**. RVSM applies **FL290–FL410**.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Khartoum (HSSS, Sudan)** and **Addis Ababa (HAAA, Ethiopia)** — the northern boundary; touches the Sudan conflict-zone and periodic-Ethiopia risk pictures owned by those FIRs' briefs.
  - **E — Mogadishu (HCSM, Somalia)** — the FIR's most security-sensitive boundary (§10).
  - **E/SE — Seychelles (FSSS)** — oceanic hand-off over the Indian Ocean.
  - **S — Dar es Salaam (HTDC, Tanzania)**.
  - **W — Entebbe (HUEC, Uganda)**.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 internal KCAA sector boundaries (Nairobi ACC sectorisation) are AIP/AIRAC-dependent — fly the current sectorisation, not a stored one. Public references note **multiple control zones** within the FIR (e.g. around HKJK, Wilson, Mombasa, Kisumu, Eldoret) — treat the exact count/extent as 🟧 pending AIP confirmation.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **ATSU:** 🟩 **KCAA — Kenya Civil Aviation Authority**, both the ANSP and safety regulator for the single Nairobi FIR.
- **Upper-airspace ATSU:** 🟧 no separate upper unit is asserted here — confirm whether Nairobi ACC works the full vertical range or splits with a distinct upper sector at the current AIP.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟧 radar/surveillance concentrated around the **Nairobi terminal area (HKJK/Wilson)**; **Mombasa (HKMO)** likely has terminal radar/approach surveillance — confirm. Much of the en-route FIR, especially north toward Sudan/Ethiopia and east toward Somalia, is expected to be **procedural** per AFI/ESAF norms — do not assume continuous surveillance.
- **FIS:** flight information / procedural service provided by KCAA outside radar cover; the **AFI IFBP** (listening watch on **126.9 MHz**) supports separation awareness where VHF/radar is thin.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Nairobi FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — **ICAO AFI RVSM**; an **RVSM approval** from the State of Registry/Operator is required. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟧 **RNAV 5** enroute where the network is PBN-based; terminal RNP where published at HKJK/HKMO — confirm the current Kenya AIP. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟩 **not an AFI requirement** — standard 25 kHz VHF applies.
- **Datalink (CPDLC / ADS-C):** 🟧 **not a mature FIR-wide capability** in HKNA. Where VHF/radar is limited the **AFI IFBP** (listening watch on **126.9 MHz**) is the mitigation, **not** a FANS CPDLC service. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode C/S transponder required.** 🟧 Any ADS-B Out requirement is not asserted here — confirm the current Kenya AIP/NOTAM at planning.
- **High-elevation performance:** 🟥 **HKJK Nairobi (5,330 ft)** and **Wilson** require high-elevation/high-density-altitude performance planning (reduced climb gradient, longer take-off/landing distances, potential weight restriction on hot days) — see the HKJK airport brief and [`OM E — Cold Weather Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/cold-weather-operations.md) for the companion high-elevation/temperature-correction topics.

- **Airway spine (where FRA not available):** the fixed ATS-route network feeds Nairobi (HKJK) and Mombasa (HKMO) and connects to the Khartoum, Addis Ababa, Mogadishu, Dar es Salaam and Entebbe boundaries.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the airway boundary fixes toward the five adjacent FIRs and the Seychelles oceanic hand-off; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Nairobi — Jomo Kenyatta Intl (HKJK)**, the East-Africa hub; **Mombasa — Moi Intl (HKMO)** on the coast; plus Wilson (Nairobi), Kisumu, Eldoret and other domestic fields. Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 Kenya's terrain is dominated by the **Great Rift Valley** and its highland shoulders. **Mount Kenya (~5,199 m / 17,057 ft)** is the principal high point, north-east of Nairobi. Nairobi itself sits on a high plateau — **HKJK at 5,330 ft** — while the coastal strip around Mombasa (HKMO) is low.
- **Boundary terrain:** 🟧 further high ground continues north into the **Ethiopian highlands** (Addis Ababa FIR) and west toward the East-African Rift shoulders shared with Uganda/Tanzania — relevant to driftdown/escape planning on routings toward those boundaries, not for a Nairobi–Mombasa coastal transit.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** relevant for routings through the Rift Valley/Mount Kenya high ground; the coastal corridor to Mombasa is comparatively low. National diversion spread (HKJK/HKMO) supports most routings.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** 🟧 relevant at the high-elevation terminals (HKJK, Wilson) — see the airport briefs and [`OM E — Cold Weather Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/cold-weather-operations.md); not a factor at the coastal Mombasa terminal.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** squawk **7600**; continue per the ICAO/Kenya AIP IFR comms-failure rules (last clearance / filed route and levels), attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency, 121.5 and the **126.9 MHz IFBP**. 🟧 Confirm the Kenya refinement at AIP ENR 1.
- **Procedural / weather-deviation contingency:** in non-radar (procedural) airspace, apply the AFI/ICAO weather-deviation and contingency procedures — broadcast intentions on the IFBP/HF, offset per the standard technique, and coordinate as able. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Long/short rains (ITCZ migration, roughly Mar–May and Oct–Dec)** — convective activity and embedded CB, particularly over the highlands and Lake Victoria basin.
  - **Harmattan-adjacent dust/haze** is less pronounced this far south/east than in West Africa, but haze and reduced visibility can affect the plateau in the dry season.
  - **Security wildcard** — the Somalia border and the Sudan/periodic-Ethiopia picture are the strategic risk that would reshape a routing (§10).

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, Somalia/Sudan/Ethiopia-boundary conflict-zone advisories (Safe Airspace / EASA CZIB / state advisories), SIGMET/AIRMET (ITCZ convection), and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
