# Niamey (DRRR) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the **ASECNA Niamey FIR** covering **Niger, Burkina Faso and an eastern Mali sector**, containing the **Diori Hamani (DRRN, Niamey) hub** plus Ouagadougou (DFFD) and Bamako (GABS) · **Parent area brief:** [Africa](../../africa.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Dakar (GOOO, ASECNA) W (shares the Mali sector) · Kano (DNKK) SE · Accra (DGAC) S · N'Djamena (FTTT, ASECNA) E · Algiers (DAAA) N (reciprocal Mali–Algeria closure, §10)
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — provisional; the ASECNA authority, RVSM/FRA facts and the Sahel security picture are corroborated against public ASECNA/OPS/Safe-Airspace sources current to mid-2026, but exact class letters, sector split and live radar serviceability are flagged 🟧

> ⚠️ **SAHEL STANDING CAUTION (🟥 — factual, re-check every operation):** the Niamey FIR sits at the centre of the **Sahel conflict/instability belt**. Niger, Burkina Faso and Mali all underwent military transitions (2020–2023), formed the **Alliance of Sahel States (AES)** and withdrew from ECOWAS (effective 29 Jan 2025), and Western military forces have fully withdrawn from Niger (last US personnel departed Oct 2025). Multiple states maintain **active altitude-floor warnings over the Mali sector** (FL320/FL260/25,000 ft AGL depending on state) and advise **against using Bamako (GABS) as a destination or alternate**. **GNSS jamming/spoofing is a recognised global aviation concern (ICAO/ITU/IMO joint advisory, March 2025)**; no Sahel-specific public NOTAM confirming GNSS interference in this FIR was found at brief-build time — treat as a plausible, unconfirmed risk and verify current NOTAMs/advisories before every operation, not just once.

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Niamey FIR is a **vast, largely procedural FIR** with limited radar coverage, per public risk-monitoring sources. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for aerodromes inside the FIR live in the airport briefings (§9). Live restrictions — NOTAMs, state security advisories and the fast-moving Sahel picture — are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Niamey FIR (**DRRR**) is one of ASECNA's six FIRs, covering **Niger** in full, **Burkina Faso** (worked via the **Ouagadougou UTA**, an ASECNA upper-airspace segment) and an **eastern sector of Mali**. Public risk reporting confirms **"ATS in Mali is provided by the GOOO/Dakar and DRRR/Niamey FIRs"** — i.e. Mali is split between the two FIRs, with Niamey covering the eastern portion including Bamako's approaches from the east.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 lower/upper split is AIP-defined — confirm at ASECNA/Niger AIP ENR 2.1. RVSM applies **FL290–FL410**. A long-standing restriction confines Mali overflights to **published ATS routes at FL320 and above** in places (§10) — this is a route/level constraint layered on top of the normal vertical structure.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **W — Dakar (GOOO, ASECNA/Senegal).** Shares the Mali sector; the two FIRs jointly provide ATS over Mali.
  - **SE — Kano (DNKK, Nigeria).** Sahel/Sahara-to-Nigeria boundary.
  - **S — Accra (DGAC, Ghana/Togo/Benin).** Boundary toward the Gulf-of-Guinea coastal states.
  - **E — N'Djamena (FTTT, ASECNA/Chad).**
  - **N — Algiers (DAAA, Algeria).** 🟧 Currently subject to a **reciprocal airspace closure** between Mali and Algeria (see §10) — not a routine transit boundary at present.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 internal ACC sector boundaries (Niamey/Ouagadougou/Bamako-east) are AIP/AIRAC-dependent — fly the current sectorisation, not a stored one.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **ATSU:** 🟩 **ASECNA** — Niamey ACC (based at Diori Hamani Intl) — provides ANS for the DRRR FIR; ASECNA also serves Burkina Faso (Ouagadougou UTA) and Mali as member states within the shared FIR structure. Niger, Burkina Faso and Mali are all ASECNA members.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟧 public risk-monitoring sources describe the FIR as having **limited radar coverage** with **staffing and maintenance impacted by political instability and international sanctions** since the 2023 Niger coup. Treat surveillance as **not guaranteed** and confirm live before relying on it.
- **FIS:** flight information / procedural service provided by ASECNA outside any confirmed radar cover; the general AFI **In-Flight Broadcast Procedure (IFBP, listening watch 126.9 MHz)** applies as the regional mitigation where VHF/radar is thin (per ICAO Doc 7030, AFI-wide).

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Niamey FIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — **ICAO AFI RVSM**; an RVSM approval from the State of Registry/Operator is required. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟧 **RNAV 5** enroute presumed (AFI PBN Implementation Plan); confirm the current ASECNA/Niger AIP. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟩 **not an AFI requirement** — standard 25 kHz VHF applies.
- **Datalink (CPDLC / ADS-C):** 🟧 not confirmed as a mature FIR-wide capability. Where VHF/radar is limited, the **AFI IFBP (126.9 MHz listening watch)** is the regional mitigation — not a FANS CPDLC service. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode C/S transponder required.** 🟧 Any ADS-B Out mandate is not asserted here — confirm the current AIP/NOTAM at planning. 🟧 **GNSS integrity note:** given the recognised global rise in GNSS jamming/spoofing, confirm current NOTAMs for any reported Sahel-specific interference before relying on GNSS-based navigation or approaches.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a transit is worked by **Niamey ACC**, with Ouagadougou UTA and Bamako-east sector handoffs depending on routing; exact sector frequencies are AIRAC/AIP-dependent — fly the current AIP frequencies.
- **HF & remote:** 🟧 **HF** and the **AFI IFBP (126.9 MHz listening watch)** cover the segments beyond VHF/radar — maintain broadcast discipline where controllers cannot see you, especially given the FIR's confirmed limited radar coverage.
- **Language:** 🟩 **English** (ICAO) for ATC; ASECNA's internal working language is French.
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air; **126.9 MHz** for AFI IFBP.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟩 **FL250 and above**, implemented across the **Niamey UTA, Ouagadougou UTA and Bamako UTA** as part of ASECNA's region-wide FRA roll-out (effective 25 Jan 2024) — direct planning between the published reporting points of each UTA's boundary. Confirm the current point set (ASECNA AIP ENR 3.5) at planning.
- **Airway spine (where FRA not available / below FL250):** the fixed ATS-route network feeds Niamey, Ouagadougou and Bamako and connects to the Dakar, Kano, Accra and N'Djamena boundaries. 🟥 **Mali overflights are additionally constrained to published ATS routes at FL320 and above** per the long-standing restriction referenced by multiple state advisories (§10) — this applies on top of normal route planning.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the FRA/airway boundary fixes toward the four adjacent continental FIRs; the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Niamey — Diori Hamani (DRRN)** in Niger; **Ouagadougou (DFFD)** in Burkina Faso; **Bamako — Modibo Keïta (GABS)** in Mali (🟥 see the alternate-use caution at §9/§10). Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 the FIR is predominantly flat Sahelian/Sahara-fringe terrain, but the **Aïr Massif** in north-central Niger rises to **≈2,022 m (≈6,634 ft, Mont Idoukal-n-Taghès)** — a genuine high belt, though remote from the principal Niamey–Ouagadougou–Bamako corridor. Burkina Faso and the Mali sector are generally low plateau (≈300–750 m).
- **Boundary terrain:** none of operational significance toward the adjacent FIRs beyond the Aïr Massif's remoteness from the main traffic flow.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** not a cruise-level constraint over the main corridor; relevant only to routings that pass near the Aïr Massif in the far north.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not a factor — arid/tropical climate; extreme heat is a more relevant surface-performance consideration for the airports (see airport briefs).

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **Military / danger / restricted areas:** 🟧 danger and restricted areas are AIP/NOTAM-published across the FIR — pull the active list at planning; expect a higher density of NOTAM-restricted military activity than in the coastal West African FIRs.
- **Conflict-zone / security exposure:** 🟥 **the Sahel tri-border area (Niger/Burkina Faso/Mali) carries active insurgent and state-security risk.** Since April 2026, coordinated armed-group attacks have been reported nationwide in Mali including near Bamako; France, Canada, the UK, Italy, Germany, the USA and EASA all maintain **current airspace warnings** recommending altitude floors of **FL320 / FL260 / 25,000 ft AGL** (varying by state) over the Mali portions of the GOOO/Dakar and DRRR/Niamey FIRs, with several states specifically advising **against Bamako (GABS)** as a destination or alternate. Niger itself underwent a July 2023 military takeover; Western (French/US) military forces have **fully withdrawn** (last US personnel departed Niger, October 2025), reducing counter-terrorism capability in border regions. Burkina Faso and Niger have also had **airspace-sovereignty friction** with neighbours since the January 2025 ECOWAS withdrawal and formation of the **Alliance of Sahel States (AES)** — a December 2025 incident saw the AES states threaten action after a foreign military aircraft reportedly lacked overflight permission over Burkina Faso.
- 🟧 **Mali–Algeria reciprocal closure:** since April 2025, **Mali has closed its airspace to Algerian-registered aircraft and to flights to/from Algerian aerodromes**, and **Algeria has reciprocally closed its airspace to Malian-registered aircraft and Mali-bound/originating traffic** — relevant if routing near the DRRR/DAAA boundary.
- 🟧 **GNSS interference:** the ICAO/ITU/IMO issued a joint "grave concern" advisory on global GNSS jamming/spoofing (March 2025); **no Sahel-specific public NOTAM or advisory confirming GNSS interference in DRRR was found** at brief-build time. Given the FIR's conflict exposure and the global trend, treat this as a **plausible but unconfirmed** risk and check current NOTAMs specifically, rather than assuming either way.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟥 overflight/landing permits for Niger, Burkina Faso and Mali carry **elevated diplomatic sensitivity** post-AES/ECOWAS split — confirm current requirements and do not assume historic ECOWAS-era arrangements still apply.

> **SUA / security currency:** the Sahel conflict, AES/ECOWAS, and Mali-Algeria pictures are all **highly dynamic — re-check live every time** (Safe Airspace / state advisories) before any operation touching this FIR; this list is durable context, not a live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** squawk **7600**; continue per ICAO/AFI comms-failure procedure (last clearance / filed route and levels), attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency, 121.5 and the **126.9 MHz IFBP**. 🟧 Confirm any ASECNA/Niger-specific refinement at the current AIP.
- **Procedural / weather-deviation contingency:** in this largely **non-radar (procedural)** FIR, apply the AFI/ICAO weather-deviation and contingency procedures — broadcast intentions on the IFBP/HF, offset per the standard technique. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Harmattan (≈ Nov–Mar)** — Saharan dust haze, a significant terminal-visibility and diversion driver across the Sahel band.
  - **Wet-season convection (≈ Jun–Sep)** — organised squall lines and embedded CB, particularly in the afternoon/evening.
  - **Extreme heat** — a surface-performance factor at Niamey, Ouagadougou and Bamako (see airport briefs).
  - **Security wildcard** — the Sahel conflict/instability picture is the dominant strategic risk factor for this FIR and would materially reshape any routing (§10).

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, **Safe Airspace / state conflict-zone advisories for the Mali sector and the wider Sahel**, the Mali-Algeria reciprocal-closure status, any GNSS-interference NOTAMs, SIGMET/AIRMET (harmattan dust, convection), the current **FRA point set** (Niamey/Ouagadougou/Bamako UTAs), and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
