# Cairo (HECC) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the **whole of Egypt** (HECC is the country's **only** FIR/UIR), spanning the Nile Valley, the Western/Eastern Deserts, the Sinai Peninsula and the Red Sea coast, containing the **Cairo (HECA) hub** and the Red Sea Riviera destinations · **Parent area brief:** [Africa](../../africa.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Tripoli (HLLL) W · Khartoum (HSSS) S 🟥 conflict-affected, see note below · Jeddah (OEJD, Saudi Arabia) E across the Red Sea 🟧 · Nicosia (LCCC, Cyprus) and Athinai (LGGG, Greece) N across the Mediterranean 🟧 confirm exact split · Tel Aviv (LLLL) / Amman (OJAC) NE 🟧 confirm exact split
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — provisional; FIR ident and ANSP confirmed against public ICAO-MID/NANSC sources, but exact RVSM band nuance, class letters, ACC sector split and the northern/eastern maritime boundaries are flagged 🟧

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Cairo FIR sits at the **AFI / MID regional seam** — Egypt's air-navigation service is aligned with the **ICAO Middle East (MID) RVSM programme** (in force since 2003, earlier than the AFI-wide 2008 rollout) even though the state sits on the African continent; the K Global Africa area brief (§3) lists it among the continental FIRs for network-planning purposes. Cairo ACC runs a busy, largely **radar-capable** FIR around the Nile Valley/Delta and the Red Sea coast. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for aerodromes inside the FIR live in the airport briefings (§9). Live restrictions — including the adjacent Sudan/Red Sea security picture — are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Cairo FIR (**HECC**) is coterminous with **Egypt** — the country's **only** FIR/UIR, covering the Nile Valley and Delta, the Western and Eastern Deserts, the Sinai Peninsula, and the Red Sea and Mediterranean coasts.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 lower controlled airspace (terminal CTAs/CTRs and the airway network) beneath the RVSM upper band; the exact lower/upper handoff level is AIP-defined — confirm at **ENR 2.1**. RVSM applies **FL290–FL410**, in force since **27 NOV 2003** under the ICAO MID-region programme.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **W — Tripoli (HLLL, Libya/LYCAA)** — see the **[Tripoli (HLLL) FIR brief](tripoli-hlll.md)** for the standing conflict-zone caution on that side.
  - **S — Khartoum (HSSS, Sudan)** — 🟥 **active conflict zone**, largely closed to civil traffic since the April-2023 conflict (see the Africa area brief §9 for durable geometry); treat the Egypt/Sudan boundary as a hard planning constraint, not a routine handoff.
  - **E — Jeddah (OEJD, Saudi Arabia)** across the Red Sea 🟧 confirm exact split; see the Middle East area brief for the Red Sea/Gulf corridor context.
  - **NE — Tel Aviv (LLLL) / Amman (OJAC)** 🟧 confirm exact split.
  - **N — Nicosia (LCCC, Cyprus) and Athinai (LGGG, Greece)** across the Mediterranean 🟧 confirm exact maritime hand-off.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 **Cairo ACC** is the single ATSU for the FIR; internal sector detail (Delta/Valley vs Sinai/Red Sea vs desert sectors) is AIP/AIRAC-dependent — fly the current sectorisation, not a stored one.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **ATSU:** 🟩 **Cairo ACC**, operated by **NANSC (National Air Navigation Services Company)**; **ECAA** is the safety/economic regulator. NANSC works the single HECC FIR.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟧 radar/surveillance around **Cairo** and the Nile Valley/Delta, plus the Red Sea coastal terminals (Hurghada, Marsa Alam, Sharm El Sheikh); confirm current coverage over the deep Western Desert.
- **FIS:** flight information / AIS function within NANSC/ECAA; Egypt has publicised a National Airspace Management Centre (NASMC) initiative for dynamic route/capacity management — confirm current operational status.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 Egypt is predominantly **low desert plateau** (Western Desert, Eastern Desert), but the **Sinai massif** rises sharply — **Mount Catherine (Jebel Katherina) ≈ 2,629 m (~8,668 ft)**, Egypt's highest point, sits close to **Sharm El Sheikh (HESH)** and St Catherine. The Nile Valley/Delta and the Red Sea coastal strip are low.
- **Boundary terrain:** no major terrain issue at the Libya/Sudan boundaries; the Sinai high ground is internal to HECC and most relevant to the HESH approach.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** a real consideration for the Sinai approach/departure corridors around Sharm El Sheikh; over the desert plateau and Red Sea coast the binding constraint is diversion-field reach, not terrain.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not generally a factor — arid/desert and coastal climate; the Sinai high ground can see occasional winter cold — apply per airport briefing where relevant.

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **North Sinai:** 🟧 **EASA CZIB-2017-09R13** ("Airspace of Egypt, North Sinai Governorate") assessed the risk of operating/overflying North Sinai **below FL250** as **HIGH**; this advisory's validity was extended to 31 DEC 2023 and is now marked **withdrawn** in the EASA register. **Re-check the current advisory status before relying on this as closed/open** — this is durable geometry context, not a live clearance.
- **Sudan / Khartoum-FIR boundary:** 🟥 the **Khartoum FIR (HSSS)** immediately south has been **largely closed to civil traffic since the April-2023 conflict**, with reported AAA/MANPADS/SAM risk and contingency-routing arrangements (Port-Sudan corridor, KFOSS routes above FL245 further south in the Juba FIR) — see the **Africa area brief §9** for the full durable geometry. Treat any Egypt/Sudan-adjacent routing as security-sensitive.
- **Red Sea corridor:** 🟧 the wider Red Sea / Gulf-of-Aden security picture (regional tension affecting shipping and, at times, aviation risk assessments) is **owned in depth by the Middle East area brief** — cross-check when routing HECC eastbound/southeastbound over the Red Sea, relevant given three of the four served destinations (HEGN/HEMA/HESH) sit directly on that coast.
- **Military / danger / restricted areas:** 🟧 AIP/NOTAM-published across the FIR (Sinai and desert training areas in particular) — pull the active list at planning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 Egypt requires **overflight/landing permits**; the CAA has been reported to operate scheduling windows for permit issuance (approx. half-day cycle) with permits typically valid for a defined period — confirm current process and lead-time.

> **SUA / security currency:** the North Sinai advisory status, the Sudan-boundary conflict geometry, and the Red Sea corridor picture are **dynamic — re-check live every time** (EASA CZIB register, Safe Airspace, state NOTAMs). This list is durable context, **not** a live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** squawk **7600**; continue per the ICAO/Egypt AIP IFR comms-failure rules (last clearance / filed route and levels), attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 confirm the Egypt-specific refinement at AIP ENR 1.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** radar-tactical via ATC given the presumed surveillance coverage over the Nile Valley/Delta and Red Sea coast; apply standard ICAO Doc 4444 procedures over any procedural desert segment.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Khamsin** — hot, dust-laden southerly wind (spring), reducing visibility across the Nile Valley and Delta.
  - **Red Sea coastal convection** — localised thunderstorm activity, particularly in the transitional seasons.
  - **Security wildcard** — the North Sinai advisory status and the Sudan-boundary conflict geometry are the strategic risks that would reshape a routing (§10).
