# Roberts (GLRB) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR — the tripartite **Roberts FIR (RFIR)** covering **Liberia, Sierra Leone and Guinea**, containing the **Roberts Intl (GLRB, Monrovia)** and Freetown (GFLL) aerodromes · **Parent area brief:** [Africa](../../africa.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Dakar (GOOO, ASECNA) N/NW · Abidjan (DIII, ASECNA/Côte d'Ivoire) E · Atlantic oceanic S/SW
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — provisional; the tripartite RFIR structure, lower/upper split and ATSU are corroborated against CANSO/RFIR-org sources, but airspace class letters, radar extent and current FRA status are flagged 🟧

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Roberts FIR is a **procedural continental/coastal FIR** run jointly by Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone through a dedicated tripartite organisation (RFIR) rather than ASECNA or a single national CAA — an unusual governance model worth knowing before overflight planning. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for aerodromes inside the FIR live in the airport briefings (§9). Live restrictions are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Roberts FIR (**GLRB**) was established 17 January 1975 by **Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone** as a joint aeronautical venture providing en-route air navigation service (above 3,000 ft AGL) across their combined airspace; each member state continues to manage its own airspace from ground to 3,000 ft AGL. 🟧 The published boundary is an irregular polygon of named lat/long turning points (per RFIR's own published description: from 09°00'N 016°55'W, to 10°53'N 015°05'W, then along the **Guinea territorial border** to 12°03'N 009°20'W, then along the limit of the **Bamako TMA (Mali)** to 008°08'W, to 10°10'N 008°00'W, then to 07°30'N 008°25'W, 06°15'N 007°27'W, 00°00'N 007°20'W and back to 09°00'N 016°55'W) — confirm the current AIP-published boundary before use; do not treat this as a plotting-quality boundary.
- **Vertical split:** 🟩 **lower airspace 3,000 ft AGL–FL245**, **upper airspace FL245–unlimited**, both worked by the RFIR Area Control Centre at Robertsfield. Approach control responsibility has progressively been raised to **10,000 ft** as RFIR capability has matured.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N/NE — Dakar (GOOO, ASECNA/Senegal).** The Roberts FIR boundary runs along the Guinea territorial border and touches the **Bamako TMA (Mali)** limit — i.e. Mali's upper airspace abuts Roberts FIR at a single boundary point, a genuine oddity worth knowing.
  - **E — Abidjan (DIII, ASECNA/Côte d'Ivoire).** Accra FIR (Ghana) does **not** border Roberts FIR directly — Côte d'Ivoire sits between them.
  - **S/SW — Atlantic Ocean**, oceanic reach currently delegated to ASECNA (§4).
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 internal RFIR sector boundaries (if any beyond the lower/upper split) are AIP/AIRAC-dependent — fly the current sectorisation, not a stored one.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **ATSU:** 🟩 **Roberts FIR Organisation (RFIR)** — a dedicated tripartite civil-aviation cooperative venture of Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone, governed by a Ministerial Council (ministers of the three states), a Technical Committee (the three states' civil aviation Directors General) and a Secretariat/Executive Management running day-to-day operations from **Robertsfield**. This is **not** an ASECNA structure and **not** a single-state CAA — a distinct model from every other FIR in this African set.
- 🟧 **Oceanic delegation:** per Liberia CAA reporting, **ASECNA currently manages the oceanic portion** of the Roberts FIR, with RFIR's Technical Committee mandated to explore taking that segment over. Confirm the current live division of responsibility before planning an oceanic-adjacent routing.
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟧 presumed procedural; RFIR has implemented a new **console, VCCS (redundant voice/data packet switching over VoIP/SIP) and ATM system** linking Robertsfield to the member-state units — a genuine modernisation, but not confirmed to include radar or ADS-B surveillance.
- **FIS:** flight information / procedural service provided by RFIR outside any confirmed surveillance cover.

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟧 mostly low coastal plain and rainforest, but the FIR's northern reach touches the **Guinea highlands (Fouta Djallon plateau, up to ≈1,500 m)**, and **Sierra Leone's Loma Mountains** contain **Mount Bintumani (≈1,945 m / ≈6,381 ft)** — the highest point in West Africa. Neither is a cruise-level factor but both matter for driftdown/escape on inland routings.
- **Boundary terrain:** the Guinea highlands sit near the N/NE boundary toward Dakar FIR; the Loma Mountains sit inland of Freetown.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** relevant mainly to inland/highland routings; the coastal corridor (Monrovia–Freetown) is low-lying.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not a factor — tropical climate.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** squawk **7600**; continue per ICAO/AFI comms-failure procedure (last clearance / filed route and levels), attempt contact on the previous/adjacent frequency and 121.5. 🟧 Confirm any RFIR-specific refinement at the current AIP.
- **Procedural / weather-deviation contingency:** in non-radar (procedural) airspace, apply the AFI/ICAO weather-deviation and contingency procedures. Squawk **7700** and declare for emergencies.
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Extreme wet-season rainfall (≈ May–Oct)** — the Monrovia/Freetown coastal belt is among the wettest in West Africa; heavy convective activity and reduced visibility are routine in-season.
  - **Harmattan (≈ Dec–Feb)** — less pronounced this far south/coastal than in the Sahel, but can still reduce visibility, particularly inland.
  - **Institutional-resilience note** — RFIR has maintained unbroken service through past civil conflict (late 1980s–1990s) and the 2014–15 Ebola outbreak; not a current operational hazard, included for context only.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, danger/restricted-area status, any Guinea/Liberia/Sierra Leone state advisories, SIGMET/AIRMET (convection, harmattan), current FRA status (if any has since been introduced), and the validated SimBrief route with assigned levels. Not stored here.
