# Dakar (GOOO) — FIR Briefing

**Scope:** single FIR/UIR — the combined **Dakar terrestrial FIR (Senegal) and Dakar Oceanic FIR/UIR**, a major North/Central-Atlantic oceanic gateway, containing the **Blaise Diagne (GOBD) hub** · **Parent area brief:** [Africa](../../africa.md) 🟩 · **Adjacent FIRs:** Niamey (DRRR, ASECNA) E · Roberts (GLRB) S/SE · Atlantic oceanic system N/W (Canary Islands / Sal Oceanic / Santa Maria Oceanic / mid-Atlantic)
**Version** v0.1 · **Updated** 2026-07-26 · **Status** Draft — provisional; the ASECNA/ANACIM authority split, oceanic ADS-C/CPDLC capability and FRA extent are corroborated against public ASECNA/OPS sources, but exact class letters, full oceanic boundary geometry and the western-Mali overlap are flagged 🟧

> **Read-me:** Strategic transit reference for the sim, not a chart or clearance. Dakar FIR/UIR is a **mixed FIR** — a conventional continental FIR over Senegal plus a very large **oceanic** sector (Dakar Oceanic) that feeds the **EUR-SAM corridor** and the wider North Atlantic oceanic system. Routings and levels here are planning context; file and fly the SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated route and comply with tactical ATC clearances. Terminal procedures for aerodromes inside the FIR live in the airport briefings (§9). Live restrictions — including the western-Mali security overlay — are pulled at planning (§11). Open 🟧 items are in §12. Flag legend 🟥 hard · 🟧 caution/unverified · 🟩 normal.

## 3. FIR structure & lateral/vertical boundaries

- **Lateral extent:** Dakar FIR/UIR (**GOOO**) covers **Senegal's national airspace** plus an extremely large **oceanic sector over the Atlantic** — one of ASECNA's six FIRs and, together with the Antananarivo, Brazzaville, Niamey and N'Djamena FIRs, part of ASECNA's 16.1 million km² area of responsibility (1.5× the size of Europe). ASECNA is itself headquartered in Dakar.
- 🟧 **Western-Mali overlap:** per current public risk reporting, **air traffic service in Mali is provided jointly by the GOOO/Dakar and DRRR/Niamey FIRs** — i.e. Dakar FIR's lateral extent reaches east into western Mali. This is a genuine and currently significant complexity: see §10 for the associated security caution.
- **Vertical split:** 🟧 continental lower/upper split is AIP-defined — confirm at ASECNA/Senegal AIP ENR 2.1. RVSM applies **FL290–FL410**. The oceanic UIR sits above the continental structure as a conventional oceanic upper-airspace layer.
- **Adjacent FIRs (by boundary):**
  - **N — Nouakchott UTA (Mauritania).** 🟧 Exact FIR/UTA relationship not fully confirmed publicly — Nouakchott UTA has its own FRA implementation (§7) suggesting a distinct upper-airspace structure adjoining GOOO to the north; confirm at planning.
  - **E — Niamey (DRRR, ASECNA/Niger–Burkina Faso–Mali sector).** Shares the western-Mali overlap noted above.
  - **S/SE — Roberts (GLRB).** Boundary runs along the Guinea territorial border; Roberts FIR's own boundary description references the **Bamako TMA (Mali)** limit, indicating the three FIRs (Dakar/Niamey/Roberts) converge near this area.
  - **N/W (oceanic) — the wider North/Central Atlantic oceanic system**: Canary Islands (Spain) oceanic structure to the N, Sal Oceanic (Cape Verde, GVSC) to the NW, feeding toward Santa Maria Oceanic / New York Oceanic further north and, across the South Atlantic, the Atlantico/Recife (Brazil) oceanic structure to the SW — the core of the **EUR-SAM corridor**.
- **Sectorisation:** 🟧 internal ACC sector boundaries (continental vs. oceanic, and any Mali-sector sub-division) are AIP/AIRAC-dependent — fly the current sectorisation, not a stored one.

## 4. Control & ATSU

- **ATSU:** 🟩 **ASECNA** provides air navigation services for the entire GOOO FIR/UIR (continental and oceanic) from **Dakar**, its own headquarters city; **ANACIM** is Senegal's national civil aviation regulator (oversight, not ANS provision) — a regulator/ANSP split, unlike the combined-model states elsewhere in this African set (e.g. Ghana's GCAA).
- **Surveillance basis:** 🟧 continental radar/surveillance around Dakar is presumed but not publicly confirmed in the sources reviewed; ASECNA has been reported to operate a "pretty decent ADS-B service" across its region generally (tier-4 corroboration, OPSGROUP). The oceanic portion runs conventional oceanic procedural separation supported by ADS-C/CPDLC.
- **FIS:** flight information / procedural service provided by ASECNA outside any confirmed radar cover.

## 5. Equipment & approvals

*Requirements below are DEFINED in OM E — Operations; this section states which apply in the Dakar FIR/UIR and references the OM E doc. It does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** 🟥 **FL290–FL410** — **ICAO AFI RVSM** continental; the oceanic segment is aligned with the broader Atlantic RVSM structure. An RVSM approval from the State of Registry/Operator is required. See [`OM E — RVSM Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/rvsm-operations.md).
- **PBN / RNP:** 🟧 **RNAV 5** enroute presumed continental (AFI PBN Implementation Plan); the oceanic segment likely requires **RNP 4 or RNP 10** for reduced lateral separation under ADS-C/CPDLC — confirm the current ASECNA AIP. See [`OM E — PBN and RNP Operations`](../../../../flight-ops/pbn-and-rnp-operations.md).
- **8.33 kHz channel spacing:** 🟩 **not an AFI requirement** — standard 25 kHz VHF applies continentally; not applicable oceanically.
- **Datalink (CPDLC / ADS-C):** 🟩 **confirmed available in Dakar Oceanic FIR** for equipped aircraft. See [`OM E — Datalink and Oceanic Procedures`](../../../../flight-ops/datalink-and-oceanic-procedures.md).
- **Transponder / ADS-B:** 🟥 **Mode C/S transponder required.** 🟧 Any ADS-B Out mandate is not asserted here — confirm the current ASECNA/Senegal AIP/NOTAM at planning.

## 6. Communications & frequencies

- **ACC sectors / frequencies:** a transit is worked by **ASECNA Dakar ACC** (continental) and/or **Dakar Oceanic Control** (oceanic) depending on routing; exact sector frequencies are AIRAC/AIP-dependent — fly the current AIP frequencies.
- **HF & oceanic procedures:** 🟧 HF and standard oceanic-procedure comms apply over the Atlantic sector, supplemented by ADS-C/CPDLC where equipped.
- **Language:** 🟩 **English** (ICAO) for ATC; ASECNA's internal working language is French — expect French-language NOTAM/AIP source material.
- **Emergency / guard:** **121.5 MHz** guard (monitor); **123.45 MHz** air-to-air.

## 7. Route structure & entry/exit

- **Free Route Airspace (FRA):** 🟧 **GOOO/Dakar FIR has FRA above FL250**, implemented as part of ASECNA's region-wide FRA roll-out (effective 25 Jan 2024) — but **only within a small area near the FIR boundary** (published reporting points include SEPOM, LUMPO, MOGSA, AKDAK, BADIA, IPUGA, NEVDI, BIKIS per the ASECNA AIP ENR 3.5 point set), **not** across the FIR's full (very large) lateral extent. Confirm the current point set at planning — do not assume free-routing across the whole FIR.
- **Airway spine (where FRA not available):** the fixed ATS-route network handles the bulk of the FIR, including the oceanic transit tracks and the western-Mali sector.
- **Major fixes / entry-exit points:** the FRA boundary reporting points above, plus the airway boundary fixes toward Niamey (E) and Roberts (S/SE); the SimBrief/current-AIRAC route provides the exact string (planning context, not a clearance).
- **Hubs & aerodromes inside the FIR:** 🟩 **Dakar — Blaise Diagne Intl (GOBD)**, Senegal's current main international gateway. Terminal detail is in the airport briefings (§9).

## 8. Terrain & MORA

- **Grid MORA / high terrain belts:** 🟩 Senegal is largely low and flat; the highest ground (≈581 m) is near the Guinea border in the far southeast of the country — not a cruise-level factor. The oceanic sector has no terrain.
- **Boundary terrain:** none of operational significance on the continental boundaries; the Guinea-highlands terrain referenced in the Roberts FIR brief sits just across the S/SE boundary.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** not a constraint continentally; the oceanic sector requires standard oceanic contingency/driftdown planning per the fleet's oceanic procedures (see OM E datalink/oceanic doc).
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections:** not a factor — tropical/subtropical climate.

## 10. Special-use airspace

- **Military / danger / restricted areas:** 🟧 not detailed in public sourcing reviewed for the Senegal continental portion — pull the active AIP/NOTAM list at planning.
- **Conflict-zone / security exposure:** 🟥 **western Mali**, worked in part by the GOOO/Dakar FIR (jointly with DRRR/Niamey), is under **active multi-state airspace-security warnings as of mid-2026**: France and Canada recommend remaining **at or above FL320** within the Mali portion of GOOO/DRRR; Germany and EASA recommend **not below FL260**; the UK and Italy recommend **not below 25,000 ft AGL**; the USA advises caution **at all altitudes**. Several states additionally advise **not using Bamako (GABS) as a destination or alternate** at all. This caution applies **only to the Mali-territory portion of the FIR** — the Senegal continental airspace and the oceanic sector proper carry no such warning.
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** 🟧 confirm current overflight/landing permit requirements for Senegal; the Mali sector carries additional diplomatic sensitivity given Mali's 2025 reciprocal airspace closure with Algeria (unrelated to Senegal but relevant if routing near that boundary).

> **SUA / security currency:** the western-Mali warning picture is **dynamic — re-check live every time** (Safe Airspace / state advisories) before any routing that touches the Mali sector of this FIR; this list is durable context, not a live clearance.

## 11. Contingency & seasonal / live-data pointer

- **Comms-failure:** squawk **7600**; continue per ICAO/AFI comms-failure procedure (last clearance / filed route and levels) continentally; oceanic comms-failure follows standard oceanic contingency procedure. 🟧 Confirm the ASECNA/Senegal AIP refinement.
- **Weather-deviation / in-flight contingency:** continental — standard AFI/ICAO procedural weather-deviation technique where non-radar; oceanic — standard oceanic weather-deviation/offset procedure (see OM E datalink/oceanic doc).
- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Atlantic tropical-cyclone genesis (≈ Aug–Oct)** — the waters off Senegal/Cape Verde are the principal genesis region for "Cape Verde-type" Atlantic hurricanes; oceanic transits in this window should account for tropical-system avoidance.
  - **Harmattan (≈ Nov–Mar)** — Saharan dust haze affecting the Dakar terminal area and inland Senegal.
  - **Western-Mali security wildcard** — the strategic risk that would most reshape a routing through the eastern portion of this FIR (§10).
