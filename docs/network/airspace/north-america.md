# North America — airspace brief
**Scope:** Post-polar descent — western Canadian domestic into western US ARTCC airspace, down to the KSFO arrival · **FIRs / OCAs covered:** Edmonton FIR (CZEG, NAV CANADA) · Vancouver FIR (CZVR, NAV CANADA) · Seattle ARTCC (KZSE, FAA) · Oakland ARTCC (KZOA, FAA) incl. Northern California TRACON (NORCAL)

## 3. Airspace structure

- **Canadian Domestic Airspace — SDA vs NDA (the reference-change on this leg):** Canadian domestic airspace is split into **Southern Domestic Airspace (SDA)** and **Northern Domestic Airspace (NDA)**. The polar inbound stream enters the **northern part of the Edmonton FIR inside the NDA** — where **all navigation, airways, procedures, runway numbering and wind are referenced to TRUE north (0° declination)** because the region is an Area of Magnetic Unreliability — then descends **south across the SDA boundary**, below which the normal **magnetic** reference resumes. The full true-track / grid-nav / magnetic-unreliability treatment is in **[Arctic](arctic.md) §3–4** — it is not repeated here; on this leg the crew's task is the **NDA→SDA transition** and setting the correct True/Magnetic FMS reference for the airspace. - **Standard-pressure region:** the **entire NDA is a standard-pressure region** (29.92 in Hg / 1013.2 hPa set throughout, all levels), whereas the **SDA is an altimeter-setting region** with a **transition altitude of 18,000 ft** — so on the descent the crew changes from standard to local QNH on leaving the standard-pressure region (below 18,000 ft in the SDA). - **Edmonton FIR (CZEG):** Canada's **largest FIR** (~8.09 M km²), NAV CANADA — **Edmonton Centre**. Spans the Northern, and Southern Control Areas across the western/central prairies and the Northwest Territories; borders **Reykjavik (BIRD), Nuuk, Gander Oceanic, Anchorage (PAZA), Winnipeg (CZWG), Vancouver (CZVR)** and, to the south, **Seattle (KZSE)** and **Salt Lake (KZLC)** ARTCCs. Historically the most active **procedural (non-radar)** airspace in Canada; that remote north is now backed by **space-based ADS-B** so the practical picture on this leg is surveillance-controlled. **Edmonton also owns the high-level airspace (FL250 and above) inside part of the Vancouver FIR — the "Cranbrook Extension"** (~20,192 km²), so a westbound/southbound descent may stay with Edmonton Centre at high level while laterally over Vancouver's FIR. (exact sector/boundary decimals — VERIFY AIP Canada ENR 2.)
- **Vancouver FIR (CZVR):** NAV CANADA — **Vancouver Centre**; British Columbia and the eastern Pacific approaches, entirely within the **SDA (magnetic reference)**. Dominated by the **Coast Mountains** and the BC interior ranges. Borders **Edmonton (CZEG)** to the east and **Seattle (KZSE)** and **Anchorage (PAZA)** ARTCCs. The **CZEG/CZVR** high-level split (Cranbrook Extension, above) means the FIR-boundary label on the chart is not always the controlling-centre boundary at cruise. - **Canadian high-level classes & bases:** Canadian high-level controlled airspace is **Class A** in the domestic control areas; the **Southern Control Area (SCA) Class A base is 18,000 ft ASL**, the **Northern Control Area (NCA) base is FL230** and the **Arctic Control Area (ACA) base is FL270** (per the Designated Airspace Handbook / [Arctic](arctic.md) §3). RVSM occupies **FL290–FL410** within this. - **Seattle ARTCC (KZSE):** FAA; ~300,000 sq mi over **Washington, Oregon, Montana, Idaho, Nevada and northern California**. Adjacent to **Vancouver & Edmonton** Centres (north), **Salt Lake** (east) and **Oakland** (south/west). This is where the stream **crosses into US airspace** and the **magnetic reference and US altimetry/transition (18,000 ft)** apply. - **Oakland ARTCC (KZOA) + NORCAL:** FAA; central/northern California and the eastern Pacific. Owns the **high/low enroute descent to the San Francisco Bay terminal area** and hands off to **Northern California TRACON (NORCAL Approach)** for sequencing to KSFO. - **US class structure on the descent:** **Class A** 18,000 ft MSL–FL600 (all enroute cruise); descending through **Class E**; the terminal area is the **San Francisco Class B** (with the adjacent OAK Class C). NORCAL vectors/clears into Class B, typically stepping down to an assigned altitude (commonly ~3,500 ft in the Bay core) for the ILS. - **Entry/exit & transition points:** FIR/ARTCC boundary reporting is radar/ADS-B-tracked (no procedural boundary reports on this leg). The filed route is the **SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated string**; the terminal end is an assigned **RNAV STAR** into NORCAL (e.g. the DYAMD-family RNAV arrivals) then radar vectors to the ILS. (exact STAR by AIRAC/runway config — pull on the day.)

## 4. Equipment, approvals & separation

*Capability topics (RVSM, PBN/RNP, CPDLC/ADS-C, transition levels) are **defined once in OM E — Operations**; this section applies them to the sector — it does not re-derive them.*

- **RVSM:** **FL290–FL410** — approval required. Canada per the **Designated Airspace Handbook (TP1820)**; US per **14 CFR 91.180 / 91.225**. Continuous RVSM airspace across all four FIRs/ARTCCs.
- **PBN / RNP:** Enroute is a published airway + PBN structure (Canada migrating legacy CMNPS/RNPC toward PBN — see [Arctic](arctic.md) §4); the KSFO arrival uses **RNAV (RNP/GNSS) STARs** into NORCAL. Standard RNAV-1/RNAV-10 airframe capability suffices for the land leg; no oceanic RNP-4/PBCS applies here (that lives in [North Atlantic](north-atlantic.md)). (exact current NDA/NRR fixed-track structure — VERIFY AIP Canada ENR 2/4.)
- **ADS-B Out — dual-mandate leg:**
  - **NAV CANADA space-based ADS-B mandate.** Aircraft must carry an ADS-B Out transponder meeting **RTCA DO-260B (or newer)** *and* the **antenna-diversity performance requirement** — either **two antennas (top and bottom)** or a single antenna capable of transmitting **both downward to ground stations and upward to the Aireon satellite constellation**. Phasing: **Class A domestic airspace effective 10 AUG 2023**; **Class B effective 16 MAY 2024**; **Class C/D/E (incl. below 12,500 ft) no sooner than 2026, pending assessment.** For our A330-900neo at RVSM levels this is **Class A → mandate applies**. (Class C/D/E timing not yet fixed — verify at planning.)
  - **FAA 14 CFR 91.225.** ADS-B Out required since **01 JAN 2020** in Class A/B/C, Class E ≥ 10,000 ft MSL (excl. ≤ 2,500 ft AGL), and above Class B/C shelves. **At and above FL180 a 1090ES (Mode-S extended squitter) transmitter is required** (UAT/978 MHz is only permitted below 18,000 ft). Our cruise/descent is 1090ES-compliant. - **Datalink:** not mandated on this VHF-continuous land leg; PDC/CPDLC available at major terminals as a convenience. The NAT datalink mandate (CPDLC+ADS-C, FANS 1/A) applies **upstream** — see [North Atlantic](north-atlantic.md) §4 / [Arctic](arctic.md) §4.
- **Transponder:** Mode S with ADS-B Out as above; continuous SSR/ADS-B/multilateration coverage.
- **Surveillance environment — contrast with the NAT:** unlike the procedural oceanic NAT, this entire leg is **surveillance-controlled**. **Aireon space-based ADS-B** (NAV CANADA + NATS the first ANSPs to go operational, **27 MAR 2019**) turned the formerly-procedural Canadian north and the North Atlantic into surveilled airspace — over the NAT it enabled longitudinal minima down to **~14–17 NM** and lateral to **~15–19 NM** for equipped aircraft. On the land leg it simply reinforces continuous ground-radar coverage.

## 5. Communications

- **VHF — continuous, primary:** the whole leg is worked on VHF: **Edmonton Centre → Vancouver Centre → Seattle Center → Oakland Center → NORCAL Approach → SFO Tower**. No HF/SATVOICE leg here.
- **HF / SATVOICE:** not required on this segment. The HF families (Reykjavik Radio, NAT stations) and Iridium-north-of-80°N requirements belong to the upstream [Arctic](arctic.md) §5 / [North Atlantic](north-atlantic.md) §5.
- **Datalink comms:** PDC/CPDLC available at major terminals (planning convenience); not a requirement.
- **Position reporting:** radar/ADS-B — no procedural (voice) boundary/waypoint reports on this leg.
- **Emergency/interpilot VHF:** emergency **121.5 MHz**; air-to-air **123.45 MHz**.

## 6. Routing & levels

- **Principal route structure:** the polar great circle comes off the Arctic segment at high level, is handed **Reykjavik → Nuuk upper → Edmonton (CZEG)**, tracks **SW across the Edmonton/Vancouver FIRs** (note the CZEG-owned high-level **Cranbrook Extension** over CZVR), crosses into **Seattle ARTCC (KZSE)**, then **Oakland (KZOA)** for the descent, and is sequenced by **NORCAL** to KSFO. The filed route is the **SimBrief/current-AIRAC-validated string**; upstream track/clearance detail is in [North Atlantic](north-atlantic.md).
- **Free-route vs airway:** Canadian high-level uses a published airway + PBN structure migrating toward PBN/free-route direct routing; US enroute uses the high-altitude **jet-route / Q-route (RNAV) / preferred-route** structure feeding the arrival gates. Confirm current FRA/preferred-route availability on the day's flight docs.
- **Typical level/flow:** RVSM **FL290–FL410**; step-descent begins under Oakland Center; the **RNAV STAR** (e.g. DYAMD-family) delivers the aircraft into NORCAL, which assigns a landing runway (KSFO 28L/28R typical for west-flow) and steps the aircraft down (commonly to ~3,500 ft in the Bay core) for the ILS. Three ILS approaches are published (19L, 28L, 28R). - **Transition altitude/level:** **18,000 ft** in both the Canadian SDA and the US (below which local QNH is set); the **NDA is standard-pressure throughout** (set 29.92 in the north until leaving the standard-pressure region). Transition-level mechanics are an **OM E** topic.

## 7. Terrain, MORA & driftdown

- **Western-cordillera terrain belts — the binding enroute/descent constraint:** the leg overflies, in sequence:
  - **Canadian Rockies** — highest **Mount Robson 3,954 m (12,972 ft)**; broad belt of 10,000–12,000 ft peaks across the CZEG/CZVR interior.
  - **Coast Mountains (BC)** — heavily glaciated; principal massifs to ~**4,000 m (~13,000 ft)** (e.g. Mount Waddington ~13,186 ft). (exact per-quadrant grid-MORA — VERIFY on NAV CANADA/FAA ENR charts.)
  - **Cascade Range** — volcanic high points **Mount Rainier 14,411 ft**, Mount Shasta ~14,180 ft, in KZSE/northern KZOA.
  - **Sierra Nevada** — **Mount Whitney 14,505 ft**, the highest in the contiguous US, on the eastern side of Oakland ARTCC.
- **MEA / MORA:** high grid-MORA across all four sectors; the enroute floor and any radar-vectored descent are terrain-limited over the Rockies/Coast Mountains/Cascades. Use published **MEA/MOCA/grid-MORA** on the descent; do not accept a lower level than terrain-clearance allows without a charted/vectored minimum.
- **Driftdown / depressurisation escape:** an engine-out or decompression driftdown over the cordillera must clear terrain to **~14,500 ft (Sierra) / ~14,400 ft (Cascades)** locally — plan the escape route to a valley/coastal descent corridor and the nearest capable field (§8). This is a **higher terrain floor than the Greenland icecap dome** that limits the upstream [Arctic](arctic.md) §7 leg, so re-evaluate the decompression profile as the route crosses from icecap-limited to cordillera-limited terrain.
- **Cold-temperature altitude corrections — Cold Temperature Airports (CTA):** the FAA maintains a list of **Cold Temperature Airports** where, below an airport-specific temperature threshold, pilots **must apply a cold-temperature altitude correction** and report the corrected altitude to ATC (mandatory since **SEP 2015**; list by ICAO ident on the FAA d-TPP site). Northern/mountain fields in CZEG/CZVR/KZSE are the relevant cases; Canada applies equivalent AIM cold-temperature corrections. KSFO itself is low/coastal and warm, but **northern diversion fields (e.g. CYZF, CYOD) and mountain alternates require the correction** — apply per the airport briefing and current OAT.

- **ETP / equal-time-point considerations:** on the land leg the divert window is short (fields dense), so no oceanic-style ETP applies here; the limiting decompression/driftdown ETP is **upstream over the icecap/oceanic segment** ([Arctic](arctic.md)/[North Atlantic](north-atlantic.md) §8) and, on this leg, **terrain-clearance over the cordillera** rather than field spacing (§7).
- **Fuel-freeze / cold-soak:** the long high-FL cold-soak risk is on the **upstream polar cruise** — monitor tank fuel temperature vs the **Jet A-1 freeze point −47 °C** per [Arctic](arctic.md) §8; by this leg the descent into warmer air resolves it.

## 9. Special-use airspace & overflight

- **Conflict / prohibited / risk zones:** none — Canada and the US domestic; no conflict zones, no overflight-permit or diplomatic-clearance states on this leg.
- **Danger / restricted / military areas:** numerous **US MOAs / restricted / prohibited areas** (e.g. military ranges across ID/NV/CA) and **Canadian CYR/CYA** advisory/restricted areas — normally below or laterally clear of a high-level jet arrival but **verify on the day's charts/NOTAMs**, especially if radar-vectored low over terrain. Cold Lake (CYOD) sits by a large military range/advisory complex. (pull live at §11.)
- **Overflight-permit / diplomatic considerations:** none — the Canada→US crossing is handled at the ATC/FIR level.

> **Conflict-zone currency:** re-check ICAO Conflict Zone Information Repository / state NOTAMs and operator routing policy at planning — durable context, not live clearance.

## 10. Contingency procedures

- **Radar/ADS-B environment:** this is **not** an oceanic-procedural leg — the standardised NAT oceanic contingency, weather-deviation and SLOP procedures **do not apply here** (they apply upstream — [North Atlantic](north-atlantic.md) §10). On the land leg, follow **ATC vectors/clearances** and standard domestic contingency handling.
- **Comms-failure:** apply the standard **domestic IFR lost-comms** procedure (route/altitude per last clearance / expected / filed; squawk **7600**); continuous VHF coverage makes prolonged loss unlikely.
- **Terrain-driven contingency — :** in an engine-out or decompression over the cordillera (§7), fly the pre-briefed **driftdown/escape route** to clear terrain (up to ~14,500 ft locally), squawk **7700**, and descend toward a valley/coastal corridor and the nearest capable field (§8). This terrain floor is the leg's defining emergency constraint.
- **Weather-deviation:** standard domestic radar-environment weather deviations by ATC clearance/vector; no oceanic offset table applies.

## 11. Seasonal, environmental & live data

- **Seasonal hazards (durable):**
  - **Mountain-wave, turbulence & icing** over the Rockies/Coast Mountains/Cascades — strongest in winter with strong cross-barrier flow; plan for turbulence and airframe icing on descent through cloud over terrain.
  - **Pacific frontal weather & marine stratus at KSFO** — low ceilings/visibility and the summer stratus "burn-off" cycle drive Bay Area arrival delays and single-runway (staggered) approaches; expect NORCAL flow control in stratus events. - **Cold-temperature altimetry** at northern/mountain fields — see §7 (CTA corrections).
  - **Residual high-latitude space-weather** affects only the **upstream** polar/Arctic segment (HF/GNSS) — see [Arctic](arctic.md) §11 / [North Atlantic](north-atlantic.md) §11; negligible on this land leg.

> **Pull at planning (T-2h):** enroute NOTAMs, SIGMET/AIRMET (mountain turbulence/icing, Bay Area stratus), the assigned **RNAV STAR / KSFO runway config**, TMA/TFR and MOA activity across the western US, and the validated SimBrief route. Not stored here.
