## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** OMSJ operates a single main passenger terminal (approx. 125,000 m² per Wikipedia) plus a substantial dedicated cargo complex of **five cargo terminals** totalling roughly 32,000 m² (Terminal 1 charter; Terminal 2 historically a Lufthansa Cargo regional hub tenancy; Terminal 3 freight forwarders; Terminal 4 customs/surplus imports; Terminal 5 scheduled cargo operations) — per Air Arabia Cargo's own facilities page.
- **Our operation uses:** Not applicable — OMSJ is not in VAMSYS. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** Standard international arrivals hall; specific hall/desk detail not confirmed. 🟧
- **Notes:** The field is the home base of low-cost carrier Air Arabia (headquartered on-airport in the Sharjah Freight Center) and hosts a mixed regional/charter/cargo carrier list (per Sleeping in Airports and Wikipedia) including Air China, Airblue, Air India, Cham Wings, Egyptair, Emirates, IndiGo, PIA, Pegasus Airlines, SCAT Airlines, Syrian Airlines, Turkish Airlines and Uzbekistan Airways — a leisure/regional/low-cost traffic mix distinct from a premium widebody hub. 🟧

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** 🟧 Passenger-apron positions 2–8 are reported Code E (heavy) capable; the dedicated cargo apron (positions 50–62) is reported Code F (A380-class) capable for freighter operations. This sits in apparent tension with a separate tier-4 note that the aerodrome is not normally available for A380-800 **passenger** diversions except Emirates flights — likely reflecting a pax-vs-freighter distinction, not independently reconciled.
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Passenger-apron positions 1A–1C and 11–26 are reported Code C (medium) capable only — not usable for a widebody.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** Not confirmed. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Not applicable — not in VAMSYS.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** See [Briefing §13](index.md) for the reported taxiway hotspot pattern (eastbound/westbound taxiway-B conflicts with runway exits; the RWY 12/B11/A12 wrong-turn trap).

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** None identified — no formal IATA slot level found in reachable sources. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **None identified.** The field's reported H24 fuel, customs and operating-hours pattern is consistent with its cargo/low-cost-carrier operating character rather than a premium-hub curfew regime.
- **CTOT / flow control:** No formal CTOT/EDCT-style program identified for OMSJ specifically. A tier-4 VATSIM-network operational reference describes **coordinated departure release with Dubai Departures/Approach and Dubai Tower** whenever multiple departures from Sharjah and/or Dubai are routing to the same exit point, plus named 3-minute longitudinal separation minima on specific SIDs feeding the Muscat FIR — this is a real Dubai-CTA flow-management dependency even without a formal slot regime. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** Because Sharjah's runway/config and departure sequencing are **subordinate to Dubai's operational state** (see Briefing §3.2), a busy or degraded Dubai TMA day is the primary schedule-integrity risk here rather than a Sharjah-specific curfew or slot constraint.

## 5. Handling agents & turnaround

- **Handling agent(s):** **Sharjah Aviation Services (SAS)** is reported as the principal ground handler for scheduled and cargo traffic and holds IATA CEIV Pharma certification (the first such certification in the GCC/Africa, per Air Arabia Cargo) for temperature-controlled cargo handling. **Gama Aviation** separately operates a dedicated Business Aviation Centre/FBO for bizjet traffic (IS-BAH Stage III accredited per Business Air News). Landing aircraft are reportedly required to contact SAS Operations prior to landing to confirm stand allocation. 🟧
- **Typical turnaround time for our type:** Not confirmed — not in VAMSYS. 🟧
- **Services:** Standard ground services (push-back, GPU/PCA, water/lav, catering) expected at a major regional cargo/LCC field; not individually confirmed for OMSJ.
- **Turnaround risk items:** Dubai-TMA-dependent runway/config changes (Briefing §3.2) and the field's genuinely high cargo-freighter/narrowbody-LCC movement density are the main plausible turnaround/ground-workload risk items; extreme summer heat may also affect ground-service throughput and ramp operations. 🟧

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** 🟥 **Dependency on Dubai Intl's operational state** — because Sharjah's active runway and departure sequencing follow Dubai's configuration and traffic flow (Briefing §3.2), a busy, weather-affected or flow-managed day at Dubai is the primary schedule-risk driver at Sharjah, more so than any Sharjah-specific factor.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Named flow-control minima apply on specific Sharjah SIDs feeding the Muscat FIR, and departure release is coordinated with Dubai Departures/Approach when exit points are shared — both are structural, not occasional, exposure. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Extreme summer heat (density-altitude performance impact) and regional shamal dust/visibility events are the additional seasonal reliability considerations; GNSS jamming/spoofing has also been specifically reported at this field as part of the wider Gulf pattern (see Briefing §3.4/§14) — a potential source of tactical vectoring/delay during affected periods. 🟧
