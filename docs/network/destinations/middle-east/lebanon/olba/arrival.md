## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain:** 🟥 The Mount Lebanon range rises close to the field's east side — **Mount Sannine (~2,628 m/8,622 ft)** roughly 19 NM ESE, with the higher Qurnat as Sawda massif (~3,088 m/10,131 ft) further north. The published ILS RWY 16 chart states **circling is not authorised east and south of the airport** — brief this before every approach into RWY 16 and treat it as the field's standing terrain caution (see [Briefing §3.1](index.md)).
- **Specific threats:** 🟧 No wake, closely-spaced-parallel or offset-approach threat identified — the runway layout is a conventional non-parallel set. Coastal location makes low cloud/reduced visibility off the sea a plausible seasonal threat (see [Briefing §14](index.md)); no AIP-sourced frequency statistic obtained.
- **Airspace / traffic:** 🟥 **STANDING CAUTION:** Beirut (OLBB) FIR status is variable and subject to periodic NOTAM-driven restriction, documented as under heightened advisory in the regional Middle East airspace picture; GNSS jamming/interference has been reported across the Eastern Mediterranean generally. Check current NOTAMs, the state airspace-status bulletin, and any active EASA Conflict Zone Information Bulletin/Information Note before every arrival — see the [Middle East airspace brief](../../../../airspace/middle-east.md). No party, cause or position is asserted here. Day-to-day, Beirut Approach provides radar service and traffic density is moderate.

## 10. Gotchas

- **High terrain lies close to the east of the field** — brief this on every arrival regardless of assigned STAR/approach; circling is not authorised east and south of the airport (published chart restriction).
- **Pull the current Beirut (OLBB) FIR NOTAM/airspace-status picture before every arrival** — the regional airspace situation is documented as variable and NOTAM-driven; see the [Middle East airspace brief](../../../../airspace/middle-east.md).
- **RWY 17/35 is the original 1954 asphalt runway and is rarely used** — expect a different surface/friction profile and confirm current serviceability/NOTAM status if ever assigned.
- **RWY 21 has only a published RNP approach** (no ILS/VOR-DME) — do not expect a conventional approach option on that runway end.
- A 2024 public user report claims the Tower frequency differs from the charted 118.900 (122.700) — confirm the active frequency on ATIS/current chart.
- Displaced thresholds materially shorten usable landing distance on several runway ends (§7) — do not assume the full physical runway length is available.
