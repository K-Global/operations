## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** A single unified terminal building replaced the airport's older two-passenger/one-cargo-terminal layout when it opened on 21 March 2013 (King Abdullah II inauguration 14 Mar 2013; full operational transfer completed overnight 20/21 Mar 2013). Designed by Foster + Partners, its distinctive roof (127 concrete domes, Bedouin-tent-inspired) is a recognisable feature. Capacity rose from an original 3.5M passengers/year to 7.5M at opening, then to 12M after a second 2016 expansion phase (~$1B), with a stated goal of 16M by 2032 (concession end).
- **Our operation uses:** The single terminal serves all scheduled international traffic; no separate concourse split for a specific carrier group is documented in reachable sources.
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** Single international arrivals hall within the unified terminal; H24 customs/immigration.
- **Notes:** Three lounges are documented (Royal Jordanian business/first, an Airport Hotel lounge near the North Concourse, and a Zain Jordan VIP lounge) — not confirmed as accessible to K Global passengers. 🟧

---

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** Code E stands are spread across the **North Apron** (N04, N08, N14, N19, N20, N21), **South Apron** (S06, S10, S24, and others), and **Hotel Apron** (28–32, mixed Code D/E, up to B747-400). **Code F is limited** to stand **N26** (North Apron, entry/exit via Taxiway Juliet only) and stand **S30** (South Apron, except AN124/AN225, entry via Taxiway Sierra only), plus Cargo Apron stand 1 (up to B747-8F) and 2A. 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Several North/South Apron Code C stands carry type-specific exclusions (e.g. excluding MD80/90, DC9, ATR, CRJ, DH8, F100 variants) — not a K Global fleet concern, but confirms these are not widebody-sized positions.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** North Apron N01–N17 (odd-numbered) and South Apron S01–S25 (odd-numbered, remote) are Code C-maximum remote stands; S01/S03 are closed and replaced by Taxiway T (medium-aircraft only, max 40 m wingspan) per the VATSIM Jordan vACC cross-check. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect a contact Code E gate at North/South/Hotel Apron for a scheduled widebody turn; Code F requires the specific taxiway/stand combination above.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** Marshaller availability is mandatory on all North/South/Hotel/Royal Pavilion stands even when Visual Guidance Docking Systems are fitted — see [Briefing §13](index.md).

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** No slot-coordination regime found in the reachable AIP — treat as **uncoordinated / Level 1** pending confirmation. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **None found.** AD operating hours (administration, ATS, customs, fuelling, handling, security, de-icing) are all published H24.
- **CTOT / flow control:** No ATFM/CTOT programme identified for OJAI in reachable sources; not expected to be a material constraint at this traffic volume. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** With no curfew or confirmed slot regime, schedule flexibility here is materially better than at a coordinated European/Gulf hub — the operative planning constraint is Code F stand/taxiway availability (§3), not time-of-day restriction.

## 5. Handling agents & turnaround

- **Handling agent(s):** Not individually confirmed in reachable public sources; full ground handling is confirmed available up to Code E, with **limited capacity for Code F**. 🟧
- **Typical turnaround time for our type:** Not independently confirmed; VAMSYS mirror gives **taxi-in 12 min / taxi-out 15 min** for planning purposes `[VAMSYS mirror 2026-07-26]`.
- **Services:** Full cargo-handling, fuelling (H24, no stated limit), and de-icing (H24) confirmed; hangar space and aircraft repair facilities for visiting aircraft are limited (private companies/maintenance/Royal Pavilion only; a stated legacy-type repair list — B727, B737, L1011, A300, A310, A32S, A330, A340 — currency not re-verified).
- **Turnaround risk items:** Code F stand/taxiway constraints (§3) and the mandatory 5 NM landing-interval rule on 26L/08L (Briefing §3.3) are the main items that could stretch a turn or the inbound sequence.

## 6. Load factors & seasonality

- **Demand peaks:** OJAI serves as Jordan's primary international gateway; regional leisure/religious-travel seasonality (e.g. Petra/Wadi Rum tourism, Hajj/Umrah-adjacent regional traffic) is a plausible demand driver but not independently quantified for the K Global operation. 🟧
- **Day-of-week / seasonal pattern:** Not independently sourced for OJAI specifically. 🟧
- **Cargo / belly capacity:** Airport-wide handled cargo has run roughly 100,000–110,000 tonnes/year (2016–2019 figures); a dedicated Cargo Apron (4 main stands plus Hotel Apron overflow) supports freighter and belly cargo operations.
- **Connection banks:** As a destination (not a base), OJAI's schedule is set by the network route register rather than local connection-bank timing — cross-ref the route register.

## 9. De-icing provisioning & season

- **Availability:** **Available, H24**, at the **Cargo Apron, Taxiways J and E**; fluid **Kilfrost ABC-3** (Type II de/anti-icing, ISO 11078/AMS 1428) `[CARC AIP AD 2 OJAI 2.4]`. 🟩
- **Season:** Not separately published; the elevated plateau location (2,397 ft) sees occasional winter cold/rain, and the wider Amman area occasionally sees light snow — de-icing provision exists for this contingency even though it is not a routine daily requirement.
- **Provisioning:** Location may be changed by the airport operator per operational coordination with the ground handler and ATC — confirm on the day in winter.
- **Commercial impact:** Low — de-icing is provisioned but not expected to be a routine seasonal delay driver at this warm-climate field; treat any activation as an exception-handling case rather than a scheduled seasonal risk.
