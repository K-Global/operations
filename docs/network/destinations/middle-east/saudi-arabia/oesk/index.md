## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain 🟩
Al-Jawf Province sits on the northern Saudi interior plateau — a low-relief desert environment with no close-in high terrain identified in reachable sources. This is not expected to be a significant CFIT driver, but the MSA ring should be confirmed on the current chart as routine practice; no primary AIP terrain data was reached this pass.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic 🟧
OESK is a **joint civil/military aerodrome** — Royal Saudi Air Force activity shares the field, which typically means scheduled deconfliction windows and possible short-notice restrictions around military movements; no specific procedure was confirmed in reachable public sources. Traffic is otherwise light-to-moderate regional/short-haul international (§1, §6). The field lies within the **Jeddah (OEJD) FIR** — see [Middle East / Gulf Airspace Briefing](../../../../airspace/middle-east.md) for the regional structure, conflict-zone picture and GNSS-interference caution that applies network-wide; Al-Jawf itself is not on the list of SW-Saudi fields closed by Houthi activity, but the region-wide GNSS jamming/spoofing caution (§9 of that brief) applies.

### 3.3 Runway excursion 🟧
**Single runway (10/28)** — no redundancy if the runway is closed (disabled aircraft, contamination, obstruction); plan diversion contingency accordingly (§16). No displaced threshold or declared-distance detail was found in reachable sources — not independently confirmed either way.

### 3.4 Weather threat 🟧
Interior-desert climate typical of northern Saudi Arabia — expect **shamal winds and blowing dust** seasonally (regional pattern, see [Middle East Airspace Briefing](../../../../airspace/middle-east.md) §11) and **high summer temperatures**, though at 2,261 ft this field does not meet the density-altitude "hot-and-high" trigger the way Taif (OETF) does. No OESK-specific climatology statistic was found this pass — see §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations 🟧
The single-runway, joint-military-use field with a newly opened terminal (Feb 2026, capacity 1.6M pax/yr) is otherwise unremarkable on the data reached — the open items are chiefly **confirmation gaps** (approach minima, RFF, hours, fuel) rather than known hard restrictions. The unresolved **runway-designator discrepancy** (§1, §18) is the standout item to close before treating this brief as flight-planning-ready.
