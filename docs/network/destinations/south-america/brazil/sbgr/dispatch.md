## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** Four passenger terminals — **T1** and **T2** (original complex), **T3** (opened 2014, built for Star Alliance/international carriers, adding significant international capacity), **T4** (added 2012–13 in the former VASP/Transbrasil cargo buildings, domestic/remote boarding).
- **Our operation uses:** International long-haul traffic historically concentrates around **T3**; exact current gate assignment for the K Global operation not confirmed. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** International arrivals process through the terminal handling the arriving flight; cross-terminal connection times not confirmed — build buffer for any onward network leg pending confirmation. 🟧
- **Notes:** GRU's terminal programme has expanded significantly since 2012 (T3, T4) to relieve historical overcrowding at T1/T2 — confirm current gate/concourse assignment with handling before finalising a stand plan. 🟧

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** GRU is publicly reported as **slot-coordinated**, with a cap of roughly **45 movements/hour** — one of five capacity-restricted Brazilian aerodromes (with Congonhas, Brasília, Belo Horizonte–Pampulha and Rio–Santos Dumont). Exact IATA coordination level and coordinator not confirmed in reachable sources. 🟥
- **Curfew / night restriction:** No hard night curfew confirmed for GRU (unlike Congonhas, which has a well-known jet-noise curfew) — verify current AIP. 🟧
- **CTOT / flow control:** Brazilian domestic ATFM/flow-management practice is expected for a congested, slot-coordinated hub; specifics not independently sourced for GRU. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** As the home-region base, schedule integrity (on-time pushback within the ~45/hour cap) has direct network-wide consequences; the shared São Paulo TMA congestion (Briefing §3.2) compounds any recovery from an off-schedule departure bank.
