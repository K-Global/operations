## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain 🟩
SBGL sits at 28 ft AMSL on flat land on **Ilha do Governador**, at the edge of **Guanabara Bay**. The aerodrome itself carries **no close-in high terrain**; Rio de Janeiro's well-known dramatic terrain (the granite peaks and the Serra dos Órgãos range) sits further inland and around the city core near Santos Dumont Airport (SBRJ), not directly at Galeão. This is a low-terrain-risk field for the aerodrome itself — verify the MSA ring on the current chart as routine practice.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic 🟧
SBGL is served by **Rio Approach ("Rio APP")** under **Curitiba (SBCW) FIR / ACC-CW (CINDACTA II)** — see the [South America Airspace briefing](../../../../airspace/south-america.md). The busy downtown **Santos Dumont Airport (SBRJ)**, roughly 9 NM south-west across Guanabara Bay, shares Rio terminal airspace and generates a real cross-traffic/coordination factor, even though SBGL is not itself capacity/slot-constrained the way GRU or Congonhas are. Brief for VFR mix and inter-airport coordination in the Rio TMA.

### 3.3 Runway excursion 🟧
Both runways at SBGL carry **displaced thresholds**: RWY 15 by 187 m, RWY 33 by 114 m — reducing the usable LDA on RWY 15/33 accordingly (§7). A real historical precedent exists on **RWY 15**: a December 1985 Air France Boeing 747 veered off the runway on landing after an uncommanded engine-power event, crossed a ditch and struck a wall on the cargo apron (fire destroyed the aircraft; no fatalities, all occupants evacuated). The lesson is generic — respect the displaced threshold and brief the excursion-recovery discipline for RWY 15/33 specifically.

### 3.4 Weather threat 🟧
Coastal/bay-side location at sea level: expect **sea-breeze effects**, **high humidity**, and **tropical afternoon/evening convective storms** typical of Rio's climate. No plateau-style radiation fog risk here (contrast the São Paulo-area fields at higher elevation) — SBGL's low elevation and coastal exposure make convective weather and gusty/shifting surface wind the more relevant seasonal threats. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations 🟧
Plan around: (1) **Santos Dumont (SBRJ) cross-traffic** in the shared Rio terminal airspace; (2) the **displaced thresholds on RWY 15/33** — do not assume full runway length is available for landing on either end; (3) since November 2016, **Terminal 1 check-in/baggage-claim has been closed** — all passengers use Terminal 2 to access boarding gates at either terminal, a connection-flow detail relevant to any ground-handling plan (§6/§13); (4) SBGL is **not** one of Brazil's publicly reported slot-coordinated fields, so schedule flexibility here is comparatively better than at GRU or Congonhas.

## 13. Ground operations

- **Stands for our types:** Two passenger terminals — **Terminal 1** (1977) and **Terminal 2** (1999). **Since November 2016, Terminal 1's check-in and baggage-claim areas are not in use** — all passengers use Terminal 2 to access boarding gates at either terminal. A new pier with 26 additional jet bridges and a new apron for ~97 aircraft were added by the RIOgaleão concessionaire (2016–17), materially expanding widebody capacity; exact current gate assignment for the K Global operation not confirmed. 🟧 An all-cargo apron sits at the original ("old Galeão") terminal area near the Air Force base.
