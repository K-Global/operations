## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain:** 🟥 The **Cerros Orientales** — the Eastern Cordillera ridgeline bounding Bogotá immediately to the east — rise to terrain in excess of **10,000 ft** within a few nautical miles of the field, against a field elevation of 8,361 ft. This is a **real, close-in CFIT threat**, not a background item: respect the charted MSA rigorously, do not descend below the sector MSA early, and treat any offset/circling approach with particular caution given the terrain proximity. Cross-ref [Briefing §3.1](index.md) and [`OM E — High-Elevation Aerodrome Operations`](../../../../../flight-ops/high-elevation-aerodrome-operations.md) §8, which names SKBO as a network example of the terrain/high-elevation co-hazard.
- **Specific threats:** Frequent **early-morning fog/mist** in the high-altitude bowl can reduce visibility sharply with limited notice (see [Briefing §3.4](index.md)) — this is the operational backdrop to the field's reported (unconfirmed) CAT III capability. Bimodal wet-season convective activity (Mar–May, Sep–Nov) adds turbulence/wind-shear exposure. No closely-spaced parallel or offset/converging approach configuration identified in reachable sources — both runways are conventional full-length parallels.
- **Airspace / traffic:** Dense, high-volume traffic as Colombia's primary hub, radar-controlled within the Bogotá FIR (SKED) terminal environment — cross-ref [Briefing §3.2](index.md).

## 6. Missed approach watch-items

- **Terrain-driven MAP notes:** The missed approach at SKBO is a **terrain problem first** — the Cerros Orientales bound the eastern side of the terminal area. Confirm the published missed-approach climb gradient and lateral path against terrain on the current chart before every approach; do not assume a standard missed-approach gradient is adequate without checking. 🟥
- **Re-sequencing environment:** Expect re-sequencing into a dense traffic flow given SKBO's role as Colombia's primary hub; holding patterns/vectoring should be anticipated. 🟧
- **Go-around traps:** At 8,361 ft, go-around/balked-landing climb gradients degrade with density altitude exactly as takeoff gradients do (see [`OM E — High-Elevation Aerodrome Operations`](../../../../../flight-ops/high-elevation-aerodrome-operations.md) §5) — confirm the aircraft meets the published missed-approach gradient at landing weight before commencing the approach, and brief the missed-approach path against terrain, not just against traffic.

## 10. Gotchas

- **Terrain, not traffic, is the primary missed-approach driver here** — brief the Cerros Orientales terrain picture on every arrival regardless of familiarity with the field.
- **Early-morning fog can appear with limited warning** — confirm current LVP/RVR status before committing to an early arrival in the fog-prone window.
- **Touchdown groundspeed is materially higher than a sea-level field for the same VREF** — recompute landing distance for the day rather than relying on habit from lower-elevation fields.
- **Runway-designator discrepancy** — confirm current AIRAC uses **14L/32R & 14R/32L**, not the legacy **13L/31R & 13R/31L**.
- **Tower/Ground frequencies are not confirmed** in reachable sources — do not assume the Briefing's frequency table is complete.
