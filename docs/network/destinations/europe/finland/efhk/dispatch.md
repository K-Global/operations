## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** **Terminals 1 and 2 merged into a single operating terminal on 21 June 2022**; internally divided **Schengen (gates 5–36)** and **non-Schengen/long-haul (gates 37–55)** rather than by building. Nameplate capacity ~16–17M pax/year (2025 actual 16.98M).
- **Our operation uses:** Most likely the **non-Schengen/long-haul section (gates 37–55)** for a scheduled international widebody service — not independently confirmed for our specific operation. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** Single consolidated arrivals/customs facility, H24, serving both Schengen and non-Schengen gate ranges within the same building.
- **Notes:** West Pier (opened Nov 2019, includes A380-capable Gate 48) and South Pier (2017, dual-boarding jet bridges at gates 52–55, 5 gates able to dual-park two narrowbodies) are the newest widebody-capable infrastructure. A third terminal was contemplated but **not decided/built**.

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** Non-Schengen area accommodates **8 widebody aircraft simultaneously**; **West Pier (9 gates, incl. A380-capable Gate 48)** and **South Pier (dual-boarding gates 52–55)** are the relevant widebody clusters. 🟩
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Schengen/domestic gates in the 5–36 range are not expected to be sized for scheduled widebody long-haul service without confirmation.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** AIP stand list (AD 2.20 §11) enumerates apron/stand numbering with PCN and VDGS availability by position; specific remote/hardstand use for our type not confirmed. 🟧 Note: stands 13/14/20/21/22 restrict certain narrowbody types (A319/E170) to special-permission docking due to reduced 1.80 m longitudinal clearance — a reminder that stand-specific constraints are individually documented in the AIP.
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect a contact widebody gate for a scheduled international arrival.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** Aircraft landing RWY 22L must not vacate via taxiway ZG unless specifically instructed; see [Briefing §13](index.md).

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** **Fully coordinated airport** per a Regulation (EC) 793/2004-equivalent regime — access requires an allocated slot except for state flights, emergencies, humanitarian flights. Coordinator: **Helsinki-Vantaa Slot Coordination Association ry**, requests via SITA/email/online coordination per IATA SSIM Ch.6 format. **PPR (prior permission required) for non-based aircraft.** 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** No blanket curfew, but **noise-driven runway-preference restrictions apply at night**: jet landings on RWY 33 and jet takeoffs on RWY 15 (both toward the SE, over populated areas) are avoided; propeller takeoffs to the SE are prohibited at night outright unless safety dictates otherwise.
- **CTOT / flow control:** Not independently confirmed beyond the fully-coordinated slot regime; expect standard EUROCONTROL Network Manager practice in peak/adverse conditions. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** Full slot coordination plus PPR for non-based aircraft makes schedule/slot compliance a real operational discipline; night noise-routing restrictions are a secondary scheduling consideration for late/early rotations.

## 5. Handling agents & turnaround

- **Handling agent(s):** **Airpro, Aviator, and Swissport** provide ground handling generally at Helsinki Airport; specific agent for our operation not confirmed. 🟧
- **Typical turnaround time for our type:** Not independently confirmed; VAMSYS mirror gives **taxi-in 12 min / taxi-out 16 min** for planning purposes `[VAMSYS mirror 2026-07-26]` — build the gate turn around these plus standard widebody service times. Note: Helsinki Airport's published minimum connection time is **35 minutes**, among the shortest in Europe — a proxy for the field's generally efficient ground-process design.
- **Services:** Push-back, GPU/PCA, potable water/lav, catering and cabin services expected at a major Nordic-hub Code E/F stand.
- **Turnaround risk items:** Winter de-icing throughput (two-tier gate/remote-apron system, §9) is the principal turnaround-stretching factor in season; CDM's TOBT/TSAT recalculation on a de-icing order should be built into the turn plan.

## 6. Load factors & seasonality

- **Demand peaks:** EFHK has historically anchored major Europe–Asia transit demand (140+ weekly flights to Asia by 2018, ~3.6M transfer pax in 2019) exploiting short northern-routing; the **February 2022 Russian-airspace closure** materially lengthened Asia routings and cut transfer traffic — 2024 traffic still ~25% below 2019 levels. 2026 network developments (Finnair Melbourne launch, China Southern Beijing Daxing, Emirates Dubai) point to a repositioning as a broader long-haul hub. 🟧
- **Day-of-week / seasonal pattern:** Not independently sourced for K Global's specific operation. 🟧
- **Cargo / belly capacity:** Dedicated **"Cool Nordic Cargo Hub"** freight terminal (35,000 m², opened Jan 2018, ~350,000 t nameplate / 450,000 t theoretical capacity); 2025 cargo throughput 184,004 tonnes (3rd-busiest Nordic airport by freight). Confirm K Global belly-cargo uplift plans against payload at planning. 🟧

## 9. De-icing provisioning & season

- **Availability:** **Available** — **two-tier system**: main **gate-position de-icing** via "Helsinki De-icing Supervisor" (127.025, HO), or a separate **Remote De-icing Apron (Aprons 6 and 8)** via "Remote De-icing Supervisor" (133.850, HO), reached outside the normal ATC manoeuvring area. Visual guidance (traffic-light-style displays) indicates bay number, "commencing," hold and "de-icing complete/all clear" states. 🟩
- **Season:** Consistent with EFHK's severe Nordic-winter climate — the harshest of the three fields in this pack; exact published start/end dates not confirmed. 🟧
- **Provisioning:** De-icing request window **0330–2330 UTC**, requested 20 min prior to TOBT; fully integrated into **Airport CDM** (Helsinki was the 7th European/1st Northern European CDM adopter, Oct 2012) — a de-icing order recalculates TSAT, and TOBT for de-icing flights = ready-time to *begin* de-icing, not ready-for-pushback. Dedicated winter-maintenance fleet of ~200 vehicles, including purpose-built Vammas PSB 5500 plough-sweeper-blowers and 1,200–1,500 hp snow blowers (able to clear a 3,500 m runway in as little as 13 minutes). Finavia began using recycled propylene-glycol de-icing fluid from Nov 2023 (~500 tonnes/season, ~1,500 t CO2 saved).
- **Commercial impact:** Winter snow/ice contamination (§3.4 Briefing) combined with the two-tier de-icing routing are the principal seasonal delay-risk drivers here — build winter schedule buffer accordingly, particularly if routed to the remote Apron 6/8. See [`OM E — Cold Weather Operations`](../../../../../flight-ops/cold-weather-operations.md).

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Winter snow/ice contamination** — the most severe seasonal exposure of the three Nordic fields in this pack, mitigated by an efficient two-tier de-icing/CDM system and a large dedicated winter-maintenance fleet, but still a recognised reliability factor.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Not independently quantified for EFHK this pass; the field's fully-coordinated slot regime and CDM discipline suggest efficient flow management generally. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Winter snow/ice events carry the highest schedule-risk exposure at this field; the shortest published minimum connection time in Europe (35 min) suggests otherwise efficient day-to-day ground processing. 🟧

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** No explicit noise-charge tariff found; the dominant documented noise mechanism is **runway-preference logic** rather than a per-movement charge (§4). 🟧
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** No blanket curfew exists; the **propeller-SE-departure ban at night** is an outright prohibition rather than a charge-based deterrent — relevant if operating alongside such traffic, not directly to K Global jet operations.
- **Scheduling guardrails:** Given full slot coordination and PPR-for-non-based-aircraft, on-time slot compliance is the primary scheduling guardrail at this field; night noise-routing restrictions are a secondary consideration for late-day/early-morning rotations on RWY 15/33.
