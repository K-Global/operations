## 3. Descent & energy plan

- **Top-of-descent trigger:** Standard descent-profile planning; the surrounding hilly terrain (§5) makes early, disciplined descent planning more important here than at a flat-field destination.
- **Speed control:** 250 KIAS below FL100 (EU/ICAO norm) — confirm current chart.
- **Altitude constraints:** Key STAR crossing constraints are pointers — fly the charted altitudes; verify MSA sectors against the current area chart given the terrain environment.
- **Energy traps:** 🟥 **Documented windshear on approach** (Iberia 1456, 2001) is the standout energy trap at this field — brief a stabilised-approach gate and an unhesitating go-around policy for any gusty, frontal, or otherwise disturbed wind day. ## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain:** 🟥 Bilbao is **surrounded by the hills of the Basque Country** at only 136 ft field elevation — **Iberia Flight 610** struck **Mount Oiz** on approach in 1985 with total loss (148 aboard). Quantify MSA sectors and named peaks from the current area/approach chart before any arrival here; this is a genuine, historically-demonstrated CFIT-risk field.
- **Specific threats:** 🟥 **Documented windshear on landing** — **Iberia Flight 1456** (2001) suffered a gear collapse after encountering windshear on approach; all aboard survived, 25 injured. Treat this as a standing, named threat for any arrival in disturbed wind conditions, not a generic caution.
- **Airspace / traffic:** 🟩 Moderate regional traffic under Bilbao APP radar service; no unusual airborne-conflict factor identified.

## 6. Missed approach watch-items

- **Terrain-driven MAP notes:** 🟥 The missed approach at LEBB is a **terrain-driven**, not traffic-driven, procedure given the surrounding hills — fly the published MAP exactly and verify climb-gradient/turn constraints on the current chart; do not improvise a shortcut climb given the Mount Oiz precedent.
- **Re-sequencing environment:** Moderate traffic density; re-sequencing under Bilbao APP radar is not itself the primary go-around concern at this field — terrain clearance is.
- **Go-around traps:** A go-around triggered by windshear on short final (per the 2001 precedent) should be flown as a firm, immediate go-around with full attention to the charted missed-approach terrain profile — do not attempt to "ride out" shear close to the ground.

## 7. Landing runway / LDA & braking / vacate

- **LDA by runway:** RWY 12 **2,540 m** (full length); RWY 30 **2,140 m** (460 m displaced threshold); RWY 10 **1,910 m**; RWY 28 **1,355 m** (555 m displaced threshold, and the last 90 m of RWY 10/28 is unusable for landing). Brief the correct displaced-threshold LDA per runway — do not assume full published runway length.
- **Braking / vacate:** Rapid-exit taxiways named in the AIP for RWY 12 (C3) and RWY 30 (C5) are indicated by dedicated indicator lights; confirm current vacate routing with Ground on the day.
- **Runway-excursion watch:** 🟧 Displaced thresholds on both RWY 28 and RWY 30 materially reduce usable landing distance — verify the correct LDA before committing to a landing, particularly for RWY 28 (1,355 m) with a heavier or faster type.

## 8. Taxi-in routing to terminal & hot spots

- **Typical taxi-in by runway → terminal:** Code E arrivals vacate RWY 12 via TWY C2 or RWY 30 via TWY C6, then taxi via TWY T to A2, proceeding to **PRKG 6A or PRKG 111** per assignment; VAMSYS mirror gives a planning taxi-in time of **10 min** `[VAMSYS mirror 2026-07-26]`.
- **Hot spots / tight taxiways:** 🟥 Multiple curved sections require **mandatory oversteer by Code E aircraft** (RWY12-C2, C2-T1, T1-C2, C2-RWY30, C6-T6, T6-C6) with wheel clearance under 4 m to the taxiway edge — taxi with vigilance and expect signalman/"SÍGAME" guidance at the gate transition.
- **Stand/gate notes:** See [Dispatch §3](dispatch.md) — **only PRKG 6A and PRKG 111 take Code E**; no fallback stand exists if both are occupied.

## 10. Gotchas

- **Documented windshear on approach** (Iberia 1456, 2001) — brief a firm go-around policy for any gusty/disturbed wind day; do not attempt to salvage a destabilised approach in shear conditions.
- **Surrounded by Basque hills with a historical CFIT accident** (Iberia 610, 1985, Mount Oiz) — verify MSA and terrain on the current chart; this is not a benign-terrain field.
- **PAPI RWY 12/30 is not usable for Code E aircraft** — do not rely on it for visual glidepath reference on a Code E arrival.
- **RWY 28 and RWY 30 both carry displaced thresholds** materially reducing LDA — confirm the correct number before committing to land.
- **Only two Code E stands exist (PRKG 6A / 111)** — a stand conflict on arrival has no fallback position; confirm allocation before descent if possible.
- **Field is not H24** — a late arrival requires PPR coordination in advance; do not plan on unconditional off-hours access.
