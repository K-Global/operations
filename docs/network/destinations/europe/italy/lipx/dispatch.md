## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** Historically a **split-function** arrangement — Terminal 1 used solely for departures and Terminal 2 (opened 2006) for arrivals, situated immediately adjacent to one another (Wikipedia-sourced). The operator has since launched a terminal-expansion programme (the "Romeo Project," approx. €68M, reported ~80% complete as of late 2024) adding security-checkpoint capacity and boarding bridges. 🟧 Current-day (post-expansion) terminal configuration not independently confirmed.
- **Our operation uses:** The single combined terminal complex; no separate international/domestic hall split identified beyond standard Schengen/non-Schengen processing. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** Standard passport-control processing on arrival; no pre-clearance programme identified.
- **Notes:** 🟧 Confirm current terminal/gate configuration with handling given the reported multi-year terminal-expansion project.

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** None identified — the main civil apron (Apron TWY P stands 31–37, Taxilane R stands 11–14/21–23, Taxilane M stands 41–47, Taxilane J stands 51–57 and 48) is narrowbody/regional-oriented; Code F aircraft taxiing on Apron TWY P must keep the outboard engine at idle per the AIP. 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Military remote stands and aprons (west side "rings") are operated by the Italian Air Force and are not for civil/commercial use.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** Not separately quantified in reachable sources; the main apron appears to be the standard civil parking area. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect a contact stand at the main civil apron for a scheduled narrowbody/regional turn.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** The apron and most of the civil taxiway up to (but excluding) the RWY 22 holding position are **not visible from the tower** due to obstruction — see [Briefing §13](index.md) and the Departure/Arrival taxi sections.

## 5. Handling agents & turnaround

- **Handling agent(s):** **AGS Handling** (opsvrn@agshandling.it) and **GH Verona** (ops-ctrl@ghverona.it) — both H24 for scheduled flights. A third agent, **Sky Services** (0800–2000, H24 on request), coordinates state/military traffic specifically. 🟧 confirm which of the two civil agents is contracted for our operation.
- **Typical turnaround time for our type:** Not independently confirmed; VAMSYS mirror gives **taxi-in 6 min / taxi-out 8 min** for planning purposes `[VAMSYS mirror 2026-07-26]` — build the gate turn around these plus standard narrowbody/regional service times. 🟧
- **Services:** Standard narrowbody/regional turnaround services expected (push-back, GPU, water/lav, catering, cabin clean); follow-me is **mandatory for ICAO Code E aircraft**, for helicopters, and whenever RVR ≤ 550 m on the main apron.
- **Turnaround risk items:** Winter fog/LVP activation (reduced ground-movement rate — one aircraft at a time when the tower cannot visually monitor the manoeuvring area), and the field's single-runway geometry (no parallel capacity to absorb a runway-occupancy delay), are the principal turnaround-risk drivers.

## 7. Fuel

- **Supplier / into-plane:** **CARBOIL** (ENI, Kuwait Petroleum Italia, Tamoil, Air BP brands) and **TAMOIL** — JET A1 only (no avgas). `[AIP Italia AD 2 LIPX 1-1]`

- **Price / tankering angle:** Not a network base — treat as a standard uplift-as-needed destination; assess tankering economics against the originating base per leg. See [OM E — Fuel Policy](../../../../../flight-ops/fuel-policy.md).

## 9. De-icing provisioning & season

- **Availability:** **Available H24, 15 November – 15 March.** `[AIP Italia AD 2 LIPX 1-1/1-3]` 🟩
- **Season:** Matches the published availability window (mid-Nov to mid-Mar) — a defined, AIP-stated season rather than an assumed one.
- **Provisioning:** 3× FMC LMD 2000 units + 1× Cristanini 6000/100 unit; fluid **Kilfrost ABC3 Type II**; 50,000 L storage; provided by the aerodrome operator.
- **Commercial impact:** The de-icing season overlaps directly with the field's peak winter-fog window (§10) — both compound into the same Nov–Mar period, making it the field's clearest seasonal delay-risk stack. See [OM E — Cold Weather Operations](../../../../../flight-ops/cold-weather-operations.md).

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Winter Po Valley fog.** RWY 04 carries the field's only instrument approach (ILS CAT IIIB, 75 m RVR operating floor); if wind or traffic favours RWY 22 in marginal visibility, there is **no instrument-approach fallback** at this field — a materially different reliability profile from a field with reciprocal-runway low-vis capability.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** No confirmed ATFM/CTOT regime (§4); the single-runway geometry means any runway-occupancy event (e.g. a Code D/E no-turn-pad scenario, §Briefing 3.3) has an outsized effect on the day's schedule. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Winter mornings (fog/LVP activation, overlapping de-icing demand) carry the field's highest schedule-risk exposure. 🟧
