## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain:** 🟥 **The field's defining hazard.** Mount Vesuvius (~1,281 m / 4,203 ft) stands roughly 10–15 NM SE/ESE; the Camaldoli ridge (~458 m) and the Naples city hill chain rise close-in to the N/NW. A 1958 departure from this field was lost on Vesuvius in fog — respect the published MSA without exception, particularly on any visual segment. Do not let the scenic Bay-of-Naples/Vesuvius view on final draw the aircraft below a safe profile.
- **Specific threats:** Single-runway field sharing local airspace with an adjacent US Navy installation — expect possible military traffic interaction (see Briefing §3.2). No wake, closely-spaced-parallel or converging-approach threat (single runway).
- **Airspace / traffic:** Radar-controlled TMA (Napoli Approach/Director); Roma FIR (LIRR) / Brindisi ACC (LIBB) en route context — no Italy FIR brief exists in-library (see Briefing §1/§18).

## 10. Gotchas

- **Vesuvius and the Camaldoli ridge ring the field** — never treat this as a routine flat-coastal approach; respect MSA on every arrival.
- **A 1958 fog-related crash on Vesuvius shortly after departure from this field** is a standing historical reminder of how quickly terrain plus reduced visibility becomes fatal here.
- **RWY 06's LDA (2,229 m) is meaningfully shorter than RWY 24's (2,438 m)** — do not assume symmetric landing performance between the two directions.
- **Curfew window is not consistently published** — do not plan an arrival that relies on the later of the two reported closure times.
- **Dual military/civil field** — be alert for possible interaction with adjacent US Navy air traffic in the local area.
- **South taxiway (TS) is notably narrower** than the north taxiway on taxi-in — a ground-routing caution for larger types.
