## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** A single passenger terminal serves EMA's leisure/low-cost carriers (Ryanair, Jet2.com, TUI Airways are the resident passenger operators); cargo operations (DHL, UPS, and other freight carriers) are handled through dedicated cargo-side facilities separate from the passenger terminal.
- **Our operation uses:** n/a for K Global — our role at EGNX is destination/cargo-freight, not scheduled passenger service. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** Customs and immigration confirmed H24 on field; not a planning factor for a cargo-only operation.
- **Notes:** 🟧 If K Global's role at EGNX evolves to include any passenger movement, terminal/stand assignment would need separate confirmation — not covered by this pass.

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** Not individually confirmed for freighter-class equipment; the public AIP describes apron surface/strength (Central, Central-West, East, West aprons, PCN 63–78 concrete) and code-letter taxiway constraints rather than a stand-by-stand assignment for cargo carriers. 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Taxiway C is restricted to Code C; taxilane MA is limited to Beech King Air (B350)-equivalent, Code B only — do not plan any K Global type onto these.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** General apron capacity exists across four apron areas; DHL/UPS-dedicated cargo ramp positions are not detailed in the public AIP. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Not confirmed — expect handling agent/airport authority-directed stand allocation (AIP: "Stand allocation will be by the Airport Authority").
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** Runway intersections **M and S are closed to aircraft larger than Code C** (max span 36 m); long-wheelbase Code F types (A340-600, A350-1000, A380-800, B777-300) are **strictly PPR** due to taxiway limitations — see [Briefing §13](index.md).

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** All scheduled flights (except GA/military) require prior approval of the Airport Managing Director **and** prior notification to **Airport Coordination Ltd (ACL)**, who act as the airport's coordination agent — a confirmed PPR/slot gate. Formal IATA Level 1/2/3 classification is not found in the reachable AIP text this pass. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **No general curfew.** East Midlands is one of the UK's few 24 h no-curfew airports, and this is the direct commercial reason DHL and UPS anchor major European overnight sort-hub operations here. The only night restriction is a ban on **Chapter-3-noncompliant jets** between 2300–0700 (2200–0600), which does not affect modern Chapter-3/4-compliant equipment. 🟧
- **CTOT / flow control:** No EGNX-specific EUROCONTROL ATFM/CTOT detail confirmed this pass; the field is not described as a major flow-constrained hub in reachable sources. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** The **absence of a general curfew** is EGNX's defining commercial advantage over most UK/European fields — it enables the overnight international-express sort wave that anchors DHL's and UPS's European hub operations here. Schedule integrity nonetheless depends on holding a live **ACL slot** and a **pre-booked handling agent** (a hard AIP requirement — see §5), not merely on curfew-avoidance.

## 5. Handling agents & turnaround

- **Handling agent(s):** Six named agents operate on field — **Signature Flight Support** (general/executive aviation), **Swissport** (passengers and cargo), **Aviation Solutions** (cargo only), **Donington Aviation** (general/executive aviation with hangarage), **Advantage Flight Support** (general/executive aviation), and **DHL Aviation** (own operations; ad-hoc cargo charters routed via Aviation Solutions). No single agent is confirmed as contracted for K Global specifically. 🟧
- **Typical turnaround time for our type:** Not confirmed; VAMSYS mirror marks taxi-in/taxi-out as **n/a** 🟧 (not set) for this field.
- **Services:** Full range of cargo handling equipment confirmed available to service aircraft up to **747-400 and An-124** scale, with specialist handling equipment available at short notice through cargo handling agents.
- **Turnaround risk items:** **All visiting aircraft must pre-book a designated handling agent prior to landing** — this is a hard AIP requirement; arriving without one booked is a real turnaround/acceptance risk. The ACL PPR/slot-approval requirement is itself a schedule-integrity gate distinct from ground-handling risk.

## 6. Load factors & seasonality

- **Demand peaks:** EGNX's defining demand pattern is **not** passenger-seasonal — it is the **overnight international express sort wave** that anchors DHL's and UPS's European hub operations here, running through the airport's 24 h no-curfew window.
- **Day-of-week / seasonal pattern:** The resident passenger carriers (Ryanair, Jet2.com, TUI Airways) show a conventional European leisure/business mix with summer peaking, but this is **not** representative of the K Global cargo-freight role at this field. 🟧
- **Cargo / belly capacity:** EGNX handles approximately **440,000 tonnes of cargo annually**, ranking as the UK's **second-busiest cargo airport (behind Heathrow)** and, per Manchester Airports Group, **"the UK's busiest 'pure' cargo airport,"** supporting 24/7 cargo logistics. Cargo handling is confirmed to 747-400/An-124 scale. `[Simple Flying / MAG, retrieved 2026-07-26]`
- **Connection banks:** The commercially significant wave here is the **overnight sort-hub bank** (inbound express freighters overnight, sorted, outbound before morning) consistent with the field's 24 h operation and the Chapter-3 restriction window bracketing normal hours — exact wave timing is not independently sourced this pass and is inferred from the general industry description of DHL/UPS hub operations here. 🟧

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** Likely low-visibility events affecting **RWY 09** specifically, given its CAT I/LTS CAT I-only capability — a fog event with wind favouring RWY 09 constrains arrivals more than the same event with wind favouring the CAT II/IIIB-equipped RWY 27. 🟧 (Not an independently sourced delay statistic — inferred from the runway-capability asymmetry.)
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Single-runway field — any runway-blocking event (disabled aircraft, contamination) fully closes the field with no parallel/reciprocal alternative. 🟧

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** Not confirmed in reachable sources — no EGNX-specific noise-surcharge structure found. 🟧
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** There is **no general curfew** to infringe; the only enforceable night restriction is the Chapter-3-noncompliant jet ban 2300–0700 (2200–0600), which is a certification-based restriction rather than a movement-cap curfew.
