## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** LPPR operates a **single, modern integrated terminal building** (linear layout with a central departures hall), reported publicly as having roughly 35 gates across three pier sections — a northern pier oriented toward international flights, a central pier for mixed operations, and a southern pier for domestic/low-cost service. 🟧 Sourced to tier-4 travel-guide material, not the AD 2 chapter — confirm current pier/gate structure before finalising a stand plan.
- **Our operation uses:** Expected assignment is the international-oriented pier for scheduled service; confirm with handling. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** International arrivals clear passport control within the single terminal building; customs/immigration confirmed H24 in the AIP.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** **Aerodrome Slot mandatory for all flights** per the AIP (referencing GEN 1.2) 🟥 — confirm slot status before planning; exact IATA coordination level (1/2/3) not separately confirmed in the reachable text.
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **Landing/takeoff forbidden by law 00:00–06:00** except a governmental exception regime capped at **11/day, 70/week, 2,100/year**. Noise-class-tiered exceptions: **Level 16 barred entirely 00:00–06:00**; **Levels 4 and 8 barred 02:00–05:00**. Force-majeure exceptions exist (humanitarian/emergency, technical failure, ATC disruption, Madeira/Azores weather diversions, weather-related late landings 05:00–06:00). 🟥
- **CTOT / flow control:** EUROCONTROL Network Manager ATFM/CTOT regulation is a reasonable expectation given the mandatory-slot regime; not independently LPPR-sourced this pass. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** The mandatory-slot requirement plus the hard curfew make **confirmed slot status ahead of schedule publication** and **buffer against the curfew window** the two commercial guardrails at this destination.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** Genuine low-visibility events (the AIP's published LVTO-to-125m-RVR procedure and LVP trigger imply real operational low-vis exposure) combined with the **corrected CAT II (not CAT III) ILS capability on RWY 17** — a lower-vis-capability field than commonly assumed, which can translate into real weather-driven delay/diversion exposure in marginal conditions.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Lower traffic density than LPPT; no capacity-ceiling signal found in reachable sources, but the mandatory-slot regime (§4) still governs schedule integrity. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Build in a wider weather buffer than a CAT III field would warrant, given RWY 17's actual CAT II capability and RWY 35's DVOR/RNP-only (no confirmed ILS) approach options. 🟧

---

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** Noise-class-tiered restrictions (§4) function as a direct scheduling constraint — Level 16 aircraft cannot operate at all in the night window, and Levels 4/8 are barred 02:00–05:00. Exact noise-charge tariff not confirmed in reachable sources. 🟧
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** The night ban 00:00–06:00 is a **hard movement restriction** by law, subject only to the capped exception regime (11/day, 70/week, 2,100/year) and documented force-majeure categories.
- **Scheduling guardrails:** Build buffer into the last rotation so an operational delay does not push a landing or departure into the curfew window; treat the **CAT II-only (not CAT III) low-vis capability on RWY 17** as a real weather-diversion risk factor ahead of the curfew itself in marginal-weather planning.
