## 2. Stand → runway taxi routing & hot spots

- **Typical routings by runway:** Confirm the exact taxi with Ground/Tower on the day; operational cross-check flags the **Taxiway A1/Taxiway A split** as a point of confusing signage en route to RWY 05R.
- **Hot spots / tight taxiways:** 🟥 **Taxiway A is reported unsuitable for heavy jets.** A **service road parallel to Taxiway H1 is not depicted on charts** and, being brightly lit at night, can be confused with the taxiway itself. Crews cleared to cross RWY 05L should confirm they remain on Taxiway A1 and have not been routed toward Terminal 2 or lined up on the runway.
- **Runway crossings / read-back-required points:** Any crossing of the closely-spaced parallel pair should be treated as a full read-back item given the reduced runway separation (Briefing §1).
- **Low-vis taxi caveats:** Runway/taxiway centreline lighting is reported **low-intensity with no centreline lights on some sections** — a material low-vis/night taxi caution. 🟧

## 3. Runway / SID selection logic

- **By wind / config:** Not independently confirmed as a formal wind-based preferential logic; the field's usual landing runway is 05R, with the reciprocal (23L/23R) used when wind dictates (see Arrival page).
- **By departure direction:** **RWY 05L departures are more obstacle-restricted than 05R**; **RWY 23L/23R departures carry a higher workload due to terrain**. An isolated spot elevation of **10,200 ft AMSL at 038°/22 NM** is a relevant consideration for any direct routing toward the north-east (e.g., toward Tampico/Pachuca-area waypoints).
- **Noise / preferential-runway program:** No MMMX-specific published NADP confirmed; fly the type's standard FCOM noise-abatement departure procedure. 🟧
- **Interaction with arrivals:** The **closely-spaced dependent-parallel geometry** (<300 m separation) means a departure off one runway interacts directly with arrival/departure traffic on the adjacent parallel — this is not an independent-parallel operation.

## 9. Performance watch-items for our types

- **Field-length / weight:** 🟥 Both runways are nominally long (~3,900–3,950 m), but MMMX is **thrust/climb-gradient-limited, not field-length-limited**, per [`OM E — High-Elevation Aerodrome Operations`](../../../../../flight-ops/high-elevation-aerodrome-operations.md) §4 — do not assume the long runway removes the constraint. Note the reduced usable **TORA/LDA on the displaced-threshold ends** (Briefing §7).
- **Density altitude / temperature:** 🟥 **7,316 ft field elevation — this is the dominant performance driver.** Expect a real RTOW/payload trade on warm-day departures; time-of-day planning (cooler morning departure where the network allows) can recover payload.
- **Contamination / wet-runway:** Rubber-contaminated, slow-draining runway surfaces reported near the runway ends (Briefing §3.3) — a wet-runway braking-action caution independent of the hot-and-high performance case.
- **Link to OM B:** See [`OM B — Fleet Capability Matrix`](../../../../../fleet/capability-matrix.md) (Table D, hot-and-high sensitivity) for the per-type tendency; compute the actual RTOW/gradient at dispatch for the day.

## 10. Gotchas

- **RWY 05L departures are more obstacle-restricted than 05R; RWY 23L/23R departures carry a higher workload due to terrain** — brief the assigned runway's specific obstacle picture.
- **Push-back from most Terminal 1 stands is reported to block the main taxiway** — expect delay and consider a one-engine-on-stand start technique where locally appropriate.
- **"Packs-off" high-elevation takeoff technique** is common — if packs are not reconfigured by 10,000 ft a cabin-altitude warning follows, and above 14,000 ft cabin altitude the emergency oxygen masks deploy; brief this explicitly to avoid a distraction trap during the climb.
- **Taxiway A is reported unsuitable for heavy jets**, and the **A1/A split** is a reported signage-confusion point.
- **A service road parallel to Taxiway H1 is not depicted on charts** and can be mistaken for the taxiway at night.
- **Low-intensity runway/taxiway lighting** with no centreline lights on some sections — a material night-taxi caution.
- **Hot/hung engine starts are a recognised risk at this elevation** — verify FCOM start technique before engine start.
- **Closely-spaced dependent parallel runways** — never assume independent simultaneous departure/arrival operations.
