## 3. Runway / SID selection logic

- **By wind / config:** **12L/12R** is the standard configuration; **30L/30R** is used specifically in **winter or heavy-rain conditions** per operational cross-check.
- **By departure direction:** **RWY 12R is the usual departure runway.** Reported SIDs route most departures to a waypoint on the airway at approximately **2,000 ft**, or direct to the **Havana FIR** boundary for eastbound/oceanic-adjacent routings.
- **Noise / preferential-runway program:** No MMUN-specific published NADP confirmed; fly the type's standard FCOM noise-abatement departure procedure. 🟧
- **Interaction with arrivals:** **12L** is the usual landing runway while **12R** is the usual departure runway — a standard segregated-parallel operation reported usable simultaneously, a materially simpler interaction picture than MMMX's closely-spaced dependent pair.

## 10. Gotchas

- **RWY 12R is the usual departure runway; 12L is the usual landing runway** — a standard segregated-parallel operation, contrast MMMX's closely-spaced dependent pair.
- **RWY 12L SIDs are based on the NCP VOR; RWY 12R SIDs on the CUN VOR** — confirm the correct VOR reference for the assigned departure runway.
- **Contact Tower/Clearance/Ground ~10 minutes prior to ETD** per operational cross-check — plan the clearance call accordingly.
- **30L/30R configuration is used in winter or heavy rain** — if assigned, note the PAPI-visibility caution carried over from the arrival picture (Briefing §3.3) applies to any circling/visual reference on those runways.
- **Hurricane season (Jun–Nov)** is the dominant seasonal departure-planning risk — check the tropical-cyclone forecast track before departing on any rotation transiting the region in season.
