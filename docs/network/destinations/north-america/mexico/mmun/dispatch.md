## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** **Four terminals** — more than any other Mexican airport. **Terminal 1** (charter/private aviation, historically closed for reconstruction/renovation with a reopening planned in the current cycle — verify status). **Terminal 2** (the original terminal; domestic and international flights, primarily to Central/South America plus a subset of long-haul European services). **Terminal 3** (dedicated exclusively to international flights, ~21 gates reported; the primary hall for major North American and European carriers). **Terminal 4** (opened October 2017, capacity expansion ongoing through the current cycle). 🟧
- **Our operation uses:** Not confirmed — most likely **Terminal 3** given its dedicated-international role, but confirm with the local handler. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** International arrivals clear immigration/customs in the assigned terminal (T2 or T3 depending on route); an inter-terminal shuttle service is reported running at short (~10-minute) intervals. 🟧
- **Notes:** 🟧 Terminal 1's status and Terminal 4's phased expansion are both reported as in-progress in the current cycle — confirm current gate/terminal assignment before finalising a stand plan.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** Not independently confirmed as formally slot-coordinated. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** None found in reachable sources. 🟧
- **CTOT / flow control:** No formal ATFM/CTOT program independently confirmed; expect seasonal peak-period (winter high season, spring break, Jul/Aug) sequencing delay rather than a structural capacity cap — contrast with MMMX's saturation declaration. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** 🟥 The dominant commercial-scheduling constraint at MMUN is **not** slots/curfew but **hurricane-season ground-stop risk (Jun–Nov)** — the airport has a documented history of suspending operations with limited notice ahead of an approaching storm, driving mass cancellations and fleet-repositioning decisions. Build this into seasonal schedule-resilience planning rather than treating MMUN as a routine leisure destination year-round.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** 🟥 **Tropical-cyclone/hurricane-season ground-stop risk (Jun–Nov, peak Aug–Oct)** — the airport has suspended operations for multi-day periods ahead of major storms historically (e.g., Hurricane Beryl, hundreds of cancelled flights). Outside hurricane season, heavy-rain events can trigger a runway-configuration switch (12L/12R → 30L/30R) with associated PAPI-visibility caution (Briefing §3.3).
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Seasonal peak-period (winter high season, spring break) sequencing delay expected; no structural capacity-cap signal found (contrast MMMX). 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Hurricane season is the dominant annual reliability risk window; build schedule/fleet-positioning contingency for it specifically rather than treating MMUN as risk-uniform year-round. 🟧
