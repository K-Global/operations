## 2. STAR / transition selection

- **STARs (names only — verify current AIRAC):** ULUBA ONE (per AIP chart index) — verify current assignment. 🟧
- **Selection by arrival direction / runway:** RWY 10 is the routine arrival runway given its published RNAV/VOR procedures; RWY 28 arrivals are flown as a vectored visual approach regardless of equipment carried.
- **Transition to approach:** Juliana Approach vectors inbound traffic to the RWY 10 VOR Z final (or equivalent) in routine ops, using the field's two radar systems (50 NM/250 NM range).
- **Speed / flow constraints on the STAR:** Standard 250 KIAS below FL100 — verify any local constraint on the current chart.

---

## 3. Descent & energy plan

- **Top-of-descent trigger:** Standard descent-profile planning to meet the first STAR/vector altitude; no unusual early-descent trigger identified.
- **Speed control:** 250 KIAS below FL100 (standard); confirm any local speed constraint on the current chart.
- **Altitude constraints:** Key constraint is remaining above the charted MSA over the high ground north/northwest of the field until established on final.
- **Energy traps:** The **RWY 10 approach is very low and close-in over Maho Beach with essentially no displaced-threshold buffer** — arrive stabilised early; do not plan to salvage a high/fast approach by "floating" over the threshold. A late runway-configuration change (10↔28) driven by a wind shift is also an energy trap given RWY 28's visual-only requirement.

## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain:** 🟥 High ground (obstacle-charted to ~1,118 ft) lies roughly 3–4 NM north/northwest of the field, off the RWY 28 extended centreline — the direct reason RWY 28 has no published instrument approach. Do not descend below charted MSA/procedure altitudes on the approach or missed approach — cross-ref [Briefing §3.1](index.md).
- **Specific threats:** 🟥 The **RWY 10 approach crosses Maho Beach at very low height** on a nominal 3° path with minimal buffer before the threshold — a stabilised-approach discipline is mandatory; there is a documented history of jet-blast injury to bystanders at the perimeter fence, underscoring how little margin exists at this threshold. No wind-shear detection equipment is installed on the field (Briefing §3.4) — treat gust-front/convective shear as unmonitored.
- **Airspace / traffic:** 🟩 Radar-equipped Juliana Approach/Tower; moderate traffic density outside peak banks; the same facility also handles approach control for several neighbouring island fields.

---

## 6. Missed approach watch-items

- **Terrain-driven MAP notes:** 🟥 Fly the published missed approach precisely — high ground north/northwest of the field is the operative concern on both a RWY 10 and a RWY 28 (visual) go-around; do not improvise a shortcut turn.
- **Re-sequencing environment:** Single-runway field with radar coverage — expect vectoring back into the approach sequence rather than a complex parallel-runway re-sequencing problem.
- **Go-around traps:** A RWY 28 go-around returns the aircraft toward the same high terrain that precludes an instrument approach to that runway in the first place — brief this explicitly before any RWY 28 visual arrival.

## 7. Landing runway / LDA & braking / vacate

- **LDA by runway:** RWY 10 **2,200 m**; RWY 28 **2,200 m** — both reduced relative to the 2,300 m full runway pavement length; no RESA published (strip only 2,320×150 m). Cross-ref [Briefing §7](index.md).
- **Braking / vacate:** No rapid-exit taxiway detail confirmed in reachable sources; confirm with Ground/Tower on the day. 🟧
- **Runway-excursion watch:** 🟥 Short LDA with no published RESA on either end — every landing is effectively a performance-limited-weight case with minimal error margin for a long/fast touchdown; verify current-day landing performance, do not assume prior-trip margins.

## 8. Taxi-in routing to terminal & hot spots

- **Typical taxi-in by runway → terminal:** After landing, taxi in via taxiways A–E to the single apron (TNCM A); VAMSYS mirror gives a planning taxi-in time of **12 min** `[VAMSYS mirror 2026-07-26]`.
- **Hot spots / tight taxiways:** 🟥 Taxiways F, G1, G2 and H are narrow (5.0–11.0 m) and PCN 9-rated — not suitable for K Global aircraft; confirm the assigned taxi-in routing uses A–E only. Cross-ref [Briefing §13](index.md).
- **Stand/gate notes:** See [Dispatch §3](dispatch.md) — single apron, widebody-capable position count not independently confirmed.

## 10. Gotchas

- **RWY 10 arrival is a very low, close-in approach over Maho Beach** — fly stabilised, do not attempt to stretch a long landing given the short/reduced LDA.
- **RWY 28 is never an instrument arrival** — visual only; if visual conditions cannot be maintained, expect vectoring for RWY 10 or a diversion, not a RWY 28 instrument backup.
- **High ground north/northwest of the field** drives both the missed-approach and the RWY 28 visual-only requirement — respect charted MSA at all times.
- **No wind-shear detection equipment** — increase vigilance for gust-front effects, especially on the low RWY 10 approach over open water/beach.
- **Documented jet-blast hazard at the RWY 10 threshold/perimeter fence** — a real ground-safety factor, not just a spotting curiosity; relevant to any go-around/balked-landing consideration near the fence line.
- **Hurricane season (Jun–Nov)** carries real diversion/closure risk — the field was severely damaged and closed for weeks after Hurricane Irma (2017).
