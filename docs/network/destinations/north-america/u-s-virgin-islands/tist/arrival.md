## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain:** 🟥 High terrain is recorded close north of the RWY 10/28 extended centreline. A **hill 4 NM SE carries lights that can be mistaken for the runway 10/28 lighting pattern** when flying a visual approach from the south — a standing, FAA-sourced caution, not a generic terrain-awareness reminder. Cross-ref [Briefing §3.1](index.md).
- **Specific threats:** **Night visual illusion on the RWY 10 approach** — false vertical-guidance cues from visual cues alone; fly the ILS glide path and cross-check the altimeter. A **~50 ft AGL sailboat mast sits ~100 ft west of the RWY 10 approach threshold** — a genuine close-in obstacle on short final. **RWY 28 has no straight-in instrument approach** (circling VOR-A only) and its usable landing distance is cut to **3,870 ft** by a 2,300 ft displaced threshold — a materially different, more demanding case than a RWY 10 landing.
- **Airspace / traffic:** San Juan CERAP (TJZS) works a dense mix of inter-island commuter/GA/airline traffic converging on this small field from multiple directions (St. Croix, St. John, the BVI, San Juan) — expect a more procedurally active environment than the airport's size alone would suggest. Cross-ref [Briefing §3.2](index.md).

## 6. Missed approach watch-items

- **Terrain-driven MAP notes:** The missed approach for either runway is a terrain question first — high ground close north of the field is the operative constraint; verify the charted missed-approach climb gradient and turn constraints rather than assuming a generous margin at this tightly-sited field. 🟥
- **Re-sequencing environment:** San Juan CERAP will need to re-sequence a missed approach back into a traffic mix that includes inter-island commuter and GA arrivals — expect vectoring rather than an immediate re-approach.
- **Go-around traps:** A go-around from a RWY 28 approach compounds the terrain question with the runway's non-precision, circling-only character — brief this case specifically rather than treating it as a generic go-around.

## 10. Gotchas

- **Do not fly the RWY 10 visual picture alone at night** — use the ILS glide path and cross-check the altimeter; a recorded visual-illusion hazard exists here.
- **Do not mistake the lit hill 4 NM SE for the runway on a visual approach from the south.**
- **RWY 28 is a different approach altogether** — circling-only, no straight-in instrument approach, and a landing distance barely half of RWY 10's — brief it specifically, don't treat it as "the other end of the same runway."
- **Sailboat-mast obstruction close-in on the RWY 10 approach** — a genuine, low but close obstacle on short final.
- **Runway-crossing readback discipline** — standing FAA caution; treat every crossing instruction as a discrete, briefed event during taxi-in.
- **Currently active nightly construction runway closure (2200–0500 AST, 01 Dec 2025–31 Dec 2026)** — verify live NOTAM before planning any arrival near the edges of the operating day.
