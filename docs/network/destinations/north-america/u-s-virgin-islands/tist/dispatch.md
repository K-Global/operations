## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** Single terminal building; first floor ~190,000 sq ft with 11 gates, second floor ~60,000 sq ft. No separate international/domestic hall split identified beyond the CBP Foreign Inspection Station used for international general-aviation arrivals.
- **Our operation uses:** The single terminal/gate complex — no alternative concourse exists at this field.
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** International/general-aviation arrivals process through a Foreign Inspection Station (FIS) on the ramp at a designated Foreign Arrivals Entrance; government CBP desk hours are 0700–1800 AST. 🟧
- **Notes:** The terminal sustained substantial damage from two Category 5 hurricanes in September 2017 and was subsequently repaired; a three-level parking facility was added in 2025, and the airport operator has selected a private-partner redevelopment programme for further modernisation — confirm current terminal condition/capacity if this becomes an active route. 🟧

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** None identified — non-coordinated U.S. domestic field. 🟩
- **Curfew / night restriction:** No formal noise curfew found. **ARFF is unavailable 2300–0600 daily** — a real operational constraint on any late-night arrival/departure or diversion planning. Separately, a **dated construction NOTAM currently closes the runway nightly 2200–0500 AST, effective 01 Dec 2025 through 31 Dec 2026**, for a pavement-resurfacing project — this is a time-limited restriction tied to a specific project window, not a permanent curfew; re-verify current status against live NOTAM, particularly as the stated end date approaches. 🟥
- **CTOT / flow control:** No ATFM/CTOT regime identified for this field.
- **Commercial impact:** The construction-closure window materially compresses the usable operating day for any schedule touching the edges of 2200–0500 AST while it remains in effect; build schedule buffer around it and re-confirm before committing a timetable that depends on late or very early movements here.

## 8. Customs / immigration & PoE for pax

- **Port of Entry:** Yes — U.S. Customs & Border Protection Port of Entry, processed through a Foreign Inspection Station on the ramp.
- **CBP / immigration hours:** Government desk hours **0700–1800 AST**. 🟧 One on-field FBO separately advertises 24/7 customs-clearance capability for general-aviation traffic; applicability/relevance to a scheduled commercial K Global operation is not confirmed.
- **Pre-clearance / visa-transit notes:** As a U.S. territory, domestic U.S.-origin passengers do not require a passport for USVI travel, but onward travel to the continental United States is treated as re-entry requiring standard document checks — a routine pax-flow planning factor, not a dispatch action item.
- **Late/overnight/diversion caveat:** The 0700–1800 AST government CBP window, combined with ARFF being unavailable 2300–0600, makes very late or very early international movements a genuine planning constraint here. 🟧

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** Tropical convective weather and, seasonally, Atlantic tropical storm/hurricane activity (Jun–Nov, peak Aug–Oct); the single-runway, no-crosswind-alternative geometry means any wind excursion outside comfortable limits is a real delay/diversion driver rather than a manageable non-issue.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Not independently sourced as a formal ATFM statistic; treat the currently active nightly construction closure (§4) as an additional, time-limited exposure while it remains in effect. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Hurricane-season months and any late-evening/early-morning schedule proximity to the construction-closure window carry the highest schedule-risk exposure at this field. 🟧

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** Not confirmed in reachable sources. 🟧
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** No formal noise curfew was found to carry a charge/penalty regime; the operative hard constraints are the ARFF coverage gap (2300–0600) and, while in effect, the construction-driven nightly runway closure (2200–0500 AST) — a movement attempted outside the runway's available hours during that window is not merely penalised, it is not possible.
- **Scheduling guardrails:** Build buffer so that no rotation depends on operating in the 2200–0600 window while the construction closure remains in effect (through 31 Dec 2026 per the current published project dates); re-verify the closure's status at each planning cycle as the end date approaches.
