## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** A single historic **Main Terminal** (eligible for the National Register of Historic Places) houses ticketing, the small loading-bridge-equipped "Z Gates," and the **International Arrivals Building (IAB)** for CBP/immigration/agriculture processing. Four **midfield concourses — A, B, C and D** — sit apart from the Main Terminal and are reached by the **AeroTrain** automated people mover (serving the Main Terminal and Concourses A, B and C) or by **mobile lounges/"plane mates"** (serving all concourses, including D, and providing the dedicated sterile-corridor link to the IAB for international arrivals).
- **Our operation uses:** Concourses A/B (≈1.1M sq ft combined; 31 loading-bridge gates for international/domestic carriers plus 31 hardstand positions for regional aircraft) and Concourses C/D (≈923,000 sq ft; 58 positions, 50 loading-bridge gates, historically United-operated) are the widebody-capable clusters; confirm current gate assignment for a K Global arrival with handling. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** International arrivals are bused via mobile lounge or "plane mate" directly to the **International Arrivals Building**, a dedicated sterile corridor separate from the AeroTrain — this is a materially different (and typically slower) connection flow than a jet-bridge-direct arrival; build connection time accordingly.
- **Notes:** 🟧 A significant Master Plan update (adopted July 2025) targets extending AeroTrain coverage and eventually phasing out the mobile-lounge fleet over a multi-decade horizon — confirm current-year terminal/connection routing before finalising a schedule assumption.

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** Concourse B's 41 loading bridges are documented as configured for large widebody aircraft used primarily by international carriers; Concourses C/D add further loading-bridge capacity. **Two gates specifically support A380 upper- and lower-deck boarding.** 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Concourse A/B's 31 hardstand regional positions are sized for regional/narrowbody equipment — do not plan a widebody onto them.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** 31 hardstand regional positions at Concourse A/B; the runway/taxiway system carries FAA-approved Modifications of Standards for **Group VI** (A380/B747-8-class) aircraft, meaning some Group VI movements follow specifically approved routes rather than unrestricted taxi. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect a contact widebody gate at Concourse B or C/D for a scheduled arrival; confirm with handling.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** **Taxilane A (between A1–A5) is closed to aircraft with a wingspan over 118 ft**; **TWY E1 is restricted to aircraft under 79 ft wingspan**; **B747-8 aircraft are capped at 17 kt taxi speed on TWY J** — see [Briefing §13](index.md) and the Departure/Arrival taxi sections.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** None identified — no IATA Level 2/3 slot-coordination regime found for KIAD in reachable sources. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **None.** KIAD explicitly operates without nighttime flight restrictions, unlike sister-airport KDCA's nighttime noise rule. Overnight **engine run-ups (2200–0700 local) require prior Airport Operations approval**, and flight training is prohibited 2200–0700 local — neither restricts scheduled airline movements. 🟩
- **CTOT / flow control:** No slot-specific CTOT regime identified; standard FAA ATCSCC ground-delay/flow-management programs apply as for any major US gateway during adverse weather or high-density periods. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** The absence of a curfew or slot ceiling removes the two biggest scheduling constraints seen at many international hubs — KIAD's principal schedule-integrity risk is winter weather/de-icing throughput and PCT flow programs rather than a hard regulatory movement cap.

## 8. Customs / immigration & PoE for pax

- **Port of Entry:** Yes — the **International Arrivals Building**, rated to process ≈2,400 passengers/hour; processed 3,812,781 passengers in 2024 per the MWAA/CBP-linked figure. 🟧
- **CBP / immigration hours:** Not confirmed in reachable public sources; assumed coverage aligned to scheduled international arrival banks. 🟧
- **Pre-clearance / visa-transit notes:** Standard US CBP entry per nationality; international arrivals are kept in a **sterile corridor** (mobile lounge/"plane mate" direct to the IAB) separated from domestic passengers per the Immigration and Nationality Act — no pre-clearance programme at KIAD.
- **Late/overnight/diversion caveat:** No curfew constrains a late arrival at KIAD; the operative late-arrival risk is **IAB desk staffing outside peak international-bank hours**, which is not independently confirmed. 🟧

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Winter snow/ice and de-icing throughput**, and PCT flow-management programs during Mid-Atlantic convective weather or DC-metro traffic saturation.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Elevated given operation inside the dense, multi-airport Potomac Consolidated TRACON complex (KIAD/KDCA/KBWI) and the overlay of the DC SFRA — expect ATCSCC flow management in peak/adverse conditions. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Winter storm events and summer convective-weather afternoons carry the highest schedule-risk exposure at this destination. 🟧
