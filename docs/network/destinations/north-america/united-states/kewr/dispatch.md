## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** **Terminal A** (fully rebuilt, reopened 2023, 33 gates) — domestic/short-haul carriers. **Terminal B** (opened 1973, home to over 30 airlines and most non-United international/mixed-carrier traffic; served ~11.5M passengers in 2025 against an original design capacity of ~6.8M — a near-term $200M modernisation is funded ahead of a full replacement planned for the mid-2030s). **Terminal C** — exclusively **United Airlines** and United Express, three concourses (C-1/C-2/C-3), 68 gates total; the **C-3 international concourse** is purpose-built for widebody international service (up to 12 widebody or 19 narrowbody aircraft).
- **Our operation uses:** 🟧 Not independently confirmed for a K Global service pattern; a long-haul international arrival/departure would most plausibly route through **Terminal B** or the **Terminal C-3 international concourse** depending on carrier grouping — confirm gate assignment with handling before finalising a stand plan.
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** International arrivals clear passport control in Terminal B (Global Entry and Mobile Passport Control kiosks available); confirm connection-time implications if the onward network leg departs from a different terminal — inter-terminal movement at EWR is via the **AirTrain** monorail (operates 24/7; a new $3.5B system is planned to enter service around 2030).
- **Notes:** 🟧 Terminal B is **mid-redevelopment** (near-term $200M programme, full replacement targeted mid-2030s) — confirm current gate/concourse assignment with handling before finalising a stand plan.

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** Terminal C-3 international concourse (United-operated, up to 12 widebody or 19 narrowbody) is the confirmed purpose-built widebody cluster; Terminal B's widebody gate allocation is not confirmed amid its ongoing redevelopment programme. 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Terminal A's domestic/short-haul gate footprint is not sized for scheduled widebody international service — do not plan a widebody onto Terminal A.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** Not confirmed in reachable sources — verify with handling if a contact gate is unavailable. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect a contact widebody gate for a scheduled international destination turn, subject to confirmation.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** Several taxiways are **wingspan-restricted** (>171 ft / >118 ft) — see [Briefing §13](index.md) and the Departure/Arrival taxi sections; confirm current routing for any Code F tail.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** **None (EU/IATA-style Level 2/3 regime)** — Newark is not IATA slot-coordinated in the European sense. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **No formal curfew** identified in reachable sources (Port Authority terms of operation, sourced 2011 — status not independently re-confirmed as current in every particular). Overnight **engine run-up restrictions** do apply (§11). 🟧
- **CTOT / flow control:** The operative capacity constraint is an **FAA administrative rate order** capping combined arrivals+departures at **72/hour** (through 24 Oct 2026, eased from a 28–34/hour construction-period cap earlier in 2025); routine ATC ground-delay-program (GDP) assignment during flow events should be treated as standard practice, not exceptional, at this field. 🟥
- **Commercial impact:** Schedule reliability at Newark is dominated by **airspace/ATC capacity constraints** rather than a slot regime — build schedule buffer around known peak-bank congestion and treat any FAA flow-program notice as a first-order planning input.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Airspace/ATC capacity constraints** — the dominant reliability factor at KEWR, not weather or handling. The FAA's ongoing Philadelphia TRACON Area C transition (relocated from New York TRACON/N90 in 2024 amid staffing shortfalls) produced multiple 2025 telecommunications/radar outages and prompted the current administrative rate-cap order. 🟥
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** High and quantified — BTS data for July 2025 recorded a **63.65% total on-time rate** at KEWR (60.7% arrivals, 66.6% departures), with Newark and LaGuardia both ranking among the most-delayed US airports for 2025. 🟥
- **On-time reliability picture:** Treat KEWR as a **structurally delay-prone field** for scheduling purposes — build buffer into connections and rotations touching this airport regardless of season; winter weather (§9) compounds the underlying airspace-capacity constraint. 🟧

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** KEWR is designated a "Noise Sensitive Airport, 24 hours" with a historical departure noise limit of ~112–112.9 PNdB measured at community noise monitors (sourced 2011 — not independently re-confirmed current). 🟧 No specific per-movement noise surcharge confirmed as currently in force.
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** No formal curfew identified — the operative commercial constraint at this field is the **FAA rate-cap order**, not a curfew/noise-charge regime in the EU sense.
- **Scheduling guardrails:** Build buffer around the FAA's administrative hourly rate cap (currently 72 combined ops/hour through 24 Oct 2026) and around known ATC flow-program exposure (§10) rather than around a curfew window.
