## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** Five terminals were active going into 2026 — **Terminal 1**, **Terminal 4** (Delta's primary hub terminal, the busiest at JFK), **Terminal 5** (JetBlue's exclusive terminal, the former TWA Flight Center building), **Terminal 7**, and **Terminal 8** (American Airlines; a reported USD 125M commercial redevelopment underway in 2026). Terminal 2 closed in 2023; Terminal 3 was demolished in 2013. **New Terminal One** — an all-international, 23-gate facility being built on the former Terminal 1/2/3 site, directly adjacent to Terminal 4's Concourse A — has its **Phase A (initial widebody gates) scheduled to open in 2026**, with full 23-gate completion reported around 2030. A new **Terminal 6** (JetBlue-adjacent, north side) is bringing its **first gates into service in 2026**, with full completion (10 gates) reported for 2028; Terminal 5 and Terminal 6 are planned to eventually connect into a combined JetBlue-anchored campus. 🟧 *(current-affairs/secondary sourcing — confirm exact 2026 gate counts before finalising a stand plan.)*
- **Our operation uses:** As the home hub, plan for a **widebody international gate** in whichever terminal our K Global operation is assigned — pending confirmation of the current (transition-year) terminal programme. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** Federal Inspection Services (FIS) historically located in Terminals 1, 4, 7 and 8; connecting passengers re-clear security after FIS before any domestic onward leg — build connection time accordingly.
- **Notes:** 🟧 The terminal programme is **mid-transition in 2026** — New Terminal One Phase A and the first Terminal 6 gates are both opening this year while Terminal 1's older facility winds down. Confirm current gate/terminal assignment with handling before finalising a stand plan.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** JFK is one of three US airports (with LGA and DCA) operating under a **direct FAA Order** rather than an independent IATA-style coordinator; the FAA nonetheless generally applies the **IATA Worldwide Slot Guidelines (WSG)** framework for scheduling-constraint considerations (terminal, gate, customs, curfew) `[89 FR 41486]`. 🟥
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **None** — the field operates H24 (confirmed, Briefing §6). Noise-abatement restrictions are handled administratively (contact line, §Briefing §12), not via a movement ban. 🟩 **Note:** the "Perimeter Rule" sometimes associated with New York-area airports is a **LaGuardia (KLGA)-specific** distance restriction — it does **not** apply to JFK and should not be assumed here.
- **CTOT / flow control:** JFK's own scheduling is capped by the **FAA Order at 81 operations/hour, 0600–2259 local**, with an **80% minimum-usage rule** for Operating Authorizations (OAs) and FAA-run reallocation/lottery procedures for withdrawn or surrendered OAs `[89 FR 41486]`. Separately, EDCT/ground-delay-program flow control is common in practice given N90/JFK airspace density, independent of the scheduling cap. 🟥
- **Delayed-flight nuances:** The Order's 81/hour figure is a **scheduling** cap (i.e., governs how many operations may be *scheduled* in a given hour), not a same-day tactical ATC limit — but combined with routine ATFM/EDCT flow management, on-time pushback within the assigned Operating Authorization window is the primary commercial guardrail here.
- **Commercial impact:** Schedule integrity against the assigned OA time, and buffer against ATFM/EDCT ground-delay exposure (winter nor'easter and summer convective seasons especially), are the two biggest scheduling guardrails at the home hub.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **N90/JFK airspace and traffic density**, compounded by the airfield's own intersecting non-parallel runway geometry and the converging RWY 13R/22L dependent-approach procedure (Briefing §3.2/§3.3) — this combination is JFK's defining reliability threat rather than any single weather phenomenon.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** High — JFK is a frequent origin/destination of FAA ground-delay programs and EDCTs, driven by winter nor'easters, summer convective weather, and routine N90-area capacity constraints; the FAA-Order scheduling cap (§4) also structurally bounds how much demand can be scheduled into the busiest hours.
- **On-time reliability picture:** Winter nor'easter events and the 2026 terminal-transition period (New Terminal One Phase A / Terminal 6 first gates) carry the highest schedule-risk exposure at the home hub. 🟧

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** Noise-sensitive airport with an active restrictions/queries contact line (212-435-3782, normal business hours); historic Port Authority measures (a 1959 departure noise limit and a 1986 per-exceedance penalty) are dated tier-4 historical figures — current fee schedule not independently confirmed. 🟧
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** **Not applicable — JFK has no hard curfew** (H24 operation confirmed). Do not confuse this with LaGuardia's separate Perimeter Rule or curfew-style considerations, which do not apply here.
- **Scheduling guardrails:** The FAA-Order 81-ops/hour scheduling cap (§4) — not a curfew — is the binding commercial constraint; build the schedule to the assigned Operating Authorization windows and hold buffer against routine ATFM/EDCT exposure in winter/summer peak-weather seasons.
