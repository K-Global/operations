## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** **Terminal 2** — East Concourse (~13 gates, numbered in the low 20s) and West Concourse (~19 gates, into the 50s), each with its own security checkpoint; the field's established international/major-carrier terminal. **Terminal 1** — a new facility opened in phases from September 2025 (Phase 1A: 19 gates, ticketing hall with 69 kiosks/66 counters, 13-lane security checkpoint), growing to 30 gates by early 2028 as later phases complete; primarily domestic-oriented.
- **Our operation uses:** **Terminal 2** for an international widebody arrival/departure — confirm exact gate assignment with handling given the field's restricted-gate-space profile. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** Terminal 2 hosts the international arrivals/CBP processing function; confirm current hall capacity and connection-time implications for any onward network leg. 🟧
- **Notes:** 🟧 Terminal 1's multi-year phased rollout (2025–2028) may shift gate allocation/available capacity across the field over the life of this brief — re-verify at each update cycle.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** None identified in reachable sources — KSAN is not confirmed as an IATA Level 2/3 coordinated field. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **No hard legal curfew**, but a long-standing (since 1976) voluntary/regulatory nighttime noise-restriction program: takeoffs generally restricted **23:30–06:30**, with additional Stage-3-equivalent/EPNdB-based restrictions **22:00–23:30** and **06:30–07:00** for aircraft exceeding 104 EPNdB at the takeoff reference point. Nighttime residential noise is limited to 70 dB. 🟥
- **CTOT / flow control:** No dedicated ATFM/CTOT program identified for KSAN specifically; standard FAA domestic flow-management applies in marine-layer or capacity events given the single-runway constraint. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** Every scheduled operation at KSAN should be built with a firm buffer against the 23:30 restriction window — a late-day delay risks a civil-penalty-bearing infringement rather than a simple schedule slip, since there is no hard closure to force an earlier decision the way a legal curfew would.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Coastal marine-layer stratus/fog**, concentrated October–February, reported as the field's number one weather-related cause of delay, diversion and cancellation.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Elevated during marine-layer events given the **single-runway constraint** — any reduced-visibility approach restriction directly caps the field's arrival rate with no parallel runway to absorb the loss. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Early-morning arrivals in the Oct–Feb fog season carry the highest schedule-risk exposure; build buffer and a firm KLAX/KLAS diversion trigger for this window. 🟧

---

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** Nighttime noise-restriction program applies tiered EPNdB thresholds (approximately 90 EPNdB daytime 07:00–19:00, 85 EPNdB evening 19:00–22:00, 80 EPNdB night 22:00–07:00) at the takeoff reference point, with escalating civil penalties for infringement (see below) rather than a per-movement noise surcharge structure. 🟧
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** Violations of the time-of-day noise restriction carry escalating financial penalties assessed by an airport noise-review panel: **$2,000** (first infraction in a rolling six-month period), **$6,000** (second), **$10,000** (third) — a real, non-trivial cost exposure for late-running operations, though not a hard movement ban.
- **Scheduling guardrails:** Build a firm buffer into the last scheduled rotation of the day so that a normal operational delay does not push a departure into the 22:00–23:30/23:30–06:30 restricted windows; treat any schedule slippage toward the evening restriction as a financial-exposure trigger, not merely an operational inconvenience.
