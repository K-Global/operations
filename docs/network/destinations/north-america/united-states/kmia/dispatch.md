## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** KMIA is organised into **three terminal areas — North, Central and South — spanning six concourses (D through J), for a reported ~131 gates total.** **North Terminal (Concourse D, 51 gates)** is used exclusively by a single carrier (not named per governance) and its regional/commuter operation. **Central Terminal (Concourses E 18 gates, F 19 gates, G 14 gates)** is mixed use: Concourse E serves the anchor carrier and its alliance partners plus some Caribbean/Latin American carriers (E's satellite has an A380-capable gate); Concourses F and G serve other domestic/Canadian carriers. **South Terminal (Concourse H 13 gates, Concourse J 15 gates)** is the primary non-alliance international terminal — H is largely narrowbody international (Central/northern South America) plus some widebody, and J is the main widebody international terminal (also A380-capable at one gate) and the principal hub for transcontinental non-alliance widebody flights. A ~$5B, 15-year improvement plan (through 2035) is underway, including eventual demolition of Concourse G — expect the gate map to keep evolving.
- **Our operation uses:** Given K Global's non-alliance-specific, network-wide international widebody profile, the **South Terminal (Concourse H/J)** or **Central Terminal Concourse E satellite** are the most likely widebody gate clusters for scheduled K Global service — **not independently confirmed for our specific operation this pass.** 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** Three Immigration/Customs processing areas are reported: **Central Terminal E (1st level), North Terminal D (1st level), South Terminal J (3rd level).** Connection-time planning should account for which terminal/level a given arrival clears through relative to any onward K Global departure gate.
- **Notes:** 🟧 Terminal/gate assignment across the ongoing multi-billion-dollar improvement program (through 2035) should be reconfirmed periodically rather than treated as fixed.

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** Concourse E's satellite terminal and Concourse J each report at least one gate capable of taking an A380-class aircraft; broader Code E/F allocation across the South and Central Terminal concourses is expected but not individually itemised in reachable sources. 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Narrowbody-oriented gates on Concourses F/G and the regional wing of Concourse D are not sized for Code E/F widebodies — do not plan a widebody onto them.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** Not confirmed in reachable sources; remote/hardstand use for scheduled widebody international service is atypical at a major gated hub — confirm with handling if a contact gate is unavailable. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect a contact widebody gate for a scheduled international turn.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** KMIA's **non-parallel, angled four-runway layout** and its five FAA-charted hot spots (see [Briefing §13](index.md)) affect ground routing between stands and the runway system more than at a conventional parallel-runway hub — build extra taxi-time margin, consistent with the VAMSYS planning figures below.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** None identified for this field in reachable sources — unlike EU Level 2/3 hubs, KMIA does not appear formally slot-coordinated. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **None formal.** The airport operates H24 with no published curfew or hard movement-hour restriction. Miami-Dade Aviation Department's Noise Abatement Office issues **voluntary** flight-procedure guidance over noise-sensitive residential areas — airlines are encouraged but not compelled to comply. 🟧
- **CTOT / flow control:** No field-specific slot/CTOT regime identified; standard FAA Air Traffic Control System Command Center (ATCSCC) ground-delay/ground-stop programs apply as at any major US airport, with particular exposure during convective weather and, in season, hurricane-approach ground stops. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** The absence of a formal curfew or slot regime gives more scheduling flexibility than a European Level 3 hub; the principal commercial exposure is **weather-driven flow management** (afternoon convective activity most of the year, hurricane-season ground-stop risk Jun–Nov) rather than a regulatory movement cap.

## 5. Handling agents & turnaround

- **Handling agent(s):** Multiple third-party ground handlers are licensed on field, including **Swissport** (operating at MIA since 1979, 20+ airline customers), **Menzies Aviation** (ground-handling license effective 1 July 2025), and **Worldwide Flight Services (WFS)** (cargo/freighter-focused, e.g. handling Emirates SkyCargo's 747/777F operation). The specific handler contracted for a K Global operation is not confirmed. 🟧
- **Typical turnaround time for our type:** Not independently confirmed; VAMSYS mirror gives **taxi-in 15 min / taxi-out 18 min** for planning purposes `[VAMSYS mirror 2026-07-26]` — build the gate turn around these plus standard widebody service times.
- **Services:** Push-back, GPU/PCA, potable water/lav, catering and cabin services expected at a major-hub Code E/F stand (standard US large-hub provision).
- **Turnaround risk items:** Gate availability during the ongoing multi-terminal improvement program, weather-driven ground-stop/flow delays (convective season and hurricane season), and ground-routing complexity around the field's non-parallel runway layout and hot spots can each add unplanned turn time.

## 6. Load factors & seasonality

- **Demand peaks:** MIA anchors year-round demand as one of the largest US gateways to Latin America and the Caribbean, plus significant transatlantic and growing Asia/Africa long-haul service; South Florida's winter "snowbird"/leisure season and event-driven peaks (e.g. major sporting/entertainment events in the Miami area) add seasonal lift. 🟧
- **Day-of-week / seasonal pattern:** Not independently sourced for a K Global-specific pattern — treat as a standard large-hub business/leisure mix pending network-schedule confirmation. 🟧
- **Cargo / belly capacity:** MIA is the **busiest international cargo airport in the United States** (since 2021) and a major belly and dedicated-freighter gateway; a substantial cargo complex sits west of the field inside the triangle formed by Runways 12/30 and 9/27, with further freighter facilities (FedEx, UPS, Centurion Cargo) elsewhere on the airport. Confirm K Global belly-cargo uplift plans against payload at planning.
- **Connection banks:** Not independently sourced for a K Global-specific wave structure — cross-ref the route register for inbound/outbound timing at this field.

## 7. Fuel

- **Supplier / into-plane:** Jet A-1 and AVGAS 100LL reported available; fuel-system management by **Allied Aviation** (on field since 2004); into-plane operators include **Tursair Fueling** (on-site since 1958, 24/7/365), **Aero Fuels Miami** (24/7 licensed operator) and **SUAT Fuels**. 🟧 Specific K Global contract provider not confirmed.
- **Uplift availability & hours:** H24 reported.
- **Price / tankering angle:** Not independently sourced this pass — assess price differentials to/from KMIA at planning per leg. See [`OM E — Fuel Policy`](../../../../../flight-ops/fuel-policy.md).
- **Quality / freeze notes:** Standard Jet A-1; no cold-soak/fuel-freeze consideration at this sub-tropical field. World Fuel Services (headquartered in Miami) has a commercial agreement with DHL Express for blended Sustainable Aviation Fuel delivery to MIA — cargo-side, general market-development note rather than an operational planning factor.

## 8. Customs / immigration & PoE for pax

- **Port of Entry:** Yes — three terminal-area processing points: **Central Terminal E (1st level), North Terminal D (1st level), South Terminal J (3rd level).**
- **CBP / immigration hours:** The CBP administrative port office is reported open Mon–Fri 08:00–17:00 — this is **administrative office** time, not passenger-processing coverage; scheduled international arrivals are processed per the flight schedule, including outside those hours. 🟧 Exact off-peak/overnight staffing depth not confirmed.
- **Pre-clearance / visa-transit notes:** Standard US visa/ESTA entry requirements per nationality; no pre-clearance programme at KMIA (pre-clearance is an outbound-from-abroad concept, not applicable to inbound MIA arrivals).
- **Late/overnight/diversion caveat:** 🟧 Given MIA's role as a major scheduled-international-arrivals gateway, off-peak CBP staffing for an irregular-ops/diversion arrival is expected but not independently confirmed — build contingency time into any late/diverted-arrival passenger flow.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Weather** — near-daily afternoon convective/thunderstorm activity in the warm season, and hurricane-season (Jun–Nov) ground-stop/schedule-disruption risk, are the dominant reliability drivers at this field, not capacity or curfew constraints.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Expect FAA ATCSCC ground-delay and, in the most severe tropical-system cases, full ground-stop programs during the Jun–Nov season; afternoon convective activity can also drive tactical ground stops/reroutes in the warm months generally. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Hurricane season and the general summer convective pattern carry the highest schedule-risk exposure at this field; the field's non-parallel runway layout and multiple hot spots add ground-movement time variability independent of weather. 🟧
