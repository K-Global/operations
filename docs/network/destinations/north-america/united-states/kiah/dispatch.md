## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** Five terminals (A, B, C, D, E) with roughly 116 gates total, linked airside by the **Skyway** automated people mover and landside by the **Subway** APM connecting all five terminals plus the airport hotel. Terminal A is used primarily by non-hub domestic carriers; Terminal B by regional/mainline hub-carrier traffic; Terminal C by mainline hub-carrier domestic service; **Terminal D and Terminal E both hold international arrivals facilities**, with Terminal D providing gate infrastructure that supports super-jumbo aircraft (A380/747-8-class).
- **Our operation uses:** Terminal D/E for international widebody service — exact gate/pier assignment for the K Global operation not confirmed. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** Terminal D houses the Federal Inspection Service (FIS) facility; Terminal E also supports international arrivals for a portion of carriers. Confirm current-day gate/FIS routing with handling.
- **Notes:** Terminal B has an ongoing multi-year renovation/expansion programme; confirm current gate map is not affected for our planned stand. 🟧

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** Terminal D is the confirmed super-jumbo-capable cluster (A380/747-8-class gates); Terminal E also handles widebody international arrivals. Exact stand-by-stand code-letter breakdown not confirmed. 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Narrowbody/regional-oriented piers at Terminals A/B/C are not sized for Code E/F — do not plan a widebody onto them.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** Not confirmed in reachable sources; remote/hardstand use for scheduled widebody international service is atypical at a major hub — confirm with handling if a contact gate is unavailable. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect a contact widebody gate for a scheduled destination turn.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** The dense set of wingspan-restricted taxiways (TWY WD, NR, WW, NK, NJ, RC, SF, WC) affects ground routing between stands and the runway system for our larger widebodies — see [Briefing §13](index.md) and the Departure/Arrival taxi sections.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** **None identified** — IAH is not one of the FAA's Level 2/3 slot-coordinated fields.
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **None found** in reachable sources; ATCT attendance is continuous (H24). 🟧
- **CTOT / flow control:** No permanent regime; **ad hoc ATCSCC ground-delay/EDCT programs** can apply during severe Gulf Coast convective weather or hurricane-related traffic management (§6/§10). 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** Absent a curfew or slot regime, the primary schedule-integrity risk at KIAH is **weather-driven ground-delay exposure** (thunderstorm/hurricane season) rather than a regulatory movement cap.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Gulf-of-Mexico convective/thunderstorm activity** (summer afternoons) and the **Atlantic hurricane season (Jun–Nov)** are the field's principal weather-driven delay/disruption risks; fog is a secondary factor.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Ad hoc ATCSCC ground-delay/EDCT programs are likely during significant convective weather or tropical-cyclone activity; no permanent flow-control regime otherwise. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Summer thunderstorm season and the Jun–Nov hurricane season carry the highest schedule-risk exposure at this field; winter freeze events are rare but can cause outsized disruption when they occur given minimal standing de-icing infrastructure. 🟧
