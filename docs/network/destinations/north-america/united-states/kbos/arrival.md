## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain:** 🟩 None. Flat coastal/harbor-fill site at 19.1 ft AMSL — no close-in high terrain relevant to any arrival path.
- **Specific threats:** 🟥 The **intersecting six-runway geometry** (not parallels) sustains a high configuration-change frequency and crossing-traffic dependency; harbor **boat obstacles** and **on-airport cranes** are AIP-flagged near several thresholds; bird activity is flagged on and around the airport.
- **Airspace / traffic:** 🟥 High-density regional terminal airspace under the **Boston Consolidated TRACON (A90)**, which also manages several busy satellite fields — brief for potential re-sequencing/vectoring, especially during a configuration change or winter weather event.

## 10. Gotchas

- **RWY 14 is never a landing runway** (takeoff-only, no approach authorised) — do not expect it as an arrival option under any configuration.
- **The noise-preferred landing runway (33L) is not guaranteed** — brief for a late runway/configuration change given the intersecting six-runway layout and wind sensitivity; watch the ATIS wind trend.
- **CAT II/III capability exists only on 04R and 33L** — do not assume low-visibility capability on the other instrument runways (22L, 27, 15R are CAT I/LOC only).
- **Harbor/water obstacles and on-airport cranes** are AIP-flagged near several thresholds — maintain obstacle vigilance on final, especially at night or in reduced visibility.
- **Bird activity on and around the airport** is AIP-flagged — maintain a heightened visual scan at low level near the harbor.
- **No formal curfew**, but expect a voluntary over-water arrival routing preference (≈2300–0600) and the RNAV (RNP) approach to 33L to be the assigned procedure when conditions allow — request/expect it rather than assuming a straight-in ILS.
- **Winter nor'easter/snow-ice events** can force reduced-rate or single-runway operations — brief a holding/diversion contingency in season.
