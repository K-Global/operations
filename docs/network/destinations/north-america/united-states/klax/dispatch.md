## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** Nine terminals in a horseshoe around the Central Terminal Area — **Terminals 1–8** (T1/T3 on the north side, T4–T8 on the south side) plus the **Tom Bradley International Terminal (Terminal B / TBIT)** on the west, the airport's primary international facility. Corridors connect T1/T2/T3; skybridges connect TBIT to T3 and T4; a tunnel links T4/T5/T6; a ground-level walkway joins T6/T7/T8.
- **Our operation uses:** **Tom Bradley International Terminal (TBIT)** for widebody international service — the West Gates (201–225, Code E/F capable) and the original concourse (gates 130–159). 🟧 Confirm current gate assignment given LAWA's active terminal/gate renumbering project (targeted ahead of the 2028 LA Olympics; no final numbering decision announced as of the most recent reachable reporting).
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** International arrivals clear CBP at the TBIT Federal Inspection Station (68 Automated Passport Control kiosks plus Enhanced Passenger Processing technology reported); onward domestic connections re-clear security after the FIS hall — build connection time accordingly. Typical peak-hour immigration processing reported at 45–90 minutes. 🟧
- **Notes:** Terminal 5 is reported under a major rebuild ahead of the 2028 Olympics; the wayfinding/renumbering project ($43M, contracted 2026) covers terminals, gates and the future Automated People Mover station naming. 🟧 Confirm current gate/concourse assignment with handling before finalising a stand plan.

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** TBIT West Gates (201–225) and the original TBIT concourse (130–159) are the confirmed international widebody clusters; skybridge access to T3/T4 exists for connecting traffic. 🟧 Exact current per-gate aircraft-size capability not independently confirmed.
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Domestic-oriented gates across T1–T8 are generally narrowbody-sized; do not plan a Code E/F aircraft onto a domestic contact gate without confirming jetway/wingspan clearance.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** **West remote gates 406–409** exist; use of open gates as a taxi path is prohibited, and a southbound turn is not available from gates 408/409 (Chart Supplement remark) — relevant if a remote stand is assigned. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect a contact TBIT gate for a scheduled home-base turn; confirm remote/hardstand use with handling if a contact gate is unavailable during a bank.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** The **wingspan/length-based taxiway restrictions** (Twy D D7–D8 >157 ft; >214 ft contact Airside Ops; Twy D8 outbound turn >198 ft; Twy C10 left turn >155 ft; length >240 ft on Twy C7–C9) and the **HS1–HS3 hot-spot cluster** affect ground routing to/from TBIT — see [Briefing §13](index.md) and the Departure/Arrival taxi sections.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** **FAA Level 2 — schedule facilitation.** LAX is not a formally IATA Level 3 slot-coordinated airport; the FAA and carriers conduct schedule facilitation twice yearly (Winter/Summer seasons) to manage demand against runway capacity. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **No legal curfew.** The voluntary **Over-Ocean Operation** (since 1985; departures 24L/25R, straight out to shoreline) and **Preferential Runway Use Program** (outer runways for daytime arrivals, inner runways for daytime departures; inboard runways maximized 2200–0700) govern runway assignment. **Noise-abatement single-runway ops 0000–0630 local: arrivals RWY 06R, departures RWY 25R.** Airport maintenance restricted 2300–0600. 🟧
- **CTOT / flow control:** No formal IATA slot regime; peak-hour congestion and marine-layer disruption (May–Jul mornings) can still generate practical ground-delay/flow-management exposure at one of the world's busiest airports.
- **Commercial impact:** As the base, on-time performance drives the network's connection banks; marine-layer-season mornings (May–Jul) are the principal schedule-risk window — build arrival/turn buffers accordingly.

- **Handling agent(s):** Full major-hub handling available at LAX generally; the specific ramp handler contracted for our operation is not confirmed in reachable public sources. Signature Aviation and Atlantic Aviation are confirmed on the GA/FBO fuel side. 🟧
- **Typical turnaround time for our type:** Not independently confirmed; VAMSYS mirror gives **taxi-in 20 min / taxi-out 22 min** for planning purposes `[VAMSYS mirror 2026-07-26]` — build the gate turn around these plus standard widebody service times.
- **Services:** Push-back, GPU/PCA, potable water/lav, catering and cabin services all expected at a home-base Code E/F TBIT stand (standard major-hub provision); aircraft use minimal power taxiing near terminals due to blast hazard (Chart Supplement remark).
- **Turnaround risk items:** TBIT gate availability during international arrival/departure banks; CBP FIS-hall throughput at peak (45–90 min reported); ground-routing complexity around the HS1–HS3 area and the wingspan-restricted taxiways can each add unplanned taxi time.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Coastal marine-layer stratus/fog (strongest May–Jul)** dropping ceiling/visibility at the field into the morning even when the LA Basin inland is clear — the airport's defining reliability threat. CAT II/III on 24R/25L mitigates but does not eliminate the associated arrival-rate reduction and holding exposure.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** High at peak hours given LAX's overall traffic volume and the closely-spaced parallel-runway geometry within each complex; occasional Santa Ana wind events force a configuration change to East Ops, which can further constrain throughput during the transition.
- **On-time reliability picture:** Summer-morning arrivals into LAX carry the highest delay/holding exposure in the West Coast network; hold fuel and buffers should reflect this. 🟧

---

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** No specific published per-movement noise-charge schedule found in reachable sources; LAWA administers the Over-Ocean Operation and Preferential Runway Use Program as the primary noise-management mechanism, and monitors for deviations (early turns, prohibited east departures during noise-sensitive hours), issuing notifications and requesting a response when found. 🟧
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** No legal curfew to infringe; the noise programs are administrative/voluntary rather than a hard movement ban, though repeated deviations draw LAWA scrutiny.
- **Scheduling guardrails:** Prefer the standard Normal-ops runway assignment (arrivals 24R/25L, departures 24L/25R) for daytime/evening movements; expect the noise-abatement single-runway plan (arrivals 06R, departures 25R) for any movement scheduled 0000–0630 local.
