## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** Two terminals, seven concourses, ~200 gates total. The **Domestic Terminal** (west side) spans Concourses **T, A, B, C, D**; the **Maynard H. Jackson Jr. International Terminal** (east side) comprises Concourses **E and F**. All terminals/concourses are linked airside by an underground pedestrian mall and **"the Plane Train"** — a 24/7 automated people-mover — plus moving walkways.
- **Our operation uses:** International widebody arrivals/departures concentrate on **Concourse F** (the international gateway concourse, home to the field's Group VI/A380-capable gate(s)) and secondarily **Concourse E**. 🟧 Specific K Global gate/concourse assignment is not confirmed via VAMSYS this pass.
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** International passengers arriving on Concourse F clear passport control/CBP within that concourse before exiting landside; passengers arriving on Concourse E who are ending their journey in Atlanta transit via a dedicated underground walkway to the Concourse F CBP checkpoint, while connecting passengers on Concourse E are processed at a separate on-concourse checkpoint. Build connection time accordingly for any onward network leg.
- **Notes:** 🟧 Exact current gate/concourse assignment for a non-Star-Alliance-style independent international operator (i.e. K Global) is not confirmed — confirm with handling before finalising a stand plan.

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** Concourse F is the confirmed Group VI-capable cluster (able to accept A380-class aircraft, i.e. the K Global A388); Concourse E also handles widebody international traffic. Exact stand-by-stand code-letter assignment not independently confirmed. 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Domestic-oriented Concourses A, B, C, D and T are generally narrowbody/regional-gauge and not sized for Code E/F — do not plan a widebody onto them without specific confirmation.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** Not confirmed in reachable sources; remote/hardstand use for scheduled widebody international service is atypical at a field of this scale — confirm with handling if a contact gate is unavailable. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect a contact widebody gate on Concourse F/E for a scheduled international arrival.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** The extensive **wingspan-based taxiway restrictions** (TWY V >171 ft, TWY W >171 ft/tail height >45 ft, TWY F Group VI >214 ft, mandatory centerline adherence on TWY L/M, E/F, SC/SJ when a >214 ft-wingspan aircraft is present) directly affect ground routing between Concourse F stands and the runway system for the K Global A388 — see [Briefing §13](index.md) and the Departure/Arrival taxi sections.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** No formal IATA Level 2/3 slot-coordination scheme found in reachable public sources — treated as **not slot-coordinated** pending contrary evidence. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **No flight-movement curfew found.** A landside terminal-access restriction (23:00–04:30 local, ticketed passengers/authorised personnel only) governs terminal building, parking-deck and SkyTrain access for security/loitering-management reasons — it does **not** restrict aircraft movements. Noise is managed via a Noise & Operations Monitoring System (NOMS) rather than a curfew. 🟧 (confirm no informal night flow restriction exists)
- **CTOT / flow control:** No formal slot scheme, but ATFM/ground-delay/EDCT-style flow programs are common in convective-weather periods given the field's traffic volume and the busy overlying TRACON. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** With no formal slot/curfew constraint, the primary commercial guardrail at KATL is **convective-weather flow-program exposure** (§10) rather than a schedule-hard-stop; build tactical buffer for summer-afternoon ground-delay programs rather than a curfew-driven last-rotation buffer.

## 9. De-icing provisioning & season

- **Availability:** Available; specific pad locations, count and holdover-fluid provisioning not confirmed in reachable sources. 🟧
- **Season:** Roughly **November–March**, with the notable caveat that this is a comparatively **low-frequency, high-relative-impact** de-icing season versus a northern hub — the January 2014 regional ice event is the standout modern example. 🟩/🟧
- **Provisioning:** Not confirmed. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** Because severe winter weather is infrequent here, a winter-precipitation forecast should be treated as a **higher relative delay/cancellation risk** than its raw frequency suggests — the field and regional infrastructure have less routine practice at large-scale de-icing/snow operations than a northern hub. See [`OM E — Cold Weather Operations`](../../../../../flight-ops/cold-weather-operations.md).

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Summer-afternoon convective/thunderstorm activity** — the standard Southeastern US seasonal pattern — is the more frequent driver of ground-delay/flow programs at this traffic volume; the rare winter ice event is lower-frequency but higher-impact.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** High, given the field's status as the world's busiest by passenger traffic and the scale of the overlying Atlanta TRACON ("A80") — expect ATFM/ground-delay programs in convective/adverse conditions. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Summer convective afternoons and any winter precipitation event carry the highest schedule-risk exposure for a KATL rotation. 🟧
