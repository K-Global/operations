## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain

Nairobi sits on the Kenyan highlands at **5,330 ft**, in a high-terrain environment. **Ngong Hills** lie to the SW; the **Aberdare Range** and **Mt Kenya** region rise to the N/NE; "MUA HILL" is noted on the radar-minima chart. Cleared levels incorporate terrain restrictions and the area MEA is **FL100**. Read exact MSA sectors and the 10-1R radar-minima chart on the current AIRAC. With high terrain around, do not descend below MSA/charted step-downs early, and brief the missed approach.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic

Busy international hub with **radar control** (Nairobi Approach/Radar). Single runway 06/24 concentrates arrival/departure flow — expect sequencing and possible holding. Wilson (HKNW) GA traffic is co-located a few miles away. Watch frequency/runway changes. Cross-ref OM C Airspace (Nairobi FIR) when built.

### 3.3 Runway excursion

Single runway **06/24, 4,117 × 45 m** asphalt with 200 ft (61 m) stopway each end — long, not normally length-limited even at high density altitude. **Declared distances are not published in reachable sources** — verify per direction before a performance-limited case. THR 24 is ~49 ft lower than THR 06 (5,281 vs 5,330 ft). High density altitude raises true groundspeed on touchdown — plan braking accordingly.

### 3.4 Weather threat

High-altitude tropical station. **Long rains Mar–May** and **short rains Oct–Dec** bring afternoon convective build-ups, heavy showers and gusts; otherwise relatively dry. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations

High-elevation field (5,330 ft) — density-altitude penalties on take-off performance and climb gradients over surrounding terrain, especially warm afternoons. **Slot-coordinated (SCR Level 3)** — handling and slot required; build into planning. ILS CAT, PCN and declared distances are provisional — re-verify on current AIRAC.
