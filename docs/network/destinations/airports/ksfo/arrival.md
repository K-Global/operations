## 2. STAR / transition selection

- **STARs (names only — verify current AIRAC):** ALWYS THREE (RNAV), **BDEGA FOUR (RNAV)** *(reference OFP arrival)*, BIG SUR THREE, DYAMD FIVE (RNAV), MODESTO NINE, PIRAT THREE (RNAV), POINT REYES THREE, RISTI ONE (RNAV), SERFR FOUR (RNAV), STINS FOUR, STLER FOUR (RNAV), WWAVS TWO (RNAV), YOSEM THREE (RNAV).
- **Selection by arrival direction / runway:** BDEGA FOUR feeds the 28 configuration from the north/northwest (the reference EDDF–KSFO polar arrival); SERFR/DYAMD-type RNAV STARs feed from the south; POINT REYES/BIG SUR are coastal feeds. NORCAL assigns the STAR-to-approach transition for the active config.
- **Transition to approach:** Expect an RNAV-STAR-to-ILS transition with radar vectors to the 28R/28L final in the west plan; verify the charted transition.
- **Speed / flow constraints on the STAR:** RNAV STARs carry published speed/altitude gates — pointer only, verify chart; expect additional flow speed control in the high-density environment.

## 3. Descent & energy plan

- **Top-of-descent trigger:** Standard ~3:1; plan TOD to meet the first STAR altitude gate. Marine-layer/GDP holding can compress the descent — brief an early-descent contingency.
- **Speed control:** STAR speed gates, 250 KIAS below FL100, and SFO Class B speed limits within the surface area — confirm current chart.
- **Altitude constraints:** Key STAR crossing constraints (e.g. BDEGA FOUR ladder) are pointers — fly the charted altitudes.
- **Energy traps:** Late runway/config changes (28↔19/01 ahead of a front), tailwind-on-descent in the 28 plan, and holding-then-repositioning during marine-layer flow are the main energy traps. 🟧

## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain:** 🟩 Low. Bay-fill field at 13 ft MSL; most finals track over water. Distant terrain (San Bruno 1,319 ft ~8 NM south, Montara 1,901 ft, East Bay hills) is clear of the standard 28/19/01 approach corridors but relevant to a vectored missed/MSA sector (Briefing §3.1).
- **Specific threats:** 🟥 **Closely-spaced parallel runways 28L/28R (750 ft apart)** — the defining threat. **As of 2026 the FAA permanently requires staggered/offset approaches** in place of former simultaneous side-by-side landings, cutting the arrival rate (mid-50s/hr → ~36/hr). Expect single-stream/staggered arrivals, extended vectoring and holding in marine-layer conditions. Wake from the adjacent parallel is a consideration.
- **Airspace / traffic:** Class B, radar-vectored, one of the busiest US hubs (NORCAL Approach). High-density arrival environment; brief for re-sequencing.

## 6. Missed approach watch-items

- **Terrain-driven MAP notes:** Terrain is not the driver — most missed-approach paths climb over the bay/water. Fly the published MAP; verify climb-gradient/turn on the current chart.
- **Re-sequencing environment:** A go-around drops you back into a high-density radar environment with a reduced-capacity (staggered) arrival stream — expect extended vectoring and possible holding before re-sequencing.
- **Go-around traps:** Config/wake awareness on the closely-spaced parallels during the miss; watch the traffic scan even under radar/TCAS.

## 7. Landing runway / LDA & braking / vacate

- **LDA by runway (mind displaced thresholds — Briefing §7):** 28R **11,236 ft** (300 ft displaced thr), 28L **10,275 ft** (300 ft displaced thr), 19L 8,650 ft, 10L 11,193 ft. The 28 pair is ample for the A330-900; brief the **displaced-threshold LDA**, not the physical length.
- **Braking / vacate:** High-speed Twy T is grooved full-width between 28R and 28L for rapid exit; plan the vacate to minimise runway occupancy in the high-density flow.
- **Runway-excursion watch:** 🟧 Displaced thresholds all four primary ends; a config swap ahead of a front can leave a tailwind on the previously-into-wind runway — check ATIS wind trend. Grooved surfaces aid wet braking.

## 8. Taxi-in routing to terminal & hot spots

- **Typical taxi-in by runway → terminal:** After landing 28R/28L, taxi in to the **International Terminal (Boarding Areas A/G)** per Ground; use Twy T for the initial exit.
- **Hot spots / tight taxiways:** 🟥 Two **Tower obstructed-vision areas** on Twy A (between Gates 88/89 and between Gates F20/F21) — Tower cannot confirm those areas clear; taxi with vigilance. The **Twy Zulu ≥171 ft wingspan restriction** is primarily an outbound issue but note the geometry inbound. Several **hold-position signs mounted on the right**, not the left. Strict nose-gear-line adherence, no oversteering.
- **Stand/gate notes:** International Terminal Code E stands (A/G) — see Dispatch §3.

## 10. Gotchas

- **Staggered/offset approaches are now mandatory on 28L/28R** — the arrival rate is structurally lower; expect vectoring/holding, especially in marine-layer mornings.
- **Marine-layer fog can be below minima at the field while the city is clear** — brief a CAT II/III (28R) plan and a holding/diversion contingency Jun–Sep.
- **Brief the displaced-threshold LDA** (28R 11,236 ft / 28L 10,275 ft), not the physical runway length.
- **28L is SA CAT II** — needs operator/crew Special Authorization; do not plan it as the low-vis primary without that.
- **Config swap ahead of a winter front** (28s → 19s/01s) can introduce a tailwind and a late runway change — watch the ATIS.
- **Tower obstructed-vision areas on Twy A** and right-mounted hold signs on taxi-in — do not assume standard placement.
- **KOAK (~10 NM) shares the same marine-layer weather** — of limited value as a fog alternate; consider KSJC (~26 NM).
