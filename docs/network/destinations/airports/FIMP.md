## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain

Mauritius is a volcanic, mountainous island. Plaisance sits on the low coastal plain in the **south-east**, near the shoreline, but terrain rises inland. High ground (Black River Gorges massif, highest peak ~828 m / 2,717 ft; Pieter Both / Le Pouce ~810–823 m near Port Louis) lies **NW/W and N**. Exact heights, bearings, and **MSA sectors are on the AFC area chart — read them there**. With **no radar and no MVA**, terrain clearance is procedure-based: fly the charted SID/STAR/approach precisely, honour step-downs, and do not improvise headings below MSA.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic

All ATS in the Mauritius FIR is **procedural — no radar** (ADS / position reports only, ICAO Annex 2 §3.6.3). No radar vectors; longer in-trail spacing. CTR **Class C, SFC–3,500 ft AMSL** (arcs ~8–13 NM about the ARP); TMA Class C above to FL245; FIR Class A above FL245. TA **4,000 ft**; first usable level **FL060**. Helicopter ops (coastal / Riche en Eau) and skydiving (Mon Loisir TSA, NW) add traffic. Cross-ref OM C Airspace (Mauritius FIR / SW Indian Ocean) when built.

### 3.3 Runway excursion

Single runway 14/32, **3,370 × 45 m** (with starter extension), PCN 98/F/B/W/T — strong, long, not normally length-limited. **RWY 14 lands to LDA 3,040 m** (330 m shorter than its TORA); fly the correct aiming point. SE trade winds usually favour RWY 14 (136° M); on RWY 32 watch tailwind/crosswind and wet-surface braking.

### 3.4 Weather threat

**Tropical cyclone season ≈ November–April**; the eAIP states the airport "may be closed due to tropical cyclones." Year-round **SE trade winds** dominate. Convective showers and gusts mainly in the wet season. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations

Procedural, non-radar, island-terrain environment, single runway, asymmetric approach fit (ILS to 14 only). Treat any RWY 32 instrument arrival, night/low-vis arrival, or cyclone-season op as elevated workload — brief approach and missed approach early; have the diversion (FMEE Réunion) live before top of descent.

## 4. Cautions & Warnings

- High terrain inland (N/NW); do not descend below MSA early — **no radar/MVA backup**.
- **RWY 32 has no ILS** — plan a VOR/DME or RNP approach for 32.
- RWY 14 lands short of full length (LDA 3,040 m, displaced/threshold geometry).
- Tropical-cyclone closures Nov–Apr — check TC bulletins before committing.
- Helicopter & east-coast traffic can restrict early left turns off the RWY 14 North SID — climb on the SID as charted.
- PAPI RWY 14 is **3.5° (left side)** — long-body eye-to-wheel check over the threshold.

## 13. Ground operations

- **Stands for our types:** GA / private apron stands (Citation-class reference). **Confirm a G650ER fits a GA stand** — larger than the charted C680 reference; a parked G650 or an ACJ321 may be assigned a commercial stand. Tower assigns the bay on arrival.
- **Push-back:** Stand-specific (see §11); larger types push/tow off the main apron.
- **Helicopter segregation:** Helicopters hold at Beau-Vallon / Le Souffleur and TLOFs ALPHA/PAPA to segregate from fixed-wing on FATO 14/32; can restrict early left turns off the RWY 14 North SID.
- **Hot spots / tight taxiways:** **TWY B closed** (per FIR data) . Narrow taxiways K (18.7 m) and L (15 m). Main taxiways 23–31 m; aprons G/H 57 m.
- **Follow-me:** available for stand entry per tower.

## 14. Weather & seasonal (durable)

- **Climate / prevailing wind:** Tropical maritime; persistent **SE trade winds**, generally favouring RWY 14.
- **Seasonal hazard:** **Tropical cyclone season ≈ Nov–Apr** — airport may close (eAIP AD 2.7). Monitor SW Indian Ocean TC bulletins (RSMC La Réunion). Wet-season convective showers/gusts.
- **Local effects:** Terrain-induced turbulence and showers; trade-wind crosswind on RWY 32 ops.
