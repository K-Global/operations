## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain

Benign. Sal is a **low, flat, arid island**; the field sits at **179 ft** with no significant terrain in the immediate approach corridors. AIP AD 2.22 publishes **MSA in three sectors on the CVS VOR/DME, 20 nm radius**: **NE (R360–R090) 2,500 ft · South (R090–R270) 1,700 ft · NW (R270–R360) 2,000 ft.** These are low because there is little relief — the diversion CFIT risk is **energy/approach management, not terrain**. One published obstacle of note: a **mast at ~463 ft** N of the field (AD 2.10, lit). **Transition altitude 7,000 ft.** Read exact sectors/minima off the current AIRAC plate.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic

Low-density field, **Class C** SAL CTR (GND–FL85), **radar available** (Sal Approach vectors at/above 1,700 ft; below that, monitoring/procedural). As a diversion you'd likely be the priority movement. **Note the double hat:** GVAC also sits under **Sal Oceanic (GVSC)** — the same FIR/oceanic control the leg is crossing — so the diversion controller and the enroute controller are the same authority. Arriving traffic is speed-capped **250 KIAS from 30 DME CVS** (AD 2.22). Cross-ref OM C Airspace (Sal Oceanic / EUR-SAM corridor) when built.

### 3.3 Runway excursion

**3,000 m × 45 m, flat (0.05% slope), asphalt** — length is a non-issue for a 777-300ER diversion. Two live watch-items:
- **Crosswind:** the runway is aligned **~360°/180°** and the prevailing wind is the **NE trade** — expect a **quartering crosswind** in normal conditions, stronger when the trades are up. A single-engine / heavy diversion into a crosswind is the real handling threat, not runway length.
- **Pavement:** **PCN 58/F/A/W/U.** A heavy 777-300ER on flexible pavement can present an **ACN above 58** — a technical overload. For a genuine emergency diversion that is acceptable under ACN/PCN overload provisions, but **brief it and expect a pavement inspection** before any subsequent movement. *(Reasoning from PCN + type, not a sourced ACN figure)*

### 3.4 Weather threat

Two seasonal signatures dominate and **must be re-checked at the §14 pull**:
- **Harmattan (roughly Dec–Feb):** Saharan dust plumes reach the archipelago and **reduce visibility** — the classic Cape Verde "brown-out" haze. On a single-ILS field, dust that pushes 01 below CAT I minima **and** forces a non-precision 19 is the worst-case combination.
- **NE trade wind, year-round:** steady, sometimes strong, giving the crosswind in §3.3. Trade-wind cumulus and the occasional easterly wave / late-season tropical system (Cape Verde is the genesis region for Atlantic hurricanes, **Aug–Oct**) round out the picture.

### 3.5 Operational considerations

**This field's whole reason for being in the pack is ETOPS.** GVAC is the **NORTH ETP diversion alternate**, paired with **SBFZ Fortaleza** as the south alternate; **the GVAC↔SBFZ stretch is the committed ETOPS-120 segment**, equal-time point **ETP1 near N07°43.5′ W032°25.5′**. If the 777 loses an engine or depressurises in the **northern half** of the crossing (before ETP1), **GVAC is the divert** — and the 120-minute single-engine rule sizes the whole exercise. Consequences:
- **Single ILS (01):** plan the diversion on **RWY 01 ILS** where wind allows; a **19 landing is non-precision** (RNAV GNSS / circling) and you may be flying it single-engine or unpressurised. Brief both ends before top-of-descent into the divert.
- **Isolated island:** the alternate-for-the-alternate is thin (§16). Commit the fuel/decision early.
- **RFF CAT 9 exactly meets** the 777-300ER requirement — a **NOTAM downgrade to CAT 7/8 would break the diversion plan** for a full-weight arrival. Check §15 at planning.

## 4. Cautions & Warnings

- **Use the AIRAC magvar (7.6°W), not the AIP's 10°W** — the AIP epoch is 2013.
- **RWY 19 has no ILS.** A wind-forced 19 arrival is **non-precision only** (RNAV GNSS / circling to 01) — plan for it before you need it.
- **RFF CAT 9 = the 777-300ER requirement exactly — zero margin.** Any NOTAM'd downgrade invalidates a heavy diversion. Verify at planning.
- **PCN 58/F/A/W/U may be an overload** for a heavy 777-300ER — fine for an emergency divert, but expect pavement scrutiny.
- **Harmattan dust (Dec–Feb)** can push visibility below CAT I on the only precision runway. Re-check the TAF.
- **NE trade-wind crosswind** on the N/S runway is the standing handling threat.
- **Night stop-parking limited** (AD 2.20): stands 01, 07 and TWY A2; **TWY A2 use needs prior TWR coordination.**
- **Level 3 slots waived for safety diversions** — do not let the slot regime deter a divert (§16).

## 5. Aerodrome category, restrictions & crew qualification

- **Special-airport / crew-qualification designation: NONE FOUND** — nothing in AIP AD 2 GVAC designating it a Cat B/C or crew-qual airport. Treat as **none published**.
- **Slots** — **IATA Level 3, schedule-coordinated** since winter 2017 (AD 2.23, Decree-Law 10/2016). Requests to slot.coordination@asa.cv in IATA SSIM format. **Not a gate for an unscheduled safety diversion.**
- **Operating restrictions / bans:** none published beyond the night stop-parking note (§4/§13). **RWY 19 non-precision only** is a capability limit, not a ban.
- **Overflight / entry / permits:** state overflight/landing permits per Cabo Verde; confirm on OFP.
- **Operations notes:** removal of disabled aircraft is **operator's responsibility** (AD 2.6/2.20), failing which the AD authority removes it at the operator's expense. Relevant if a diversion goes off the paved area.

## 14. Weather & seasonal (durable)

- **Climate / prevailing wind:** arid, oceanic; **steady NE trade wind** most of the year → standing **crosswind** on the N/S runway (§3.3). Reference temperature **27.4 °C** (AD 2.2).
- **Seasonal hazards:** **Harmattan (Dec–Feb):** Saharan **dust haze → reduced visibility** — the key IMC driver on a single-ILS field . **Hurricane genesis season (Aug–Oct):** Cape Verde is the birthplace of Atlantic tropical waves; occasional easterly wave brings convection/gusts .
- **Local effects:** trade-wind cumulus, mechanical turbulence off the low terrain in strong NE flow.

## 16. Fuel & alternate planning notes

- **Typical role for us:** **ETOPS-120 ETP diversion alternate — NORTH.** Paired with **SBFZ Fortaleza** (south alternate); **GVAC↔SBFZ is the committed ETOPS-120 segment**, ETP1 near **N07°43.5′ W032°25.5′**. GVAC is the go-to for an **engine-out / depressurisation** diversion in the **north half** of the EDDF→São Paulo crossing (before ETP1). *This is why the field is in the pack.*
- **Nearest suitable alternates (from GVAC — distances approximate, derived):** **GVBA** Boa Vista ~35 nm S; **GVNP** Praia/Santiago ~120 nm S; **GOOY** Dakar, Senegal ~330–360 nm E. Canaries (GCLP/GCTS) ~800+ nm N — too far to be a practical alternate-for-alternate.
- **Fuel-uplift notes:** **Jet A-1, H24** . Common ETOPS/tech-stop tanker point in the mid-Atlantic.
- **Range/perf flags for our fleet:** **3,000 m at 179 ft, PCN 58** — length ample for a 777-300ER diversion at any realistic landing weight; the constraints are **crosswind, single-ILS, RFF-exactly-meets, and possible pavement overload**, not runway length. Commit the diversion decision and fuel **early** — the alternate-for-alternate is thin.

## 17. Fleet-specific notes (optional)

**Boeing 777-300ER (OM B → B77W); FF777v2 in X-Plane 12** (the 777-300ER expansion is XP12-only):
- **777-300ER = Code E** (64.8 m span) → **RFF CAT 9 required**, exactly matched by GVAC's CAT 9. **No margin** — treat a NOTAM'd RFF downgrade as a diversion-plan breaker.
- **Heavy-weight diversion pavement:** a full-weight 777-300ER on **PCN 58/F** flexible pavement is likely an **overload** — acceptable for an emergency divert, brief it (§3.3).
- **Single-ILS discipline:** set up **RWY 01 ILS (109.90 "SL", 3.0°)** as primary and **prep a RNAV (GNSS) 19** as the non-precision backup before descending into the divert — you may be flying it engine-out or unpressurised.
- **VOR CVS 115.30 on field** is a clean raw-data backup for situational awareness on the approach.
