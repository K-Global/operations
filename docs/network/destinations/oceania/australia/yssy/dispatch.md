## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** Sydney Airport operates **three passenger terminals**: **Terminal 1 (International)**, separated from the domestic **Terminal 2** and **Terminal 3** by RWY 16R/34L — connecting passengers between T1 and T2/T3 should allow 30 minutes to over an hour.
- **Our operation uses:** **Terminal 1 (International)** — the sole international gateway at the field; Pier B (17 gates + remote bays historically) and Pier C (12 gates) split by alliance-style grouping in the terminal's own operational history. 🟧 Confirm current gate/pier assignment for our operation.
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** International arrivals and departures both process through T1; the runway separation from the domestic terminals is the operative connection-time driver for any T1↔T2/T3 network leg.
- **Notes:** No confirmed T1 redevelopment materially affecting international widebody capacity found in reachable sources this pass. 🟧

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** **Sydney Slot Management Scheme** — all airline/aircraft operators require an **Airport Coordination Limited (ACL)** slot. 🟥
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **Hard curfew 2300–0600 local** under the Sydney Airport Curfew Act 1995. Limited scheduled and approved take-offs/landings permitted in the shoulder periods (2300–2400 and 0500–0600) only; the Act restricts aircraft type, runway used and total shoulder-period movements rather than banning all overnight activity outright. Historical fines for curfew breaches have reached **A$550,000** (current maximum not reconfirmed this pass). 🟥
- **80 movements/hour cap:** A **separate, independent constraint** (Sydney Airport Demand Management Act 1997) — the field is capped at 80 total aircraft movements per hour at all times, not only overnight. This drives peak-hour scheduling discipline distinct from the curfew. 🟥
- **CTOT / flow control:** **Ground Delay Program (GDP) Inbound** run on demand 2000–1300 UTC (1 hr earlier during daylight saving); **Airport Collaborative Decision Making (A-CDM)** runs continuously H24 with a mandatory Target Off-Blocks Time (TOBT). Estimated airborne holding for arrivals: 20 min daily, rising to 30 min for non-PRM-capable aircraft 2100–0100 UTC Mon–Fri (1 hr earlier during daylight saving).
- **Commercial impact:** The curfew makes the **last scheduled arrival/departure before 2300** and the **first after 0600** the two hard schedule guardrails; the 80/hour cap makes peak-bank timing (rather than just night-time timing) a year-round commercial constraint.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** The **curfew** and the **80 movements/hour cap** together, rather than weather, are this field's defining reliability constraint — a delay that threatens to push a flight past 2300 or into a curfew shoulder period is a schedule-integrity event, not a routine delay.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** GDP Inbound run on demand; estimated airborne holding up to 30 min for non-PRM-capable aircraft during the 2100–0100 UTC Mon–Fri peak (1 hr earlier during daylight saving).
- **On-time reliability picture:** Peak-bank timing against the 80/hour cap, and last-rotation timing against the curfew, are the two dominant on-time risk factors at this base. 🟧

---

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** Governed through the **Long Term Operating Plan (LTOP)** runway-rotation/noise-sharing scheme rather than a simple per-movement dB surcharge found in reachable sources this pass. 🟧
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** A hard movement restriction under the Sydney Airport Curfew Act 1995 — historical fines have reached A$550,000 per breach (current maximum not reconfirmed). Breach is a legal/regulatory event, not merely a charge.
- **Scheduling guardrails:** Build buffer into the last rotation of the day so an operational delay does not push a landing or departure into the curfew; separately, respect the **80 movements/hour cap** at all hours when building peak-bank schedule density — this is a year-round guardrail, not just a night-time one.
