## 3. Runway / SID selection logic

- **By wind / config (historical):** RWY 13 was the default, used most of the time given Hong Kong's prevailing wind direction; a shift to RWY 31 followed a wind change.
- **By departure direction:** Not applicable — no current route structure exists.
- **Noise / preferential-runway program (historical):** RWY 31 was also used for early-morning noise-abatement landings (arrival context); no current noise programme applies since the field does not operate.
- **Interaction with arrivals:** Not applicable — closed aerodrome, single runway, no current traffic.

## 5. Initial climb, speed & noise-abatement departure

Not applicable — closed aerodrome. Historically, RWY 31 departures required a **sharp 65° left turn shortly after take-off** to clear the hill range ahead (including Beacon Hill, approx. 1,500 ft/460 m) — essentially the reverse of the RWY 13 landing turn (Wikipedia — Kai Tak Airport, retrieved 2026-07-26). This is a heritage/TEM-literacy note only; there is no current NADP, altitude constraint or climb procedure to apply it to.

---

## 6. Engine-out / emergency-turn & obstacle considerations

Not applicable — closed aerodrome, no current engine-out procedure exists. Historically, the close-in terrain on both runway directions (§3.1 of the Briefing) meant an engine-out departure would have been a serious obstacle-clearance challenge on either runway; no published historical engine-out procedure was located, and none is fabricated here. 🟧

---

## 7. Low-vis takeoff & de-icing on departure

Not applicable — closed aerodrome. RWY 13 in particular could not support low-visibility operations at all, historically, since the mandatory visual turn on approach had a parallel practical limitation for any low-vis departure planning of the era; de-icing was never a consideration in Hong Kong's subtropical climate.

## 10. Gotchas

- 🟥 **This is a closed aerodrome — there is no departure to fly.** Do not use any figure on this page as current guidance.
