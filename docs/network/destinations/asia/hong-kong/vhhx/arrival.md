## 2. STAR / transition selection

Not applicable — closed aerodrome, no current STAR exists. Historically (heritage description only, not a chart): inbound traffic for the Checkerboard approach tracked westbound at a minimum altitude of roughly 6,500 ft past Cheung Chau, continued to "Point Golf" south of Lantau Island (in the vicinity of today's Chek Lap Kok/VHHH site), then turned right to intercept the Runway 13 IGS localizer (Wikipedia — Kai Tak Airport, retrieved 2026-07-26). No current-AIRAC transition of any kind applies.

---

## 3. Descent & energy plan

Not applicable — closed aerodrome. Historically, the autopilot was disconnected at roughly 2,500 ft on the IGS approach and the remainder of the approach flown manually — an energy-management point of genuine historical interest, but with no current descent-planning application (Wikipedia — Kai Tak Airport, retrieved 2026-07-26).

## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain (historical):** Close-in hills reaching roughly 2,000 ft (610 m) sat less than 4 km north/northeast of the former RWY 13 threshold, with additional high ground east of RWY 31 and across the harbour on Hong Kong Island (up to ~2,100 ft/640 m) — the field's defining terrain consideration, and the reason the approach could not be flown as a simple straight-in instrument procedure (Wikipedia — Kai Tak Airport, retrieved 2026-07-26). Cross-ref [VHHX — Briefing §3.1](index.md). 🟥 (historical)
- **Specific threats (historical):** The mandatory low-altitude 47° visual turn, flown manually with no ILS guidance through the turn itself, is the single most distinctive threat this field is remembered for — widely regarded as one of the most demanding visual airline approaches ever flown in scheduled commercial service.
- **Airspace / traffic (historical):** Procedural, single-runway operation in the field's operating era; no current airspace consideration attaches to VHHX itself. Current-day airspace above the former site is part of the Hong Kong (VHHK) FIR — see the [Hong Kong (VHHK) FIR brief](../../../../airspace/fir/asia/hong-kong-vhhk.md) and the [Asia airspace overview](../../../../airspace/asia.md) for the live picture.

## 10. Gotchas

- 🟥 **This is a closed aerodrome — there is no arrival to fly.** Do not use any figure on this page as current guidance.
- The Runway 13 "Checkerboard turn" is genuinely one of aviation history's most famous approach manoeuvres — a low-altitude, fully visual 47° turn with no ILS guidance through it — but it is heritage/history, not a procedure available today at any current airport.
- Do not confuse VHHX with the live **VHHH — Hong Kong Intl**, 30 km west at Chek Lap Kok, which has handled all Hong Kong international traffic since 6 July 1998.
