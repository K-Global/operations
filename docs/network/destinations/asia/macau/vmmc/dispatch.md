## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** VMMC operates a single passenger terminal complex on the reclaimed-land site, connected to the runway by two causeway bridges; historical public data reports **~24 aircraft parking positions, 10 gates and 5 jetways**, though this is likely outdated given the ongoing masterplan/expansion works (2016 planning, 2020 construction start, a further phase begun 2025 targeting ~2030 completion). 🟧
- **Our operation uses:** Not confirmed — terminal/gate assignment for a company outside the field's established carrier groupings would be set by CAM/handling on a case-by-case basis. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP hall:** All arriving passengers clear Macau immigration and customs at the terminal (H24); Macau is a **separate customs/immigration territory from mainland China and Hong Kong**, so passengers continuing to either destination still clear Macau formalities first. A "two customs, one checkpoint" bus-shuttle service exists for transfers directly to the Macau ferry terminals without passing Macau immigration — background context only, not relevant to a scheduled air arrival.
- **Notes:** 🟧 Ongoing expansion/masterplan construction works are under way at the airport (target completion around 2030 per public reporting); no terminal-level operational impact identified for current-day planning, but re-check at the next update cycle.

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E) stands:** AVDGS-equipped stands (**A1–A15, A17, B1–B6, B8**) and the colour-coded B7/B10 pair (which differentiate Breakaway Point 1 for wingspan <36 m vs Breakaway Point 2 for wingspan ≥36 m) are the stands most likely to serve Code D/E aircraft; exact stand/pier allocation for a K Global widebody not independently confirmed. 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Not confirmed in reachable sources — the cargo apron (stands G01–G15) is governed by tow/breakaway-point procedures distinct from passenger-terminal stands and is not a passenger gate area.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** Not confirmed in reachable sources. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Not confirmed — expect a contact gate as the default assumption for a scheduled international widebody arrival, pending local confirmation.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** The runway–apron connection is via **two causeway bridges** (Bridge H, 23–39 m wide; Bridge C1, 23 m wide), both rated to a calculated resistance up to 3,970 kN — equivalent to a loaded B747-400. See [Briefing §13](index.md) and the Departure/Arrival taxi sections.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** No IATA slot-coordination level confirmed in reachable public sources. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **No general curfew published** — the AIP lists ATS, customs, fuelling, handling and security as H24, corroborated by public reporting that VMMC has no night curfew. A narrower rule bars **ICAO Annex 16 Chapter 2 noise-classified aircraft** from operating 00:00–08:00 local `[AIP Macao AD 2 VMMC 2.21]` — does not apply to modern K Global equipment. 🟩
- **Delayed-flight nuances:** Engine ground-runs above idle power are not normally permitted 22:00–07:00 local (case-by-case exceptions) — a scheduling consideration for late maintenance/engine-run activity, distinct from any curfew on scheduled movements.
- **CTOT / flow control:** No EUROCONTROL-style CTOT/ATFM program identified for VMMC in reachable sources; note that Macau's terminal traffic is worked externally by **Zhuhai Approach or Hong Kong Radar** (§Briefing §3.2), so any flow-management measure would originate from those units, not a dedicated Macau ATFM program. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** The absence of a general curfew is commercially favourable; the operative scheduling guardrails at VMMC are instead the **reduced-LDA runway** (Briefing §7) and the **externally-controlled approach environment** (Briefing §3.2), which can add sequencing variability rather than a hard time-window constraint.

- **Handling agent(s):** Not confirmed for a K Global operation in reachable public sources; **Menzies Macau** (ground handling) and **Servair Macau** (catering) are named generically as airport tenants in public reporting. 🟧

- **Services:** Push-back (mandatory colour-coded procedure by stand group), GPU/PCA, potable water/lav, catering (Servair Macau, per public reporting) and cabin services expected; not individually confirmed for our operation.
- **Turnaround risk items:** The **colour-coded, sequential push-back/tow procedures** (Briefing §13) — including no-simultaneous-pushback restrictions on certain stand pairs — are a genuine ground-choreography risk item at a compact single-runway field; typhoon-season weather (§6) is the dominant seasonal risk.

## 8. Customs / immigration & PoE for pax

- **Port of Entry:** Yes — single international terminal, H24.
- **CBP-equivalent / immigration hours:** **H24** `[AIP Macao AD 2 VMMC 2.3]`. 🟩
- **Pre-clearance / visa-transit notes:** Macau is a **separate customs/immigration territory from mainland China and Hong Kong** — all passengers, including those continuing to mainland China or Hong Kong, clear Macau immigration/customs first. A "two customs, one checkpoint" ferry-shuttle service exists for transfers directly to the Macau ferry terminals (background only).
- **Late/overnight/diversion caveat:** With **no general curfew and H24 customs/immigration**, VMMC does not carry the off-hours passenger-processing caveat seen at some other Asian fields — an unplanned diversion here at any hour should find customs/immigration staffed. 🟩

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **NW-Pacific / South China Sea typhoon season (~May–Nov, peak Jul–Sep)** — a coastal, single-runway, reclaimed-land field is a genuine weather-disruption risk in season, and a significant typhoon can degrade the whole Pearl River Delta cluster (VMMC/VHHH/ZGSZ/ZGGG) simultaneously.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Not independently confirmed; the externally-controlled approach environment (Zhuhai Approach / Hong Kong Radar, Briefing §3.2) is a plausible source of sequencing variability during peak periods or PRD-wide weather events. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Typhoon season and heavy monsoon rain (wet-runway braking-action management on the reduced-LDA runway) carry the highest schedule-risk exposure for VMMC operations. 🟧

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** Not independently confirmed in reachable public sources for VMMC. 🟧
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** No general curfew exists; the narrower **Chapter 2 noise-aircraft ban (00:00–08:00 local)** does not apply to modern K Global equipment. Engine ground-runs above idle power outside 07:00–22:00 local require case-by-case coordination.
- **Scheduling guardrails:** The absence of a curfew removes the dominant scheduling constraint seen at some other Asian fields; build buffer instead around **typhoon-season disruption** and the **externally-controlled approach environment**, which can add irregular-operations exposure without a fixed time-window trigger.
