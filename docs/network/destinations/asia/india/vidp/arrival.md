## 3. Descent & energy plan

- **Top-of-descent trigger:** Standard descent-profile planning; plan TOD to meet the first STAR altitude gate. Fog-driven reduced-rate arrivals in winter can compress the descent — brief an early-descent contingency during the Dec–Jan season.
- **Speed control:** STAR speed gates and 250 KIAS below FL100 (ICAO norm) — confirm current chart.
- **Altitude constraints:** Key STAR crossing constraints are pointers — fly the charted altitudes.
- **Energy traps:** Late runway/configuration changes ahead of a wind shift, and holding/resequencing during winter fog events, are the main energy traps at this field. The **closely-spaced parallel runway pair** also raises the risk of a late runway reassignment during busy banks.

## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain:** 🟩 None. Flat Indo-Gangetic plain at 778 ft AMSL — no close-in high terrain relevant to any arrival path. The AIP's obstacle table lists numerous urban obstacles (buildings, trees, poles, a metro overhead-traction structure near RWY 27 approach/RWY 09 departure) but all are height-limited to roughly 180–200 ft AGL — an obstacle-awareness item, not a CFIT threat.
- **Specific threats:** 🟥 **Closely-spaced parallel runways 11L/29R and 11R/29L** sustain continuous high traffic density and require disciplined crossing/vacate procedures — the AIP specifically flags the "Land, Vacate, Hold and Proceed" principle after landing on 11R/29L. Winter fog events can force reduced-rate, closely-managed arrival streams across the runway set. Large threshold displacements (27: 152 m; 11R: 640 m; **29L: 1,460 m**) must be correctly briefed — don't assume full physical runway length is landing-usable.
- **Airspace / traffic:** 🟥 Very high density under **Delhi Approach/Delhi Control** (Delhi FIR — VIDF) — one of the world's busiest terminal areas, with an AIP-published peak capacity of 86 movements/hour. Brief for re-sequencing and extended vectoring, especially in marginal weather. See [Asia airspace brief](../../../../airspace/asia.md).

## 6. Missed approach watch-items

- **Terrain-driven MAP notes:** Terrain is not the driver (flat plain) — fly the published MAP and verify climb-gradient/turn on the current chart. Mind the close-in urban obstacle environment near the RWY 09/27 and RWY 10/28 sectors on any low go-around.
- **Re-sequencing environment:** A go-around drops you back into a very high-density, closely-spaced-parallel-runway radar environment — expect extended vectoring and possible holding before re-sequencing, especially during a winter fog event.
- **Go-around traps:** Traffic/configuration awareness on the parallel-runway pair during the miss; the AIP-flagged crossing-clearance discipline for 11L/29R–11R/29L remains a ground-workload factor for any traffic already on the surface.

## 10. Gotchas

- **Closely-spaced parallel runways 11L/29R and 11R/29L** — expect disciplined crossing-clearance handling and the "Land, Vacate, Hold and Proceed" principle after landing on 11R/29L.
- **Winter dense-fog events (Dec–Jan, sometimes Nov–Feb)** can drop the field toward CAT III minima with limited warning — brief the low-visibility plan and a holding/diversion contingency in season; recent seasons have seen 70–230+ flights delayed/cancelled in a single disruption event.
- **RWY 29L's 1,460 m threshold displacement** is unusually large — never assume full physical runway length is landing-usable on this end.
- **RWY 09/27 is restricted-use** — do not expect this runway to be assigned for a non-scheduled arrival.
- **86-movements/hour capacity ceiling** — expect vectoring/holding at busy banks even in good weather, materially more during a fog event.
- **Eastern Link taxiways** — confirm taxiway identification carefully; the AIP specifically calls out read-back accuracy here.
