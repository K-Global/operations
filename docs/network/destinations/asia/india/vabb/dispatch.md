## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** Two physically separated terminal complexes connected only landside (15–20 min drive), sharing the same airside/runway system. **Terminal 1** (Santacruz) — domestic-only, used by low-cost carriers (Akasa Air, Alliance Air, IndiGo (some flights), SpiceJet, Star Air); 8 contact gates, 23 bus gates. **Terminal 2** (Sahar, Vile Parle East) — the sole international terminal, also handling domestic flights for Air India, Air India Express and IndiGo (some flights); 43 contact gates (Level 3/4), 8 bus gates; X-shaped structure across four floors, ~192 check-in counters, 60 departure / 76 arrival immigration counters, 48 escalators, 75 elevators.
- **Our operation uses:** **Terminal 2** — the only terminal with international customs/immigration/PoE facilities.
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** Terminal 2 only; CISF-staffed security checkpoint, no reverse entry once past the checkpoint (relevant to any transfer/connection planning).
- **Notes:** 🟧 The existing **Terminal 1** building is publicly flagged for demolition/reconstruction on safety/structural grounds (T1A/T1B are 30–60+ years old); as of the most recent public reporting the closure has been deferred to align with the ramp-up of the second Navi Mumbai (NMIA) terminal, meaning **Terminal 1 is expected to remain operational until at least 2029** — confirm current status before relying on a multi-year terminal assumption.

## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** Terminal 2's **MARS stands** are the relevant widebody-capable positions — a single MARS stand accommodates one widebody or two narrowbodies in either domestic or international configuration; exact widebody-dedicated count within the 43 T2 contact gates is not broken out in reachable sources. 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Terminal 1's gates are domestic/narrowbody-oriented and not relevant to an international widebody operation.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** Regulator figures (2025) cite **131 Code-C-equivalent parking stands total** (17 occupied by obsolete/non-operational aircraft); bus gates total 31 (T1: 23, T2: 8) for remote-stand passenger transfer. An older public figure (pre-2025) cites 108 physical stand positions — treat the exact current count as 🟧, figures vary by source/vintage.
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect either a contact MARS stand or a bus-gate remote stand at T2, depending on demand/bank timing; confirm with handling.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** RWY 09/27 and RWY 14/32 physically intersect south of the terminal buildings — ground routing between T2 stands and the active runway crosses this intersection zone; see [Briefing §13](index.md) and the Departure/Arrival taxi sections. Isolation-bay locations (for security/emergency parking) are published: primary at TWY E9 (RWY 09/27 in use), secondary at the RWY 27 end near TWY N11 (RWY 14/32 in use).

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** Not independently confirmed from a primary source this pass. 🟧 India operates a national slot-coordination framework for congested airports; Mumbai's demand consistently exceeds its structural runway capacity (~44 ATMs/hr against ~900+ average daily movements), which functions as a de facto scarcity constraint regardless of formal coordination level.
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **None found** — ATS, fuelling, handling and security are all H24; only the aerodrome operator's administrative office observes restricted hours (Mon–Sat 0330–1200 UTC / 0900–1730 IST, closed 2nd/4th Sat and all Sun). 🟩
- **CTOT / flow control:** Not independently sourced for VABB specifically this pass; India's air navigation service provider applies flow-management measures at capacity-constrained airports as standard practice — treat ATFM-style delay exposure as routine at this field given its structural single-effective-runway ceiling. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** With no hard curfew, the dominant commercial constraint is **capacity, not clock-time** — schedule padding against the ~44 ATMs/hr ceiling (especially in the monsoon, when achievable rates fall further) is the primary scheduling guardrail, not a last-rotation curfew buffer.

## 5. Handling agents & turnaround

- **Handling agent(s):** The Mumbai ground-handling market has been in transition since May 2025, when a major international handler's security clearance was revoked by the Government of India and its Mumbai/Ahmedabad contracts were terminated; other agents — **AISATS, Bird Worldwide Flight Services, AIASL (Air India Airport Services Ltd), Indo Thai Airport Management Services** — have been onboarding the affected staff and stations. The specific agent contracted for our operation is not confirmed in reachable public sources. 🟧
- **Typical turnaround time for our type:** Not independently confirmed; VAMSYS mirror gives **taxi-in 15 min / taxi-out 18 min** for planning purposes `[VAMSYS mirror 2026-07-26]` — build the gate turn around these plus standard widebody service times.
- **Services:** Push-back, GPU/PCA, potable water/lav, catering and cabin services expected at a major-hub Code E/F stand; specific service-level detail per agent not confirmed.
- **Turnaround risk items:** Monsoon-season waterlogging/reduced-capacity delay cascades (§10), single-effective-runway sequencing pressure feeding into pushback/taxi delay, and the 2025 ground-handling transition are the principal turnaround risk items at this field.

## 6. Load factors & seasonality

- **Demand peaks:** VABB is India's second-busiest airport (~54–55 million passengers/year, ~330,000 annual aircraft movements); domestic top destinations include Delhi, Bangalore and Goa, international top destinations include Dubai, London and Abu Dhabi. 66 domestic and 52 international destinations served (2025 regulator figures).
- **Day-of-week / seasonal pattern:** Not independently sourced for K Global's specific schedule — treat as a high-volume, business-and-leisure-mixed major hub pending network-schedule confirmation. 🟧
- **Cargo / belly capacity:** VABB handles substantial cargo volumes (~0.89 million tonnes/year, roughly 0.24 Mt domestic / 0.65 Mt international per 2025 regulator figures); confirm K Global belly-cargo uplift plans against payload at planning. 🟧
- **Connection banks:** Not independently sourced for VABB-specific connection-bank timing; as a non-base destination, cross-ref the K Global route register for inbound/outbound wave structure relative to network scheduling.

## 7. Fuel

- **Supplier / into-plane:** **MAFFFL** (Mumbai Aviation Fuel Farm Facility Ltd) — a joint venture of MIAL, Indian Oil Corporation (IOCL), Bharat Petroleum (BPCL) and Hindustan Petroleum (HPCL) — operates the common-user Jet A-1 hydrant system. `[AAI eAIP AD 2.4 VABB]`
- **Uplift availability & hours:** **H24**, Jet A-1. Storage: 30,000 KL at the Sahar (Terminal 2) fuel farm, 17,000 KL at Santacruz; Hydrant Refuelling System (HRS) with 34 dispensers (19 + 15 across the two operating entities) and 15 refuelling vehicles (8 × 15,000 L, 7 × 27,000 L). AVGAS/WMM/JP-5 are separately handled by IOCL in barrels only, with no dedicated refuelling facility for those products. 🟩
- **Price / tankering angle:** Not independently sourced for VABB-specific pricing this pass — assess tankering economics per leg at planning. See [`OM E — Fuel Policy`](../../../../../flight-ops/fuel-policy.md). 🟧
- **Quality / freeze notes:** Standard Jet A-1; no cold-soak/fuel-freeze consideration at this tropical/near-equatorial field.

## 8. Customs / immigration & PoE for pax

- **Port of Entry:** Yes — **Terminal 2 only** (60 departure immigration counters, 76 arrival immigration counters).
- **CBP-equivalent / immigration hours:** Not confirmed in reachable public sources; T2 international operations run continuously alongside H24 ATS/handling/security. 🟧
- **Pre-clearance / visa-transit notes:** Standard Indian visa/immigration entry per nationality; a government-led Fast Track Immigration – Trusted Traveller Programme exists for eligible Indian passport holders and OCI cardholders, reducing processing time for that subset of passengers.
- **Late/overnight/diversion caveat:** With no hard curfew, overnight/late arrivals are not barred by a clock-time restriction; the more relevant caveat is confirming Terminal 2 immigration/CISF staffing depth during off-peak overnight hours, which is not independently confirmed. 🟧

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** The **intersecting-runway, single-runway-at-a-time capacity ceiling** (~44 ATMs/hr) is the field's structural delay driver year-round; the **SW monsoon (Jun–Sep)** compounds this by cutting achievable movement rates further through heavy rain, reduced visibility and waterlogging.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** High and effectively routine, given sustained demand against a single-effective-runway ceiling — treat ATFM-style flow management as a standing planning assumption rather than an exceptional event, especially in the monsoon. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Monsoon-season (Jun–Sep) days, and any period where RWY 14/32 is substituted for RWY 09/27 (maintenance/emergency), carry the highest schedule-risk exposure at this field. 🟧
