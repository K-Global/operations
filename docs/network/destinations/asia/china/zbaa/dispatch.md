## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** Three terminal buildings. **Terminal 1** — repurposed in 2023 as a satellite extension of Terminal 2, linked by a walkway. **Terminal 2** — handles a mix of domestic and international/regional (Hong Kong/Macau/Taiwan) service; includes at least one A380-capable gate. **Terminal 3** — the field's primary international-service terminal and one of the largest airport terminal complexes ever built in a single phase (986,000 m² floor area at its 2008 opening); structured as three linked concourses — a main hall (domestic and international check-in), a domestic satellite concourse, and the international concourse (handling all international, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan traffic) — connected by an underground automated people-mover (roughly 2–5 minutes between concourses).
- **Our operation uses:** A K Global international widebody service would be expected to route through the **Terminal 3 international concourse**; the exact gate/pier assignment for this operation is **not confirmed**. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** International passenger processing is concentrated in the Terminal 3 international concourse (and, for some carriers, Terminal 2); confirm current CIQ (customs/immigration/quarantine) hall assignment with handling.
- **Notes:** 🟧 The three-terminal, multi-concourse layout with an underground inter-concourse people-mover materially lengthens minimum connection time compared with a single-terminal field — build buffer accordingly for any onward network leg.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** **IATA Level 3 (fully coordinated)** — specific coordinator not independently confirmed in reachable public sources. 🟥
- **Curfew / night restriction:** No confirmed formal overnight curfew for scheduled passenger service. Documented restrictions apply specifically to **general aviation**: no GA departures 0700–0900 local; GA peak-hour (0900–2200 local) movement caps of one arrival-or-departure per day per operator, with two-slot days only recently becoming feasible as slot congestion eased; GA tech-stops/crew-rests were historically discouraged under slot pressure. These GA-specific rules are cited here as a strong signal of how tightly CAAC manages this field's capacity overall, not as a direct restriction on scheduled carriers. 🟧
- **Event-driven restrictions:** CAAC has, on documented past occasions (e.g. a September 2018 diplomatic-forum week), imposed a **full-stop restriction on non-participating business/private aviation** at ZBAA, capped scheduled movements to two per hour, and explicitly redirected traffic to **ZBTJ (Tianjin Binhai)** and **ZBSJ (Shijiazhuang Zhengding)** — the same two fields carried as this operation's company alternates. Treat this as a durable pattern (short-notice, event-driven, region-specific restriction is a real feature of operating into Beijing), re-verified each time via current NOTAM. 🟥
- **CTOT / flow control:** CAAC's centralised ATFM regime (CTOT, minutes-in-trail, ground holding) is standard, routine practice in Chinese airspace generally and applies to ZBAA specifically as one of the network's busiest nodes — see [Asia — Airspace Briefing §6](../../../../airspace/asia.md).
- **Commercial impact:** Level 3 slot integrity and CAAC flow control together make **on-time slot compliance** and a **standing awareness of event-driven full-stop risk** the two biggest schedule-integrity guardrails at this field.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** Two distinct seasonal drivers — **spring sand-dust events** (Mar–May, can force region-wide flow restriction, not just a local closure) and **winter snow/ice weather** (Dec–Feb, documented multi-day disruption in recent winters) — compounded year-round by the **IATA Level 3 slot regime** and **CAAC's routine, centralised ATFM/flow control**.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** High — ZBAA is one of Asia's busiest airports (442,046 movements, 2025) operating an independent/dependent triple-parallel-runway geometry under a fully slot-coordinated regime; expect CAAC flow-control measures (CTOT, minutes-in-trail, ground holding) as routine practice, not an exception. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Spring dust season and winter weather season carry the highest known seasonal schedule-risk exposure; the field's dual role alongside Beijing Daxing (ZBAD) means the current route/carrier split should be re-confirmed periodically rather than assumed static. 🟧
