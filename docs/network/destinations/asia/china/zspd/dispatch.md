## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** **Terminal 1** (opened 1 Oct 1999, 280,000 m², ~20M pax capacity) and **Terminal 2** (opened March 2008 for the Beijing Olympics window, ~40M pax and 4.2 Mt cargo capacity). The **satellite concourse (S1/S2)** — the world's largest single-building satellite terminal (622,000 m²), completed September 2019 — connects to T1 and T2 via an automated people mover (West line T1↔S1, East line T2↔S2), adding substantial gate capacity without a new standalone terminal building. A **Terminal 3** began construction in 2021 on the airfield's south side, planned to add ~50M annual passengers and raise total capacity from 60M to 80M passengers plus 6 Mt of freight when complete; opening date and current build status not confirmed this pass. 🟧
- **Our operation uses:** Not confirmed — international widebody service typically routes through T1/T2 and the S1/S2 satellite gates; confirm current gate/terminal assignment with handling. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** International arrivals clear immigration in the T1/T2 international halls or via the satellite-concourse connection; ZSPD is China's busiest international gateway (35.25M international passengers), so expect a substantial, well-staffed international arrivals flow, though exact hall/desk detail is not confirmed. 🟧
- **Notes:** 🟧 The Terminal 3 build-out (started 2021) is a multi-year, evolving programme — confirm current gate/concourse assignment with handling before finalising a stand plan, and expect this picture to keep shifting over the life of this brief.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** ZSPD is widely reported alongside Beijing Capital, Guangzhou Baiyun, Shenzhen Bao'an and Shanghai Hongqiao as a slot-coordinated Chinese hub; the exact IATA coordination level (2 vs 3) was not confirmed from a primary IATA list this pass. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **No published hard flight-movement curfew found** in reachable sources — the airport in fact markets an extended/night-flight service window (~01:00–06:00) as a passenger convenience, which is inconsistent with a blanket overnight closure. Treat as no confirmed curfew, but verify against AIP GEN 2.2 / AD 2 hours of operation before relying on it. 🟧
- **CTOT / flow control:** **China's centralised CAAC Air Traffic Flow Management (ATFM)** applies in full at this high-density field — expect CTOT slots, MINIT spacing, ground holding and short-notice flow restrictions, coordinated by the regional ATMB. See the [Asia airspace brief](../../../../airspace/asia.md) §6. 🟥
- **Commercial impact:** Schedule integrity matters at a slot-coordinated field, but the **dominant commercial risk driver here is CAAC flow control compounded by typhoon-season ground stops** (§10/§11), not a fixed curfew — build schedule buffer around both.

## 6. Load factors & seasonality

- **Demand peaks:** As China's busiest international gateway and the main hub for **China Eastern Airlines** (also parent of hub carrier **Shanghai Airlines**), plus a major international hub for **Air China** and secondary hub for **China Southern Airlines**, and home to LCC hub carriers **Juneyao Air** and **Spring Airlines**, ZSPD carries very high year-round scheduled demand; roughly half of the field's ~85M annual passengers (2025) are international.
- **Day-of-week / seasonal pattern:** Not independently sourced for ZSPD specifically; expect a business/leisure mix typical of a major East-Asian international gateway, with a pronounced seasonal dip risk during the Jul–Sep typhoon peak (§10). 🟧
- **Cargo / belly capacity:** ZSPD is the world's second-busiest cargo airport by tonnage (sources report 2024 volumes in the 3.4–3.8 Mt range; exact figure and world rank #2/#3 vary slightly between sources 🟧), and hosts dedicated Asia-Pacific freighter hubs for **FedEx** (Shanghai Hub, ~36,000 packages/hr, 66 weekly flights), **UPS** and **DHL** (Asia's largest express hub), plus the **PACTL** cargo terminal operator (~2M tonnes/year throughput across 70+ airlines). Confirm K Global belly-cargo uplift plans against payload at planning; no dedicated K Global freighter operation at ZSPD is confirmed this pass.
- **Connection banks:** Not independently sourced for ZSPD as a K Global destination; as a network destination (not a base), connection-bank timing here is set by the inbound/outbound route register rather than local hub-wave structure.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **NW-Pacific/East China Sea typhoon season (~May–Nov, peak Jul–Sep)** — a recurring, near-annual source of mass delay/cancellation at this field. Documented cases include **Typhoon Bebinca** (Sept 2024, essentially all flights cancelled), **Typhoon Chanthu** (Sept 2021, ~80% of flights cancelled from the morning of 13 Sept), **Typhoon Lekima** (2019, ~83% of PVG movements delayed an average 2 hours), and **Typhoon Noul** (2026, ~650 delays/60 cancellations at the field). Plan for the realistic possibility of a same-day ground stop in season.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** High — **China's centralised CAAC ATFM** (CTOT/MINIT/ground holding) applies routinely at this very-high-density, dual-airport-metroplex field (ZSPD shares the Shanghai ZSHA terminal airspace with Hongqiao/ZSSS, ~23 NM away). 🟥
- **On-time reliability picture:** Jul–Sep typhoon season carries the highest schedule-risk exposure; summer afternoon convective activity and the multi-year Terminal 3 transition are secondary risk factors. 🟧
