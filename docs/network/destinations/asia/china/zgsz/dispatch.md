## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** **Terminal 3** (opened 28 Nov 2013, designed by Studio Fuksas) is the field's sole passenger terminal — a distinctive "manta ray"/"giant flying fish" honeycomb-shelled design, 450,000 m² across six floors (four above, two below ground), with two long cross-shaped concourse arms (1,050 m and 650 m). It has 62–63 boarding gates and a stated design capacity of ~45 million passengers/year, handling roughly 14,000 pax/hour at peak. A satellite concourse (designed by Aedas, opened December 2021, directly north of the main building, ~22 million pax/year capacity) connects to the main terminal via an automated people mover (APM). Former Terminals A, B and D (all domestic/international, pre-2013) were fully closed on 27 Nov 2013 when Terminal 3 opened.
- **Our operation uses:** Terminal 3 and its satellite concourse — no other passenger terminal is in service at this field. 🟧 Specific gate/pier assignment for our operation not confirmed.
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** Located on the **second floor (2F) of Terminal 3**; reported queue peaks 09:00–11:00 and 20:00–22:00 local.
- **Notes:** 🟧 A new **Terminal 2 Complex** and **North Cargo Area** began construction in August 2025 as part of the airport's published **"3+3+3+1" master plan** (three terminal areas, three cargo areas, three runways, one satellite hall) targeting an ultimate 80 million passengers/4.5 million tonnes of cargo per year — confirm current construction-phase impact on terminal operations before relying on this page beyond its build date.

- **Curfew / night restriction:** No published night curfew or noise-based movement restriction found; cargo/customs operations are stated to run 24 hours. 🟧

## 6. Load factors & seasonality

- **Demand peaks:** ZGSZ is a major Chinese domestic and regional hub (52.7 million passengers in 2023 per CAAC data, the fourth-busiest airport in mainland China; 2024 passenger throughput exceeded 60 million, with 2025 expected to exceed 65 million) — treat as a high-volume, high-connectivity destination rather than a seasonal/leisure field. 🟧 (traffic figures are operator/CAAC-attributed via secondary reporting, not independently primary-sourced this pass).
- **Day-of-week / seasonal pattern:** Not independently sourced for ZGSZ specifically — treat as a standard major-hub business/leisure mix pending network-schedule confirmation. 🟧
- **Cargo / belly capacity:** ZGSZ has a **major cargo/logistics role** — it is the hub for cargo carrier **SF Airlines** (linked to the SF Express logistics group), an Asia-Pacific cargo hub for **UPS Airlines**, and hosts scheduled freighter service from China Southern Cargo, Ethiopian Cargo, FedEx and DHL among others. The field operates a dedicated **SF Express Cargo Park** and a **Bonded Logistics Center** offering 24-hour customs clearance, simplified transit procedures with Hong Kong (exempted from inspection at the Huanggang land port for air-to-land transit), and dangerous-goods/live-animal handling capability. This is a significant belly-cargo and freighter-interaction environment — confirm K Global belly-cargo uplift plans against payload at planning. See [`OM E — Fuel Policy`](../../../../../flight-ops/fuel-policy.md) for the tankering-angle interaction with cargo payload.
- **Connection banks:** Not independently sourced for ZGSZ specifically; as a non-base destination, connection-bank timing is set by the K Global network schedule rather than local demand data. 🟧

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Typhoon-season disruption (~May–Nov, peak Jul–Sep)** is the dominant, confirmed reliability threat at this field — within the past week of this brief's build, an active tropical system (Typhoon Noul) produced roughly 455 delays and 409 cancellations at ZGSZ alone, as part of a 36-airport South China disruption event. A secondary, distinct driver is **winter radiation fog/haze**, with low-visibility events concentrated in the 02:00–08:00 local (CST) window per a published meteorological study of this field.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** High — CAAC centralised ATFM/flow control applies across the congested Pearl River Delta cluster, and tightens sharply during any tracked typhoon system or major fog event. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Treat the Jul–Sep peak-typhoon window and the winter early-morning fog window as the two highest schedule-risk periods at this field. 🟧
