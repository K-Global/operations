## 2. Terminals & concourses

- **Terminal layout:** **Terminal 1** (part of the original 2004 build; Main Terminal plus Areas A and B, East Piers 1/2 dedicated to international flights since Jan 2016) — **closed for renovation from 02:00 local, 7 May 2026**, with its flights redistributed to Terminal 2 or Terminal 3. **Terminal 2** (opened 26 Apr 2018, ~808,700 m², one of the largest airport terminal facilities in the world, designed for 45M+ pax/year across four levels) — western "B-gates" domestic-only, eastern "A-gates" international-only, with swing gates 168–173 able to serve either. **Terminal 3** (opened 30 Oct 2025 alongside the fifth runway, ~422,000 m², BIM-designed with a "flower" architectural theme and China's first open-air airport observation deck) — the newest facility, located near the airport's eastern (03/21 runway) side.
- **Our operation uses:** Terminal 2 (A-gates) and/or Terminal 3 depending on current gate assignment; confirm with handling given the ongoing Terminal 1 renovation transition. 🟧
- **International arrivals / CBP-equivalent hall:** International arrivals clear passport control in Terminal 2's A-gate concourse or in Terminal 3; free inter-terminal shuttle bus and intercity rail connect all three terminals — build connection time accordingly for any onward network leg, particularly to/from Terminal 3, which is **not yet served by Guangzhou Metro** (Line 22 extension planned).
- **Notes:** 🟧 The terminal programme is **mid-transition in 2026** — Terminal 1 closed 7 May 2026, its associated Baiyun Airport South railway station and Guangzhou Metro Airport South station both closed concurrently for the renovation period. Confirm current gate/terminal assignment with handling before finalising a stand plan.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** **IATA Level 3** — fully slot-coordinated. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **None.** The current airport was purpose-built without the former Baiyun site's curfews, enabling round-the-clock, including overnight, scheduled operations — a genuine commercial asset for schedule flexibility relative to curfewed hubs elsewhere in the network. 🟩
- **CTOT / flow control:** CAAC's strict, centralised Air Traffic Flow Management regime (CTOT/MINIT-style ground-holding and short-notice flow restriction) applies as standard practice across Chinese continental airspace — see the [Asia airspace brief](../../../../airspace/asia.md) §6. Compounded locally by the field's own dynamic five-runway allocation strategy (Briefing §3.2). 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** The absence of a curfew removes the single biggest scheduling constraint seen at several other network fields, but **Level 3 slot compliance and CAAC ATFM exposure** mean on-time pushback and route-wide schedule integrity still carry real network consequences, particularly during Pearl River Delta peak-traffic periods and typhoon season (§10/§11).

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Typhoon season (~May–Nov, peak Jul–Sep)** — the field's direct South China Sea exposure has produced full-stop operational suspensions and hundreds of cancellations in single events (Typhoon Ragasa, Sep 2025: 150+ cancellations, ~20% of the day's schedule at peak impact; Typhoon Noul, Jul 2026: 681 delays / 308 cancellations in one event).
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** High — CAAC's centralised ATFM regime applies across Chinese continental airspace, and the field's own Pearl River Delta multi-airport TMA congestion (Briefing §3.2) compounds exposure to ground-holding and flow restriction, particularly during the ongoing five-runway operational-mode transition. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Typhoon season (May–Nov) carries the highest schedule-risk exposure at this field; the Terminal 1-closure transition period (2026) adds a secondary, terminal/gate-assignment-driven reliability variable. 🟧
