## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** 🟧 INS-checkpoint data (AIP AD 2.8) shows numerous bays across Aprons A–D, the cargo apron (501–525) and remote parking (601–615) rated up to B77W/B748/B744, with several — including C2, C3, D6, 607, 615 and 522–525 — rated to **A380**. A genuine Code E/F stand set exists at the field; exact assignment for our operation is not confirmed.
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** Not applicable at Category L; note for situational awareness that **A380 is prohibited on TWY E, P6, P9, P10, Q6 and Q7**, relevant to shared ground routing.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** Remote-parking apron 601–615 (PCN 99/107, R/B–C/W/T) provides additional widebody-rated positions away from the main terminal aprons.
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Not confirmed — expect either a contact or remote-parking position pending handling assignment. 🟧
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** The three AIP-published ground Hot Spots (cargo-apron runway crossings at TWY N11/L2 and N9/L1, and the service-road/TWY S8 crossing of RWY 05R/23L) affect ground routing between the remote/cargo aprons and the runway system — see [Briefing §13](index.md).

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** 🟧 Not confirmed as formally IATA-leveled in reachable sources. RCTP instead publishes scheduled runway-closure maintenance windows (Briefing §7) and specific ATC flow-management windows (radar departures 0000–0300 & 0600–1000 UTC daily) to manage throughput.
- **Curfew / night restriction:** None found as a hard movement ban; noise-driven restricted windows apply — no engine ground-run 1600–2200 UTC, training/test flights only 1700–2200 UTC daily. 🟧
- **CTOT / flow control:** ATC "may initiate radar departures" in the stated windows to expedite flow and reduce ground delay — a flow-management tool, not a confirmed ATFM/CTOT programme; verify current practice. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** Absent a confirmed slot/curfew regime, the two schedule-shaping factors identified are the noise-driven ground-run/test-flight windows and, in season, typhoon-driven disruption risk (§6/§10) — build buffer around both.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** 🟥 **Typhoon season (~Jun–Oct, peak Jul–Sep)** — Western Pacific tropical cyclones routinely force ground stops, terminal-area re-routing and network-wide schedule disruption across Taiwan.
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** 🟧 ATC-initiated radar-departure flow windows (0000–0300 & 0600–1000 UTC daily) suggest routinely congested departure flow at peak times; scheduled runway-closure maintenance windows (Briefing §7) further constrain throughput at fixed times of day.
- **On-time reliability picture:** 🟧 Typhoon-season days and any active scheduled-closure/LVP window carry the highest schedule-risk exposure at this field.
