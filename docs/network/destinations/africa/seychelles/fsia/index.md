## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain 🟥
FSIA sits on the low SE coast of Mahé, largely on land reclaimed during the airport's construction/expansion. Mahé's interior, however, rises quickly to the **Morne Seychellois massif (~905 m/2,969 ft)**, inland and to the west/northwest of the field — a real close-in terrain consideration on that side despite the airport's own sea-level elevation. Exact MSA sectors and quadrant heights are **not confirmed this pass** — read the current area/approach chart rather than estimating.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic 🟧
ATS is provided within the **Seychelles FIR (FSSS)** — one of the SW Indian Ocean island FIRs alongside Antananarivo (FMMM) and Mauritius (FIMM) (see the [Africa Airspace brief](../../../../airspace/africa.md)), generally worked procedurally across the region's oceanic tracts. Exact control type and terminal-airspace classification at FSIA itself are **not confirmed this pass** — verify current AIP/chart. A domestic terminal a short distance north of the international terminal generates frequent inter-island traffic (historically up to a departure every 10–15 minutes at peak) — expect a busy local circuit alongside international arrivals/departures.

### 3.3 Runway excursion 🟧
Single runway **13/31, 2,987 × 46 m concrete**, with a **displaced threshold of ~305 m (1,001 ft) reported on both ends** by tier-4 sources — this materially reduces the usable landing distance relative to the gross runway length. Exact declared TORA/TODA/ASDA/LDA figures and any PCN/contamination data are **not confirmed this pass** — do not assume the full geometric length is available without confirming the current AIP.

### 3.4 Weather threat 🟩
Seychelles' equatorial position (~4–5°S) places it largely **outside the main SW Indian Ocean tropical-cyclone belt** that affects Mauritius, Réunion and Madagascar further south — a materially different seasonal risk profile from the rest of this network. The dominant seasonal pattern is monsoon-driven wind reversal: a **NW monsoon (≈ Dec–Mar)**, generally wetter and squally, and **SE trade winds (≈ May–Sep)**, generally drier. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations 🟥
A **Seychelles Public Defence Force (SPDF) military base** sits at the SE end of RWY 13, on an island joined to Mahé during the airport's construction — a genuine co-location to brief, distinct from a purely civil field. The domestic-terminal inter-island traffic flow (§3.2) adds local-circuit density beyond the international schedule. Runway-length/displaced-threshold detail (§3.3) should be confirmed before planning a performance-limited operation.

## 4. Cautions & Warnings

- **Displaced threshold ~305 m (1,001 ft) on both RWY 13 and RWY 31** — confirm actual usable landing distance before relying on the gross 2,987 m runway length.
- **Morne Seychellois massif (~905 m/2,969 ft)** rises close inland/west of the field — do not shortcut visually toward high ground on that side.
- **Military base (SPDF) co-located at the SE end of RWY 13** — a real ground-operations/airspace-sharing consideration, not just a civil field.
- Busy **domestic inter-island traffic** from the terminal just north of the international terminal — expect local-circuit density.
- Seychelles is largely **outside the main cyclone belt**, but monitor SW Indian Ocean regional advisories as a matter of course given the network's broader exposure.
