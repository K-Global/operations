## 3. Stands / gates (by code letter & our fleet)

- **Widebody (Code E/F) stands:** Multiple apron groups accommodate Code E/F; push-back is mandatory for aircraft ≥12,000 kg or wingspan >24 m at A/B/C/D/E/F/G aprons, except designated self-manoeuvring stands (C9–C12, C13–C24, D31–D48, G1/G3/G5, F1–F7 — within wingspan limits). 🟧
- **Stands NOT usable by our types:** C Apron is Class C-and-smaller only (max B737-900) — do not plan a widebody there. Taxiway M is Code C maximum (wingspan <36 m / outer main gear <9 m) with all aircraft towed in/out of Apron M.
- **Remote / hardstand positions:** Not detailed in reachable sources. 🟧
- **Contact vs remote for our arrivals:** Expect a contact widebody gate for a scheduled home-base turn; confirm with handling.
- **Ground-movement stand caveats:** Code F aircraft restrictions apply on Taxiway A past D Apron when other Code F traffic is present (§13 of Briefing); jet-blast cautions at multiple taxiway/runway crossing points.

## 4. Slots, curfew & CTOT regime

- **Slot coordination level:** Not confirmed in reachable AIP extract — treat as uncoordinated pending confirmation. 🟧
- **Curfew / night restriction:** **No hard curfew** — H24 AD/ATS operation. The operative night restriction is **no runway/taxiway-intersection takeoffs and no engine run-ups between 2000–0400**.
- **CTOT / flow control:** Not confirmed — no ATFM programme found in reachable sources for FAOR specifically. 🟧
- **Commercial impact:** With no hard curfew, the base's principal scheduling guardrail is the **hot-and-high performance regime** (reduced RTOW on hot afternoons) rather than a slot/curfew constraint — see [Briefing §3.5](index.md).

## 5. Handling agents & turnaround

- **Handling agent(s):** **Bidair, Menzies, Swissport** all present at the field (per AIP AD 2.4 cargo-handling contacts); specific agent contracted for K Global operations not confirmed. 🟧
- **Typical turnaround time for our type:** VAMSYS mirror gives **taxi-in 15 min / taxi-out 18 min** for planning purposes `[VAMSYS mirror 2026-07-26]` — build the gate turn around these plus standard widebody service times.
- **Services:** Push-back (mandatory at most widebody stands), GPU/PCA, water/lav, catering and cabin services all expected at a home-base Code E/F stand.
- **Turnaround risk items:** Hot-afternoon performance planning (weight/fuel trade may affect load planning timing) and the rotating nightly runway-pair maintenance closures (§3.3 of Briefing) are the field's distinctive turnaround-risk items beyond standard ground-handling variability.

## 7. Fuel

- **Supplier / into-plane:** **ORTAFS** — Jet A-1 only.
- **Uplift availability & hours:** H24 confirmed per AIP; hydrant system 3,000 L/min on all aprons except M, plus four bowsers (60,000 L total capacity) and one 20,000 L de-fuel truck.
- **Price / tankering angle:** As the home base, FAOR is typically the tankering reference point for outbound network legs. See [`OM E — Fuel Policy`](../../../../../flight-ops/fuel-policy.md).
- **Quality / freeze notes:** Standard Jet A-1; no cold-soak/fuel-freeze consideration specific to FAOR itself.

## 10. Typical delays / reliability

- **Signature delay driver:** **Afternoon convective thunderstorm activity** in the Oct–Apr season, and the **hot-and-high performance regime** constraining weight/schedule on hot afternoons — see [Briefing §3.5](index.md).
- **Ground-delay / flow-program exposure:** Not confirmed — no ATFM programme found in reachable sources for FAOR specifically. 🟧
- **On-time reliability picture:** Summer afternoon convective weather is the highest-probability reliability risk at this base; winter is generally stable dry-season flying. 🟧

---

## 11. Curfew & noise-charge commercial impact

- **Noise-charge regime:** No specific noise-charge/QC scheme found in reachable sources beyond the general intersection-takeoff/run-up night restriction (2000–0400). 🟧
- **Curfew infringement consequence:** No hard curfew exists; the operative restriction (no intersection takeoffs/run-ups 2000–0400) carries no confirmed financial-charge consequence in reachable sources.
- **Scheduling guardrails:** The principal scheduling guardrail at this base is **hot-and-high performance planning** rather than curfew/noise — build weight/fuel margin into hot-afternoon rotations rather than schedule-timing buffer.
