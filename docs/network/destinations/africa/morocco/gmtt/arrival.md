## 5. Approach hazards (terrain / CFIT / specific threats)

- **CFIT / terrain:** 🟥 Rif foothill terrain rises close-in to the south/southeast of the field. On **23 December 1973**, a Royal Air Maroc-operated Sud Aviation Caravelle (on lease) crashed near the airport during a night, rain-affected approach to **RWY 28** after turning too far east of course and overflying the high terrain in that sector — all 106 aboard were lost `[Wikipedia, sourced onward to Aviation Safety Network]`. Fly the current MSA/terrain chart precisely on any RWY 28 approach, especially at night or in reduced visibility, and do not improvise a turn toward the high ground on a go-around. Cross-ref [GMTT Briefing §3.1](index.md).
- **Specific threats:** No closely-spaced parallel, offset, or converging-approach configuration exists (single runway pair). Wake-turbulence spacing is standard. A November 1988 landing accident involving a Gibraltar Airways Vickers Viscount (damaged beyond economic repair) is also on record at this field `[Wikipedia]` — no further operational detail obtained; treat as a general reminder of the runway-excursion caution in [GMTT Briefing §3.3](index.md).
- **Airspace / traffic:** Control type (procedural vs radar) not confirmed — verify current AIP. The field's proximity to the Strait of Gibraltar and the Gibraltar/Spanish airspace environment across the water is a general situational-awareness item — see [GMTT Briefing §3.2](index.md) and [Africa — General Airspace](../../../../airspace/africa.md).

## 10. Gotchas

- **Terrain south/southeast of the field is real and historically significant** — the 1973 CFIT accident occurred on the RWY 28 approach; brief this specifically before any RWY 28 arrival in marginal visibility or at night.
- **RWY 28's 500 m displaced threshold** — confirm the correct landing point is used; do not assume full physical runway length is available.
- Runway **07/25 is permanently closed** — confirm no chart or FMS reference treats it as usable.
- Strait of Gibraltar **gap-wind effect** can produce a late, sharp wind-direction change — hold a mental "runway change" contingency on approach.
- No crosswind-runway alternative exists at the field itself if 10/28 becomes unfavourable.
