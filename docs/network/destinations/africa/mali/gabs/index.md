## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain 🟧
Bamako–Sénou sits at 1,247 ft AMSL. While the general Sahel terrain is not classed as a high belt in the regional airspace picture, a historical accident record is a relevant data point: on 24 July 1971 a scheduled DC-3 crashed into a hill approximately 67 seconds after a night takeoff from runway 24, with the loss of all six occupants. This indicates locally significant relief near the field that is not independently quantified in this research pass — verify the current MSA/obstacle picture on the chart and treat this as a live watch-item rather than a cleared non-factor.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic 🟧
Control-type continuity (procedural vs radar) is not independently confirmed this pass. Per the [Africa airspace brief](../../../../airspace/africa.md) §9, Mali (with Niger and Burkina Faso) sits within the Sahel's documented elevated-risk belt, including a history of ATC disruption. Confirm current ATS provision at planning rather than assuming baseline availability.

### 3.3 Runway excursion 🟧
Single asphalt runway, 3,200 × 45 m. A documented historical precedent exists for wet-weather runway loss of control: on 30 June 1996 a Boeing 707 became unstable shortly after landing during a sudden burst of rain and veered off the runway, striking a bunker and detaching a wing (all four occupants survived; the aircraft was written off). Treat wet-runway braking action and directional control in sudden rain as a standing, evidence-based watch-item at this field.

### 3.4 Weather threat 🟥
**Harmattan dust** (roughly Nov–Mar) is a significant, durable visibility hazard at this inland Sahel field. Wet-season convective activity (roughly Jun–Sep) brings the sudden-onset rain risk documented in §3.3. Re-check both at §14/§15.

### 3.5 Operational considerations 🟥
This field's overriding operational consideration is **security, not procedure**. The airport is adjacent to **Base Aérienne 101** (Malian Air Force) and has suffered **two confirmed direct armed attacks**:
- **17 September 2024:** JNIM (Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal Muslimin) militants stormed the airport as part of coordinated attacks across multiple locations in Bamako. The group claimed to have destroyed aircraft and opened fire at the presidential hangar; an aircraft used for humanitarian work by the World Food Programme was also damaged. Wider reporting on the coordinated Bamako attacks that day put the combined toll at more than 70 killed.
- **25 April 2026:** the airport was attacked again by JNIM as part of a larger coordinated offensive across Mali.

**This is a live, evolving security picture, not historical background — re-check the current NOTAM and security-advisory status (ICAO CZIR / EASA CZIB / Safe Airspace / OPSGROUP) before every single operation into this field**, independent of anything else in this durable brief.
