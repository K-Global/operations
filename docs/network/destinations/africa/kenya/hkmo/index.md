## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain  🟩
HKMO sits at 200 ft AMSL on the low-lying Kenyan coast near Port Reitz/Kilindini Harbour — there is **no significant close-in high terrain** relevant to arrival, departure or missed-approach paths. This is a non-issue field for terrain; verify the MSA ring on the current chart as routine practice, but CFIT is not the operative threat here (contrast with HKJK's highland CFIT profile).

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic  🟧
Traffic density and control type (procedural vs radar) are not independently confirmed in reachable public sources; Mombasa's terminal airspace sits within the wider **Nairobi FIR (HKNA)** overlay structure — cross-ref the [Africa Airspace brief](../../../../airspace/africa.md) for the continental picture and re-verify local control type/frequencies against the current AIP. A **security note**: in 2002, two shoulder-launched (MANPADS) missiles were fired at a departing airliner from just outside the airport perimeter; neither hit the aircraft. There is no current standing restriction tied to this event, but it is a recognised historical threat-awareness item for departures from this field — maintain standard SA and defensive-departure awareness per company policy. 🟧
### 3.3 Runway excursion  🟧
Main runway **03/21 (3,350 × 46 m, lighted)** is long relative to our fleet's needs at sea level and not expected to be performance-limiting; declared distances (TORA/TODA/ASDA/LDA) are **not published in reachable sources** — verify before treating any figure as authoritative. The secondary runway **15/33** (~1,260–1,363 m, unlighted, source figures disagree — see §18) is not relevant to a scheduled K Global operation on this fleet.

### 3.4 Weather threat  🟧
Coastal tropical station under the **Indian Ocean monsoon regime**: the **Kaskazi (NE monsoon, roughly Dec–Mar)** brings drier conditions, while the **Kusi (SE monsoon, roughly Apr–Nov)** brings the wetter, more convective pattern with the "long rains" embedded in Apr–Jun. Afternoon coastal convective showers and gusty sea-breeze effects are the standing weather threat; the field sits at the **northern edge of the SW Indian Ocean tropical-cyclone belt** (cyclones more typically affect Madagascar/Mozambique further south) — treat direct-cyclone exposure as low but re-check seasonal advisories (Nov–Apr) at planning. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations  🟧
Sea-level field — **no density-altitude/hot-and-high consideration** (a direct contrast to HKJK, HAAB and other East-African highland hubs). The main planning gaps are administrative rather than performance-related: RFF category, declared distances, PCN, exact ATS hours and control type are all unconfirmed in reachable public sources and should be verified against the KCAA eAIP or an equivalent current-AIRAC source before this brief is treated as Verified.
