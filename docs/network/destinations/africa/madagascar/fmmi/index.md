## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain 🟥
Ivato sits on Madagascar's **central highlands plateau at ~1,280 m (4,198 ft)** — Antananarivo itself is famously built across a series of hills, and the surrounding highland terrain rolls higher still in places. This is fundamentally a **high-elevation, elevated-terrain environment**, not a coastal/low-lying field — treat MSA and any driftdown/escape planning accordingly and read exact grid-MORA figures off the current area chart; specific per-quadrant terrain heights are **not confirmed in this research pass** (primary AIP unreachable). See [`OM E — High-Elevation Aerodrome Operations`](../../../../../flight-ops/high-elevation-aerodrome-operations.md).

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic 🟧
ATS at Ivato is provided by **ASECNA** within the **Antananarivo FIR (FMMM)** — one of ASECNA's six member FIRs alongside Dakar Oceanic/Terrestre, Niamey, N'Djamena and Brazzaville (see the [Africa Airspace brief](../../../../airspace/africa.md)). As Madagascar's principal international gateway, expect the busiest traffic mix on the island here; exact control type (radar vs procedural) and terminal airspace classification at the field are **not confirmed this pass** — verify current AIP/chart.

### 3.3 Runway excursion 🟧
Single runway **11/29, 3,100 × 45 m asphalt**, no displaced threshold reported by tier-4 sources. Declared distances (TORA/TODA/ASDA/LDA), PCN and any contamination/braking-action data are **not confirmed this pass** — do not assume full geometric length is usable without confirming the current AIP.

### 3.4 Weather threat 🟥
Antananarivo's **subtropical highland climate** brings a warm, wet season (≈ November–April) with afternoon convective thunderstorms, and a cooler, drier season (≈ May–October) that can bring morning mist/radiation fog at this elevation. This is general regional climatology, not a field-specific sourced figure this pass — confirm seasonal risk against current METAR/TAF history and SIGMET at planning. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations 🟥
The dominant, durable planning factor at Ivato is its **high elevation (4,198 ft)** combined with warm-season temperatures — a genuine density-altitude/hot-and-high performance consideration for any of our types, distinct from the coastal/sea-level profile of FIMP, FMEE and FSIA. Treat every departure planning cycle here as a high-elevation performance case; cross-ref [`OM E — High-Elevation Aerodrome Operations`](../../../../../flight-ops/high-elevation-aerodrome-operations.md).

## 4. Cautions & Warnings

- **High-elevation field (4,198 ft)** — plan density-altitude/hot-and-high performance for every departure, especially in the warm wet season.
- Central-highlands/hilly terrain surrounds the field — do not descend below MSA early; exact figures not confirmed this pass, read the current area chart. 🟧
- **Single runway (11/29)** — no reciprocal-runway fallback if the assigned runway is unusable.
- Cool-season (≈ May–Oct) morning mist/fog is plausible at this elevation — confirm current METAR/TAF trend before an early-morning arrival. 🟧
- Warm-season (≈ Nov–Apr) afternoon convective storms are a recognised regional pattern — build weather-avoidance margin into afternoon arrivals/departures.
