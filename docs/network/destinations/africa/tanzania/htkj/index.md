## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain  🟥
HTKJ sits at a modest 2,932 ft, but it is bracketed by two major volcanic massifs: **Mount Kilimanjaro** (Africa's highest peak, ~19,341 ft / ~5,895 m) roughly to the east-northeast, and **Mount Meru** (~14,977 ft / ~4,565 m) to the west. Unlike a hot-and-high field, the threat here is **close-in high terrain against a low field elevation** — a very large vertical differential in a short lateral distance. Read the MSA ring and obstacle table on the current-AIRAC AD/area chart; do not descend below MSA/charted step-downs early, and treat any visual manoeuvring near the mountains with a wide margin.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic  🟧
HTKJ is **Approach-controlled** (Kilimanjaro Approach); the field lies in the **Dar es Salaam FIR** (HTDC) per current network allocation, close to the Kenya border and the Nairobi (HKNA) FIR boundary — cross-border coordination/handoff procedures are plausible but not confirmed in reachable sources. See [Africa — General Airspace Notes](../../../../airspace/africa.md). Traffic density is moderate (regional/international gateway, not a super-hub).

### 3.3 Runway excursion  🟧
Single runway 09/27, 3,600 m, hard-surfaced — ample length for any K Global type; no displaced-threshold or per-direction declared-distance data was found in reachable public sources, so treat TORA/TODA/ASDA/LDA as **not independently confirmed** pending an AIP cross-check.

### 3.4 Weather threat  🟧
Northern Tanzania follows a **bimodal rainfall pattern**: the *masika* long rains (roughly March–May) and the *vuli* short rains (roughly October–December), both ITCZ-driven, with drier conditions in between. The proximity of Kilimanjaro and Meru can generate **local orographic convection, low cloud on the peaks, and terrain-induced wind effects** in the vicinity of the field — treat as a standing caution rather than a quantified figure pending a dedicated climatology source. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations  🟧
The defining consideration at HTKJ is **terrain awareness rather than aircraft performance** — the runway and elevation are non-limiting, but the surrounding high peaks demand disciplined MSA/step-down compliance on arrival, departure and any missed approach. Approach names, minima, and declared distances are unconfirmed in reachable public sources — pull the current AIRAC cycle before planning.

## 4. Cautions & Warnings

- **Mount Kilimanjaro (~19,341 ft) and Mount Meru (~14,977 ft) both close to the field** — do not descend below MSA early; give high ground a wide margin, especially in marginal visibility.
- **A documented navigation error at a nearby airfield** underscores the value of positive runway identification in this terrain-close environment — verify you are tracking to HTKJ, not a similarly-oriented nearby strip, before committing to the approach.
- Approach names/minima, declared distances, RFF category and ATC frequency detail are **unverified in reachable public sources** — flag and verify on the current AIRAC/AIP.
- East African bimodal rains (masika Mar–May, vuli Oct–Dec) bring convective activity — check current wx closely in season.
