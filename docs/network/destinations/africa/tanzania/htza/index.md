## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain  🟩
HTZA sits at 54 ft AMSL on **Unguja Island**, a flat, low-lying coral-limestone landmass — there is **no close-in high terrain** relevant to arrival, departure or missed-approach paths. This is a non-issue field for terrain; verify the MSA ring on the current chart as routine practice, but CFIT is not the operative threat here.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic  🟧
A tier-4 secondary source reports both Approach and Tower on the same frequency (121.200), which may reflect a genuine combined ATC position at a moderate-traffic island gateway, or simply incomplete source data — **needs primary confirmation.** The field lies in the **Dar es Salaam FIR** (HTDC) — see [Africa — General Airspace Notes](../../../../airspace/africa.md). Traffic mix likely includes significant regional/charter and general-aviation movements given the tourism role of the island.

### 3.3 Runway excursion  🟧
Single runway 18/36, 3,022 m, hard-surfaced. This is the **shortest runway among the network's current East-Africa fields** (cf. HAAB ~3,700–3,800 m, HTKJ 3,600 m) — while non-limiting for most operations, confirm weight/performance margins for the heaviest widebody legs and check for any displaced threshold or contamination note; no per-direction declared-distance data was found in reachable public sources.

### 3.4 Weather threat  🟧
Zanzibar follows the same **bimodal East African rainfall pattern** as the mainland — *masika* long rains (~Mar–May, often the heaviest) and *vuli* short rains (~Oct–Dec). The island's low latitude (~6°S) sits outside the core Southwest Indian Ocean tropical-cyclone belt (systems typically intensify further south, e.g. the Mozambique Channel/Madagascar), which moderates but does not eliminate exposure to heavy rain bands and squalls associated with nearby tropical systems in season. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations  🟧
The defining considerations at HTZA are the **comparatively short runway** (3,022 m — confirm performance margins) and the **coastal/island environment** (humidity, sea-breeze effects, possible bird activity near the shoreline) rather than terrain or altitude. Approach names, minima and declared distances are unconfirmed in reachable public sources — pull the current AIRAC cycle before planning.
