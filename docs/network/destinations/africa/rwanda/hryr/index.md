## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain  🟥
Kigali sits at **4,859 ft** amid Rwanda's characteristic hilly terrain ("land of a thousand hills") — close-in high ground surrounds the Kigali basin in most quadrants. Further NW, near the Rwanda–DRC border, the **Virunga volcanic massif** (including the active **Nyiragongo** volcano near Goma) rises well above the surrounding terrain and is a recognised regional hazard for both terrain and volcanic-ash purposes (see the [Africa Airspace brief §7/§11](../../../../airspace/africa.md) and [`OM E — Volcanic Ash Avoidance`](../../../../../flight-ops/volcanic-ash-avoidance.md)). Numeric MSA/obstacle data was not obtained in this pass — pull the current AIRAC's MSA rings and area chart before planning; do not descend below MSA/charted step-downs early on either runway end, and brief the missed approach with terrain specifically in mind.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic  🟧
Kigali is RwandAir's home hub with a mix of scheduled international/regional and general-aviation traffic (Akagera Aviation, business-jet operators); control type (radar vs procedural) is not independently confirmed. A **historical security note**: on 6 April 1994, an aircraft carrying two regional heads of state was shot down on final approach to **RWY 28**, an event that preceded the outbreak of the Rwandan genocide. There is no current standing operational restriction tied to this event, but it is a recognised historical threat-awareness reference for approaches to this runway end — maintain standard SA per company security policy. 🟧

### 3.3 Runway excursion  🟧
Single runway **10/28 (3,500 × 45 m)** with displaced thresholds each end (**≈700 ft/215 m on 28**, **≈387 ft/118 m on 10** per SkyVector) — the **usable landing distance is measurably less than the full physical runway length**, and this interacts with the field's high elevation (reduced margin, higher true groundspeed at touchdown). Declared distances (TORA/TODA/ASDA/LDA) are **not published in reachable sources** — do not assume the full 3,500 m is usable in either direction; verify against a primary AIP/current-AIRAC source before treating any figure as authoritative.

### 3.4 Weather threat  🟧
Equatorial highland climate with **two wet seasons** — the "long rains" (roughly Mar–May) and the "short rains" (roughly Sep–Dec) — bringing afternoon convective build-ups typical of high-elevation East-African terrain. Terrain-induced wind and turbulence from the surrounding hills is a plausible local effect; not independently quantified this pass. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations  🟥
**High-elevation field (4,859 ft)** — density-altitude penalties on take-off performance and climb-gradient compliance over the surrounding hilly terrain are the dominant planning consideration at HRYR, compounded by the **displaced-threshold reduction in usable landing distance each runway end**. See [`OM E — High-Elevation Aerodrome Operations`](../../../../../flight-ops/high-elevation-aerodrome-operations.md) for the methodology applied across the network's high-elevation stations (HKJK, HAAB, FAOR and others). RFF category, exact declared distances, PCN and control type remain unconfirmed pending primary AIP access.
