## 9. Performance watch-items for our types

- **Field-length / weight:** 🟧 RWY 10/28 is 3,500 m physical, but **displaced thresholds each end (≈387 ft on 10, ≈700 ft on 28)** reduce the usable take-off/landing distance — do not plan against the full physical length. Combined with the field's high elevation, this is a genuine performance-planning item.
- **Density altitude / temperature:** 🟥 **Hot-and-high field (4,859 ft)** — this is the dominant performance consideration at HRYR. Check take-off weight, V-speeds and climb-gradient compliance over the surrounding hilly terrain against actual OAT/density altitude for every departure.
- **Contamination / wet-runway:** The "long rains" (Mar–May) and "short rains" (Sep–Dec) bring afternoon convective showers — factor wet-runway braking action in season; no published contamination-specific data.
- **Link to OM B:** See [`OM B — Fleet Capability Matrix`](../../../../../fleet/capability-matrix.md) and [`OM E — High-Elevation Aerodrome Operations`](../../../../../flight-ops/high-elevation-aerodrome-operations.md) for the hot-and-high performance methodology applied at this field.

## 10. Gotchas

- **Hot-and-high field (4,859 ft)** — density-altitude take-off performance and climb-gradient margins are the standing planning risk, compounded by the displaced-threshold-reduced usable runway length.
- **Displaced thresholds each end** (≈387 ft on 10, ≈700 ft on 28) — do not plan against the full 3,500 m physical runway.
- **Single runway (10/28)**, no parallel or crossing runway — expect sequencing delay at busy periods.
- **Virunga volcanic massif (Nyiragongo) to the NW** near the DRC border — terrain and volcanic-ash relevance for any departure routed that direction.
- **Historical 1994 shoot-down on final approach to RWY 28** — an arrival-side event, but a useful situational-awareness cue for crews working this runway environment.
