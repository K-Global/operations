## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain 🟩
DNKN sits at 1,562 ft AMSL on the northern Nigeria savanna plain. No close-in high terrain relevant to arrival, departure or missed-approach paths was found in reachable sources; verify the MSA ring on the current chart as routine practice.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic 🟧
Kano is worked within the northern half of the single **Kano (DNKK)** FIR by **Kano ACC**. Surveillance is radar-assisted around the field but the wider FIR is mixed radar/procedural, and Nigerian radar-serviceability has been publicly reported as a watch item. Cross-ref [Kano (DNKK) FIR brief](../../../../airspace/fir/africa/kano-dnkk.md) — the FIR brief also flags this northern region as carrying additional Sahel-border security-watch considerations relative to the southern fields.

### 3.3 Runway excursion 🟧
RWY 06/24 (the civil runway) shows no displaced threshold in the reachable extract, with TORA/LDA equal both ends (3,300 m). RWY 05/23 **does** carry a 150 m (492 ft) displaced threshold on RWY 05, reducing its landing distance to roughly 2,300 m in that direction, and is noted **VMC only** — this runway is associated with the co-located Nigerian Air Force base and is not the intended civil-traffic runway. Do not plan a scheduled widebody operation onto 05/23.

### 3.4 Weather threat 🟥
**Harmattan dust haze (≈ Nov–Mar) is the defining hazard at Kano** — the most northerly and most exposed of the three fields in this pack to the Sahara-fringe dry season. Public reporting describes visibility dropping to roughly 300 m or worse in severe events, with Nigeria's meteorological agency (NiMet) warning of widespread flight delays and cancellations during the season; dust haze can obscure runway markings and airfield lighting over a wide area, making visual reference extremely difficult. The wet season (≈ Apr–Oct) brings the counter-season convective-activity hazard typical of the West African interior. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations 🟥
Plan around: (1) **harmattan-season operational risk** as the standing seasonal threat at this field — treat any Nov–Mar operation as needing a firm low-visibility contingency; (2) the **co-located military runway (05/23)**, VMC-only and not for scheduled civil widebody use; (3) the **unset K Global aerodrome category**, meaning crew-qualification/category-restriction status is not yet reconciled — confirm before treating this as a routine destination; and (4) a positive recent data point: **NCAA issued Kano a fresh aerodrome certificate on 19 December 2025** under Nigeria's domesticated ICAO Annex 14, Volume I regulations, indicating the field's navigational aids (including ILS) were assessed as ICAO-compliant at that time.

## 4. Cautions & Warnings

- **Harmattan dust haze (Nov–Mar) can reduce visibility to a few hundred metres** — the single most important seasonal hazard at this field; build a firm low-visibility/diversion contingency for any dry-season operation.
- **RWY 05/23 is VMC-only and military-associated** (Nigerian Air Force base co-located on the south side) — do not plan scheduled widebody operations onto this runway; RWY 06/24 is the civil-traffic runway.
- **RWY 05/23 carries a 150 m displaced threshold on RWY 05** — reduces landing distance in that direction if ever considered.
- 🟧 **K Global aerodrome category is not yet set** — confirm crew-qualification/category status before treating this as a routine base or alternate.
- 🟧 **ILS runway assignment/ident/frequency not confirmed** — do not assume a specific ILS procedure without checking the current AIRAC.
- The two VAMSYS-preferred alternates (**DRRN** Niamey and **FTTJ** N'Djamena) are cross-border and materially distant — harmattan/Sahel weather can affect the whole corridor simultaneously; do not treat these as automatically clear when Kano itself is dust-affected.
