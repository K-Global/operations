## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain  🟥
Bole sits on the **Ethiopian Plateau at ≈7,656 ft**, in steep, high terrain. The **Entoto Mountains** north of the city reach ~3,200 m (~10,500 ft), and the broader highlands exceed 4,000 m. The combination of **very high terrain and high density altitude** (reduced climb performance) makes departures and missed approaches the critical CFIT phases — a degraded climb gradient over rising terrain is the threat. Read the MSA ring and obstacle table on the current-AIRAC AD/area chart; do not descend below MSA/charted step-downs early, and brief the missed-approach climb gradient before commencing any approach.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic  🟧
Major continental hub with **radar control** (Bole Approach). Two parallel runways. Bole CTR Class C (GND–14,000 ft); TMA Class A (FL145–FL240); **Addis Ababa FIR** Class G to FL660 outside controlled airspace. Expect structured flows and frequency changes; adjacent FIRs Khartoum, Nairobi, Asmara, Mogadishu. Cross-ref [Africa — General Airspace Notes](../../../../airspace/africa.md) for regional FIR/airspace context.

### 3.3 Runway excursion  🟧
Two parallel runways, 07L/25R (3,700 m) and 07R/25L (3,800 m), asphalt, with **displaced thresholds** on all ends (≈250–376 m). Runways are long because of the altitude — not normally length-limited, but **high density altitude raises true groundspeed at touchdown** so braking distances grow. Fly the correct (displaced) aiming point. Declared distances are provisional — verify per direction.

### 3.4 Weather threat  🟥
The **kiremt (main rainy season) runs Jun–mid-Sep**: heavy convective rain, embedded thunderstorms, and notably **morning fog/low cloud** that has repeatedly forced diversions at Bole. Dry season Oct–May. See §14.

### 3.5 Operational considerations  🟥
**High-elevation, high-terrain field.** At ≈7,656 ft pressure altitude plus typical 20–25 °C temps, **density altitude routinely exceeds 10,000 ft** — significantly reduced thrust, longer take-off rolls, reduced climb gradients (critical over surrounding terrain), and higher landing groundspeeds. Treat take-off performance limits and SID climb-gradient compliance as the headline planning items. Procedures (SIDs/STARs) are actively changing by NOTAM — pull the current AIRAC cycle. See [`OM E — High-Elevation Aerodrome Operations`](../../../../../flight-ops/high-elevation-aerodrome-operations.md) for the network-wide hot-and-high procedure.
