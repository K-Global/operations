## 3. Threat & Error Management (TEM)

### 3.1 CFIT / Terrain 🟩
DRRN sits at 732 ft AMSL near the Niger River, in the Niamey Capital District. There is no close-in high terrain relevant to arrival, departure or missed-approach paths — CFIT is not the operative threat at this field.

### 3.2 Airborne conflict / traffic 🟧
Niamey hosts one of **ASECNA's five continental air-traffic-zone centres** (confirmed via tier-4 corroboration), reflecting its regional ATC significance — but control-type continuity (procedural vs radar) is not independently confirmed this pass, and per the [Africa airspace brief](../../../../airspace/africa.md) §9, the Niamey/DRRR FIR sits within the Sahel's documented **history of ATC disruption**. Confirm current ATS provision at planning rather than assuming baseline availability.

### 3.3 Runway excursion 🟧
The active paved runway (09R/27L, 3,000 × 45 m bitumen, lighted) has no confirmed declared-distance or displaced-threshold detail this pass. The second runway (09L/27R, 3,200 × 30 m, unlit laterite surface) is **narrow, unpaved and unlit — not usable for network operations**; do not plan or brief it as an available alternate configuration.

### 3.4 Weather threat 🟥
**Harmattan dust** (roughly Nov–Mar) is a significant, durable visibility hazard at this inland Sahel field — materially more severe than at coastal West African fields. Wet-season convective activity (roughly Jun–Sep) is the secondary seasonal hazard. Re-check both at §14/§15.

### 3.5 Operational considerations 🟥
This field's overriding operational consideration is **security, not procedure**. Diori Hamani International Airport is co-located with **Base Aérienne 101**, a Nigerien Air Force base with a long history of foreign military presence (French, American, and — since 2024 — Russian forces have all used the base at various times; the U.S. completed its withdrawal in July 2024). The airport/airbase complex has suffered **two confirmed direct armed attacks in 2026**:
- **29 January 2026:** an Islamic State-linked assault on the base — heavy gunfire and blasts were reported near the airport, and FlightRadar24 data showed commercial aircraft diverting away from the field in real time. Nigerien authorities reported 20 attackers (including one foreign national) killed, 11 arrested, and 4 soldiers injured; satellite imagery confirmed damage to buildings in the military area.
- **18 June 2026:** a JNIM (Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal Muslimin) assault on the airport — fighting lasted nearly two hours before Nigerien security forces repelled the attackers and sealed the area. Eleven soldiers, two civilians and 22 attackers were killed, with 20 more suspects arrested.

**This is a live, evolving security picture, not historical background — re-check the current NOTAM and security-advisory status (ICAO CZIR / EASA CZIB / Safe Airspace / OPSGROUP) before every single operation into this field**, independent of anything else in this durable brief.
